"How much does a website cost in Nigeria?" is probably the most common question we get at Joetech. And it makes sense — every business needs a website in 2026, but the pricing landscape is opaque. Some developers quote ₦150,000. Others quote ₦5,000,000. Both can be right, depending on what you need.

This guide breaks down exactly what Nigerian businesses should expect to pay for a website in 2026, what determines the price, and the hidden costs most agencies won't mention until you're already committed.

## Why the Range Is So Wide

A website can cost anywhere from ₦150,000 to over ₦10,000,000 in Nigeria. Understanding why helps you budget properly and avoid paying for things you do not need — or getting a site that costs more in lost revenue than you saved on development.

The price depends on four main factors:

### 1. Type of Website

A simple landing page with five sections and a contact form is fundamentally different from a multi-vendor marketplace with payment integration, inventory management, and real-time shipping quotes. The more functionality you need, the more time it takes to build, test, and deploy.

### 2. Design Complexity

A custom design tailored to your brand identity takes longer than using a pre-built template. Custom illustrations, animations, interactive elements, and complex layouts all add to the cost.

### 3. Developer Expertise and Location

An experienced developer in Lagos who has delivered 50+ projects charges more than a student who built their first site last month. Agency rates are higher than freelancer rates, but you get project management, accountability, and typically better reliability. Location within Lagos also matters — developers on the Island often charge more than those on the Mainland, though rates are converging as remote work becomes standard.

### 4. Maintenance and Support

The cost of building the site is just the beginning. Hosting, domain renewal, security updates, content updates, and technical support are ongoing expenses. A reputable agency will be transparent about these from the start.

## Hidden Costs Most Agencies Don't Mention

We believe in transparency, so here are the costs you should budget for beyond the initial build.

### Domain Name (₦15,000 — ₦100,000/year)

A .com.ng domain costs around ₦6,000 — ₦10,000 per year. A .com domain is ₦15,000 — ₦25,000. Premium domains (short, memorable, keyword-rich) can cost significantly more — some sell for millions.

### Web Hosting (₦50,000 — ₦500,000/year)

Reliable hosting is non-negotiable. Cheap hosting (₦15,000/year) often means slow loading times and frequent downtime. For a business website, budget at least ₦50,000 — ₦120,000 per year for quality hosting. E-commerce sites and web apps need more robust hosting — ₦200,000 — ₦500,000/year.

### SSL Certificate (₦0 — ₦150,000/year)

Free SSL (Let's Encrypt) works fine for most sites. Premium SSL with extended validation starts at around ₦50,000 per year and is mainly needed for e-commerce sites handling sensitive customer data.

### Maintenance and Updates (₦50,000 — ₦300,000/year)

Websites need regular updates to remain secure and functional. This includes software updates, plugin updates, security monitoring, backups, and content changes. Some agencies include this in the initial cost; others charge separately. Always ask.

### Content Creation (₦50,000 — ₦500,000+)

Professional copywriting, photography, and videography are often treated as separate costs. If you need a copywriter to write your website content or a photographer for product images, budget accordingly.

## Budget Website vs Professional Website: The Real Cost Difference

You can get a website built for ₦150,000 from a junior developer or a freelancer on a gig platform. Many small businesses start here, and it can work — for a while. But there are trade-offs.

### What a ₦150,000 Website Typically Looks Like

- A pre-built template with your logo and colours swapped in
- Limited or no mobile optimisation
- Basic hosting (shared, often slow)
- No SEO foundation
- Minimal or no support after delivery
- No content strategy
- Generic stock images

### What a ₦500,000+ Website Typically Looks Like

- Custom or heavily customised design aligned with your brand
- Fully responsive on all devices
- Optimised for speed (Core Web Vitals)
- SEO foundation (structured data, meta tags, sitemap, schema markup)
- Ongoing support and maintenance agreement
- Content strategy and copywriting guidance
- Performance tracking and analytics

The ₦150,000 site may need to be rebuilt within 12 months as your business grows. The ₦500,000 site can scale with you for 3-5 years with regular updates. In the long run, the more expensive option is often cheaper.

## How to Budget for Your Website

Use this checklist to prepare before approaching a developer or agency:

1. **Define your requirements clearly** — List every feature you need. This is the single biggest factor in pricing.
2. **Set a realistic budget** — Use the ranges above as a guide and add 20% contingency.
3. **Ask about ongoing costs** — Hosting, domain, maintenance, content updates.
4. **Request a detailed proposal** — Itemised quotes make it easy to compare apples to apples.
5. **Plan for content creation** — Factor in the cost of copywriting, photography, and branding if needed.
6. **Think long-term** — A cheaper site that needs rebuilding in 12 months is more expensive than a quality site that lasts 3-5 years.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Website Costs in Nigeria

### Can I build a website myself to save money?

Yes, you can use DIY platforms like Wix, Squarespace, or WordPress.com to build a basic website for as little as ₦50,000 — ₦100,000 per year (subscription costs). However, DIY websites have limitations. They are harder to customise, often lack proper SEO foundations, and can look generic. For a business that needs to appear professional and rank on Google, the investment in a professional build usually pays for itself within months through increased credibility and traffic.

### Do I need to pay for a website every year?

The initial build is a one-time cost (with occasional redesigns every 3-5 years). The recurring costs are domain renewal, hosting, and optional maintenance. Budget ₦70,000 — ₦300,000 per year for these ongoing expenses depending on your hosting needs.

### Is ₦500,000 too much for a small business website?

It depends on what you need and what the website will deliver for you. If a website helps you gain just one new client per month at ₦50,000 profit, it pays for itself in 10 months. The question is not "is ₦500,000 too much?" but "what return on investment will this website generate?" A professionally built website is a revenue-generating asset, not an expense.

### What if I have a very tight budget?

If your budget is under ₦300,000, consider a phased approach. Start with a simple one-page landing page that establishes your online presence and captures leads. As your business grows, reinvest profits into expanding to a full multi-page site or e-commerce store. At Joetech, we offer flexible payment plans and can help you prioritise the most impactful features for your budget.

### What payment methods do developers in Nigeria accept?

Most Nigerian web developers accept bank transfers. Payment schedules are typically milestone-based: 30-50% deposit to start, 30-40% on design approval, and the remaining balance on completion. Be cautious of developers who demand 100% payment upfront — this is a red flag.
