Ask a Nigerian small business owner what she is doing on Thursday and the honest answer is often "I don't know yet — it depends on who calls." The week happens to her. Customers call, deliveries slip, a supplier shows up unannounced, NEPA takes light in the middle of a task, and by Friday she has worked very hard and cannot say what she actually achieved.

It does not have to be that way. Google Calendar is free, already on your phone, and it is the simplest planning tool a Nigerian SME can adopt. It does not need a laptop, a subscription, or a training course. It needs twenty minutes of setup and five minutes a day. In return, you get a week you can see, a day you can control, and a business that stops running on memory.

This guide shows you how to set up Google Calendar for your business, plan a realistic Nigerian week around traffic, power, market days and customers, and build a habit that actually sticks.

## Why Google Calendar Works for Nigerian Small Businesses

Most planning tools fail small businesses for the same reason: they cost money, need a laptop, or assume a stable office life. Google Calendar avoids all three problems.

- **It is free.** A Google account costs nothing, and Calendar comes with it. Paid planning apps charge monthly fees for features you will never use.
- **It lives on your phone.** The Calendar app runs on Android and iPhone on MTN, Airtel, Glo or 9mobile data. Your plan is in your pocket at the market, in the car, and at a client's office.
- **It works offline.** Events you have already created stay visible without network. When your data finishes or the network drops on the Lekki-Epe Expressway, your day is still there.
- **It reminds you.** Notifications arrive before an event, so you stop missing client calls, delivery windows and payment deadlines. A reminder at the right time is worth more than a perfect plan you never look at.
- **It syncs everywhere.** Change an event on your phone and it updates on your laptop, your tablet, and your assistant's phone if you share the calendar. One plan, everywhere at once.
- **It shares easily.** You can share a calendar with staff, a business partner, or your family, so everyone knows when you are busy and when you are free — without a phone call to ask.

The honest limit: Calendar is a planning tool, not a project manager. It will not run your inventory or chase your debtors for you. But for organising a week — who you will see, what you will do, and when you will do it — nothing beats it for the price, which is zero.

## Step 1: Set Up Google Calendar in Ten Minutes

Open the Calendar app on your phone, or go to calendar.google.com on a computer. Sign in with your Google account — the same one you use for Gmail. If you use a personal Gmail for business, consider creating a separate Google account for the business later, but do not let that delay you. Start with the account you have.

First, check your settings. Tap the menu, then Settings, then General. Set your time zone to West Africa Time (Lagos) so event times stay correct when you travel or when a client in Abuja sends you an invite. Set the week to start on Monday, which matches how most Nigerian businesses think about the week. Set the default event duration to 30 minutes and the default notification to 30 minutes before — long enough to finish what you are doing and get moving.

Next, create separate calendars for the different parts of your business. In Google Calendar, a calendar is a layer with its own colour, and you can turn layers on and off. Go to Settings, then Add calendar, then Create new calendar. Create these:

- **Business — Sales and Customers** (blue): client meetings, calls, follow-ups, order deadlines.
- **Business — Operations** (green): deliveries, supplier visits, market runs, stock checks.
- **Business — Marketing** (orange): content creation, posting, adverts, campaigns.
- **Business — Money** (red): payment due dates, rent, salaries, bank visits, tax dates.
- **Personal** (grey or purple): family, church, health, rest. Yes, rest goes in the calendar. If it is not scheduled, it does not happen.

Give each calendar a different colour so a glance at your week tells you what kind of week it is. A week that is all red is a money week. A week that is all blue is a customer week. A week with no green is a week where operations will surprise you.

## Step 2: Block Your Fixed Commitments First

A weekly plan fails when it pretends every hour is free. Start by blocking the things that are already fixed, before you plan anything new.

Open the week view and add your recurring commitments as repeating events:

- **Opening and closing times.** If your shop opens at 8am and closes at 6pm, block that as your working window. Anything outside it is either overtime or personal time — and you should be able to tell which.
- **Market days and supplier runs.** If you buy stock at Balogun on Tuesdays or visit the Mile 12 market on Thursdays, block those mornings as repeating events. Traffic in Lagos means a "quick" market run is never quick; block three hours, not one.
- **Delivery windows.** If your dispatch rider collects at 11am daily, block 10:30 to 11:30 for packing and handover. Missed handover means missed delivery, and missed delivery means an unhappy customer.
- **Staff meetings and family commitments.** A weekly staff check-in, a church service, school runs, a regular family call — block them. A plan that ignores your real life will be abandoned by Wednesday.
- **Prayer, rest and meals.** A 30-minute lunch block and a hard stop at the end of the day are not luxuries. They are what keep you able to work the next day. Burnout is the most expensive business cost in Nigeria, because there is no sick leave for a sole trader.

Once the fixed blocks are in, the empty spaces are your real planning time. Most business owners discover they have far fewer free hours than they thought — and that discovery is the point. A plan built on imaginary free time collapses on Monday morning. A plan built on real free time survives the week.

## Step 3: Plan the Week on Sunday Evening

The single most valuable habit in this guide takes fifteen minutes every Sunday evening. Sit down with your phone, a drink, and your calendar, and plan the coming week.

**First, look at what is already there.** Check the fixed commitments you blocked in Step 2, and any events other people added. A client meeting on Wednesday, a delivery on Friday, a bank appointment on Monday — these are the anchors of the week.

**Second, list the week's top three outcomes.** Not tasks — outcomes. "Collect ₦150,000 in outstanding balances." "Launch the new Ankara collection." "Deliver all ten pending orders." Write the three outcomes at the top of a note. Every task you schedule this week should serve one of them. If it serves none of them, it does not belong in this week's calendar.

**Third, schedule the work that produces those outcomes.** Break each outcome into tasks and put each task in a specific time slot:

- Monday 9am–11am: call the five customers with outstanding balances.
- Tuesday 10am–12pm: photograph the new collection.
- Wednesday 2pm–4pm: write product descriptions and post them.
- Thursday 9am–12pm: pack and dispatch all pending orders.

Give every task a start time and an end time. A task without a time is a wish. A task with a time is an appointment with yourself, and you should treat it with the same respect you give a client appointment.

**Fourth, leave buffer space.** Lagos traffic, a customer who runs late, a power cut in the middle of a task — something will go wrong every week. Leave at least one empty hour each day and one lighter day, usually Friday afternoon, for spillover. A calendar with no gaps is a calendar that breaks at the first surprise. A calendar with buffers bends and survives.

**Fifth, set the reminders.** For client meetings, set a reminder one day before and one hour before. For deliveries, set a reminder the evening before so you can pack in advance. For money events like rent or salary day, set a reminder three days before so you can arrange the funds. Reminders are the difference between a calendar and a decoration.

## Step 4: Run Each Day From the Calendar

Planning the week is half the job. The other half is opening the calendar each morning and letting it run the day.

**Every morning, take three minutes.** Open the day view before you check WhatsApp. Look at today's events in order. Note the first event, the hardest event, and any event that needs preparation. If a client meeting needs a sample, pack it now. If a delivery needs an address, confirm it now. Three minutes of looking saves three hours of scrambling.

**Work in time blocks, not in a to-do list.** A to-do list lets you do the easy tasks first and avoid the hard ones. A time block says "9am to 11am is for calling debtors" and you do it, whether it is pleasant or not. Group similar work into blocks: all calls in one block, all packing in one block, all content creation in one block. Switching between different kinds of work all day is exhausting and slow.

**Use the travel time honestly.** Lagos traffic is not wasted time if you plan for it. A two-hour journey to a client in Ikeja is a chance to make calls on hands-free, listen to a business podcast, or plan the next day. Block travel time in the calendar as its own event — "Travel to Ikeja, 7:30–9:30am" — so you never schedule a 9am meeting you cannot reach.

**End each day with a five-minute review.** Before bed, open tomorrow's calendar. Move anything unfinished to a new slot. Add any new follow-ups from today's calls and chats. Check that tomorrow's reminders are set. This tiny habit means you never wake up to a surprise, and your Sunday planning session gets easier every week.

## Step 5: Use Calendar for Customers, Follow-Ups and Money

Google Calendar is not only for meetings. It is the memory your business needs for everything with a date on it.

**Customer follow-ups.** When a customer says "call me next Tuesday", create an event for Tuesday at 10am titled "Call Mrs Eze — Ankara gown enquiry" and put her number in the event description. When the reminder fires, you call. When a customer says "I will pay on Friday", create a Friday event titled "Collect ₦35,000 from Mr Okonkwo — ORD-042". Your follow-ups stop depending on your memory, which is exactly the point.

**Delivery promises.** When you promise a customer delivery on Thursday, put it in the calendar as an event with the customer's name, address and phone number in the description. A promise in the calendar is a promise you will keep. A promise in a WhatsApp chat is a promise you will forget.

**Recurring money dates.** Rent, salaries, supplier payment days, loan repayments, savings contributions, tax filing dates — create them as repeating events with reminders days in advance. Many Nigerian businesses do not fail because they have no money; they fail because money was due and nobody saw it coming. A red calendar event three days before rent is due is a cheap early-warning system.

**Content and marketing.** Block two hours a week for content: photographing products, writing captions, recording a short video, and scheduling posts. Marketing that is not in the calendar is marketing that happens "when I have time", which means never. A recurring Wednesday 4pm–6pm marketing block will do more for your business in three months than any single advert.

**Repeating events are your friend.** A weekly stock check every Monday, a monthly bank reconciliation on the 1st, a quarterly review of prices — create them once as repeating events and they will appear forever. You set them up once and benefit every week.

## Step 6: Share the Calendar With Your Team and Family

A calendar you keep to yourself only helps you. A shared calendar helps the whole business.

**Sharing with staff.** Share your Operations calendar with your assistant or shop attendant as a viewer, or as an editor if they need to add events. They can see when deliveries are due, when the shop is closed, and when you are out — without calling you to ask. When a staff member leaves, remove their access the same day. Sharing a calendar is not sharing your Google password; never give anyone your password.

**Sharing with a business partner.** If you run the business with a partner, share the Sales calendar with each other. You will both know who is meeting which client and when, and you will stop double-booking the same customer or both travelling to the same supplier on the same day.

**Sharing availability with clients.** Google Calendar lets you create an appointment schedule: a booking page where clients pick a free slot for a consultation, a fitting, or a meeting. You set your available hours, share the link on WhatsApp or your website, and clients book themselves. No more back-and-forth "are you free on Thursday?" messages. For a tailor, a consultant, a salon owner or a photographer, this one feature can save hours a week.

**Sharing with family.** Share your Personal calendar, or just the events that matter, with your spouse or family so they know when you are working late and when you are free. A business that eats every evening and every weekend is a business that will cost you your family life. The calendar is where you defend the boundary, and sharing it is how your family helps you keep it.


## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Planning in your head.** A plan in your head is forgotten by 10am. A plan in the calendar is visible all day.
- **Scheduling every minute.** A calendar with no gaps breaks at the first delay. Leave buffers every day.
- **Ignoring travel time.** Two meetings in Lekki and Ikeja at 10am and 11am is not a plan; it is a fantasy. Block the travel.
- **Creating events and never opening the calendar.** The plan only works if you look at it. Check it every morning and every evening.
- **Putting everything on one calendar.** One colour for everything tells you nothing at a glance. Use the separate calendars from Step 1.
- **Never reviewing the week.** Without a Friday review, the same mistakes repeat for months. Fifteen minutes of review is the cheapest business education available.
- **Planning alone and telling nobody.** If your staff and family cannot see the plan, they cannot help you keep it. Share the right calendars.
- **Giving up after one bad week.** The first week will go wrong. Traffic will happen, a customer will cancel, NEPA will strike. That is normal. Adjust the plan on Sunday and try again. By week four, the habit will feel natural.


## Final Word

You do not need a fancy planner, a paid app, or a business degree to run an organised week. You need a free tool you already own, fifteen minutes on Sunday evening, and the discipline to look at the plan each morning. Google Calendar will not sell your products for you. It will make sure you have time to sell them, time to follow up, time to collect your money, and time to rest — and that is the difference between a business that survives and a business that grows.

Set it up today. Create the five calendars, block your fixed commitments, and plan next week on Sunday evening. Give it four weeks. By then, the question will not be whether the calendar works. It will be how you ever ran the business without it.
