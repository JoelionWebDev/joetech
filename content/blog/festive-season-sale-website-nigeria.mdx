Every year, the weeks before Christmas turn into the biggest shopping season in Nigeria. Salaries arrive, bonuses are paid, families prepare for celebrations, and customers are ready to spend. For a business with a website, this is the best chance of the year to win new customers and bring old ones back. But a festive sale only works if it is planned properly. A sale announced the night before, with unclear discounts and a website that cannot handle the orders, loses more trust than it gains.

The good news is that a successful festive sale does not need a large advertising budget. It needs a clear offer, a simple plan, and a website that makes buying easy. This guide walks through each step, from choosing what to discount to following up with customers after the season ends.

## Start early and choose one clear offer

The biggest mistake small businesses make is starting too late. Customers begin planning festive purchases weeks ahead, and by the time December arrives, many have already decided where they will shop. Start planning your sale at least three to four weeks before it begins. That gives you time to prepare your products, update your website, and tell your customers more than once.

Choose one clear offer instead of discounting everything at random. A single offer is easier to explain and easier for customers to understand. It could be a percentage off a selected range, a buy-two-get-one offer, a free delivery offer, or a special festive bundle. The offer should be simple enough to fit in one sentence on your homepage, because that is how most customers will first see it.

Before you fix the discount, check your numbers. Work out what each product costs you, including delivery and packaging, and make sure the sale price still leaves you a profit. A sale that brings in many orders but loses money on each one is not a success. If the numbers do not work for a deep discount, offer a smaller discount on more products, or add value instead of cutting price, such as free delivery or a small free gift with each order.

## Prepare your website before you announce anything

Your website is your shop during the sale, so prepare it before a single customer arrives. Create a clear sale page or a visible banner on your homepage that states the offer, the dates, and the products included. Customers should not have to search for the sale or guess which products are discounted.

Check every product in the sale. Make sure the prices are correct, the photos are clear, and the stock levels are accurate. Nothing damages trust faster than a customer paying for a product that is out of stock. If stock is limited, say so honestly on the product page. Real limits create urgency. False limits destroy trust when customers discover the product was never available.

Test the buying journey yourself. Go through the whole process as a customer would: find a product, add it to the cart, and complete the payment. Check that the payment options your customers use actually work, and that the order confirmation arrives. If you have not set up smooth payment collection on your site yet, read our guide on [how to collect payments on your website in Nigeria](/blog/collect-payments-website-nigeria) before the sale begins, because a customer who cannot pay is a customer lost. Also test your website on a phone, because most of your customers will shop on mobile data, and a slow page will send them elsewhere.

## Tell your customers more than once

A sale nobody hears about is a sale that never happens. Tell your customers about it through every channel you already use: your WhatsApp status and broadcast list, your Instagram page, your email list, and your website itself. Each channel reaches different people, and most customers need to hear about a sale more than once before they act.

Write the message the same way everywhere, with the same offer, the same dates, and the same link to your website. Consistency matters. If your Instagram says the sale ends on Friday and your WhatsApp says it ends on Sunday, customers will not trust either date.

## Handle orders, delivery, and problems with care

When orders start coming in, speed and honesty matter more than anything. Confirm each order quickly, with the total amount, the payment details, and when the customer should expect delivery. Keep a simple list of every order: the customer name, the products, the amount paid, and the delivery status. That list is your control room during the sale.

Delivery is where festive sales most often go wrong, because everyone is ordering at the same time and delivery riders are busy. Be honest about delivery times from the start.

## Finish well and keep the customers you won

The sale ends, but the work does not. Thank every customer who ordered, even with a short message. A thank you costs nothing and makes the customer feel valued. Then ask for honest feedback about the experience. What was easy? What was confusing? The answers are your plan for the next sale.

Review your numbers simply. How many people visited your website during the sale? How many ordered? Which products sold best? Which channel brought the most orders? Write the answers down. They tell you what to repeat next year and what to fix. If many people visited but few ordered, the problem may be the price, the delivery cost, or a confusing checkout. If few people visited at all, the problem is that not enough people heard about the sale.

Stay in touch with the new customers you won, in a helpful way, not only when you want to sell again. Share useful tips, answer questions, and tell them first when the next offer is ready. A festive sale is not just a week of extra income. It is a chance to grow the list of people who trust your business, and that list is worth more than any single sale.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**When should I start planning a festive season sale?**

Start three to four weeks before the sale begins. That gives you time to choose your offer, check your prices and stock, prepare your website, and tell your customers more than once. Planning early also lets you fix problems, such as a payment page that does not work, before customers arrive.

**How much discount should I offer?**

Only as much as your numbers allow. Work out the full cost of each product, including delivery and packaging, and make sure the sale price still leaves you a profit. A smaller honest discount is better than a large discount that loses money on every order. Adding value, such as free delivery or a small gift, can work better than cutting the price further.

**Should I discount everything on my website?**

Usually not. Discounting everything is expensive and hard to manage. Choose a selected range of products, or create festive bundles, and make it clear which products are included. A focused offer is easier to explain, easier to stock, and easier for customers to understand.

**What if my website goes down during the sale?**

Keep your phone charged and your WhatsApp ready as a backup way to take orders. If the website has a problem, tell customers quickly through WhatsApp and Instagram, and take orders manually until it is fixed. After the sale, find out what caused the problem so it does not happen again next year.

## Make this festive season count

A festive season sale rewards the businesses that plan it. Choose one clear offer that still makes you a profit, prepare your website and your stock before you announce anything, tell your customers more than once, and handle every order with speed and honesty. Then thank your customers, study your numbers, and keep the new customers you won. That is how a few weeks of selling become a stronger business for the whole year ahead.
