Walk through almost any busy street in Lagos, Abuja, Onitsha, or Ibadan and you will see a small kiosk or shop with a POS machine on the counter. People queue there every day to withdraw cash, send money, pay bills, and recharge their phones. That small machine is the heart of one of the most accessible small businesses in Nigeria today: the POS, or point-of-sale, agency business.

A POS business does not require a university degree, a large shop, or years of experience. It requires a modest starting amount, a reliable machine, a good location, and honest daily habits. Many Nigerians start it as a side business alongside a shop, a pharmacy, or a provision store, while others run it full-time and grow from one terminal to several locations. This guide explains exactly how to start, how much it costs, how to choose a provider, and how to avoid the mistakes that close many new POS businesses in their first few months.

## What a POS Business Really Is

A POS agent, also called an agency banking operator, helps people carry out basic banking transactions without visiting a bank branch. Customers come to you to withdraw cash from their bank accounts, deposit money, transfer funds to another person, pay for electricity, television subscriptions, or internet, and buy airtime and data.

You earn money from the charges on each transaction. For example, if a customer withdraws ₦10,000, you might charge ₦100 or ₦200, depending on your area and your provider's guidance. If a customer transfers ₦50,000, the charge might be ₦200 or ₦300. Each charge looks small on its own, but across 50 or 100 transactions a day, it adds up to a meaningful daily income. Your profit is the total charges you collect, minus the fees your provider takes and your running costs such as rent, data, and transport to get cash.

The business works because bank branches are often far away, queues are long, and ATMs frequently run out of cash or go offline. A POS agent close to a market, a bus stop, a school, or a residential estate solves a real daily problem. People will return to an agent they trust, especially when the agent is polite, fast, and always has cash available.

## Step 1: Understand the Startup Cost Honestly

Before you start, you need a clear picture of the money involved. Costs vary by location and provider, but a realistic starting budget for a single POS point in 2026 looks like this.

A POS terminal itself is the biggest single cost. Some providers give you a terminal for free if you meet their conditions, while others sell or lease it. Buying a terminal outright can cost from about ₦35,000 to ₦85,000 depending on the brand and model. Some providers charge a caution fee or a one-time registration fee instead. Always ask whether the fee is refundable and what happens if the machine develops a fault.

You also need working capital, which is the cash you keep on hand for withdrawals. This is the money that makes the business work. If you have only ₦50,000 in cash and ten customers want to withdraw ₦10,000 each in the morning, you will run out before midday and lose the rest of the day's income. Most new agents start with at least ₦100,000 to ₦200,000 in cash, plus a balance in their agent wallet for transfers and deposits. In a busy market location, agents often keep ₦300,000 or more. Start with what you can afford, but understand that more cash means more transactions and more profit.

Other costs include a small shop or kiosk space, a chair and table, a signboard, a phone, and data. If you already have a shop, your extra cost is very low. If you need to rent a kiosk, rent in many Lagos neighbourhoods can range from ₦5,000 to ₦30,000 per month for a small space, while a space in a busy market may cost more. A simple signboard costs around ₦5,000 to ₦15,000. Add a small amount for branding, a notebook for records, and a secure cash box.

In total, many people start a basic POS business with between ₦150,000 and ₦400,000, including the terminal, cash float, and setup. You can start smaller if you already own a shop and a phone, but do not start with so little cash that you close by noon every day. That is the fastest way to lose customers.

## Step 2: Choose the Right POS Provider

Your provider is the company that gives you the terminal and processes your transactions. Popular providers in Nigeria include Moniepoint, Opay, Palmpay, Kuda agents, Firstmonie, and several bank agency platforms. Each provider has different charges, different terminal prices, and different levels of customer support. Choosing well matters more than choosing quickly.

Compare at least three providers before you decide. Ask each one these questions. What is the charge on withdrawals, transfers, and deposits, and how much of that charge stays with me? Is the terminal free, leased, or sold, and what is the total cost? How fast are settlements, and can I withdraw my earnings easily? What happens if the machine is faulty or stolen? Is there a daily target I must meet, and what happens if I miss it? How good is the network in my area, and does the machine work well on the network providers available there?

Also ask other agents in your area which provider they use and whether they are happy. An agent in Ikeja may praise a provider that struggles in Ajah because network strength differs by location. Real experience from nearby agents is more useful than an advert.

Avoid providers that promise unrealistic profits or ask you to pay a large sum before you have seen any agreement. Read the terms carefully. Check whether there are hidden daily charges, monthly fees, or penalties. A good provider should explain its charges clearly, give you a written agreement, and provide a contact number for support when transactions fail.

## Step 3: Register and Get Your Terminal

Registration is usually straightforward. You will need a valid means of identification, such as a National Identity Number slip or card, a voter's card, a driver's licence, or an international passport. You will also need a Bank Verification Number, a passport photograph, a phone number, and proof of your business location, which can be a shop address or a utility bill. Some providers also ask for a guarantor or a referee.

Visit the provider's office, an accredited aggregator, or register through their official app or website. Be careful to use only official channels. Never give your BVN, password, or OTP to a stranger who claims they can get you a terminal faster. No genuine provider will ask for your OTP or your bank password.

After registration, you will be trained on how to use the terminal. Pay attention during this training. Learn how to do withdrawals, deposits, transfers, balance enquiries, bill payments, and reversals. Learn what to do when a transaction is pending or when a customer says money was debited but no cash was given. That situation will happen, and knowing the process for resolving it calmly will protect your reputation.

Test the machine thoroughly before you open to customers. Do a small withdrawal and a small transfer with your own account or a trusted family member's account. Check that receipts print correctly and that your wallet balance updates. If anything is wrong, report it immediately and do not open for business until it is fixed.

## Step 4: Find a Location That Brings Customers to You

Location is the single biggest factor in a POS business. A good location can make a modest operator profitable, while a poor location can make even a hardworking agent struggle.

The best locations have high daily foot traffic and limited nearby competition. Busy markets, bus stops, school gates, hospital entrances, residential estates with few banks, and streets with many small shops are all strong choices. A location near a market in Mile 12, a bus park in Ojuelegba, or a student area in Yaba will see more transactions than a quiet residential street with three other POS agents already operating.

Visit your chosen area at different times of day before you commit. Count how many people pass by in the morning, at midday, and in the evening. Notice whether there is already a POS agent nearby and how busy they are. If two agents on the same street are both busy all day, there may still be room for a third. If one agent sits idle for hours, the area may not have enough demand.

Consider safety and visibility. Your kiosk should be easy to find, well-lit in the evening, and close to other businesses so you are not isolated. A location inside or beside an existing shop is often safer and cheaper than a standalone kiosk on an empty street. If you run the POS inside your provision store or pharmacy, customers who come for cash will also buy other items, and your existing customers will become your first POS customers.

Negotiate rent carefully. Do not sign a long agreement or pay a full year in advance before you know the location works. Start with a monthly or three-month arrangement if possible, and keep a written record of what you paid and when the next payment is due.

## Step 5: Set Your Charges Fairly

Charges are where many new agents make mistakes. Charge too much and customers will walk to the next agent. Charge too little and you will work all day for very little profit.

Find out what other agents in your area charge for common transactions and stay within that range. In many parts of Lagos, a ₦5,000 withdrawal might attract a ₦100 charge, while a ₦10,000 withdrawal might attract ₦100 or ₦200. A ₦50,000 withdrawal might attract ₦300 to ₦500. Transfers often attract a flat charge based on the amount. Bill payments and airtime may attract a smaller charge or none at all, depending on your provider.

Display your charges clearly on a small board or poster where customers can see them before they transact. Hidden charges destroy trust. If a customer agrees to a charge before the transaction, there will be no argument afterwards. If you change your charges, update the board the same day and tell regular customers why.

Do not undercut everyone in the area just to win customers. A price war helps nobody. Customers value trust, speed, and availability more than a ₦50 difference. An agent who is always available, always has cash, and handles failed transactions honestly will keep customers even if a competitor is slightly cheaper.

## Step 6: Manage Your Cash Flow Every Day

Cash flow is the daily discipline that separates successful POS agents from those who close down. You need enough physical cash for withdrawals and enough wallet balance for transfers and deposits. Running out of either means turning customers away.

Start each day by checking both balances. Count your cash and check your agent wallet. Decide how much cash to carry to the shop and how much to keep safely at home or in the bank. Never carry all your cash in one place, and never leave large amounts of cash in an unlocked drawer overnight.

During the day, track every transaction in a notebook or a simple spreadsheet, even if the machine also keeps records. Write the time, the type of transaction, the amount, and the charge. At the end of the day, count your cash again and compare it with your records. If there is a difference, find it the same day. Small errors that are ignored become large losses over weeks.

When your physical cash runs low, you need a plan to get more. That might mean going to a nearby bank, using a trusted money agent, or transferring from your wallet to your bank and withdrawing. In busy areas, some agents arrange a regular cash supply from a nearby business that receives a lot of cash daily, such as a market trader or a filling station. Whatever your method, never leave your shop unattended with cash inside, and be discreet when moving large amounts of cash. Avoid counting large sums in public.

Keep your business money separate from your personal money. Use a separate wallet or bank account for the business. At the end of each week, calculate your total charges, subtract your costs, and record your profit. That weekly habit will show you whether the business is growing and whether your charges and location are working.

## Step 7: Protect Yourself From Fraud and Failed Transactions

POS agents are targets for fraud because they handle cash daily. You need simple security habits from day one.

First, never share your terminal PIN, your agent app password, or any OTP with anyone, including someone who claims to be from your provider. Providers do not ask for these details by phone or WhatsApp. Second, always confirm the customer's identity for large transactions. Ask for the name on the card or account and check that it matches the person in front of you. Third, always give a receipt, and keep a copy for yourself when the machine allows it. Receipts protect both you and the customer when a transaction is disputed.

Failed transactions are common. Sometimes a customer's account is debited but the POS does not dispense cash or does not complete the transfer. Stay calm, do not argue, and follow your provider's process. Give the customer the transaction reference, advise them to contact their bank if needed, and log the incident in your notebook with the date, time, amount, and reference number. Customers who see you handle a problem honestly are more likely to return than customers who never had a problem at all.

Be careful with fake alerts and fake transfer screenshots. Always confirm a transfer on your own terminal or bank app before you hand over cash. Never release cash based on a screenshot alone. Fraudsters often show a fake alert and rush the agent. Take your time. A genuine customer will wait for you to confirm.

Keep your terminal charged and your phone data active. A machine that is off or offline loses customers. Have a backup plan, such as a second SIM from a different network or a small power bank, so network problems do not shut you down for the whole day.

## Mistakes That Close New POS Businesses

The most common mistake is starting with too little cash and running out by midday. Customers who find you closed or out of cash twice will stop coming. Start with enough float, and grow it as your daily volume grows.

Another mistake is poor record keeping. Agents who do not track transactions cannot tell whether they are making a profit or losing money through errors and fraud. A simple notebook, filled in daily, is enough to prevent most losses.

A third mistake is mixing business money with personal spending. When you use business cash to buy food, pay for transport, or lend to friends, your float shrinks and your business suffers. Pay yourself a fixed daily or weekly amount and leave the rest in the business.

A fourth mistake is ignoring customer service. Rudeness, long delays, and arguing over failed transactions push customers to competitors. Greet customers, work quickly, explain charges clearly, and apologise when something goes wrong.

A fifth mistake is choosing a provider based only on who offers a free terminal. A free terminal from a provider with poor network, slow support, and high hidden fees will cost you more than a paid terminal from a reliable provider. Compare the full picture, not just the upfront offer.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Starting a POS Business

**What happens if my POS machine is stolen or damaged?**

Report it to your provider immediately and follow their process for blocking the terminal and requesting a replacement. Keep your terminal secure, do not leave it unattended, and ask your provider about their replacement policy before you start. Some providers charge for replacement, while others cover faults that are not your doing.

## Final Thoughts

Starting a POS business in Nigeria is one of the most practical ways to earn a steady daily income with a modest starting amount. The business rewards consistency more than cleverness. Choose a reliable provider, pick a location with real foot traffic, keep enough cash available, charge fairly, keep honest records, and treat every customer with respect. Do those things daily and your small kiosk can grow into a trusted neighbourhood service and, over time, into multiple locations. Start with one terminal, learn your area, and let your reputation do the marketing for you.
