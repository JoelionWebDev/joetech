Walk into a busy market in Lagos today and you will notice something different. The shoe seller, the food vendor, and the phone accessories trader all have a small sign on their table that says "We accept OPay and PalmPay". Customers scan a code or type a number, and the payment arrives before the seller has finished wrapping the goods.

OPay and PalmPay are mobile money apps that let anyone send and receive money with just a phone number. They became popular because they are simple, they work on almost any phone, and their transfers are often free or very cheap between users of the same app. For a small business owner, that means more customers can pay you quickly, without bank charges eating into every sale and without the delays that sometimes come with bank apps.

This guide explains how to accept OPay and PalmPay payments in your small business in Nigeria. You will learn how both apps work, how to set up your accounts, how to take payments in your shop and on WhatsApp, how to handle transfers, withdrawals, and fees, and how to protect yourself from the scams that target wallet payments.

## What OPay and PalmPay Are and How They Work

OPay and PalmPay are fintech wallets licensed to operate in Nigeria. Each one gives you an account number tied to your phone number, and you can send money, receive money, buy airtime and data, and pay bills from inside the app. You download the app, register with your phone number, verify your identity, and you are ready.

When a customer pays you with OPay or PalmPay, they open their own app, enter your phone number or account number, type the amount, and confirm with their PIN. If both of you use the same app, the transfer is usually instant and free. If the customer pays from a different app or a bank, it still works, but it may take a little longer and may attract a small fee.

Your wallet balance is real money. You can keep it in the wallet and spend it from there, transfer it to your bank account, or withdraw it as cash from an agent. Many small business owners keep a working balance in their wallet for daily expenses and move the rest to their bank account at the end of the day.

Both apps also work without a smartphone in some cases. A customer with a basic phone can still send money using the app's USSD option, and OPay and PalmPay both have networks of agents across Nigeria who can help customers deposit cash into their wallets.

## Why Accepting OPay and PalmPay Helps Your Business

The first reason is speed. A bank transfer can take minutes when the network is slow, and a customer standing at your counter will get impatient. An OPay or PalmPay transfer between users of the same app arrives in seconds. That speed keeps your queue moving, especially on busy days like Saturdays.

The second reason is cost. Transfers between users of the same wallet are often free, and even transfers to a bank usually cost less than a traditional bank transfer fee. When you make dozens of small sales a day, those small fees add up. Always check the current fees inside the app, because they can change.

The third reason is that your customers already use these apps. Many Nigerians opened an OPay or PalmPay account because a friend sent them money through it, or because an agent helped them set it up. When a customer asks "Do you accept OPay?" and you say no, you are telling them to go through extra stress to pay you. When you say yes, paying becomes the easiest part of the sale.

The fourth reason is trust and record keeping. Every wallet payment comes with a notification that shows the sender's name, the amount, and the time. You can search your transaction history in the app at any time, which makes it easy to confirm a payment or settle a dispute. A cash sale leaves no record. A wallet payment leaves a trail you can follow.

## How to Set Up OPay for Your Business

Setting up OPay is straightforward. First, download the OPay app from the Play Store or App Store and register with the phone number you use for your business. Use a number you control and can access easily, because the app sends verification codes and payment alerts to it.

Second, verify your identity. The app will ask for your full name as it appears on your ID, your date of birth, and your Bank Verification Number, known as BVN. You may also need to upload a photo of your ID and take a selfie for verification. Use your real details, because a mismatch can limit your account or block your withdrawals later. Verification usually takes a short time if your details are clear.

Third, set a strong transaction PIN and keep it private. This PIN is what confirms every payment you send, so choose one that is not your birthday, your phone number, or a simple sequence like 1234. Never share your PIN with staff, friends, or anyone who calls claiming to be from OPay. OPay will never ask you for your PIN or for a code sent to your phone.

Fourth, set up your business profile inside the app. Add your business name, so customers see it when they pay you, and generate your payment QR code. Print the code and display it at your counter, on your shop wall, or on your delivery packages. A customer who can scan your code does not need to type your number, which removes the risk of a typing error sending money to the wrong person.

## How to Set Up PalmPay for Your Business

Setting up PalmPay follows the same pattern as OPay, and having both is worth the small extra effort. Start by downloading the PalmPay app and registering with your business phone number. PalmPay also asks for your full name, date of birth, and BVN, and it may ask for a photo of your ID and a selfie. Complete every step, because an unverified account has limits on how much money it can receive and withdraw.

Once your account is verified, set your transaction PIN. Use a different PIN from your OPay PIN, so that if one is ever compromised, the other stays safe. PalmPay also lets you set up a payment QR code and a personal payment link. Generate both. The QR code is for customers in your shop, and the payment link is for customers who order on WhatsApp, Instagram, or Facebook, where they cannot scan a printed code.

PalmPay also offers a business account option with extra features, such as a dashboard that shows your daily sales and a way to receive payments from customers who use other banks. If your sales grow and you find yourself checking the app many times a day, the business features are worth exploring.

Keep both apps updated. OPay and PalmPay release updates that fix problems, add features, and improve security. An old version of the app can fail at the worst moment, such as when a customer is waiting to pay.

## How to Take OPay and PalmPay Payments in Your Shop

In your shop, make wallet payments visible and easy. Print your OPay and PalmPay QR codes on one clear sign near the counter, with your business name above them and a short line under each one, such as "Scan to pay with OPay" and "Scan to pay with PalmPay". A customer who can see both options will choose the one they already use, and the payment will go through in seconds.

When a customer is ready to pay, tell them the exact amount first, then point to the code or give them your phone number. Stand where you can see your own phone, because your notification is the only confirmation that matters. Wait for the alert to arrive before you hand over the goods.

Keep a small notebook at the counter and record each wallet payment: the date, the customer's name, the amount, and which wallet they used. At the close of business, compare your notebook with the transaction history in each app. If a payment is missing from your notebook, you will catch it the same day, not a week later.

Train anyone who works in your shop to follow the same rule: no notification on the shop phone, no goods. Staff who accept screenshots or verbal promises will lose your money.

## How to Take OPay and PalmPay Payments Online

Most small businesses in Nigeria take orders on WhatsApp, and wallet payments fit that flow well. When a customer orders, send a short message with the total amount and your payment details. For example: "Total is ₦18,000. Pay with OPay to 0803 123 4567 (Adaeze Stores) or PalmPay to 0803 123 4567. Send a screenshot after payment." Keep it short, so the customer can follow it without asking questions. Save the message as a quick reply on WhatsApp Business so you can send it in one tap.

If you use a payment link, send the link instead of your phone number. Both OPay and PalmPay let you create a payment link that shows the customer the amount and lets them pay in a few taps. A link also reduces errors, because the customer does not have to type your number. Test your link yourself before you share it, by sending it to your own phone and opening it.

Set a clear rule for online orders: payment confirms the order. Tell the customer you will only pack and dispatch their goods after the payment is confirmed in your wallet. This protects you from customers who order and never pay, and it stops you from holding stock for people who are not serious. Give a deadline, such as payment within two hours, and be polite but firm about it.

Always confirm a payment with a short reply: "Payment received, thank you. Your order will be delivered tomorrow." That message reassures the customer and starts the delivery conversation.

## How to Stay Safe from Wallet Payment Scams

Wallet payments are safe when you follow the rules, but scammers target small businesses every day. The most common scam is the fake alert. A customer shows you a text message that looks like a payment notification, but the money never arrived. The rule is simple: only trust the notification inside your own app or the balance shown in your wallet. Never accept a screenshot, a forwarded message, or a notification shown on the customer's phone.

Another common scam is the wrong amount. A customer pays ₦2,000 instead of ₦20,000 and shows you the alert quickly, hoping you will not read the figure. Always read the amount on your own phone before you hand over the goods. Also check that the sender's name matches the customer standing in front of you. If the name is different, ask questions before you release the goods.

Never share your PIN, your password, or any code sent to your phone. No staff member of OPay or PalmPay will ever call you and ask for these details. If someone calls claiming your account will be blocked unless you share a code, it is a scam. Hang up and contact the company through the official number inside the app. Also beware of fake agents and fake QR codes. Only print QR codes generated inside your own app, and check them regularly to make sure nobody has pasted a different code over yours.

Keep your phone locked with a PIN or fingerprint, and log out of the apps if you lend your phone to anyone. If your phone is stolen, contact OPay and PalmPay at once to freeze your accounts, then contact your bank. Acting fast limits the damage.

## Common Mistakes Small Businesses Make with OPay and PalmPay

The first mistake is releasing goods before confirming the payment in your own app. It happens when a shop is busy and the seller trusts a screenshot. The rule must be absolute: no alert on your phone, no goods. The second mistake is using one phone number for everything. If your business number is also your personal number, customers will call you at all hours and your payment alerts will get lost among personal messages. Use a dedicated business number for your wallets.

The third mistake is not displaying your payment options. If customers do not know you accept OPay and PalmPay, they will assume you take cash only, and some will leave to find an ATM. Put your QR codes where customers can see them, and add "We accept OPay and PalmPay" to your WhatsApp profile and social media bios. The fourth mistake is ignoring the fees. A seller who never checks what each transfer costs will be surprised at the end of the month. Review your fees regularly and build them into your prices.

The fifth mistake is leaving large balances in a wallet for too long. A wallet is for receiving payments and daily spending, not for storing your savings. Move money to your bank account regularly, so a lost phone or a frozen account does not lock up your working capital. The sixth mistake is failing to keep records. A seller who relies only on memory cannot answer when a customer says "I paid you last week" or when the accounts do not balance. Check your transaction history daily and write down your sales.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need a bank account to use OPay or PalmPay for my business?

No, you can open a wallet with just your phone number, your BVN, and a valid ID. You do not need a traditional bank account to receive payments. However, having a bank account makes it easier to move large amounts out of your wallet and to keep your business money separate. Most business owners link a bank account to their wallet for transfers and withdrawals.

### How much does it cost to accept OPay and PalmPay payments?

Transfers between users of the same app are often free. Transfers to a bank account or withdrawals through an agent usually attract a small fee, which varies by amount and by app. Check the current fee schedule inside each app before you set your prices, because fees can change. The cost is generally lower than the fees on many bank transfers and POS transactions.

### What should I do if a customer's payment does not arrive?

Ask the customer to check their own app for the transaction status. Sometimes the network is slow and the payment arrives a few minutes later. If the customer was debited but you received nothing, ask them to contact OPay or PalmPay support with the transaction reference. Never hand over goods until the money has arrived in your wallet.

### Can a customer pay me with OPay if I only use PalmPay?

Yes, but it works best when both sides use the same app. A customer can also pay from OPay to your bank account, or from their bank to your PalmPay account, using your account number. To make payment easy for everyone, set up both OPay and PalmPay for your business, so customers can pay with whichever app they already use.

### Is it safe to keep money in my OPay or PalmPay wallet?

Yes, for day-to-day business amounts, as long as you protect your PIN and keep your phone locked. Both apps are licensed and regulated in Nigeria. Still, treat the wallet like a cash till, not a savings account: move larger amounts to your bank account regularly, so a lost phone or a frozen account does not lock up your working capital.
