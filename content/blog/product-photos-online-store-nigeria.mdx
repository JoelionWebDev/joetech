When a customer walks into a physical shop, they pick up a product, turn it over, and check it from different angles. Online, they cannot do any of that. Your photos are the only way they can judge what they are buying, so a blurry, dark, or misleading photo does more damage than no photo at all.

Good product photos do not require a studio or expensive equipment. A phone camera, natural daylight, a plain background, and a little patience will produce photos good enough to sell from, for most small Nigerian businesses. What matters is consistency, honesty, and showing the product the way a careful customer would want to see it.

## Start with the product itself

Before you pick up the camera, prepare the product. Clean it, iron it if it is clothing, remove dust, stickers, and price tags, and check it for damage. A creased shirt or a dusty shoe looks worse in a photo than in real life, because the camera picks up details the eye overlooks in a shop.

Gather everything that comes with the product: the box, charger, accessories, or extra parts. Customers want to know exactly what they will receive, and showing the full set in one photo prevents misunderstandings and returns later. If you sell clothing, try it on a model or a mannequin where possible, rather than photographing it flat on a bed, so people can see how it falls and how long it is. If a model is not available, lay the item flat neatly and photograph it from directly above so the shape is clear.

## Use daylight, not your room light

Light is the single biggest difference between amateur and professional-looking product photos. Ordinary room bulbs often cast a yellow tint and harsh shadows, which make colours look wrong. A customer who orders a white dress and receives a cream one because of bad lighting will be unhappy, and you will bear the return.

The simplest reliable light is daylight near a large window, on a bright but cloudy day, or in open shade outdoors. Avoid direct midday sun, which creates hard shadows and washed-out colours. Morning light, before the sun gets harsh, is usually kindest. Place the product so light falls on it from the side or from behind you, not from behind the product, which would turn it into a dark silhouette. If you must shoot indoors in the evening, use two lamps of the same type, one on each side, to reduce shadows, and keep the setup the same for every product.

## Keep the background plain and consistent

A busy background distracts from the product and makes your store look untidy. A plain white, light grey, or beige background works for almost everything, because it keeps the focus on the item and matches most website designs.

You do not need a professional backdrop. A large sheet of white cardboard, a clean white bedsheet stretched flat, or a plain wall will do. For small items, a sheet of white paper curved up behind the product creates a clean, seamless look with no hard line where the surface meets the wall. Use the same background for every product in your store. A store where one product is photographed on a bed, the next on a tiled floor, and the next in a garden feels disorganised, and customers notice.

## Show every angle a customer would check

One front photo is not enough. A careful online shopper wants to see the product the way they would examine it in person. As a rule, prepare at least these shots for each product:

- The front, straight on, filling most of the frame
- The back, so the customer sees the full design
- A close-up of the material, texture, stitching, or key detail
- The product in use or in context, such as worn, held, or placed next to a familiar object for scale
- Any labels, tags, or important features, such as pockets, zips, or buttons

For shoes, show both shoes together and a side view. For bags, show the inside. For skincare or food products, show the packaging and the label clearly, so the customer can read the contents. The more questions your photos answer, the fewer messages you will get asking for more pictures, and the fewer returns you will face. Be honest in every shot: do not hide a flaw by cropping it out, and do not use a filter that changes the colour.

## Steady hands, light editing, and true colours

Blurry photos are the most common phone photography mistake, and they are easy to avoid. Hold the phone with both hands, rest your elbows on a table if needed, and tap the screen on the product itself to focus, waiting a moment for the camera to settle before shooting. Fill the frame with the product, leaving a small margin around it. Photos taken from too far away force customers to zoom in and still see little detail. Take several shots of each angle and check each one at full size, not just as a thumbnail, before moving on.

Editing should make a photo clearer, not different. The safe edits are: straighten a tilted photo, crop out distractions at the edges, brighten a slightly dark photo, and sharpen it a little. Avoid heavy filters, strong colour changes, or smoothing that removes texture. A customer who receives a product that looks different from the photo will feel misled, even if the difference was unintentional. Keep your edits consistent across products, so the whole store looks coherent.

## Size and name your files properly

Before uploading, resize your photos to a sensible size for the web. Very large phone photos, straight from the camera, will slow your store down, and slow pages lose customers. Most store platforms will compress images for you, but starting from a reasonably sized file helps pages load faster and keeps quality good.

Use clear file names that describe the product, such as "black-leather-handbag-front.jpg," rather than the camera's default name, and add a short description to each image when your platform allows it. ## A product photo checklist

Before you publish a new product, check it against this list:

- Is the product clean, tidy, and complete, with all accessories shown?
- Is the lighting natural and even, with true colours?
- Is the background plain and the same as your other products?
- Do you have front, back, detail, and in-use shots?
- Is every photo sharp when viewed at full size?
- Have you edited lightly, without changing the product's true colour?
- Are the files reasonably sized for fast loading, with clear names?

If any answer is no, fix that shot before publishing. A product page with five good photos will outsell a page with one poor photo, even if the product itself is identical.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need a professional camera for product photos?**

No. A modern phone camera is good enough for most small online stores, provided you use good daylight, a plain background, and a steady hand. Spend your effort on lighting and preparation, not on new equipment, until your store grows enough to justify a photographer.

**How many photos should each product have?**

Aim for at least four or five: front, back, a detail close-up, and the product in use or for scale. Products with important features, such as bags with compartments or clothing with a lining, deserve extra shots of those features. More useful photos mean fewer customer questions.

**Can I use the supplier's photos instead of taking my own?**

You can, but your own photos are usually better. Supplier photos are used by many sellers, so your store looks identical to competitors, and they may not match the exact batch you received. Your own photos build trust, because customers see the actual item they will get from you.

**What if my product colour is hard to photograph accurately?**

Photograph it in natural daylight, avoid filters, and compare the photo with the real product before publishing. If the colour still shifts, mention it honestly in the description. Customers respect honesty far more than a perfect-looking photo that disappoints on delivery.

## Photos that let customers buy with confidence

Product photography is not about art. It is about answering a customer's questions before they ask them: What does it look like? What is it made of? How big is it? What will I actually receive? When your photos answer those questions clearly and honestly, customers buy with confidence, and they come back.
