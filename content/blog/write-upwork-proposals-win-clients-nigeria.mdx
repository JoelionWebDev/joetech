You find a job on Upwork that fits you perfectly. You have done this exact work before. You can do it well. You write a proposal, send it, and wait. Nothing happens. You send another. Nothing. You send ten more. Still nothing.

This is the experience of most new freelancers on Upwork — including many talented Nigerians. The problem is rarely your skill. The problem is usually the proposal itself.

## Understand What the Client Is Really Buying

Before you write a single word, read the job post twice. Slowly. Most freelancers skim it, spot a keyword that matches their skill, and start typing. That is why their proposals sound generic — they never actually understood the job.

The client is not buying your skills. They are buying a result: a website that brings in customers, a logo that looks professional, a problem that goes away. Your job in the proposal is to show that you understand the result they want, not just the task they described.

## The First Two Lines Decide Everything

Upwork shows clients only the first two lines of your proposal before they decide whether to click and read more. Those two lines are the most important thing you will write.

Never waste them on "Dear Hiring Manager" or "I am writing to apply for this job." The client already knows you are applying. Instead, open with something that proves you read the post and understand the job. Compare "Hello, I am a web developer with 5 years of experience in HTML, CSS, and JavaScript" with "I can rebuild your restaurant website so customers can see your menu and order on their phones." The first could be sent to any job. The second could only be sent to this one. That is the difference that gets a click.

## A Simple Structure That Works

You do not need a complicated formula. A strong Upwork proposal usually follows this structure:

### 1. The Hook (2–3 sentences)

Show you understood the job. Mention something specific from the post — the client's goal, their business, or their problem — so it is clear this proposal was written for them, not copied and pasted.

### 2. Your Relevant Proof (3–5 sentences)

This is where most freelancers go wrong. They list every skill they have. Do not do that. Instead, give one or two examples of similar work you have done, with a concrete result. "I built a booking website for a hotel in Lagos, and their online bookings grew after launch" beats a long list of tools every time.

If you are new and have no client work yet, use personal projects. A website you built for yourself, a design you made for a friend's business, or a sample project that solves the client's exact problem — all of these count as proof, as long as you present them honestly.

### 3. Your Plan (3–5 sentences)

Briefly explain how you would approach the job. Not a full project plan — just enough to show you have thought about it. For example: "I would start by reviewing your current site and your competitors, then design the homepage and booking page first so you can approve the look before I build the rest. I can deliver a first version within five days." A short plan shows competence and gives the client something to respond to.

### 4. A Question (1–2 sentences)

End with a genuine question about the job — about their timeline, their preferred style, or a detail that was unclear in the post. A question shows you are engaged and gives the client an easy reason to reply. For example: "Do you already have the photos and text ready, or would you like me to help with that too?"

### 5. A Short Close

Keep it brief and confident. "I would be glad to help with this. Let me know a good time for a quick chat." No begging, no desperation. Confidence is persuasive; desperation is not.

## Pricing: Do Not Race to the Bottom

Many Nigerian freelancers make the same mistake: they bid the lowest price, hoping to win on cost. It rarely works. The cheapest proposal is often the first one a client ignores, because very low prices signal low quality or inexperience.

Instead, price based on the value of the work and the client's budget. Read the budget range in the job post. If it fits your rate, bid within it. If the client's budget is far below what the work is worth, it is better to skip the job than to win it and regret it.

If you are new on Upwork and have no reviews yet, you may need to price slightly below your ideal rate to get your first few jobs — but "slightly below" is very different from "cheapest on the platform." Compete on quality and clarity, not on being the lowest number.

## Tailor Every Proposal — Really

Tailoring does not mean writing every proposal from scratch. It means having a structure you reuse, but filling it with details specific to each job: the client's business, their goal, their problem, and your relevant experience for that exact job.

A good test: if you can send the same proposal to a different job without changing a word, it is not tailored enough. At least the hook, the proof, and the question should be specific to the job in front of you. Yes, this takes more time than copy-pasting. But one tailored proposal that wins a client is worth more than fifty generic ones that get ignored.

## Mistakes That Kill Your Proposal

- **Talking only about yourself.** The client cares about their problem, not your life story. Every sentence should connect back to what you can do for them.
- **Writing a very long proposal.** Clients are busy. If your proposal is longer than the job post itself, most of it will not be read. Aim for short paragraphs and clear, direct language.

## Your Profile Matters Too

Your proposal does not stand alone. When a client is interested, the next thing they do is open your profile. If your profile is empty, outdated, or full of spelling errors, the proposal's good work is wasted.

Before you send proposals, make sure your profile is strong: a professional photo, a clear title, an overview that explains who you help and how, and your best work in the portfolio. ## How Many Proposals Should You Send?

Quality beats quantity, but you still need volume — especially when you are starting out. A realistic target for a new freelancer is three to five well-written, tailored proposals per day, focused on jobs that genuinely fit your skills. Do not apply to every job you see: applying to jobs you cannot do well wastes your connects and hurts your response rate.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I have no reviews yet. How do I win my first client?

Focus on jobs with smaller budgets and clear requirements, where the client is more willing to try a new freelancer. Use personal projects as proof, write a tailored proposal, and consider pricing slightly below your ideal rate for the first two or three jobs. Once you have a few good reviews, winning gets much easier.

### Should I mention that I am based in Nigeria?

You do not need to hide it, and you should not make it the focus of your proposal either. Clients care about whether you can do the work, communicate clearly, and deliver on time. Mention your timezone only when it matters — for example, if the client needs overlap with their working hours.

### What if the client does not reply?

Do not take it personally, and do not send multiple follow-up messages. Clients receive many proposals, and silence is normal. Move on to the next job. Your time is better spent on the next tailored proposal.

## Your Next Proposal Could Be the One

Every successful freelancer on Upwork started with zero reviews and zero clients. The difference between those who broke through and those who gave up was rarely talent. It was persistence, and proposals that showed the client they were understood. Read the job properly. Open with something specific. Prove you can do it. Show your plan. Ask a question. Then send it and move on to the next one. Do that consistently, and the replies will come.
