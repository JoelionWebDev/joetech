You do not need a website to start selling online in Nigeria. Every day, thousands of small businesses — fashion vendors, food sellers, skincare brands, phone accessories dealers, bakers, makeup artists — make their sales entirely on WhatsApp. Their customers find them on Instagram, Facebook or through referrals, and the sale happens in a chat.

WhatsApp works so well for selling because it is where your customers already are. They do not need to learn a new app or create an account. They simply message you, ask questions, see your products, pay, and arrange delivery — all in a conversation that feels personal and trustworthy.

This guide shows you how to sell on WhatsApp properly, without a website: how to set up your business profile, organise your products, handle payments, follow up with customers, and grow from a chat-based hustle into a real business.

## Set Up WhatsApp Business Properly

The first step is to use WhatsApp Business, not the regular WhatsApp app. It is free, made by WhatsApp for small businesses, and it gives you tools the regular app does not have. Download WhatsApp Business from the Play Store or App Store. If you already use regular WhatsApp for your business, you can switch — your chats move across, and your number stays the same.

Once installed, set up your business profile properly. This is your shopfront, and many sellers skip it. Go to Settings, then Business settings, then Profile. Add your business name exactly as customers know it, a clear profile photo — your logo or a clean photo of your best product — and a short description that says what you sell and who it is for. "Affordable ready-to-wear dresses for working women in Lagos. Nationwide delivery." tells a customer far more than "DM for price."

Add your business details: your location, your opening hours, your email address, and your website if you have one. If you have a shop or a pickup point, add the address so customers can find you. A complete profile looks professional and answers the questions customers ask before they even message you.

Set up the tools that save you time. Create a greeting message that replies automatically when a new customer messages you for the first time, and an away message for when you are closed or busy. Save quick replies for the questions you answer every day — your prices, your delivery fees, your account details — so you can send them with two taps. You can also create labels to organise your chats: new customers, pending payments, paid orders, delivered. Labels turn a messy inbox into a simple order system.

Finally, keep your business number separate from your personal number if you can. A dedicated business line keeps your personal life private and makes you look professional. ## Build a Catalogue Customers Can Browse

Your WhatsApp catalogue is your shop shelf. It lets customers browse your products, see prices and photos, and add items to a message — without you sending photos one by one. Many sellers underuse it, and it is one of the most powerful free selling tools available.

To build it, go to your business profile and open Catalogue, then add products one at a time. Use clear, well-lit photos of each product — your real product, not a picture downloaded from the internet. Add the product name, the price, and a short description that answers the obvious questions: sizes available, colours, material, what is included. A customer who can see the price and the details is far more likely to buy than one who has to ask "how much?" for every item. When a customer asks about a product, share it directly from your catalogue, and share the catalogue link on your Instagram bio and Facebook page so people can browse even when you are offline.

## Take Payments and Deliver with Confidence

Selling on WhatsApp means handling payments in the chat, and doing it well is what separates trusted sellers from the ones customers avoid. Be clear about your payment terms from the start: full payment before delivery, or a deposit with the balance on delivery — whichever you choose, state it in your profile and your first message to a new customer.

Always confirm payment before you dispatch goods. Ask the customer to send a screenshot of the transfer, then check your own bank app to confirm the money is actually in your account. Screenshots can be faked, and releasing goods on the strength of an image alone is how many sellers lose money. Send a clear receipt for every payment — a short message with the item, the amount paid, the delivery address and the expected delivery date — and keep a copy for yourself. For a fuller look at the tricks to watch for, read our guide on [how to protect your business from online scams in Nigeria](/blog/how-to-protect-business-online-scams-nigeria).

Be honest and specific about delivery. State your delivery fee before the customer pays, not after. Give a realistic delivery time, and message the customer when the order is on its way and when it has been delivered. If there is a delay, say so early — customers forgive delays far more easily than silence. For larger orders, put the agreement in writing in the chat: the items, the total price, the delivery fee, the delivery date and your refund or exchange policy.

## Turn Chats into Repeat Customers

Getting a first sale on WhatsApp is good. Getting the same customer to buy again is where the real money is. The sellers who grow fastest are the ones who treat every chat as the start of a relationship, not a one-time transaction.

Reply fast, and reply like a human. Customers message several sellers at once, and the one who replies first with a helpful answer usually wins the sale. Use your quick replies for speed, but personalise them — use the customer's name and answer their actual question. A friendly, human tone is one of the biggest advantages a small business has over a big store.

Follow up after the sale. A short message a day or two after delivery — "Hope you love the dress! Let us know if you need anything else" — costs you nothing and makes the customer feel valued. It also opens the door to a review, a referral or a repeat order. Save happy customers' feedback, and with their permission share it as testimonials on your status. Social proof sells better than any advert.

Use your WhatsApp Status like a shop window. Post your new arrivals, your best sellers, customer reviews and behind-the-scenes photos regularly. Your status is seen by people who already know and trust you, which makes it one of the best free advertising spaces you have. Post consistently — a few times a week — rather than flooding your status with twenty posts in one day and then going silent for a month. And when a customer is happy, ask them to share your number or catalogue link with a friend — word of mouth is the cheapest and most trusted marketing in Nigeria.

## Growing Beyond WhatsApp When You Are Ready

WhatsApp is a brilliant place to start, but it has limits. You can only reach people who have your number or find your link, and your products are invisible to Google — nobody searching online for what you sell will find you. That is why many successful WhatsApp sellers eventually add a website. A website puts your business on Google, lets new customers find you without a referral, and gives you a professional home for your products, prices and contact details. It does not replace WhatsApp — it feeds it.

You do not need to rush into this. Grow your WhatsApp sales first, learn what your customers want, and build your reputation. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need WhatsApp Business, or can I sell with regular WhatsApp?

You can sell with regular WhatsApp, but WhatsApp Business is free and gives you a business profile, a product catalogue, automated greeting and away messages, quick replies and chat labels. Those tools save you hours every week and make your business look far more professional.

### How do I get customers to find me on WhatsApp?

Share your WhatsApp link or number everywhere your customers already are: your Instagram bio, your Facebook page, your status updates, and in person when you meet people. Ask happy customers to share your number, and post your products consistently on social media with your WhatsApp number attached.

### Should I accept payment on delivery?

It depends on your business and your customers. Payment before delivery protects you from customers who order and disappear, but some customers prefer to pay on delivery because it feels safer for them. Many sellers use a middle ground: full payment before delivery for new customers, and payment on delivery for trusted repeat customers. Whatever you choose, state it clearly before the customer pays.
