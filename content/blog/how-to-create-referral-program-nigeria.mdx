Think about the last time you tried a new restaurant, a new tailor or a new hair vendor in Lagos. Chances are, somebody told you about it. A friend said the food was good. A colleague showed you her dress. Your sister sent you the vendor's WhatsApp number. You trusted that recommendation far more than any advert, because it came from someone you know.

That is referral marketing, and it is the most powerful — and cheapest — way for a Nigerian small business to grow. A referred customer arrives already trusting you. They convert faster, they complain less, they spend more over time, and they often refer other people in turn. Best of all, you do not pay for them the way you pay for adverts. You only reward the referral after the new customer has actually bought something.

Yet most small businesses in Nigeria leave referrals to chance. They hope happy customers will mention them, but they never ask, never reward, and never track it. This guide shows you how to build a simple referral programme that runs on WhatsApp, a notebook and a small reward — no software, no budget, no complexity.

## Why referrals work so well in Nigeria

Nigeria runs on trust networks. We ask our neighbour which mechanic is honest. We ask our church member which school is good. We ask our friend in the office which vendor delivers on time. In a market where scams are common and quality is uncertain, a personal recommendation is the strongest signal a buyer can get.

That makes referrals especially powerful here. A customer who finds you through an Instagram advert is still testing you. A customer who comes because her best friend buys from you every month arrives with the testing already done. She is more likely to buy on the first visit, more likely to pay your price without haggling it to the ground, and more likely to come back.

Referrals are also cheap. An Instagram advert might cost you ₦5,000 to reach a thousand people and bring in three buyers — that is over ₦1,600 per customer, before you have sold anything. A referral reward of ₦1,000 or a 10 percent discount, paid only after a real sale, costs you less and brings a better customer. The maths is not close.

And referrals compound. One happy customer tells two friends. One of those friends buys and tells two more. A business with a working referral programme grows a little every week without spending a kobo on adverts. That is how many of the most successful small businesses in Lekki, Ikeja and Enugu actually grew — not through big marketing budgets, but through customers who kept bringing their people.

## First, make sure your business is worth referring

A referral programme cannot fix a business people do not want to recommend. Before you ask anyone to refer you, be honest about three things.

Is your product or service genuinely good? A referral is a person putting their reputation on the line for you. If your food arrives late, your dress tears after one wash, or your delivery never comes, nobody will risk their friend's anger by recommending you. Fix quality first.

Is the buying experience smooth? Can a new customer reach you easily on WhatsApp, get a clear price, pay by transfer, and receive their order when promised? Every friction — slow replies, unclear prices, failed deliveries — is a reason not to refer.

Would you refer this business to your own sister? If the honest answer is not yet, work on the answer before you work on the programme. The best referral programme in the world is a product people are proud to talk about.

## Design a reward people actually want

The reward is the engine of the programme. It must be valuable enough to motivate an action, cheap enough that you still profit, and simple enough that anyone can understand it in one sentence.

Start with the simplest structure: reward both sides. The existing customer who refers gets something, and the new customer they bring also gets something. This "give and get" design works because the referrer feels generous rather than salesy — they are giving their friend a discount, not just earning one for themselves.

What should the reward be? Cash is clear and motivating — ₦1,000 or ₦2,000 paid by transfer after the new customer's first order. But cash is not always the best choice for a small business, because it leaves your account directly. Discounts keep the money in your business: "Refer a friend and you both get 10 percent off your next order." Free products work beautifully for consumables: a skincare seller can offer a free mini product, a food vendor a free extra portion, a laundry service a free wash. Free delivery is another strong reward in Lagos, where delivery fees of ₦1,500 to ₦3,000 often stop a purchase.

Pick a reward worth roughly 5 to 10 percent of the new customer's first order value. If your average order is ₦20,000, a ₦1,000 to ₦2,000 reward is about right. If your average order is ₦5,000, a ₦500 reward or a 10 percent discount fits better. The reward must never cost more than the profit on the new customer's first order — otherwise you are paying to lose money.

Make the rules dead simple. "Refer a friend. When they buy, you both get 10 percent off." That is a programme anyone can explain to a friend in five seconds. Complicated rules — tiered points, expiry windows, minimum spends, category exclusions — kill referrals, because nobody can remember them well enough to repeat them.

Put a time limit on the reward if you need urgency, but keep it generous: the reward is valid for 30 days, or until the end of the month. Short, aggressive deadlines create pressure that feels like a scam. A calm 30-day window feels like a genuine thank-you.

## Give every customer a simple way to refer

A referral programme only works if referring is effortless. In Nigeria, that means WhatsApp. Almost every referral you get will travel through a WhatsApp chat, a status post, or a forwarded message. Design for that.

The simplest method is the name method. Tell customers: "Tell your friend to mention your name when they order, and you will both get 10 percent off." No codes, no links, no apps. When the new customer orders, you ask one question — "Who referred you?" — and you write the answer in your notebook. This method works for any business, including a market stall with no website at all.

If you want something a little more trackable, use simple referral codes. Give each regular customer a short code based on their name — for example, "ADA10" for Ada, or "CHIDI10" for Chidi. When a new customer uses the code, you know exactly who to reward. Print the code on a small card you include with every order, or send it by WhatsApp after a purchase: "Your referral code is ADA10. Share it with friends — when they order with it, you both get 10 percent off."

You can also create a shareable message your customers can forward. Write it for them, in friendly language, with your WhatsApp link and the offer included: "I buy my Ankara from [Your Business Name] and the quality is excellent. Use my code ADA10 for 10 percent off your first order. Chat with them here: wa.me/2348012345678." When you write the message for them, you control what it says, and the customer only has to forward it. Most people will forward a ready-made message who would never write their own.

For businesses with a website or Instagram page, a short referral link in your bio helps too, but do not make the link the only method. Many of your best referrers are not tech-savvy, and a name or a code they can say out loud will always beat a link they cannot remember.

## Ask at the right moment

Most businesses never ask for referrals at all. Those that do often ask at the wrong time — weeks after the purchase, when the excitement has faded, or in a cold broadcast message that feels like spam.

The best moment to ask is right after a happy moment. The customer has just received their order and told you they love it. They have just complimented your food at a party. They have just reordered for the third time. They have just left a good review or posted your product on their status. That is the moment to say: "Thank you so much. If you know anyone who would love this too, send them your code — you will both get 10 percent off."

Build the ask into your normal routine. After every delivery, send a short follow-up message: "Your order has arrived. I hope you love it. If you have a friend who would enjoy this too, share your code with them." After every positive review, reply with thanks and the referral offer. On your WhatsApp status, once or twice a month, post a simple reminder: "Love what you bought? Share your code with a friend and you both save 10 percent."

Train your staff to ask too. Your sales girl, your rider, your assistant — anyone who talks to customers should know the referral offer and mention it naturally. A rider handing over a package can say, "If you like it, tell a friend and mention your name — you both get a discount next time." That single sentence, repeated fifty times a week, is a marketing department that costs you nothing.

Never beg, and never pressure. One friendly ask is marketing. Repeated asks after someone has said no is harassment, and it will cost you the customer you already have.

## Track every referral with a notebook and your phone

A referral programme without tracking is a promise you cannot keep. If a customer refers a friend and never gets the reward, they will never refer again — and they will tell people you do not keep your word.

You do not need software. A notebook or a simple Google Sheet with five columns is enough: date, referrer name, new customer name, order amount, and reward given. Every time a new customer says "Ada referred me," you write it down. Every time you pay or apply a reward, you tick it off.

Check the sheet every week. How many referrals came in? How many turned into paying customers? Which referrers are your best advocates? A customer who has brought you five new buyers is a VIP — treat them like one. Send them a personal thank-you, a small gift, or an early look at new stock. Your top referrers are the most valuable marketers you have, and they cost you almost nothing.

Pay rewards fast. The moment the new customer's payment clears, send the referrer their reward or confirm their discount. A reward that arrives in two days builds trust. A reward that takes three weeks and two reminders destroys it. If you offer cash, transfer it the same day and send a screenshot. If you offer a discount, apply it immediately and tell the referrer their next order is already discounted.

Keep the whole programme honest. Never invent referrals. Never promise a reward you cannot afford. And never run a referral scheme that pays people for signing up without buying — that attracts people who want free money, not customers, and it will be shared in groups as a "free money" trick until your phone cannot cope. Your programme rewards real purchases by real customers. That is the whole point.

## Promote the programme without being annoying

Your customers cannot use a programme they do not know exists. Tell them, but tell them in the right places and at the right frequency.

Put the offer on your WhatsApp Business catalogue or your pinned message. Mention it on your Instagram bio or in a highlights story. Print it on a small card inside every delivery bag: "Love it? Share your code, you both save 10 percent." Add one line to your email signature or your invoice. Mention it in your WhatsApp status once or twice a month — not every day.

The best promotion, though, is a story. When a customer earns a reward, ask if you can share it: "Ada referred three friends this month and got ₦3,000 off her next order." Real stories make the programme feel real. A poster that says "Refer and earn" is an advert. A story that says "Ada earned ₦3,000 by telling her friends" is proof.

Seasonal pushes work well too. In December, when people are buying gifts and attending parties, a referral push can double your new customers. Before a new product launch, tell your best customers first and give them a referral bonus for launch week. Tie the programme to moments when people are already talking — holidays, weddings, back-to-school, festive seasons — and it will ride on conversations that are already happening.

## Mistakes that kill referral programmes

The most common mistake is making the reward too small to matter. A ₦100 reward on a ₦25,000 order is an insult, not an incentive. Make the reward feel generous, even if it is modest in absolute terms.

The second mistake is making it hard. If a customer needs to fill a form, download an app, or remember a twelve-digit code, they will not bother. Name, code, or forwarded message — that is the ceiling of effort you can ask for.

The third mistake is forgetting to reward. Every unrewarded referral is a broken promise, and broken promises travel fast. If your tracking is messy, fix the tracking before you promote the programme further.

The fourth mistake is rewarding the wrong behaviour. Do not pay for clicks, sign-ups, or "likes." Pay only for paying customers. A programme that rewards sign-ups fills your list with people who never buy. A programme that rewards purchases fills your business with buyers.

The fifth mistake is treating referrals as a one-time campaign. A referral programme is not a promo you run for two weeks. It is a permanent feature of your business, like good customer service. Keep it running, keep reminding people gently, keep paying rewards promptly, and it will keep producing customers month after month.

## Your first 30 days: a simple launch plan

Week one, design the programme. Choose your reward, write your one-sentence offer, and create your tracking sheet. Pick five of your happiest, most loyal customers and tell them personally — by voice note or chat — that you are starting a referral programme and they are the first to get a code.

Week two, tell everyone. Put the offer on your WhatsApp status, your catalogue, your delivery cards, and your Instagram page. Add the referral ask to your post-delivery message. Train your staff to mention it.

Week three, follow up. Check your tracking sheet. Who has referred someone? Pay their reward immediately and thank them publicly if they are comfortable with it. Who has not? Send a gentle reminder to your best customers.

Week four, review. How many referrals came in? How many became customers? What did you spend on rewards, and what did you earn from new customers? If the numbers work — and for most businesses they do — keep the programme running and make it a permanent part of how you do business.

A single loyal customer in Lagos who loves what you do is worth more than a thousand advert views. Give that customer a reason to talk, a reward for talking, and an easy way to do it, and they will do your marketing for you — happily, honestly, and for free. That is the whole programme. Start with five customers this week, and let their friends become your next customers.
