If you sell anything in Nigeria — clothes, food, hair, cakes, phone accessories, services — your customers are already on WhatsApp. Many small businesses in Lekki, Lagos and across the country run their entire business from WhatsApp. But there is a big difference between a personal WhatsApp account and a WhatsApp Business profile that is set up to sell. One looks like a hobby. The other looks like a business customers can trust with their money.

WhatsApp Business is a free app made for small businesses. It gives you a business profile, a product catalogue, automatic greeting messages, quick replies, labels for organising customers, and business statistics. Setting it up properly takes less than an hour, and it changes how customers see you from the very first message. This guide walks you through every step, in plain language, so your WhatsApp starts working like a real shop front.

## Download WhatsApp Business and Move Your Number

The first step is to get the WhatsApp Business app. It is free on both the Google Play Store and the Apple App Store. Search for "WhatsApp Business" — the one made by Meta — and install it. If you already run your business on your regular WhatsApp number, you can move that number to WhatsApp Business. Your chats and contacts move with you, so you will not lose your customers' conversations.

When the app asks for a number, you have a choice to make. Ideally, use a dedicated business number — a second SIM or a number you keep only for the business. This keeps your personal chats separate and makes your business look professional. If you only have one number for now, that is fine; move that number to WhatsApp Business and keep going.

## Fill In Your Business Profile Completely

Tap on Settings, then Business Settings, then Profile. This is the most important screen in the whole app, because it is the first thing a new customer sees when they open your chat. Fill in every field.

Start with your profile photo. Use your business logo, or a clear photo of your product if you do not have a logo yet. A photo of your face also works for personal brands like makeup artists and hairstylists. Avoid blurry photos and memes — your profile photo is your shop sign.

Next, write your business description. You have about 256 characters, so make them count. Say what you sell, who it is for, and where you are. For example: "Handmade cakes and small chops in Lekki. We deliver across Lagos. Order via WhatsApp." That one line tells a customer everything they need to know before they even say hello.

Then add your address, business hours, email and website or social media link. Set your business hours honestly — if you reply between 8am and 8pm, say so. If your shop is at a physical location, add the address so customers can find you. If you work from home and do deliveries only, say "Delivery only" so nobody shows up at your door unannounced.

## Set Up Your Product Catalogue

The catalogue is what turns WhatsApp Business from a chat app into a shop. Customers can browse your products, see prices, and tap to ask about an item — all without you sending photos one by one every time someone asks "what do you have?"

To add a product, go to Business Tools, tap Catalogue, then Add New Item. Upload a clear photo of the product — good light, plain background, and the product filling most of the frame. Add the product name, the price in Naira, and a short description answering the questions customers always ask: size, colour options, material, and how long delivery takes.

Add your best sellers first, then the rest. Keep prices honest and current. Nothing kills trust faster than a catalogue price of ₦15,000 and a chat price of ₦20,000. If a product is sold out, hide it from the catalogue instead of deleting it, so you can bring it back when it is restocked.

## Use Quick Replies, Greeting Messages and Away Messages

As your business grows, you will answer the same questions again and again: "How much is delivery to Ikeja?" "Do you have size 42?" "What is your account number?" Quick replies let you answer in seconds instead of typing the same message fifty times a day.

Go to Business Tools, then Quick Replies, and create shortcuts for your most common answers. For example, type "/" followed by "price" to send your full price list, or "/delivery" to send your delivery rates and locations. Set up quick replies for your price list, delivery information, payment details, and your location. It saves you hours every week and customers get instant answers.

Greeting messages are automatic messages sent to people who message you for the first time, or after 14 days of no contact. Write something warm and useful: "Hello! Thank you for contacting [Your Business Name]. Please check our catalogue to see our products, or type what you are looking for." A good greeting message does the work of a shop assistant.

Away messages are just as important. Set one up for the hours you are not available, so customers are not left hanging. Something simple works: "Thank you for your message. We are closed for today and will reply by 9am tomorrow." Customers are far more patient when they know when to expect a reply. Set your business hours in the app so the away message switches on automatically.

## Organise Customers With Labels and Broadcast Lists

When you have many customers, keeping track of everyone gets messy. Labels solve this. WhatsApp Business lets you tag chats with coloured labels — for example, "New customer", "Paid", "Pending payment", "VIP", or "Delivery today". To add a label, open a chat, tap the customer's name, and choose a label. You can then tap on a label to see everyone in that group at once.

Labels help you follow up properly. At the end of the day, check your "Pending payment" label and send a gentle reminder. Check your "New customer" label and thank people who bought for the first time. This kind of follow-up is what turns one-time buyers into repeat customers.

## Share Your WhatsApp Link Everywhere Customers See You

A great WhatsApp Business profile only works if customers can find it. WhatsApp gives you a short link — wa.me followed by your number — that opens a chat with you directly. Put this link everywhere: your Instagram bio, your Facebook page, your Google Business Profile, your flyers, your shop banner, and your email signature.

For printed materials like flyers, business cards and product packaging, a QR code works even better than a typed link. Customers simply point their phone camera at it and a chat with you opens — you can make one in seconds with Joetech's free [QR code generator](/tools/qr-code-generator). One scan and a stranger becomes a customer in your inbox.

The easier you make it to reach you, the more sales conversations you will start.

## Check Your Statistics and Keep Improving

WhatsApp Business shows you simple statistics: how many messages were sent, delivered, read, and received. Check them weekly. If many messages are read but few turn into orders, your prices or your replies may need work.

Pay attention to what customers actually ask. If ten people ask the same question this week, add the answer to your profile description, your catalogue description, or a quick reply. Your profile should keep getting better as you learn what customers want to know.

Also keep your status updated. WhatsApp Status is free advertising to everyone who has your number saved. Post new products, customer reviews, and restock announcements a few times a week so your business stays visible. A business that posts regularly looks alive; a silent one looks closed.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is WhatsApp Business really free?

Yes. WhatsApp Business is completely free to download and use, on both Android and iPhone. There are no monthly fees. WhatsApp also offers a paid API for large companies, but small businesses do not need it — the free app has everything covered in this guide, including the catalogue, quick replies and labels.

### Can I use the same number for WhatsApp and WhatsApp Business?

No, one number can only be on one of the two apps at a time. If you move your number to WhatsApp Business, your regular WhatsApp on that number stops working, but your chats and contacts move over with you. Most business owners keep a personal number on regular WhatsApp and a business number on WhatsApp Business.

### How do I stop customers from messaging me at midnight?

Set your business hours in the app and turn on the away message. Outside your hours, customers automatically receive your away message telling them when you will reply. You can also mute notifications at night. Customers respect clear boundaries when you communicate them.

### Why should I add prices to my catalogue instead of saying "DM for price"?

Because "DM for price" slows everything down. Customers compare options quickly, and many will simply move to the next seller who shows a price. Showing prices in your catalogue filters in serious buyers, reduces time-wasting questions, and builds trust. If your prices vary by size or quantity, show the starting price and explain the range in the description.
