If you sell on WhatsApp in Nigeria, you already know the feeling. You wake up to forty unread chats. Some are people asking for your price list. Some paid yesterday and are waiting for delivery. Some promised to pay "tomorrow" three days ago. And somewhere in that pile is a returning customer who wants to order again — if you can find her chat before she gives up and buys from someone else.

This is the daily reality for thousands of Nigerian small businesses: the fashion vendor in Lekki, the food seller in Surulere, the phone accessories dealer in Ikeja Computer Village, the skincare brand shipping to Abuja and Port Harcourt. WhatsApp is where the money is made, but it is also where customers quietly get lost. A chat that slips down the list is a sale that never happens.

This guide shows you how to use WhatsApp Business labels to organise your customers step by step, with a labelling system that fits the way Nigerian businesses actually sell — cash on delivery, bank transfers, instalment payments, and plenty of "I will get back to you" conversations.

## What Are WhatsApp Business Labels?

Labels are small coloured tags you add to a chat or a contact inside WhatsApp Business. Each label has a name and a colour, and you can attach more than one label to the same person. When you open the label view, WhatsApp shows you only the chats carrying that label.

Think of them like the coloured stickers a market trader puts on different bags of goods, or the way a good shopkeeper keeps separate notebooks for credit customers and cash customers. The label itself does not change anything about the chat — it simply lets you find people fast and treat different groups of customers differently.

WhatsApp Business comes with five default labels already created for you: New customer, New order, Pending payment, Paid, and Order complete. You can keep them, rename them, delete them, and add your own — up to twenty labels in total. Twenty sounds like a lot until you start creating one for every small situation, so the trick is to keep your system simple and disciplined.

Labels are private. Your customers never see them. A customer labelled "Owing me" will never know, and a customer labelled "VIP" will simply notice that you remember her name, her usual order, and that you reply quickly. That is the whole point: labels let you give personal service at scale, even when you are the only person running the business.

## Step 1: Switch to WhatsApp Business (If You Have Not Already)

Labels only exist in WhatsApp Business, not in the regular WhatsApp app. If you are still selling from a personal WhatsApp account, switching is the first step, and it is free.

Download WhatsApp Business from the Play Store or App Store and register with your business number. If your current selling number is already on regular WhatsApp, the Business app will ask if you want to move that number over — your chats and groups move with it, so you do not lose your customers.

Once you are in, set up your business profile properly: your business name, a clear description of what you sell, your location, your opening hours, and your catalogue. A complete profile builds trust before a customer even says hello, and it makes the label system far more useful, because the people you label are arriving at a business that looks like a business.

One practical note for Nigerian sellers: many people run the business on a second phone or a dual-SIM phone so that personal chats and business chats stay separate. If you can afford a second phone, even a modest one, it makes labelling much easier — you open the Business app and every chat in it is a customer, not a mix of customers, family, and old school friends.

## Step 2: Design Your Label System Before You Create Anything

The biggest mistake sellers make with labels is creating them on the fly. You tag one person "New", another "New customer", a third "Fresh lead", and within a week you have three labels that mean the same thing and none of them is reliable. Decide your system first, write it down, and then create the labels.

A good system for a Nigerian small business usually has three kinds of labels: where the customer is in the buying journey, what kind of customer they are, and what action you still owe them. You do not need all three kinds from day one. Start with the buying journey — that is where the money is.

Here is a starter system that works for most businesses selling physical products or services:

- **New Enquiry** — someone who asked about a product or price but has not ordered yet.
- **To Follow Up** — someone who showed interest, asked questions, and went quiet. This is your most valuable label.
- **Order Placed** — the customer has confirmed an order and you are preparing it.
- **Paid** — payment confirmed, whether by transfer, POS, or cash.
- **Pending Payment** — the customer confirmed an order but has not paid yet, or paid a deposit and owes a balance.
- **Delivered** — the order has reached the customer.
- **Repeat Customer** — someone who has bought from you more than once.
- **VIP** — your best customers: they buy often, pay quickly, or order in large quantities.

That is eight labels, and eight is enough. You can add a ninth or tenth later for something specific to your business — a food vendor might add "Weekly Subscriber", a fashion seller might add "Pre-order", a service business might add "Consultation Booked". But resist the urge to create a label for every situation. A system with twenty labels is a system nobody maintains.

Write your list on paper or in your phone's notes app. Next to each label, write one sentence explaining exactly who gets it. For example: "Pending Payment — customer confirmed an order and I am still waiting for full or part payment." That sentence is what keeps you consistent three months from now.

## Step 3: Create Your Labels in the App

Creating a label takes less than a minute, and you can do it two ways: from the labels menu, or directly from a chat.

To create labels from the menu, open WhatsApp Business, tap the three dots in the top right corner, tap "Labels", and tap the plus sign or "Add label". Type the name, pick a colour, and save. Repeat for each label on your list.

To label someone from a chat, open the chat, tap the person's name at the top, scroll down to "Labels", and tick the ones that apply. You can also long-press a chat on your chat list, tap the label icon at the top, and choose the labels there. Labelling from the chat list is faster when you are working through a long list of chats in one sitting.

Choose colours with meaning so your eyes learn the system. Many sellers use green for money received (Paid, Delivered), red or orange for money owed (Pending Payment), blue for new people (New Enquiry), and purple or gold for their best customers (VIP, Repeat Customer). There is no official rule — what matters is that you can glance at a chat list and know, without reading a single message, who needs your attention.

One more practical point: label names should be short. WhatsApp shows the label under the contact name, and long names get cut off. "Pending Payment" is fine. "Customer Who Has Not Paid Balance Yet" is not.

## Step 4: Label Your Existing Chats (The One-Hour Clean-Up)

This is the step most people skip, and it is the step that makes the whole system work. Labels only help if your current customers are labelled. Set aside one hour — perhaps on a Sunday evening when orders are slow — and work through your chat list from the top.

Start with the most recent chats, because those are the live ones: people waiting for deliveries, people who paid today, people who asked a question this morning. Label each one according to your system. Then work backwards through the older chats, labelling the ones that still matter and archiving or deleting the ones that do not.

Be honest during this clean-up. That chat from six months ago where someone asked for a price and never replied? Label it "To Follow Up" only if you genuinely plan to message them. If you will never follow up, archive the chat and move on. A label system full of people you will never contact is just clutter with colours.

A realistic target: label your last fifty to one hundred chats. That is enough to cover your active customers and your recent enquiries. Anything older than that can wait — you will label people as they message you from now on, which is a much lighter habit than trying to organise a year of chat history in one sitting.

If you have a staff member or a family member who helps with the business, do this clean-up together. Agree on what each label means, so that when your assistant labels a chat, it means the same thing as when you label one. A label system that two people use differently is worse than no system at all.

## Step 5: Use Labels Every Day as Part of Your Routine

A label system only pays off if using it becomes a habit. The good news is that the habit is tiny: every time a chat changes state, you change its label. It takes about three seconds.

Build it into a simple daily routine:

**Morning — check Pending Payment and To Follow Up first.** Before you post your morning status or reply to new messages, open those two labels. These are the chats where money is waiting. Send a polite reminder to anyone whose payment is overdue: "Good morning! Just checking in on your order — your item is ready whenever you are." Keep it friendly and short. Many Nigerian customers genuinely forget, or they were waiting for salary day, or their network failed during the transfer. A gentle nudge recovers sales that silence would have lost.

**During the day — label as you go.** When a new person messages you, label them "New Enquiry" immediately, before you even reply. When someone confirms an order, change the label to "Order Placed". When they send proof of payment and you confirm it in your bank app or POS, change it to "Paid". When the dispatch rider confirms delivery, change it to "Delivered". Three seconds each time, and your chat list always tells the truth.

**Evening — review Order Placed and Pending Payment.** Before you close for the day, check whether any order placed today is still unpaid, and whether any delivery is still outstanding. This five-minute check prevents the most common small-business embarrassment: a customer who paid and never got their goods because the chat slipped down the list.

## Step 6: Send Broadcasts to Labelled Groups (Your Secret Weapon)

Here is where labels start making you money directly. WhatsApp Business lets you send a broadcast message to everyone carrying a particular label, and each person receives it as a private message from you — not as a group chat.

This is far better than posting on your status and hoping the right people see it. A status disappears in twenty-four hours and reaches whoever happens to be online. A broadcast to your "Repeat Customer" label reaches your proven buyers directly, in their inbox, where they will actually see it.

Some broadcasts that work well for Nigerian businesses:

- **New stock alert to Repeat Customers and VIPs:** "Our new Ankara collection just landed. VIP customers get first pick before I post it publicly — send me a message if you want photos." This makes loyal customers feel special, and it often sells out a batch before it ever reaches your status.
- **A gentle reminder to To Follow Up:** "Hello! You asked about our glow package last week. We still have a few in stock, and delivery to Lekki is free this weekend." One message like this, sent once, can revive a conversation that went cold.
- **A payment reminder to Pending Payment:** keep it short, polite, and specific — mention the item, the amount, and your account details so the customer can pay without asking questions.
- **A festive greeting to Delivered customers:** at Christmas, Sallah, or Easter, a short greeting to people who have bought from you before keeps your business in their mind for the next time they need what you sell.

But use broadcasts with restraint. WhatsApp limits broadcasts to 256 contacts at a time, and — this is important — a broadcast only reaches people who have your number saved in their phone. That is another reason to ask customers to save your number, and to keep your own contact list tidy.

Never broadcast every day, and never broadcast the same offer to everyone. A customer who gets a sales message from you three times a week will mute you or block you, and a blocked number is a customer lost forever. Once or twice a week, to the right label, with something genuinely useful or exclusive — that is the rhythm that works.

## What to Do Next

1. **Create your eight labels today.** Open WhatsApp Business, go to Labels, and create the starter set from Step 2 — New Enquiry, To Follow Up, Order Placed, Paid, Pending Payment, Delivered, Repeat Customer, and VIP. Pick your colours and write down what each one means.

2. **Do the one-hour clean-up this week.** Work through your recent chats and label them. Start with today's chats, then move backwards. When you finish, your chat list will finally show you who owes you money, who is waiting for delivery, and who needs a follow-up.

3. **Send your first labelled broadcast this week.** Pick one label — Repeat Customers is the safest first choice — and send those customers something useful: early access to new stock, a small thank-you, or a limited offer. Keep it short, keep it personal, and watch how many reply. That reply rate is the proof that the system works.

Labels will not sell your products for you, but they will stop you losing sales you already earned. In a market where most sellers run their business from memory and a chaotic chat list, the seller who knows exactly who owes what, who is waiting, and who to follow up with has a genuine advantage — and it costs nothing but an hour of setup and three seconds per chat.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are WhatsApp Business labels free?

Yes. Labels are a built-in, free feature of WhatsApp Business on both Android and iPhone. There is no subscription, no payment, and no limit on how often you use them. You can create up to twenty labels per account.

### Can my customers see the labels I give them?

No. Labels are visible only to you (and anyone else who uses your WhatsApp Business account). A customer will never know they are labelled "Pending Payment" or "VIP" — they will only notice that you remember them and follow up properly.

### How many labels can I create on WhatsApp Business?

You can create up to twenty labels. WhatsApp Business comes with five default labels — New customer, New order, Pending payment, Paid, and Order complete — which you can rename, delete, or keep alongside your own.

### Can I add more than one label to the same customer?

Yes. A single chat can carry several labels at once. For example, a customer can be both "Paid" and "Repeat Customer", or both "Order Placed" and "VIP". Use this to combine the buying-journey label with the customer-type label.

### Do labels work with broadcast lists?

Yes, and this is one of their best uses. When you create a broadcast in WhatsApp Business, you can select recipients by label, so a message goes only to the people in that group. Remember that broadcasts only reach contacts who have saved your number.

### What happens to my labels if I change my phone?

If you back up your WhatsApp Business chats to Google Drive (Android) or iCloud (iPhone) and restore that backup on your new phone, your labels should come with your chats. Always confirm they are intact after restoring, and keep a written copy of your label list as a backup.
