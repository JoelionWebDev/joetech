A fashion designer in Lekki wants to sell a new collection. A pharmacy in Ikeja wants customers to find it on Google and trust it enough to walk in. Both ask for "a website", but they do not need the same thing. One needs a single focused page that sells one offer. The other needs a full site that answers many questions over time.

This confusion costs Nigerian small businesses money every year. Some pay for a full website when a single landing page would have done the job faster and cheaper. Others build one landing page and then wonder why Google never sends them visitors, or why customers keep asking questions the page never answers. A landing page and a full website are different tools for different jobs, and this guide explains how to choose the right one.

## What a landing page actually is

A landing page is a single web page built around one goal and one audience. It has one offer, one message, and one clear action for the visitor to take: buy a product, book a consultation, join a waiting list, or send a WhatsApp message.

Everything on the page serves that single goal. The headline states the offer plainly. A short section explains the benefit. Photos show the product. A few lines answer the most common objections. Then the page asks the visitor to act, and it asks again further down. There is no navigation menu leading visitors away, because the page has no other pages.

Landing pages are commonly used for a product launch, a seasonal promotion, or an advert campaign. A skincare brand launching a new soap can send Instagram traffic to a landing page for that soap alone. A training provider running a Facebook advert for a weekend class can send clicks to a page about that class alone. The strength of a landing page is focus: with nothing else to click, the visitor's attention stays on the one thing you want them to do. The weakness is scope: it cannot tell your whole business story or rank on Google for many different searches.

## What a full website actually is

A full website is a collection of pages that together present your whole business: a homepage, an about page, services or products pages, and a contact page. Many businesses also add a blog, a gallery, a pricing page, and a frequently asked questions page.

Each page has its own job. The homepage gives the overview. The services pages explain each offer in detail. The about page builds trust by showing who is behind the business. The contact page makes it easy to reach you. A visitor can move between pages, compare options, and learn at their own pace before deciding.

A full website also works for you when you are not watching. Google can find and rank each page for different searches, so a customer searching for a service you offer can land on the exact page that answers them. The strength of a full website is depth and staying power: it builds trust over time and brings in visitors from search. The weakness is that it takes more time, more content, and more money to build well.

## When a landing page is the right choice

Choose a landing page when you have one clear offer and one clear audience, and you need results quickly. It is the right tool in these situations:

- You are running adverts on Facebook, Instagram, or Google and need a page that matches the advert exactly
- You are launching a single product, a seasonal offer, or a limited-time promotion
- You want to collect bookings, orders, or sign-ups for one specific thing
- You are testing whether people want a new product before investing in a full site
- You have a small budget and a short deadline, and one offer matters more than everything else right now

A landing page also works well as an addition to a website you already have. Many businesses with a full site still build landing pages for each advert campaign, because a visitor who clicks an advert about one service should land on a page about that service, not on a general homepage. ## When a full website is the right choice

Choose a full website when your business needs to be found, understood, and trusted over time. It is the right tool in these situations:

- Customers search on Google for what you offer and you want them to find you
- You offer several services or products that each need their own explanation
- Customers need reassurance before buying, such as your location, your story, your prices, and your past work
- You want a professional home on the internet that belongs to you, not to a social media platform
- You plan to publish articles, answer common questions, or build authority in your field

A full website is also the safer long-term investment. Social media accounts can be restricted or lost, and their reach changes with every algorithm update. A website you own keeps working, keeps ranking, and keeps collecting enquiries regardless of what happens on any single platform.

## How to decide: five questions to ask yourself

If you are still unsure, answer these five questions honestly:

1. **How many things am I selling right now?** One offer points to a landing page. Several offers point to a website.
2. **How will visitors find me?** Adverts and social media links point to a landing page. Google searches point to a website.
3. **How much trust do I need to build first?** A quick purchase needs little trust, so a landing page works. A high-value service needs more trust, so a website works better.
4. **What is my deadline?** Days point to a landing page. Weeks point to a website.
5. **What happens after this campaign?** If the offer ends and you will need something else, a website gives you a home to build on. If this is a one-time test, a landing page is enough.

Many Nigerian businesses end up with both, and that is often the right answer: a full website as the permanent home of the business, plus landing pages for specific campaigns, promotions, and adverts. Start with whichever one your next goal needs, and add the other when the business is ready for it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can a landing page rank on Google?**

It can rank for a very specific search related to its one offer, but it will rarely rank for many different searches. A full website with several pages has far more chances to appear in search results, because each page can target a different topic.

**Can I start with a landing page and expand it into a full website later?**

Yes, and this is a sensible path for many small businesses. A landing page can grow into a homepage, and new pages can be added around it as the business grows. Just make sure the landing page is built on a proper domain and platform you own, so the expansion is easy.

**Do I still need a landing page if I already have a website?**

Often, yes. Your website is your home, but a landing page is the right destination for an advert about one specific offer. Sending advert traffic to a focused landing page usually produces more enquiries than sending it to a general homepage.

**Which one should I build first if my budget is small?**

Build the one that matches your next goal. If your next goal is to sell one product or fill one class, build a landing page. If your next goal is to be found on Google and look established to new customers, build a simple website with the essential pages and grow it over time.

## Choose the tool that fits the job

A landing page is a sharp tool for one job. A full website is a complete workshop for your whole business. Neither is better in general. The right choice is the one that fits what your customers need from you right now, how they will find you, and what you can afford to build well.

Start with the goal, not with the technology. If the goal is one offer to one audience, build a landing page. If the goal is a lasting, trusted presence that brings customers from search and answers their questions, build a website. And when your business grows enough to need both, add the second one with confidence, knowing each is doing the job it was built for.
