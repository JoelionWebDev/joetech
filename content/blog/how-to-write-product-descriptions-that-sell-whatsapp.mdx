A customer opens your WhatsApp catalogue. They see a photo of a bag they like. Below it, the description says only "Bag available." No price. No size. No colour. No delivery information. What does the customer do? Most of them close the catalogue and move on. The few who message you will ask the same five questions, one at a time, over an hour. By the time you reply, they have bought from someone else.

This is the reality of selling on WhatsApp in Nigeria. Your product description is your salesperson. It works while you are busy, while you are sleeping, and while you are attending to another customer. A good description answers questions before they are asked, builds trust, and makes ordering feel easy. A weak description does the opposite: it creates doubt, slows everything down, and loses sales.

Writing a good WhatsApp product description is a skill you can learn in one afternoon. You do not need big grammar or long sentences. You need to know what your customer wants to know, write it clearly, and end with a simple instruction to order.

In this guide, you will learn the exact structure of a description that sells, how to write headlines that grab attention, how to handle price, size, and delivery, and how to use your catalogue, status, and broadcast messages to sell more.

## Why Your Words Matter as Much as Your Photos

A good photo makes a customer stop. Good words make them buy. On WhatsApp, where customers cannot touch the product or ask a shop assistant, your description is the only thing standing between interest and payment.

Think about your own buying habits. When you see a product on WhatsApp, what do you check first after the photo? The price. Then the size, the colour, and how to get it. If any of that information is missing, you either message the seller or you leave. Most people leave, because messaging a stranger takes effort and they are not sure the seller will reply quickly.

A complete description removes that friction. When a customer can see the price, the size, the available colours, and the delivery arrangement in one place, they can decide immediately. They do not need to wait for your reply. They can pay and send their address. That speed is a real advantage, especially when you sell products that many other sellers also sell.


## The Six Things Every Description Must Include

Every product description on WhatsApp should answer six questions. If your description answers all six, it will sell better than most of your competitors' descriptions.

First, what is the product? State the name clearly. "Handmade leather slippers" is better than "Slippers." "Ankara midi dress with pockets" is better than "Dress." Be specific, because customers search their memory for exactly what they want, and a specific name helps them recognise it.

Second, how much does it cost? Always include the price. Sellers who hide the price believe it forces customers to message them, but in practice it drives most customers away. When the price is visible, only serious buyers message you, and you spend less time on people who were never going to buy. Write the price plainly: "Price: 25,000 naira." If you have a discount, show the old price and the new price so the customer can see the saving.

Third, what sizes, colours, or options are available? List them. "Available in sizes 38 to 43" or "Available in black, brown, and navy blue." If an item comes in only one size, say so: "One size fits all" or "Only size 42 left." This saves you from answering the same question ten times a day.

Fourth, what is it made of, and what makes it special? Customers want to know the material and the quality. "Genuine leather" means something different from "synthetic leather." "Cotton Ankara" is different from "polyester." Mention the key detail that justifies your price: the handmade stitching, the soft inner lining, the long battery life, the pure shea butter with no additives. One or two honest details are enough.

Fifth, how does the customer get it? State your delivery arrangement. "Delivery within Lagos: 2,000 naira. Delivery outside Lagos: 3,500 naira." Or "Free delivery within Lekki." Or "Pickup available at our shop in Ikeja." Customers who live far away need this information before they pay, and including it prevents cancelled orders.

Sixth, how does the customer order? End every description with a clear instruction. "Send us a message with your size and address to order." "Tap the message button to place your order." "Payment confirms your order." A customer who knows exactly what to do next is more likely to do it.

## Start With a Headline That Grabs Attention

The first line of your description is the most important, because it is what the customer sees first in your catalogue and on your status. A strong first line makes the customer keep reading. A weak first line makes them scroll past.

A good first line does one of three things: it names the benefit, it names the problem the product solves, or it creates urgency. "Soft leather slippers that feel like walking on clouds" names the benefit. "Tired of shoes that hurt your feet after one hour?" names the problem. "Only three pieces left in size 42" creates urgency. All three are better than "Slippers available."

Keep your first line short. One sentence is enough. Customers do not read long introductions. They read benefits and prices.

Use simple, everyday language. Write the way you talk to a customer in your shop. "This dress is made from soft cotton Ankara that does not fade after washing" is better than "This premium-quality garment features superior fabric technology."

## Write for the Customer, Not for Yourself

Many sellers write descriptions about themselves: how long they have been in business, how many products they have, how hard they work. Customers do not buy because of your story. They buy because the product solves their problem or makes them feel good.

Instead of writing "We sell the best quality bags in Lagos," write what the bag does for the customer: "This bag fits your laptop, your phone, and your makeup bag, and it still looks neat on your shoulder." Instead of "Our cream is very good," write "This cream clears dark spots in two to three weeks of daily use, and it does not bleach your skin."

Focus on the outcome. A customer buying a school bag cares that it is strong enough to last the whole school year. A customer buying a wig cares that it looks natural and is easy to maintain. A customer buying a power bank cares that it charges a phone three times on one charge. Describe the outcome in plain words, and the customer will see themselves using the product.

Also write for your specific customer. If you sell baby clothes, your customer is a mother who cares about soft fabric and easy washing. If you sell men's shoes, your customer cares about comfort and durability. Think about the one person most likely to buy, and write directly to them. Use the word "you" often.

## Use Short Lines and Simple Formatting

WhatsApp is read on a phone, usually in a hurry, often on a bus or between tasks. Long blocks of text are hard to read on a small screen. Your description should be easy to scan in five seconds.

Break your description into short lines. Put each piece of information on its own line. Use line breaks between the headline, the details, the price, and the call to action. For example:

Soft leather slippers, handmade in Lagos.
Genuine leather, soft inner lining, no blisters.
Sizes 40 to 44. Colours: black and brown.
Price: 25,000 naira.
Delivery in Lagos: 2,000 naira. Outside Lagos: 3,500 naira.
Send a message with your size to order.

That is easy to read, and the customer can find the price and the size in one glance. Compare it with the same information written as one long paragraph, and you will see the difference immediately.

Use simple symbols to organise the information, but do not overdo it. A dash or a full stop is enough. Avoid filling your description with too many emojis, stars, and capital letters. A description full of decorations looks unprofessional and is hard to read.

Keep the whole description short. For most products, five to eight lines are enough. If you need more space, put the extra detail in a separate message after the customer shows interest, not in the catalogue description itself.

## Two Templates You Can Copy and Adapt

Here are two description templates you can use for almost any product. Replace the details with your own and you will have a complete description in minutes.

Template for a physical product:

[Product name and main benefit]
Made from [material]. [One special detail].
Available in [sizes or colours].
Price: [amount] naira.
Delivery in Lagos: [amount] naira. Outside Lagos: [amount] naira.
Send a message with your size and address to order.

A filled-in example:

Ankara midi dress with pockets, soft cotton that does not fade.
Made from pure cotton Ankara with strong stitching.
Available in sizes 10 to 16. Colours: blue, green, and yellow.
Price: 18,000 naira.
Delivery in Lagos: 2,000 naira. Outside Lagos: 3,500 naira.
Send a message with your size and address to order.

Template for food or a service:

[Product name and what it is]
[What is inside it, or what the customer gets]
[How long it lasts, or how it is delivered]
Price: [amount] naira.
[How to order, and how early to order]

A filled-in example:

Fresh chin chin, crunchy and lightly sweetened.
Made with flour, butter, sugar, and milk. No preservatives.
Available in 1kg and 2kg packs. Stays fresh for two weeks.
Price: 5,000 naira for 1kg. 9,500 naira for 2kg.
Order before 12 noon for same-day delivery in Lagos.
Send a message to place your order.

Notice what both templates have in common: the price is visible, the options are clear, and the customer knows exactly how to order. Save these templates in a note on your phone and reuse them for every new product.

## Handle Price Questions Before They Are Asked

Price is the question every customer asks, and it is the question most sellers handle badly. Some hide the price. Some write "DM for price" and then take hours to reply. Some quote one price in the catalogue and a different price in the chat, which destroys trust.

The best approach is simple: show the price, and explain the value in the same description. When a customer sees "Price: 25,000 naira" next to "Genuine leather, handmade, soft inner lining," they understand why it costs 25,000 naira. When they see only "Price: 25,000 naira" with no explanation, they compare it with the cheapest option they can find and decide yours is expensive.

If your price is higher than your competitors', say why. "Our cream costs 12,000 naira because it is made with pure shea butter and no bleaching agents" gives the customer a reason to pay more. "Our shoes cost 30,000 naira because the sole is stitched, not glued, so it lasts longer" does the same. Customers will pay more when they understand what they are paying for.

If you offer a discount, show it clearly. "Was 30,000 naira, now 25,000 naira" is better than just "25,000 naira," because the customer can see the saving. If the discount ends on a certain day, say so: "Offer ends on Sunday." A deadline encourages the customer to order now instead of postponing.

For products with different prices, list them all. "Small size: 8,000 naira. Medium size: 12,000 naira. Large size: 15,000 naira." This prevents the customer from ordering the wrong size and arguing about the price later.

## Use Your Catalogue, Status, and Broadcasts Well

Your WhatsApp Business catalogue is your shop window. Set it up properly. Add every product with a clear photo and a complete description. Use the same format for all your products so customers can compare them easily. Remove products that are out of stock, and update prices immediately when they change. A catalogue with old prices and unavailable products makes customers lose trust.

Your WhatsApp status is your daily advert. Post your best products there, with a short description and the price. Do not post ten products at once, because customers will skip most of them. Post two or three at a time, at the hours when people check their phones: early morning, lunch time, and evening.

Broadcast lists let you send a message to many customers at once, but use them carefully. Only send broadcasts to customers who have bought from you or asked to receive updates. Send them once or twice a week at most. Each broadcast should have one clear message: a new product, a discount, or a reminder that a popular item is back in stock. If you send broadcasts every day, customers will block you or mute your messages.

## Common Description Mistakes to Avoid

Writing "DM for price" is the most common mistake, and it costs the most sales. Customers who cannot see the price assume it is expensive, or they simply do not have the time to message you. Show the price.

Copying another seller's description word for word is another mistake. Customers who follow several sellers will notice, and they will trust you less. Use templates, but write the details in your own words.

Using too many words is also a problem. A description that takes a minute to read will not be read at all. If your description is longer than eight lines, cut it down. Keep only the information a customer needs to decide.

Forgetting to update your description is a mistake that creates arguments. If a product is out of stock in a certain size, update the description immediately. If the price changes, update it everywhere: in your catalogue, on your status, and in any saved messages you send to customers.

Finally, do not make promises you cannot keep. "Delivery in 24 hours" is only good if you can actually deliver in 24 hours. "Clears pimples in one week" is only good if it is true. False promises lead to complaints, refunds, and bad reviews that follow your business for a long time.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Should I always show the price in my description?

Yes. Showing the price saves you time, attracts serious buyers, and builds trust. Customers who cannot see the price usually move on to a seller who shows it.

### How long should a WhatsApp product description be?

Five to eight short lines is enough for most products. Include the product name, the main benefit, the material, the available sizes or colours, the price, the delivery information, and how to order.

### What should I do when customers say my price is too high?

Explain the value in your description before they ask. Mention the material, the quality, and what makes your product last longer or work better. If the customer still says it is too high, stay polite, restate the value, and offer a smaller size or a simpler option if you have one.

### Can I use the same description for Instagram and WhatsApp?

Yes, with small changes. Instagram allows longer captions and hashtags, while WhatsApp needs shorter lines and a clear order instruction. Use the same core information in both places, but adjust the format for each platform.

### How often should I update my catalogue descriptions?

Update them immediately when a price changes, a size sells out, or a product is restocked. Check your whole catalogue once a week to make sure every price and every size is still correct.
