A customer in Abuja finds your product on Instagram, sends you a DM, and asks, "How do I pay?" You reply with your account number. They say they will transfer, and then they disappear. Hours later, no alert. You message them again, and they say their bank app was misbehaving, or they forgot, or they changed their mind. You have lost a sale that was almost yours.

Flutterwave Payment Links solve this problem. A payment link is a simple web page with your product, your price, and a pay button. You create it in a few minutes, share it anywhere — WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook, email, or even as a QR code — and your customer pays with their card, bank transfer, USSD, or mobile money. You do not need a website, and you do not need to know anything about technology.

## What Are Flutterwave Payment Links?

A Flutterwave Payment Link is a unique web link that takes a customer to a checkout page for one product or service. The page shows the product name, a description, the price, and a button to pay. When the customer clicks the button, they choose how to pay — with a Nigerian debit or credit card, by bank transfer, by USSD, or with a mobile money wallet — and complete the payment. Flutterwave collects the money and settles it into your bank account.

Each link is for one product or service, and you can create as many links as you need. You might have one link for a wig, one for a training course, one for a cake order, and one for a ticket to your event. You can also create a link that lets the customer enter any amount, which is useful for donations, contributions, or services where the price depends on the job.

Flutterwave is one of Nigeria's biggest payment companies, and it is licensed by the Central Bank of Nigeria. It processes payments for businesses of all sizes, from small Instagram vendors to large companies. Your customers do not need a Flutterwave account to pay you — they just need their card or their bank app. Flutterwave also accepts payments from customers outside Nigeria, which is useful if you sell to Nigerians abroad or to international buyers. The money from a payment goes into your Flutterwave balance first. You can then withdraw it to your Nigerian bank account, or set up automatic settlement so the money moves to your bank on a schedule you choose.

## Why Use Payment Links Instead of an Account Number?

The biggest advantage is confirmation. When a customer pays through a payment link, Flutterwave records the payment against that specific product, and you can see it in your dashboard immediately. You know who paid, what they bought, and how much they paid. With a bank transfer, you see an alert with a name and an amount, but you have to work out which order it belongs to — and if two customers pay the same amount, you can mix them up.

The second advantage is protection against fake alerts. A common scam in Nigeria is a customer sending a fake bank alert or a doctored screenshot as proof of payment. With a payment link, you never rely on a screenshot. You check your Flutterwave dashboard. If the payment is there, it is real. If it is not there, it did not happen.

The third advantage is that payment links work while you are busy. Your link is always available. A customer can pay at midnight without waiting for you to reply. If you are in the market buying stock or attending to another customer, sales can still come in.

The fourth advantage is professionalism. A clean payment page with your business name looks far more serious than an account number typed in a chat. New customers who have never bought from you before are more likely to pay when the process looks organised, especially if they found you through an advert.

The fifth advantage is that Flutterwave accepts many payment methods in one place. Your customer can pay by card, by transfer, or by USSD, without you having to set up each option separately. That matters in Nigeria, where different customers prefer different methods, and where card networks sometimes fail. If a customer's card does not work, they can switch to transfer on the same page.

## What You Need Before You Start

Setting up Flutterwave and creating your first payment link takes about 30 minutes if you have everything ready. Here is what you need:

- A Flutterwave account. Go to flutterwave.com and sign up with your email address and a password. You will need to verify your email.
- Your Bank Verification Number (BVN). Flutterwave asks for this to verify your identity.
- A Nigerian bank account in your name or your business name. This is where Flutterwave will send your money.
- A valid means of identification, such as your National Identity Card, driver's licence, international passport, or voter's card.
- Your business details. If your business is registered with the CAC, have your certificate ready. If you are not registered, you can sign up as an individual, but Flutterwave may ask for more information as your sales grow.
- A phone number you control, and a working email address.

Flutterwave will review your account before you can receive real payments. The review usually takes a few hours to a couple of days. While you wait, you can explore the dashboard and build your payment links in test mode, but you cannot collect real money until the account is approved. Do not use another person's Flutterwave account to collect payments for your business. If there is a dispute, that person controls your money.

## How to Create a Payment Link: Step by Step

Once your Flutterwave account is approved, creating a payment link is straightforward. Here is the process:

1. Log in to your Flutterwave dashboard at dashboard.flutterwave.com on your phone or computer.
2. On the left menu, look for "Payments" and then "Payment Links", or search for "Payment Links" in the dashboard. The layout changes from time to time, but it is always under payments.
3. Click "Create Payment Link" or "New Payment Link".
4. Give your link a name. Use the product or service name, such as "Handmade Leather Slippers — Size 43" or "Beginners Photography Class". This name is what your customer sees, so make it clear and specific.
5. Write a short description. One or two sentences are enough. For example: "Handmade leather slippers, available in sizes 40 to 46. Delivery within Lagos takes 2 to 3 days, outside Lagos 4 to 6 days."
6. Set the amount in naira. You can set a fixed amount, or you can allow the customer to enter any amount. For most products, a fixed amount is better, because it removes confusion.
7. Choose the currency. For Nigerian customers, this is naira. If you also sell to customers abroad, you can create a separate link in dollars, but start with naira.
8. Add the information you want from the customer. Flutterwave lets you add custom fields, such as size, colour, delivery address, or phone number. Only ask for what you truly need. For a physical product, name, phone number, and delivery address are usually enough.
9. Upload a product image if you have one. A clear photo of the actual product increases trust and sales. Use a good photo taken in daylight, with a plain background.
10. Choose what happens after payment. You can redirect the customer to a thank-you page, or to your WhatsApp number so they can confirm their order with you directly. Redirecting to WhatsApp is a smart move, because it brings the customer straight back to you to arrange delivery.
11. Click "Create Link" and copy the link. Flutterwave will give you a link like flutterwave.com/pay/yourbusiness-productname. Save it, open it on your phone to check that it looks right, and then start sharing it.

Before you share the link publicly, test it. Ask a friend or family member to pay a small amount, such as ₦100, through the link. Confirm that the money appears in your dashboard and that you receive the email receipt. If something is wrong with the price, the description, or the redirect, fix it now.

## How to Share Your Payment Link So Customers Actually Pay

A payment link only works if customers see it at the moment they decide to buy. Here are the best places to share it:

On WhatsApp Business, add the link to your product catalog. When a customer asks to buy, send the link directly in the chat. You can also post it in your WhatsApp status with a photo of the product and the price, so viewers can tap and pay immediately. Keep your replies short and friendly, and always confirm the order after payment.

On Instagram, put your most important payment link in your bio, and use the link sticker in your stories to share product links. When you post a product photo, write the price in the caption and tell followers to tap the link in your bio or send you a DM for the payment link.

On Facebook, add the payment link to your Page's posts and shop section. In Facebook groups where selling is allowed, post the product with its price and the payment link, but always follow the group's rules.

On your invoices and quotations, include the payment link for the product or service you are quoting. If you send a quotation to a corporate client, a payment link makes it easy for them to pay a deposit without asking for your account number.

On flyers and business cards, print a QR code that points to your payment link. Customers can scan it with their phone camera and pay on the spot. This works well at trade fairs, exhibitions, and markets in Lagos and Abuja, where customers see your product in person.

The key is to reduce friction. Every extra step between a customer deciding to buy and actually paying is a chance to lose the sale. A single link that takes them straight to a checkout page is the shortest path you can offer without a website.

## What Flutterwave Charges You

Flutterwave charges a fee on every successful payment, and it is important to understand it before you price your products.

For local Nigerian card payments, Flutterwave's standard fee is 1.4% of the transaction amount. That means a ₦10,000 payment costs you ₦140, and a ₦50,000 payment costs you ₦700. For international card payments, the fee is higher, usually around 3.8% of the amount. Bank transfer payments attract a flat fee, which depends on the transfer method. USSD payments also have their own fee.

Flutterwave does not charge a monthly fee for a basic account, and creating payment links is free. You only pay when a customer pays you. That makes payment links a low-risk way to start selling online.

Build the fee into your prices. If a product costs you ₦8,000 and you sell it for ₦10,000, the Flutterwave fee of ₦140 leaves you with ₦9,860. That is still a profit, but if your margins are thin, add a small amount to your online price to cover the fee. Do not add the fee as a separate charge on the payment page, because customers dislike seeing extra charges. Just price the product to cover it.

Also check the settlement timing. Flutterwave settles your balance to your bank account on a schedule you choose — daily, weekly, or another interval. Check your bank account regularly to confirm that settlements are arriving.

## How to Confirm Payments and Manage Orders

When a customer pays through your payment link, Flutterwave sends you an email and a dashboard notification. You should check your dashboard, not just the customer's screenshot, before you deliver anything.

Each morning, open your Flutterwave dashboard and check the transactions from the previous day. For each payment, note the customer's name, the product they bought, the amount, and any delivery details they entered. Then message each customer on WhatsApp to confirm their order and agree a delivery time.

For deliveries within Lagos, confirm the delivery address before you dispatch. For deliveries outside Lagos, confirm the state and the delivery fee, and collect the fee before you send the goods.

If a customer says they paid but you cannot find the payment in your dashboard, ask them for the transaction reference from their email receipt or their bank alert. You can search for that reference in your dashboard. If it is not there, the payment did not reach Flutterwave, and the customer should check with their bank. Never deliver goods based on a screenshot alone.

Keep a simple record of your orders. A notebook or a spreadsheet is enough when you are starting out. Write down the date, the customer's name, the product, the amount, and whether it has been delivered.

## Tips to Sell More with Payment Links

Use clear product names and honest descriptions. A link called "Shoes" will not sell as well as "Men's Leather Loafers, Brown, Size 43". Customers buy what they understand.

Use good photos. A payment link with a clear, well-lit photo of the actual product converts far better than a link with no photo or a blurry one. Take photos in daylight, against a plain background, and show the product from more than one angle if you can.

Set a fixed price. Links with a fixed price are easier for customers than links where they must type an amount. Only use the "pay any amount" option for donations or services with variable pricing.

Follow up with customers who asked for your link but did not pay. If a customer requested your payment link on WhatsApp and went quiet, send a polite follow-up the next day: "Good morning, just checking if you still want the leather loafers. The payment link is still active." Many sales are closed by a single follow-up message.

Keep your links up to date. If a product is out of stock, deactivate its link or update the description to say so. Nothing frustrates a customer more than paying for a product that is not available.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

The first mistake is sending the wrong link. If you have several payment links, it is easy to send the link for the wrong product, especially when you are chatting with several customers at once. Double-check the link before you send it.

The second mistake is never testing the link. Some sellers create a link and share it without ever opening it themselves. Then a customer reports that the link shows the wrong price or a broken image. Test every link before you share it.

The third mistake is ignoring your dashboard. Some sellers rely only on bank alerts and never check Flutterwave. Then they miss a payment, or they do not notice that a settlement failed. Check your dashboard every day. It takes two minutes.

The fourth mistake is sharing your Flutterwave login with staff or an assistant. Your Flutterwave account controls your money. Never share your password or your login code with anyone. If you need staff to help with orders, Flutterwave allows you to add team members with limited permissions. Use that instead.

The fifth mistake is not completing your account verification. If Flutterwave asks for more documents, provide them quickly. An unverified account can be limited or suspended, and your money can be held. Keep your BVN, ID, and business documents ready, and respond to Flutterwave's emails promptly.
