# How to Grow Your Business on X in Nigeria

X, formerly known as Twitter, is one of the fastest-moving social platforms in Nigeria. News breaks there first, trends start there, and conversations happen in real time. For a small business, that speed is both an opportunity and a challenge. A good post can reach thousands of people in Lagos, Abuja, and beyond within hours. A bad post can disappear just as fast. The businesses that grow on X are the ones that understand how the platform works and show up consistently.

Many Nigerian business owners have an X account but rarely use it, because they are not sure what to post. Others post only adverts and wonder why nobody engages. This guide explains how to build a strong profile, what to post, how to join conversations, and how to turn followers into customers on WhatsApp and in your shop. Whether you sell fashion, food, tech services, or professional services, the steps here will help you grow on X in Nigeria.

## Why X Matters for Nigerian Businesses

X is where Nigerians go to talk about what is happening right now. When there is a new trend, a football match, a public holiday, or a debate about business in Nigeria, the conversation is on X. That makes it a good place to reach people who are active, curious, and ready to engage. Unlike other platforms, where people mainly watch photos and videos, X users read, reply, and share opinions. That is useful for a business that wants to build a reputation, not just show products.

X is also a good place for professional and business-to-business sales. Many company owners, managers, and decision-makers in Lagos and Abuja are active on X. If you offer web design, branding, consulting, or corporate services, X can connect you with clients who would be hard to reach on other platforms. A thoughtful post about business, marketing, or technology can attract the attention of someone who needs your service.

Finally, X helps with discovery. Posts on X can appear in Google search results, and a single useful thread can be shared far beyond your existing followers, bringing in new followers for weeks.

## Step 1: Build a Profile That Builds Trust

Your profile is the first thing people see when they find you on X. Start with a clear profile photo. Use your business logo, or a clear photo of you if you are the face of the business. A blurry photo or a cartoon avatar will make people less likely to follow you. Next, add a header image that shows what you do. This could be a photo of your products, your shop, or a simple design with your business name and tagline.

Write a short bio that says what you do, who you help, and where you are based. For example: "We design websites for Nigerian businesses. Based in Lekki, Lagos. DM or WhatsApp to start." That tells a visitor everything they need in one line. Add a link to your website or WhatsApp in your bio. That link is the bridge between X and your sales. Make sure it works, and test it yourself by tapping it from your phone.

Pin a post to the top of your profile. A pinned post stays at the top, even as you post new content. Pin your best post, such as an introduction to your business, a popular tip, or a special offer with your WhatsApp number. When someone visits your profile, that pinned post should tell them what you do and how to buy from you. Update your pinned post every few weeks, so it stays fresh and relevant.

## Step 2: Understand How X Works in Nigeria

X posts, still called tweets by many Nigerians, are short. A single post can be up to 280 characters for most accounts, which is about two or three sentences. You can write longer posts if you pay for X Premium, but short posts often perform better, because they are quick to read and easy to share. You can also write a thread, which is a series of connected posts that tell a longer story or explain a topic step by step.

People can like, repost, quote, and reply to your posts. A repost shares your post with the reposter's followers. A quote lets someone add their own comment above your post. Replies are where conversations happen. The X algorithm shows posts that get likes, reposts, and replies to more people, so engagement matters. A post with many replies will reach more people than a post with no replies, even if both are about the same topic.

Hashtags help people find posts about a topic, but use them sparingly on X. One or two relevant hashtags, such as #LagosBusiness or #MadeInNigeria, are enough. Too many hashtags make a post look like spam. Trending topics show what Nigerians are talking about right now. You can join a trend if it fits your business, but do not force your product into every trend. People notice when a business hijacks a serious conversation to sell something, and it damages trust.

## Step 3: Find Your Voice and Your Topics

Your voice is the way you write on X. A friendly, direct voice works best for most Nigerian small businesses. Write the way you talk to a customer. Short sentences help. A little Pidgin can help too, if that is how your customers speak. Avoid stiff, formal language that sounds like a company press release. People follow people, not press releases.

Pick three or four topics you will post about regularly. These are your content pillars. For a fashion business, they might be styling tips, new arrivals, customer stories, and behind-the-scenes posts. For a web design agency, they might be website tips, client work, pricing advice, and lessons from running a business in Nigeria. For a restaurant, they might be new dishes, cooking tips, customer reviews, and special offers.

Stick to those topics most of the time. When you post about the same topics again and again, people start to see you as an expert. If you post about everything, from football to politics to your products, people will not know what you stand for. You can still share a personal post now and then, but keep your main focus on the topics that help your customers and your business. Over time, your followers will come to you for those topics.

## Step 4: Write Posts That People Share

The best X posts are short, clear, and useful. Start with a strong first line, because that is what people see as they scroll. A good first line asks a question, shares a surprising fact, or states a clear opinion. For example: "Most Nigerian businesses lose customers because their WhatsApp replies are too slow." That kind of line makes people stop and read the rest.

Use formats that work well on X. A short tip works well: "Tip: always confirm delivery costs before a customer pays, so there are no surprises." A short story works well: "A customer in Ikeja ordered a website on Monday. By Friday, she had her first online sale." A question works well: "What is the hardest part of running a small business in Lagos?" A thread works well for a longer explanation, such as "5 mistakes Nigerian businesses make on WhatsApp," with one mistake per post in the thread.

End most posts with a reason to respond. Ask a question, invite an opinion, or ask people to share their experience. Do not end every post with "DM to order." That feels like a sales pitch. A good balance is about four useful or interesting posts for every one direct sales post. That way, people enjoy following you and do not feel they are only being sold to. When you do post a sales message, make it clear, with the product, the price, and how to order.

## Step 5: Post Consistently and Join Conversations

Consistency is what separates growing accounts from stagnant ones on X. Aim to post at least once or twice a day. That may sound like a lot, but a post can be one sentence and a question. You do not need a long thread every day. One good post in the morning and one in the evening is enough to stay visible. If you can post three times a day, even better, as long as the quality stays high.

The best times to post in Nigeria are usually early morning, around 7am to 9am, when people check their phones before work, and evening, around 6pm to 10pm, when people are home and relaxing. Lunchtime can also work well. Test different times and watch which posts get the most replies and reposts. Your audience may be different. Students, office workers, and business owners all use X at different times.

Join conversations every day. Spend fifteen minutes reading posts from people in your niche, replying with thoughtful comments, and reposting useful posts. A good reply adds something to the conversation and makes people curious about who you are. When they tap your profile, they should see a clear bio and useful posts. Replying to larger accounts in Nigeria can also help you grow, because their followers may see your reply and follow you. Make sure your replies are helpful and respectful, not just "Nice" or a sales pitch.

## Step 6: Use Threads, Spaces and Media Well

Threads are one of the most powerful tools on X for a business. A thread lets you explain a topic in detail, in small, readable pieces. For example, a web designer can write a thread called "How to choose a website designer in Nigeria," with one tip per post. A fashion seller can write a thread about "How to style Ankara for a wedding." Threads get shared more than single posts, because they offer real value, and they show that you know your work.

Photos and videos make your posts stand out in a busy feed. A clear photo of your product, a short video of you at work, or a screenshot of a happy customer review can stop someone scrolling. Use photos that are bright and clear, with the product as the focus. Short videos, under sixty seconds, work well on X, especially videos that show a result, a process, or a quick tip. Add a short caption that explains the photo or video and tells people what to do next.

X Spaces are live audio conversations, similar to a radio discussion. You can join a Space about business in Nigeria, ask questions, and share your knowledge. You can also host your own Space, for example a weekly discussion about small business in Lagos. Spaces help people hear your voice and trust you. Start by joining other people's Spaces and speaking up, then host your own when you feel comfortable. Even a small Space with twenty listeners can bring in new followers and customers.

## Step 7: Grow Your Followers the Honest Way

Grow your followers by being useful and by engaging with others. Follow people in your niche, such as other Lagos business owners, customers in your area, and people who talk about your industry. Reply to their posts with thoughtful comments. When you reply well, their followers may check your profile and follow you. This is slow, honest growth, and it builds a following that actually cares about your business.

Share your X handle on your other platforms. Add it to your Instagram bio, your WhatsApp Status, your Facebook Page, and your website. Tell your existing customers: "Follow us on X for daily tips and offers." When you post a good thread, share a link to it on WhatsApp or Instagram, so your followers there can find you on X too. Cross-promotion helps you grow on all platforms at once.

Avoid buying followers or using bots. Fake followers do not buy anything, they do not reply, and they make your account look suspicious to real customers. X can also limit accounts that use fake growth tools. It is better to have 1,000 real followers in Nigeria who might buy from you than 20,000 fake followers who never engage. Focus on real people, real conversations, and real value. Growth on X is slower than on some platforms, but the followers you gain are often more loyal and more likely to become customers.

## Step 8: Turn Followers into Customers

X is not a shop, so sales usually happen after a conversation. When someone replies to your post and shows interest, reply quickly and warmly. Answer their question, then invite them to continue on WhatsApp or by DM. For example: "Yes, we have it in stock. Send us a DM and we will send you the price list and delivery details." That moves the conversation to a place where you can close the sale.

Keep your price list ready, and know your delivery costs to Lagos, Abuja, Port Harcourt, and other major cities. When a customer asks "How much?" reply with the price and the total cost, including delivery, if you know their location. Do not hide your price when someone asks directly. Many Nigerian customers will move on if they have to ask three times before getting a price. Be clear, be polite, and be fast. A reply within an hour is good. A reply the next day may be too late.

Use direct messages carefully. If someone showed interest but did not buy, you can send one polite follow-up message. If they do not reply, leave it. Chasing people too hard will damage your reputation. Instead, keep posting useful content, so they remember you when they are ready to buy. Many customers need to see your business several times before they trust you enough to pay. Track which posts bring in the most DMs and enquiries, and make more posts like those.

## Mistakes to Avoid on X

The first mistake is only posting adverts. If every post is "Buy now, ₦20,000," people will stop following you. X is a conversation platform, not a notice board. Mix useful posts, questions, and stories with your sales posts. The second mistake is copying posts from Instagram without changing them. X has its own style, and long captions with many hashtags perform poorly there. Rewrite your content for X, in shorter, more direct language.

The third mistake is ignoring replies. If someone takes time to reply to your post and you never answer, they will not reply again. Check your notifications at least twice a day and reply to questions, especially about your products. The fourth mistake is joining heated debates about politics or sensitive topics. As a business, your goal is to attract customers, not to win arguments. Stay neutral, stay polite, and focus on your business and your customers.

The fifth mistake is posting and disappearing. X rewards people who are present. Spend time each day reading other posts, liking good ones, and replying to people in your niche. The sixth mistake is using a personal account for business without a clear bio. If people cannot tell what you sell from your profile, they will not follow you or buy from you. Keep your business profile clear, professional, and focused on your customers.

## Your X Growth Checklist

Use this checklist to grow your business on X in Nigeria. First, build a clear profile with a good photo, a header image, a short bio, a working link, and a pinned post. Second, pick three or four topics you will post about regularly. Third, post once or twice a day, in the morning and evening, with a mix of tips, questions, stories, threads, and sales posts. Fourth, end most posts with a question that invites replies. Fifth, reply to comments quickly and move interested customers to WhatsApp or DM. Sixth, engage with other Nigerian businesses and customers every day, with thoughtful replies. Seventh, use photos, short videos, threads, and Spaces to show your expertise. Eighth, share your X handle on Instagram, WhatsApp, and Facebook. Ninth, track which posts get the most replies, reposts, and DMs, and make more posts like those.

X rewards businesses that show up, speak clearly, and talk to people honestly. It will not grow your business overnight, but a consistent presence, useful posts, and quick replies will build an audience that trusts you. Open X today, fix your profile, and write your first post. Ask your customers a simple question, share a useful tip, and reply to everyone who answers. Your next customer may already be on X, waiting for a business like yours to start a conversation.
