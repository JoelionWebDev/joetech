A skincare seller in Lagos spends three months developing a new body cream. She buys ingredients, designs a label, and produces two hundred jars. On launch day, she posts a photo on Instagram and waits. A few likes come in. One person asks the price. Nobody buys. The jars sit on her shelf for weeks, and the money she spent on ingredients is stuck in stock she cannot sell.

Now imagine a different story. The same seller tells her customers a month before launch that something new is coming. She shows short videos of the cream being made. She asks her followers to join a waitlist to get the first jars at a special price. Two hundred people join. On launch day, she messages the waitlist first. Fifty people order in the first day. The product sells out in a week, and she takes pre-orders for the next batch.

The difference is not the product. The difference is the waitlist. A waitlist is a list of people who have said they want your product before it is ready. It turns a launch from a guess into a plan. Instead of hoping people will buy, you already know who is interested, and you can talk to them directly.

This guide explains how to create a waitlist and launch a new product in Nigeria. You will learn how to choose the right product to launch, how to build excitement before launch day, how to collect names on WhatsApp and Google Forms, how to price and prepare for launch, and how to turn launch day into real sales.

## Why a Waitlist Works So Well in Nigeria

A waitlist works because it solves the biggest problem of a product launch: nobody knows your product exists, and nobody is ready to buy on the day you launch.

First, a waitlist creates demand before supply. When people join a list, they make a small commitment. They have told you they are interested. On launch day, they are not hearing about your product for the first time. They have been waiting for it, and waiting makes people want something more.

Second, a waitlist tells you how much to produce. If fifty people join your waitlist, you know to produce at least fifty units. If five people join, you know the product needs more work, or your message needs to be clearer. This saves you from producing two hundred jars that nobody buys, or producing twenty when two hundred people wanted them.

Third, a waitlist gives you a direct line to buyers. In Nigeria, social media algorithms are unpredictable. Instagram may show your launch post to only a few of your followers. But a WhatsApp message to your waitlist reaches people directly. You are not depending on an algorithm. You are talking to people who asked to hear from you.

Fourth, a waitlist gives you feedback before you spend big money. While people are waiting, you can ask them what size they want, what price feels fair, and what they expect from the product. Their answers help you get the launch right the first time.

## Step 1: Choose the Right Product to Launch

Not every product deserves a waitlist. A waitlist works best for something new, something limited, or something people have been asking for.

Start with what your customers already want. Read your DMs and comments. If many customers have asked for a bigger size, a new flavour, a new colour, or a product you do not yet sell, that is your launch product. You are not guessing. You are answering a request.

Make sure the product is ready, or nearly ready, before you start the waitlist. You should know what it is, what it costs to make, and when you can deliver it. A waitlist for a product that is six months away will frustrate people. A waitlist for a product that is two to four weeks away creates excitement without a long wait.

Test the product first with a small group. Give samples to five or ten trusted customers and ask for honest feedback. If they love it, you have a product worth launching. If they find problems, fix them before you build a waitlist. A bad product launched to a big waitlist is worse than no launch at all.

Give the product a clear name and a clear benefit. "Glow Body Cream — for soft, even skin in the dry season" is clear. "Our new cream" is not. People join a waitlist when they understand what they are waiting for.

## Step 2: Set Your Launch Date and Work Backwards

Pick a launch date and plan backwards from it. Most small business launches need three to four weeks of preparation. That is enough time to build excitement without letting people forget.

Three weeks before launch, start teasing the product. Share a hint, a photo of ingredients, or a short video of the product being made. Do not show everything yet. Curiosity is powerful.

Two weeks before launch, open the waitlist. Tell people exactly what they get by joining: early access, a special launch price, or a free gift with the first orders. Make the benefit clear and real.

One week before launch, remind the waitlist. Share more details: the price, the sizes, the delivery options. Ask them to confirm they are still interested. This helps you plan how much stock to prepare.

On launch day, message the waitlist first, before you post publicly. Give them a few hours to order before everyone else. This makes them feel special, and it creates early sales that you can show publicly.

After launch, keep selling to the wider public. The waitlist is the start, not the end. Your public posts should show that people are already buying, which encourages others to buy too.

Write your dates down. A launch without dates is just an idea. A launch with dates is a plan.

## Step 3: Build Your Waitlist With WhatsApp and Google Forms

In Nigeria, the best waitlist tools are the ones your customers already use. WhatsApp and Google Forms are free, familiar, and work well on phones.

The simplest waitlist is a WhatsApp broadcast list. Ask people to message you a keyword, for example "GLOW", to join the waitlist. Save their numbers in a broadcast list. This works well if you expect fewer than a hundred people, because WhatsApp broadcast lists have limits and only reach people who have saved your number.

For a bigger waitlist, use Google Forms. Create a short form with four fields: name, WhatsApp number, what size or option they want, and where they are located. Share the form link on your WhatsApp status, your Instagram bio, and your Facebook page. A form is better than a DM because it organises the information for you. You can see all the names in one spreadsheet, and you can count how many people want each option.

Tell people exactly what will happen after they join. "Join the waitlist and we will message you on launch day with the price and the order link. No spam. You can leave at any time." Clear promises build trust.

Do not buy a list of phone numbers. Do not add people to your waitlist without their permission. A waitlist of fifty people who asked to join is worth more than a list of five thousand people who never heard of you. The fifty will buy. The five thousand will ignore you or report you for spam.

Keep your waitlist list safe. Save it in a spreadsheet with names, numbers, dates, and what each person wants. This list is one of the most valuable things your business owns.

## Step 4: Create Excitement Without Giving Everything Away

People join a waitlist because they are curious and excited. Your job before launch is to keep that excitement alive without showing the whole product too early.

Share behind-the-scenes content. Show the ingredients, the packaging, the work in progress. A short video of you making the product, packing the first samples, or testing the label will make people feel part of the journey. Nigerians love to support a business they watched grow.

Share the problem your product solves. If your cream helps with dry skin in harmattan, talk about dry skin. If your food pack saves busy workers time at lunch, talk about that. People buy solutions, not products.

Share small teasers and countdowns. "Five days to go." "The first batch is ready." "Only fifty jars in the first batch." Countdowns create urgency, but be honest. Do not say a product is almost sold out when it is not. Nigerians notice dishonesty quickly, and it damages trust.

Let your early testers speak. If five customers tried a sample and loved it, ask them to record a short video or write a short review. Share those reviews with your waitlist. A real customer's words are more persuasive than anything you can say about your own product.

Post regularly, but do not spam. Two or three updates a week is enough. Daily messages about the same product will annoy people and make them leave the waitlist.

## Step 5: Price It Right and Prepare Your Stock

Pricing can make or break a launch. Price too high, and your waitlist will not buy. Price too low, and you will lose money on every sale.

Calculate your real cost before you set a price. Add the cost of ingredients or materials, packaging, transport, delivery, and your time. Then add a profit margin that is fair and sustainable. If your cost is N2,000 per jar, and you sell at N2,500, your margin is thin. If delivery costs N1,500, you may lose money. Do the maths on paper before launch day.

Offer a launch price that rewards the waitlist. For example, if the normal price will be N5,000, offer the waitlist N4,500 for the first week, or offer a free gift with the first fifty orders. A small discount makes waitlist members feel they gained something by joining early. Do not offer a discount so big that you lose money. A small, honest discount is better than a big, fake one.

Prepare your stock before launch day. Have the products packed, labelled, and ready to send. Know your delivery plan. Which dispatch rider will you use? How much does delivery cost to Lekki, to Ikeja, to Abuja? Write it down. On launch day, you will be busy answering messages. You do not want to be searching for a rider at the same time.

Also prepare your payment details. Make sure your bank account details are correct, and decide whether you accept transfers, payment on delivery, or both. Tell customers clearly how to pay.

## Step 6: Launch Day — Message the Waitlist First

Launch day is the day your preparation pays off. Treat it like an event, not like an ordinary post.

Start early. In the morning, send a message to your waitlist. Keep it short and clear: the product name, the price, the sizes available, how to order, and how to pay. Include a photo or a short video of the product. End with a clear instruction: "Reply with your name and address to order."

Answer messages fast. On launch day, a reply after five minutes is good. A reply after five hours is too late. If you expect many messages, ask a friend or a staff member to help you reply. Keep your answers short and consistent. Prepare a few saved replies for common questions: price, delivery, and payment.

Post publicly after the waitlist has had a few hours. Your public post should show that people are already buying. "Our first batch is selling fast — fifty jars gone this morning." Real sales create more sales. People want what other people want.

Track your orders as they come in. Write down each order, the customer's name, what they ordered, and whether they have paid. Do not rely on your memory. A simple notebook or spreadsheet will prevent mistakes, double deliveries, and lost payments.

At the end of launch day, thank your waitlist publicly. "Thank you to everyone who ordered today. You made our launch a success." Gratitude builds loyalty, and loyalty brings repeat orders.

## Step 7: Follow Up and Turn Launch Buyers Into Repeat Customers

A launch is not finished on launch day. The work after launch is what turns a one-time buyer into a regular customer.

Deliver on time, and deliver well. Package the product neatly. Include a thank-you note, even a short handwritten one. A small extra, like a sample of another product, can turn a happy customer into a fan.

After delivery, message each customer. Ask how they found the product. Ask if there is anything you can improve. Ask if they would like to be told when the next batch is ready. Most customers will be happy to reply, and their feedback will help you improve the next batch.

Ask happy customers for a review or a photo. A short video of a customer using your product is powerful marketing for your next launch. With their permission, share it on your status and your page.

If the product sold out, say so, and take pre-orders for the next batch. Scarcity is real only if it is honest. If you sold out because you made fifty jars and fifty people bought them, say so. Then tell people when the next batch will be ready, and let them join the waitlist for batch two.

If the product did not sell as well as you hoped, do not panic. Ask your waitlist why they did not buy. Was the price too high? Was delivery too slow? Was the product not what they expected? Their answers are the most valuable feedback you can get, and they will help you fix the next launch.

## A Real Example: A Food Seller Launches a Lunch Pack

Consider a food seller in Lekki who sells lunch packs to offices. She wants to launch a new weekend family pack — a large pack that feeds a family of five on Saturday. Instead of cooking fifty packs and hoping they sell, she builds a waitlist.

Three weeks before launch, she posts a video of the family pack being prepared. Two weeks before, she opens a Google Form and asks people to join the waitlist for early access and a launch price. One hundred and twenty people join. She messages them a week before launch with the price and the delivery areas.

On launch day, she messages the waitlist at 8 in the morning. By noon, forty people have ordered. She posts publicly in the afternoon, and twenty more order. She sells out her first batch of sixty packs by evening. She takes pre-orders for the next Saturday, and her waitlist for batch two grows to eighty people.

She did not spend money on ads. She did not guess how much to cook. She built a list, talked to the list, and launched to people who were already waiting. That is the power of a waitlist.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

First, do not launch to an empty room. If you have no waitlist, no audience, and no plan, wait. Build the audience first.

Second, do not make people wait too long. A waitlist that waits for three months will forget you. Keep the wait to a few weeks.

Third, do not overpromise. If you say the product will arrive on Friday, it must arrive on Friday. If you say only fifty are available, do not suddenly have five hundred.

Fourth, do not spam your waitlist. One clear message on launch day, one reminder the next day, and then stop. People who want to buy will buy. People who are spammed will leave.

Fifth, do not forget to collect payment properly. Confirm payment before you dispatch. Keep records of every order and every payment. A successful launch that ends in lost money is not a success.

## Your Four-Week Launch Plan

Here is a simple plan. In week one, choose your product, test it with a small group, and set your launch date. In week two, start teasing the product and open your waitlist on WhatsApp and Google Forms. In week three, share behind-the-scenes content, confirm prices and delivery, and prepare your stock. In week four, launch to your waitlist first, then to the public, deliver on time, and follow up with every customer.

A waitlist is not complicated. It is a list of people who want what you sell, and a plan to talk to them at the right time. Build the list, keep your promises, and launch to people who are already waiting. Your next product deserves better than silence on launch day. Give it an audience.
