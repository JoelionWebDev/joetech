If you sell food, cakes, clothes, hair, cosmetics, or gift boxes in Nigeria, you know the problem. A customer sends a voice note at 10pm. Another sends three messages with different sizes. A third says "I want the red one" without saying which one, or where it should be delivered. By morning, your WhatsApp is a pile of half-finished orders, and you spend the first hour asking the same questions again: what size, which colour, where do you live, have you paid?

Google Forms solves this quietly and for free. It is a simple online form, made by Google, that collects the same details from every customer in the same order. You share one link. The customer fills it in on their phone. Their answers arrive in a neat spreadsheet, in the order they came in, with no messages lost in a chat. You do not need a website, you do not need to write code, and you do not need to pay anything.

This guide shows you, step by step, how to build an order form for a Nigerian business, what questions to ask, how to connect it to WhatsApp and payment, how to handle delivery, and how to avoid the mistakes that make customers abandon a form halfway.

## Why a Form Beats a WhatsApp Chat for Orders

WhatsApp is excellent for conversation. It is poor for record-keeping. When ten customers order in one day, their messages mix together. One customer's size is in a voice note, another's address is in a photo, and a third customer's payment proof is three screenshots down. Finding one order means scrolling, searching, and guessing.

A form fixes that by asking every customer the same questions, in the same order. Name, phone number, what they want, size or option, quantity, delivery address, and payment method. Every answer lands in one row of a spreadsheet. You can sort by date, search by name, and see at a glance which orders are paid and which are still waiting.

A form also protects you. When a customer says "I ordered the large size", you can check what they actually selected. When a rider asks for the address, you copy it from the sheet instead of retyping it and making a mistake.

A form does not replace WhatsApp. It works with it: customers discover you on WhatsApp, Instagram, or Facebook, and you send them the form link to place the order properly. The chat stays for questions; the form becomes the official order. A clean form with your business name and logo also makes you look organised, which matters for first-time buyers.

## Step 1: Create Your Google Account and Your First Form

You need a free Google account to create a form. If you already have a Gmail address, use it. If not, create one for your business — for example, yourbusinessname@gmail.com — rather than using a personal address with a nickname. Customers will see the account name in some places, and a business name looks professional.

Go to forms.google.com on your phone or computer and tap the blank form. Give the form a clear title that matches what customers see: "Order Form — [Your Business Name]". Add a short description underneath: "Please fill in this form to place your order. We will confirm your order on WhatsApp within a few hours. Delivery within Lagos takes one to two working days."

Choose a simple design. Pick one of Google's plain themes, or add your logo at the top. Keep the colours calm and readable. Avoid a busy background image that makes the text hard to read on a small phone screen, especially for customers using a low-cost Android phone with a small display and limited data.

Before you add questions, change one setting: turn off any requirement for customers to sign in to Google. Many customers do not have a Google account signed in on their phone, and a sign-in wall will make them abandon the order. Your form should open immediately when they tap the link.

## Step 2: Ask the Right Questions — and Only the Right Questions

A good order form asks everything you need to fulfil the order, and nothing you do not. Every extra question is a reason for a customer to stop halfway. Aim for eight to twelve questions, in a logical order.

Start with the customer's name. Use a short-answer question: "Your full name". Make it required, because a rider cannot deliver to "my dear" and you cannot search your sheet for a nickname.

Next, ask for a WhatsApp number. Label it clearly: "Your WhatsApp number (we will confirm your order here)". Make it required, and add a short note: "Please enter the number you use on WhatsApp, starting with 0, for example 0803 123 4567." This prevents the common problem of a customer giving a number they never check.

Then ask what they want to order. The best way depends on what you sell. If you sell a small number of items, use a multiple-choice question listing each item with its price: "Ankara dress — N25,000", "Ankara skirt — N15,000", "Ankara top — N12,000". The customer taps one option. If you sell food with daily menus, use a checkbox question so a customer can select several items at once. If your catalogue is large, use a short-answer question — "What would you like to order? Please write the item name" — and keep your price list on your WhatsApp catalogue or Instagram highlights.

Follow with size, colour, or flavour. Use a dropdown with the real options: sizes 10, 12, 14, 16; colours red, blue, black; flavours vanilla, chocolate, red velvet. Do not leave this as a free-text box — customers will write "medium" or "the fine one", and you will have to message them to clarify.

Ask for quantity with a simple number question: "How many would you like?" Set it to accept numbers only, and give a sensible starting value of one. For food vendors, this is where a customer orders three packs of small chops or five litres of soup.

Ask for the delivery address next, as a paragraph question, with a hint: "Please include your house number, street, area, and a nearby landmark, for example: 14 Allen Avenue, Ikeja, opposite the filling station." A landmark helps your rider far more than a street name alone.

Ask for the delivery area or zone as a separate multiple-choice question: Lekki, Ajah, Victoria Island, Ikoyi, Yaba, Surulere, Ikeja, or Other. This lets you sort orders by zone and apply the correct delivery fee without reading every address.

Ask how they will pay. Offer the methods you actually accept: bank transfer, Paystack or Flutterwave link, or payment on delivery if you offer it. If you accept transfer, add a short-answer question for the payment reference or the sender's name, so you can match the payment to the order.

Finish with an optional question: "Anything else we should know? (Optional)". A customer can add "Please call before you come" or "No pepper, please". Making it optional is important — a customer who has nothing to add should not be forced to write something to submit the form.

Mark the essential questions as required. Before you publish, fill in the form yourself on your phone, using mobile data. If anything feels slow or confusing, fix it now — customers will simply stop filling it in.

## Step 3: Set Prices, Delivery Fees, and Payment Inside the Form

A form that does not mention price creates the same problem as a chat that does not mention price. Customers will fill it in, then message you to ask how much they owe. Put the price where they can see it before they submit.

If your prices are fixed, include them in the question itself, as described above: "Ankara dress — N25,000". If your prices change often, keep the price out of the form and note at the top: "Prices are in our WhatsApp catalogue. Please check there before you order." Update the catalogue when prices change, instead of editing the form every week.

Show the delivery fee clearly. Add a short section near the end of the form titled "Delivery", listing your zones and fees: "Lekki and Ajah — N2,500. Victoria Island and Ikoyi — N3,500. Yaba and Surulere — N4,000. Ikeja — N5,000. Outside Lagos — we will confirm the fee on WhatsApp." A customer who sees the fee before submitting will not be surprised later.

For payment, keep it simple. Most Nigerian small businesses use bank transfer. After the customer submits the form, show a confirmation message with your account details: "Thank you for your order. Please pay N__ to [bank, account number, account name] and send your payment proof on WhatsApp to [your number]." Google Forms lets you edit that confirmation message. Use it. A customer who sees clear payment instructions immediately is far more likely to pay quickly.

If you use Paystack or Flutterwave, you can add your payment link in the confirmation message as well. Some sellers prefer this because the payment is recorded automatically and the customer does not need to type account details. Whichever method you use, do not ask customers to enter their bank card details or PIN into a Google Form. A form is not a secure payment page, and asking for card details will frighten customers and put their money at risk.

Decide your rule for payment before delivery. Most sellers in Lagos ask for full payment before dispatch, especially for food, cakes, and custom-made items. Some allow payment on delivery for regular customers. Whatever your rule is, state it in the form description so there is no argument later.

## Step 4: Read Your Orders in the Responses Spreadsheet

Every answer your customers submit is stored in the Responses tab of your form. Tap "Responses" at the top, and you will see a summary and a table of every order. Tap the green spreadsheet icon to open the full sheet in Google Sheets. This sheet is your order book.

Learn the sheet. Each row is one order. Each column is one question. The first column shows the time the order was submitted, which tells you the order in which to process them. You can sort by time, search for a name, or filter by delivery area. On your phone, the Google Sheets app makes this easy — download it and sign in with the same Google account.

Add two columns of your own at the end of the sheet: "Payment confirmed" and "Delivered". When a customer pays, mark the first column "Yes". When the rider delivers, mark the second "Yes". At a glance, you can see which orders are paid but not delivered, which are delivered, and which are still waiting for payment. This simple habit prevents the two most common errors: sending an order that was never paid for, and forgetting an order that was paid for.

Protect the sheet. Do not share its link publicly — it contains customers' names, numbers, and addresses. Check responses at set times, for example 9am, 1pm, and 6pm. Confirm each new order on WhatsApp: "Hello Ada, thank you for your order. Your total is N27,500, including delivery. Please pay to the account in the confirmation message and send your proof here."

## Step 5: Connect the Form to WhatsApp, Instagram, and Your Shop

A form only works if customers can find it. The link is the bridge between where customers discover you and where they order.

Get the link by tapping "Send" in Google Forms and copying it. The link is long, so shorten it. You can use Google's built-in short link option, or a free shortener. A short link is easier to share on WhatsApp status, where a long link looks messy and may be cut off.

Put the link in the places your customers already look. Add it to your WhatsApp Business profile as your website or catalogue link, and to your Instagram bio. Pin it in your customer WhatsApp group with a short message: "To place an order, please fill in this form. It takes two minutes." Add it to your Facebook page, your delivery package, and your printed receipt.

Teach customers to use it with a short script you can copy and paste. When a customer messages "How much is this?" and then "I want it", reply: "Thank you. Please fill in our order form here [link]. It asks for your size, address, and payment method, so your order will be ready quickly." Most customers will follow the link. A few will insist on ordering in the chat. For those few, take the order in the chat and fill in the form yourself, so the order still lands in your sheet. Do not let the exception become the rule, or you will be back to messy chats within a week.

If you have a website, add the form link to a clear button: "Place an Order". Customers who find you through Google will use it. When you send an invoice after an order, you can create it quickly with Joetech's free invoice generator at joetech360.ng/tools/invoice-generator.

## Step 6: Run a Daily Order Routine That Actually Works

A form collects orders. A routine turns them into deliveries and money. Build a simple daily routine and follow it.

In the morning, open the sheet and list the new orders. Confirm each one on WhatsApp, with the total amount and payment details. Mark orders as confirmed only after the customer has replied or paid, not before. Prepare the items for paid orders first. Unpaid orders can wait — preparing them before payment is how stock gets tied up by customers who never pay.

At midday, check for new orders and new payments. Match each payment proof to the order in the sheet, using the name or reference the customer gave. If a payment does not match any order, message the customer at once. Do not dispatch an order until the payment matches, unless your rule allows payment on delivery.

In the evening, arrange the next day's deliveries by zone. Group orders going to the same area so one rider can deliver several in one trip. This saves you money and saves the rider time. Tell each customer when to expect the rider: "Your order will arrive tomorrow between 10am and 1pm. Our rider will call you when he is close." Then update the sheet: mark orders delivered when the rider confirms.

At the end of the week, look at the sheet as a whole. Which items sold most? Which zones ordered most? How many orders were never paid for, and why? These answers tell you what to stock and whether a question in your form is confusing customers.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

The first mistake is asking too many questions. A form with twenty questions feels like an exam. Keep it to the essentials and let the chat handle the rest.

The second mistake is making every question required, including the optional comment box. A customer with nothing extra to say will abandon the form rather than invent an answer.

The third mistake is forgetting to test the form on a phone with mobile data. A form that loads slowly, or whose text is too small, will lose customers who are not on Wi-Fi. Test it yourself, on your own phone, before you share it.

The fourth mistake is not checking responses regularly. A customer who fills in a form and hears nothing for a day will assume the order failed and buy elsewhere. Check the sheet at set times and confirm every order promptly.

The fifth mistake is sharing the wrong link. Google Forms gives you an edit link for yourself and a response link for customers. If you share the edit link by accident, a customer could change your questions. Always copy the link from the "Send" button, and test it by opening it in a browser where you are not signed in.

The sixth mistake is asking for sensitive information you do not need. Never ask for a bank card number, PIN, or password in a form. If you need a payment, use a bank transfer or a proper payment link, and keep card details out of your sheet entirely.

Google Forms will not make your products better or find customers for you. What it will do is bring order to the part of your business that wastes the most time: taking the order itself. One link, the same questions every time, every answer in one sheet. Build your form today, test it yourself, share the link where your customers already are, and let the form do the paperwork while you do the work that earns money.
