Ask five developers in Nigeria how much a website costs, and you'll get five different answers — from "give me ₦30,000, done in a day" to "₦2,000,000, sir, custom everything." No wonder business owners feel like they're buying a mystery box.

Here's the truth: website prices in Nigeria vary wildly because websites vary wildly. A landing page and a multi-page e-commerce platform are as different as a kiosk and a shopping mall — yet both get called "a website."

This guide is an honest, no-cheese breakdown of what Nigerian business websites actually cost in 2026, what changes the price, and how to avoid overpaying for what you don't need or underpaying for what you do.

---

## The three rules of website pricing (read these first)

**1. You pay for scope, not for "website."** The price depends on pages, features, content, and customisation. A simpler site costs less because it takes less skill and time.

**2. Cheap is expensive in disguise.** A ₦50,000 template site that you can't edit, that loads slowly, and that breaks on launch day will cost you more in lost customers than a proper site twice the price.

**3. The website is not the product — visibility is.** Websites don't cost what they cost because of effort alone; they cost because a good one quietly generates enquiries for years.

> Think of a website as the rent of your shop, not the price of a receipt book.

## Where your money actually goes (the real breakdown)

When you pay ₦400,000 for a business site, here's what that figure represents:

**Design (15–25%).** Turning your brand into a layout that looks professional and guides visitors to *act* — not stuff they scroll past. This is not "colouring a template"; it's the difference between trust and indifference.

**Development (30–40%).** The engineering — building pages that load fast on Nigerian networks, work perfectly on phones, show in Google, and can grow.

**Content (10–15%).** Words, images, and structure. You may provide content (cheaper) or have it written for you (extra). A site with vague placeholder text converts nobody.

**Setup & integrations (10–20%).** Domain, hosting configuration, WhatsApp button, Google Business setup, analytics, payment gateways, contact forms. Many "quotes" hide this — and it's where surprise costs appear.

**Support & maintenance (the ongoing slice).** The first 1–3 months of post-launch support is usually built into the project price. Going forward, a maintenance plan keeps your site updated, backed up, and secure.

Understand this breakdown, and you'll never again accept "the price is ₦X because that's my personal price."

## The hidden costs everyone forgets to budget

The developer's quote is rarely the *whole* price. Budget for these quietly expensive items — or be shocked later:

1. **Domain name.** ₦10,000 – ₦40,000/year for a good .com. Nigeria-specific domains cost more. And you should OWN it, not your developer.
2. **Hosting.** ₦30,000 – ₦180,000/year depending on speed requirements. Cheap hosting is the number one cause of a "slow website."
3. **Professional email.** Pricing via a company email like you@yourbusiness.com looks professional and costs a little extra monthly.
4. **SSL certificate & security.** Included by most reputable hosts these days, but verify before you trust that "🔒."
5. **Photography & copy.** Real photos of your business beat stock images, and professionally written copy converts. Both add up.
6. **SEO setup.** A site that never shows on Google is a beautiful brochure. Basic SEO setup (titles, descriptions, Google Business, sitemap) costs extra if not included.
7. **Ongoing maintenance.** ₦5,000 – ₦50,000/month for updates, backups, and security checks — the "car servicing" of your website.

A business that budgets only the developer's quote for a ₦400,000 site should be ready to spend ₦100,000–₦200,000 more on the full first-year picture. ## How to get an honest quote (and avoid overpaying)

**1. Write down your requirements first.**

Pages needed, features (booking/payment/blog), how you'll update it, whether you have content. Give every developer the *same* brief — then their prices become comparable.

**2. Ask "what exactly is included?"**

Get the breakdown: design, development, how many revisions, domain/hosting, SEO setup, training, post-launch support. First to list what's *not* included.

**3. Compare three quotes on scope — never just on price.**

The trades: template-site ₦150,000 vs. custom ₦350,000 is a real choice — but compare them on what they deliver, not just the difference in naira.

**4. Be suspicious of both extremes.**

A ₦50,000 "full website" is a template dump that will hurt you. A ₦2,000,000 quote for an informational site may be overselling you. Both deserve your questions, not your silence.

**5. Put it in writing.**

Scope, timeline, milestones, payment schedule, and ownership on paper. Written agreements prevent the "we never agreed to that" dramas that cost both sides money.
