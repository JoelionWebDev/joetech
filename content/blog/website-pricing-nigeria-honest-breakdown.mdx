Mrs. Adeyemi runs a private school in Lekki. In September she decided the school needed a proper website — admissions pages, a gallery, a way for parents to ask questions on WhatsApp. She asked three developers for quotes. The first sent a single line on WhatsApp: "₦650,000, everything included." The second sent a PDF with eight lines and no prices next to them. The third sent a spreadsheet with fourteen lines, each priced, each explained. She chose the third, even though it was the most expensive. "At least I could see what I was buying," she said.

## The quote: ₦650,000 for a school website

A website quote is not a price. It is a plan written in numbers, and if you can read it line by line you can tell a fair quote from a padded one in five minutes.

The quote is for a ten-page website for a Lekki nursery and primary school: home, about, admissions, fees, classes, gallery, news, staff, FAQs, and contact. It includes a WhatsApp button, a contact form, basic SEO, one year of hosting and domain, training, and a maintenance plan. Here is the whole thing, line by line.

| Line item | Price |
|---|---|
| Design (custom, 10 pages) | ₦150,000 |
| Development and build | ₦220,000 |
| Content writing (10 pages) | ₦90,000 |
| WhatsApp integration | ₦35,000 |
| SEO basics (setup) | ₦50,000 |
| Domain + hosting, year one | ₦65,000 |
| Training and handover | ₦25,000 |
| First month of maintenance | ₦15,000 |
| **Total** | **₦650,000** |

Now let us read it properly, line by line, because each line hides a decision.

## Design — ₦150,000

Design is the look and the layout: how the homepage is arranged, how a parent moves from the admissions page to the fees page, what the site feels like on a phone. For a ten-page custom design, ₦150,000 is a fair working figure. It covers the designer's time to produce layouts for the key pages, usually the homepage and two or three inner pages, with the rest following the same system.

Check first: is the design custom, or a template with your colours? A template adapted to your brand is a legitimate cheaper option — it should cost ₦50,000 to ₦80,000, not ₦150,000. If a quote charges custom-design money for a template, that is the first padding. Ask to see the design before building starts. A developer who cannot show you a design mockup before they build is asking you to pay for a surprise.

## Development and build — ₦220,000

This is the biggest line, and it should be. Development is the engineering: turning the design into pages that load fast on Nigerian networks, work on a ₦120,000 Android phone, show up on Google, and do not break when a parent opens the site on a bus in traffic. For ten pages with a contact form, a gallery, and a news section, ₦220,000 is realistic for quality work.

Padding hides here in two ways. The first is the page count. A quote that charges per page should say how many pages, and what counts as a page. Ten pages means ten designed, written, tested pages. The second is the testing that should be inside this line: the site tested on mobile, on two browsers, with the forms actually delivering messages. A build price that does not include testing is not a finished job.

## Content writing — ₦90,000

Content is the words on the pages, and ₦90,000 for ten pages — ₦9,000 a page — is a fair rate for professional writing in Nigeria. This line is where projects stall, because many quotes quietly assume the client will write the content. If your quote has no content line at all, read it as a warning: either you are expected to write ten pages yourself, or the developer will fill the site with placeholder text and call it done.

What good content looks like on this line: each page written around what a parent actually needs to know, headlines in plain language, fees and admissions information accurate. What padding looks like: a content line of ₦150,000 or more for a small site, with no word counts and no writer named.

## WhatsApp integration — ₦35,000

This is the line most Nigerian business owners should learn to read, because it is the most commonly padded item in the country. A WhatsApp integration, for most small business sites, means a floating WhatsApp button and click-to-chat links on the contact and admissions pages. It is a small job. ₦15,000 to ₦35,000, as part of a build, is fair. This quote's ₦35,000 sits at the top of the fair range, which is fine — it includes testing the button on mobile, where most parents will tap it.

The padding version of this line is ₦80,000, ₦100,000, even ₦150,000, for the same button. A floating WhatsApp button takes an experienced developer under half an hour to add and test. If a quote charges you a six-figure sum for it, you are not paying for work. A real integration — automated replies, a chatbot, a CRM connection — is genuinely more work and should be described as such. "WhatsApp integration ₦120,000" with no further detail is a line to question, every time.

## SEO basics — ₦50,000

SEO basics means the setup that lets Google find and understand the site: a title and description for every page, a sitemap, a Google Business Profile connection, and the site registered in Google Search Console. As a one-off setup inside a build, ₦40,000 to ₦80,000 is fair. This quote's ₦50,000 is honest.

Two things to watch. The first is a quote that charges ₦150,000 or more for "SEO" on a brand-new site with no explanation. Setup is a day's work, not a month's. The second is a quote that bundles ongoing monthly SEO into the build price without saying so. Monthly SEO is a real service, but it is a separate one, and it should be quoted as a monthly figure, not buried inside a build total.

## Domain and hosting, year one — ₦65,000

Domain and hosting are the rent on the site's address and the land it sits on. A .com domain costs ₦10,000 to ₦40,000 a year, and decent hosting for a school site costs ₦30,000 to ₦80,000 a year. This quote's ₦65,000 for both, in year one, is reasonable — and it is listed separately, which is the honest way to do it.

The padding here is a markup with no disclosure. Hosting that costs the developer ₦40,000 a year, billed to you at ₦150,000 a year, is a quiet profit line. You do not need the developer's wholesale price, but you do need to know the renewal cost before you sign, and you need the accounts in your name. A domain registered in the developer's name is the single most expensive mistake in this whole article.

## Training and handover — ₦25,000

Training is an hour on a video call, walking the school's admin staff through updating the news page, changing a phone number, and adding a gallery photo, plus a short written guide. ₦25,000 is fair, and frankly it is the line most worth paying for. A site nobody on your team can update becomes a billboard that slowly goes out of date, and every small change becomes a ₦15,000 favour to the developer.

## Maintenance — ₦15,000 a month

The last line is not a build cost at all. It is the ongoing care: updates, backups, security checks, small fixes. ₦15,000 a month for a ten-page school site is a fair working rate, and the first month is included in this quote so the school is covered from launch day. What matters is the clarity: what is included, what counts as a small fix, and what would be charged separately.

## A second quote, and where ₦60,000 of padding hides

One more example, smaller and sharper. A fashion retailer in Yaba received a quote of ₦250,000 for a one-page landing site with a WhatsApp button. The lines read: design ₦80,000, development ₦90,000, WhatsApp integration ₦45,000, SEO ₦35,000. No domain, no hosting, no training.

Two lines are padded. The WhatsApp button at ₦45,000 is high for a landing page — ₦15,000 to ₦25,000 would be fair. The SEO line at ₦35,000 for a single page is also high; a one-page site's basic setup is a couple of hours. Together that is roughly ₦40,000 to ₦60,000 of padding on a ₦250,000 quote — about a quarter of the price. The retailer asked for a revised quote, and the developer agreed without argument. Padding is often a test of whether the buyer is reading. Read, and it usually disappears.

## The five padding patterns to memorise

After reading enough quotes, the padding falls into five shapes. Learn them and you will spot them in any quote, for any business, in any city.

Vague lines. "Miscellaneous ₦50,000." "SEO ₦150,000" with no breakdown. "Other services ₦40,000." A clean quote names the work, the deliverable, and the price on every line. A vague line is a place to hide money.

Double-charging. Hosting listed in the build price and again in the maintenance plan, or the same WhatsApp button charged under both "development" and "integrations." Read the lines against each other, not just one at a time.

Free tools at premium prices. An SSL certificate, Google Analytics, and Search Console registration are free. A quote that charges ₦30,000 for "SSL setup" is charging you for a checkbox.

Inflated page counts. A quote that lists twenty pages for a business with five services is selling you pages nobody will visit. Count the pages your business actually needs before you count the price.

The rush fee with no deadline. "Express delivery ₦50,000" on a quote with no launch date is not a fee. It is a surcharge on your impatience. Agree a date first, then decide whether speed is worth paying for.

## The six questions to send back

When a quote lands in your inbox, reply with these six questions before you discuss price. A serious developer will answer them in one message. A padded quote will go quiet — and that silence is itself an answer.

One: which lines are custom design and which are a template? Two: how many pages are included, and how many revision rounds? Three: does the quote include writing the pages, or do I supply the text? Four: what exactly does the WhatsApp line and the SEO line cover, in one sentence each? Five: are the domain and hosting in my name, and what is the renewal cost in year two? Six: what is included in maintenance, and what would be charged separately?

Mrs. Adeyemi sent those six questions to her three developers. The one-line WhatsApp quote never replied. The PDF with no prices sent a revised version with prices — and the prices were lower than the first quote implied. The spreadsheet developer answered in an hour. She chose the spreadsheet, paid in three stages — half to start, a third at design approval, the rest on launch — and the school's site went live on the agreed date, with the admin staff trained and the logins in the school's hands.
