You have seen it in supermarkets in Lagos and in small shops in Abuja: a printed square of black and white patterns stuck on the counter, and a customer points their phone camera at it, taps a few buttons, and the payment is done. No cash changes hands. No card is swiped. No account number is typed. That square is a QR code, and it is one of the cheapest and easiest ways for a Nigerian shop to accept electronic payments.

This guide explains how QR code payments work in Nigeria, how to get your own code from your bank, Opay, Moniepoint, or Paystack, how to display it in your shop, and how to avoid the scams that target QR payments.

## What Is a QR Code Payment?

A QR code — short for Quick Response code — is a square barcode that a phone camera can read. When it is linked to a payment account, scanning it opens a payment screen with your business name and account details already filled in. The customer only enters the amount and their PIN or confirmation.

In Nigeria, QR payments run on a national standard called NQR, which is managed by NIBSS, the Nigeria Inter-Bank Settlement System. NQR codes work across banks: a customer with a GTBank app can scan a code issued by Access Bank, and the payment will go through. That matters, because it means you only need one code, not a different code for each bank your customers use.

There are two kinds of QR codes for payments. A static QR code is printed once and stays the same. It does not include an amount, so the customer types the amount themselves each time. This is the kind you print on a card and stick on your counter. A dynamic QR code is generated fresh for each transaction and includes the exact amount. These are usually shown on a phone screen or a small display, and they are common in bigger shops and restaurants where the bill changes each time. For most small shops, a static code is enough.

QR payments are different from card payments. There is no machine to buy, no SIM card to top up, and no paper roll to replace. All you need is a printed code and a phone that can receive bank alerts. That is why market traders, food vendors, tailors, and small provision shops across Nigeria are adopting them so quickly.

## Why QR Codes Make Sense for a Small Shop

The first reason is cost. A POS terminal costs tens of thousands of naira to buy, plus transaction charges. A QR code is usually free or nearly free to generate. Printing it costs less than ₦500 at a business centre in your area, or nothing at all if you print it at home. For a small shop with thin margins, that difference matters.

The second reason is speed. A customer who pays by transfer has to ask for your account number, type ten digits correctly, select your bank from a list, and wait for the name to resolve. Any mistake means starting again. With a QR code, the customer scans, types the amount, and confirms. It is faster, and there are no account number errors.

The third reason is trust. When a customer scans your code, their bank app shows your business name before they pay. That reassures them that they are paying the right person. It also protects you from a common problem: a customer who claims they transferred money to the wrong account, or who sends a fake alert. With QR payments, you check your own alert or your bank app. If the money is there, it is real.

The fourth reason is hygiene and convenience. In a busy food stall or a provision shop, handling cash all day is messy and slow. QR payments mean less cash in the drawer, less time counting money, and less risk of accepting a fake note. They also work when your POS terminal is down, or when the customer does not have their card with them but has their phone.

## How to Get Your QR Code: Banks, Opay, Moniepoint and Paystack

You can get a QR code from several places. The best option depends on where your money already sits.

Your bank is the simplest place to start. Most Nigerian banks now offer NQR codes to their customers. Log in to your bank's mobile app — GTBank, Access Bank, First Bank, Zenith Bank, UBA, and most others support it — and look for "QR", "NQR", "Receive Payment", or "Merchant" in the menu. The app will generate your code, usually showing your name or business name. You can save the image to your phone and print it. Some banks also let you request a printed stand or sticker from a branch. If you cannot find the option in the app, visit your branch and ask for an NQR code for your account. It is usually free.

Opay also offers QR codes, and they are very popular with small traders. Open the Opay app, go to your profile or the "Receive" section, and look for your QR code. Opay lets you share or print it directly from the app. Customers can pay you by scanning the code with the Opay app or with their bank app, since Opay codes work with NQR. If you already use Opay for your business, this is the fastest option.

Moniepoint offers QR codes through its app as well. If you have a Moniepoint business account or a Moniepoint POS terminal, you can generate a QR code linked to your Moniepoint account number. Customers scan it with their bank app or a payment app, and the money lands in your Moniepoint account. This is a good choice if you already receive most of your payments through Moniepoint.

Paystack lets you create a QR code for a payment page. If you already use Paystack Payment Pages to sell online, you can generate a QR code that points to a specific payment page. When a customer scans it, they land on your checkout page and pay by card, transfer, or USSD. This is useful if you want the customer to pay a fixed price, because the payment page already has the amount set.

Whichever provider you choose, make sure the code is registered in your own name and linked to an account you control. Never use a QR code generated by an agent, a friend, or a stranger. If the code points to someone else's account, your customers' money goes to that person, not to you. Always test the code yourself before you display it: scan it with a different phone, check that it shows your name, and make a small test payment of ₦100 to confirm the money arrives.

## How to Print and Display Your QR Code

A QR code only works if customers can see it and scan it easily. Here is how to set it up properly in your shop:

Print it clearly. Save the QR code image from your bank or payment app and print it at a business centre or with your own printer. Print it at least as large as a bank card — small codes are hard to scan, especially in dim light. Use a clean white background and make sure the print is sharp, not blurry or faded. If you print it on ordinary paper, put it inside a clear plastic sleeve or laminate it, so it does not get stained or torn.

Make a proper stand. The best display is a small standing card on your counter, facing the customer, at about chest height when they are standing at the counter. Many banks give you a branded stand for free if you ask at the branch. If not, a simple plastic photo stand from a market costs a few hundred naira. Put your business name above the code and a short instruction below it, such as "Scan to pay". Do not stick the code flat on the counter where it can be covered by goods or spilt drinks.

Put it where customers pay. The code belongs at the payment point — the counter, the till, or the table where you serve customers. If your shop has more than one till, print a code for each one. If you run a restaurant, put a code on each table so customers can pay without waiting for a waiter to bring a machine.

Add it to your delivery packaging. If you deliver food, clothes, or other goods, include a small printed QR code in the package or on the receipt, so the customer can pay on delivery by scanning it. You can also send the code image to customers on WhatsApp before the rider arrives, so they can pay in advance.

## How a Customer Pays You with a QR Code

Knowing the customer's steps helps you guide them when they are confused, which happens often with first-time users.

First, the customer opens their bank app or a payment app such as Opay or Palmpay on their phone. Most Nigerian bank apps have a "Scan QR" or "Pay with QR" button on the home screen or in the payments menu.

Next, they point their phone camera at your code. The app reads it and shows your business name and the account it will pay into. The customer should check that the name matches your shop before they continue.

Then, they enter the amount. For a static code, they type the amount themselves. You should tell them the exact amount and watch them type it, because a mistake here means the wrong amount is paid. For a dynamic code, the amount is already filled in.

Finally, they confirm with their PIN, fingerprint, or face ID. The payment goes through in seconds, and both of you should receive an alert. Ask the customer to show you the success screen on their phone, and check your own alert or your bank app before you hand over the goods.

The whole process usually takes less than a minute when the network is good. If the customer's app is slow, ask them to check their data connection or try again. Do not hand over goods until you have seen your own alert or confirmed the payment in your bank app.

## Charges: What QR Payments Cost You

QR payments are cheap, but they are not always free. The cost depends on your provider and the payment method the customer uses.

NQR payments between bank accounts are often free or carry a very small charge. Some banks charge nothing for receiving NQR payments, while others charge a small flat fee per transaction. The customer usually pays nothing extra.

When a customer pays your Opay or Moniepoint QR code from their own Opay or Moniepoint wallet, the transfer is often free. When they pay from a bank app into your Opay or Moniepoint account, the normal transfer charges may apply to the customer, and you may pay a small receiving fee depending on your account type.

The important thing is to check your provider's charges before you start, and to confirm that the amount you receive matches what the customer paid, minus any fee. Check your first few QR payments carefully. If a fee seems wrong, call your provider's support line and ask them to explain it.

## Security: Avoiding QR Code Scams

QR codes are safe when used properly, but criminals have found ways to abuse them. Here is how to protect yourself and your customers.

The most common scam is code swapping. A thief sticks their own QR code over yours on your counter, so customers' payments go to the thief's account instead of yours. Check your displayed code every morning. Make sure it is the one you printed, with your business name on the stand, and that nothing has been stuck over it. If your code is behind a counter where only staff can reach it, this scam is much harder to pull off. Some shops laminate the code into the counter or frame it behind glass so it cannot be swapped.

Never accept a QR code from a customer as proof of payment. Some fraudsters show a fake success screen on their phone, or a doctored screenshot of a transfer. Always check your own phone: your bank alert, your bank app, or your Opay or Moniepoint balance. Your own record is the only proof that matters.

Be careful with codes sent to you on WhatsApp. If a supplier or a customer sends you a QR code and asks you to scan it to pay them, check the name that appears before you confirm. Scammers send codes that look like they belong to a real business but point to a different account. If the name does not match the person you expect, stop and call them on a number you already have.

Keep your code private in one sense: do not post your QR code publicly on social media unless you are happy for anyone to pay you through it. Share it in direct chats and in your shop, not on a public timeline.

Finally, reconcile your QR payments every day, just as you would with a POS terminal. Compare the alerts on your phone with your sales book. If a payment is missing, check your bank statement before you assume the customer did not pay. Network delays can hold up an alert by a few minutes, but the money should appear in your account.

## QR Codes, POS Terminals and Transfers: Which Should You Use?

You do not have to choose just one. The best shops in Nigeria offer all three, because different customers prefer different methods.

Use QR codes when the customer has their phone and a bank app, and the amount is small or medium. QR is fastest for everyday sales in a provision shop, a food stall, or a boutique.

Use a POS terminal when the customer wants to pay by card, or when the amount is large and the customer wants a printed receipt. POS is also better when your customer does not have a bank app, or when their phone battery is dead.

Use a direct bank transfer when the customer is paying from far away, or when they are paying for an order before delivery. Transfers work well for customers who do not want to scan a code, and they leave a clear record in both bank accounts.

Many shop owners display their QR code right next to their POS terminal, and print their account number on the same card as a backup. That way, no customer is ever stuck. If one method fails, another is right there.

## Your Checklist: Start Accepting QR Payments This Week

Day one: open your bank app or your Opay or Moniepoint app and generate your QR code. Check that it shows your business name, and save the image to your phone.

Day two: print the code at a business centre, at least as large as a bank card, on a clean white background. Laminate it or put it in a plastic sleeve.

Day three: place the printed code on your counter in a stand, facing the customer, with your business name above it and "Scan to pay" below it. Print a spare copy and keep it in a drawer.

Day four: make a test payment. Scan the code with a different phone, pay ₦100, and confirm that the money arrives in your account and that the name shown is correct.

Day five: start using it with real customers. Tell each customer at the counter that they can scan to pay, and check your own alert before you hand over the goods.

A QR code costs almost nothing, takes an afternoon to set up, and gives your customers one more easy way to pay you. In a market where customers carry their phones everywhere and cash is getting rarer, that small square on your counter can quietly add sales every day.
