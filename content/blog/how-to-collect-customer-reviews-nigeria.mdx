A customer in Lagos is choosing between two hair salons in Lekki. Both post good photos on Instagram. One has 42 Google reviews with an average of 4.8 stars, and recent comments mention clean chairs, on-time service and fair prices. The other has no reviews at all. Which one will she try first? The one with reviews, almost every time.

That is the power of customer reviews in Nigeria. People trust other customers more than they trust adverts. A review is social proof: evidence from a real person that your business delivers what it promises. For a small business without a big advertising budget, reviews are the cheapest and most believable marketing you can get. They help you rank higher on Google, they answer the doubts a new customer has before they message you, and they give you honest feedback about what to fix.

Yet most Nigerian SMEs have very few reviews, not because customers are unhappy, but because nobody asks. This guide shows you how to collect reviews systematically, where to collect them, what to do with negative ones, and how to turn the good ones into sales — without paying for fake reviews or begging awkwardly.

## Why Reviews Matter So Much in Nigeria

Nigeria is a high-trust, high-caution market. Online scams are common, delivery disappointments are familiar, and customers have learned to check before they pay. Before a first-time buyer sends ₦25,000 to a vendor they found on Instagram, they will check the comments, ask a friend, or search the business name on Google. Reviews are often the deciding factor.

Google reviews directly affect your visibility. When someone searches "best pharmacy in Ikeja" or "fashion designer near me" on Google Maps, businesses with more reviews and higher ratings appear first. That is free visibility you cannot buy cheaply any other way. A Google Business Profile with 30 genuine reviews will outperform a profile with none, even if the second business is better in real life.

Reviews also shorten the sales conversation. A customer who has read five good reviews about your cakes, your tailoring or your cleaning service arrives already half convinced. You spend less time proving yourself and more time taking the order. For WhatsApp-based businesses, a screenshot of a happy customer's message in your status or catalogue does the same job.

## Set Up Your Review Channels First

Before you ask anyone for a review, make sure there is somewhere easy for them to leave it. You need three channels, and setting them up takes less than an hour.

First, claim and complete your Google Business Profile. Search your business name on Google, claim the profile, and fill in your address, phone number, opening hours, services and photos. This is where Google reviews live. If you have not claimed it, customers cannot leave a Google review for you, and you lose the search benefit entirely. Add a short description that says what you do and where you are, in plain language.

Second, make your WhatsApp Business profile review-friendly. Set your catalogue, your business description and your away message. Many Nigerian customers will send their feedback as a WhatsApp message rather than going to Google. That is fine — you can screenshot it, with permission, and use it as a testimonial on Instagram or your website.

Third, decide where testimonials will appear on Instagram and Facebook. A pinned highlight called "Reviews" on Instagram, a pinned post on Facebook, or a reviews section on your website gives new visitors one place to check. Do not scatter screenshots randomly and hope people find them.

Once these are ready, create a short review link for Google. In your Google Business Profile dashboard, go to the option to share a review link or generate a QR code. Save the link in your phone notes. You will use it in almost every ask that follows.

## When to Ask for a Review

Timing decides whether you get a review or an awkward silence. Ask when the customer is happiest, not a week later when the excitement has faded.

For a restaurant or salon, ask at the point of payment, while the customer is smiling and the experience is fresh. For a product delivered by dispatch rider, send a WhatsApp message two to four hours after delivery, once the customer has opened the package. For a service like cleaning, tailoring or a website build, ask the day the work is completed and approved. For a hotel or event hall, ask the morning after the event, while the memory is still good.

Never ask before the customer has received value. Asking a new customer for a review before they have used your product feels pushy and usually produces nothing. And never ask a customer who has just complained. Fix the problem first, then ask later, once they are satisfied.

Build the ask into your normal process so you do not have to remember it each time. Add it to your delivery checklist, your payment confirmation message, or your handover script. When it is part of the system, it happens every time.

## How to Ask Without Being Awkward

Most business owners do not ask for reviews because they feel shy about it. The trick is to make the request short, specific and easy. You are not begging. You are giving a happy customer a simple way to help a small business they like.

Here are scripts you can copy and adapt for WhatsApp, which is where most Nigerian review requests will happen:

1. After a delivery: "Hi Ada, your order has been delivered. I hope you love it. If you have a minute, could you leave a quick Google review for us? It really helps our small business. Here is the link: [your link]. Thank you so much."
2. In person, at the till: "Thank you for coming in today. If you enjoyed the service, a Google review would mean a lot to us. I can send you the link on WhatsApp right now if you like."
3. After a service is completed: "Your website is live and looking great. If you are happy with the work, a short review on Google would help other businesses find us. Here is the link."
4. For a repeat customer: "You have been with us for a while now, and we really appreciate it. Would you mind sharing a few words about your experience on Google? It helps new customers trust us."

Keep the message to three or four lines. Include the link every time. Never make the customer search for where to leave the review. Every extra step loses people.

If the customer says yes, send the link immediately. If they say they are busy, say "No problem at all, I will send the link and you can do it whenever you have a moment," and send it anyway. A gentle follow-up after two days is acceptable: "Just a quick reminder about the review link, whenever you have a minute. Thank you." After that, stop. Chasing a customer three times for a review damages the relationship you are trying to celebrate.

## Make It Easy: Links, QR Codes and Follow-Up

Friction kills reviews. A customer who needs to search your business name, open Google Maps, find the review button and type a comment will often give up. Your job is to reduce the task to one tap.

Save your Google review link as a shortcut in your phone. On WhatsApp Business, save it as a quick reply so you can send it in two taps. Print the link as a QR code and put it on your counter, your receipt, your packaging insert, or a small table card in your restaurant or salon. A customer waiting for their food or their nails to dry is a customer with time to scan a code.

Add the link to your email signature and to the thank-you page or message after an online order. If you send invoices, add one line at the bottom: "Enjoyed our service? Leave us a Google review here." Small placements add up across dozens of customers a month.

## How to Respond to Every Review

Replying to reviews matters almost as much as collecting them. Customers read the replies to judge how you treat people. A business that replies to every review looks attentive and professional. A business that ignores reviews looks absent.

Reply to positive reviews within 48 hours. Thank the customer by name, mention something specific from their review, and invite them back. "Thank you, Chiamaka, we are so glad you loved the braids. We look forward to seeing you again next month" is better than a plain "Thanks." Specific replies show you actually read the review.

Reply to negative reviews faster, within 24 hours if possible, and always stay calm and professional. Never argue, never blame the customer in public, and never write in anger. A good reply has four parts: apologise for the experience, take responsibility for what you can, explain briefly what you will do about it, and move the conversation offline. For example: "We are sorry your order arrived late, Tunde. That is not the standard we aim for. We have spoken with our dispatch team about the delay. Please send us a WhatsApp message so we can make this right for you."

That reply does two jobs. It shows the unhappy customer that you care, and it shows the dozens of people who will read the review later that you handle problems well. Many customers will forgive a mistake if the response is good. They will not forgive being ignored.

## What to Do With Bad Reviews

A bad review feels personal, especially when you have worked hard. Treat it as information first and emotion second. Read it twice before you respond. Ask yourself whether there is truth in it, even if the tone is unfair.

If the complaint is valid — late delivery, wrong item, rude staff — fix the underlying problem before you reply. Change the delivery arrangement, retrain the staff member, or tighten your quality check. Then reply publicly with what you have changed. "We have added a second quality check before dispatch" tells future customers that the problem is solved, not just apologised for.

If the complaint is partly wrong, stay factual and polite. "Our records show the order was delivered on Tuesday at 2pm, but we understand the packaging was damaged and we have sent a replacement" corrects the record without calling the customer a liar. Keep it short. Long public arguments help nobody.

Contact the customer privately after your public reply. A phone call or WhatsApp message, an apology, and a concrete offer — a replacement, a discount on the next order, a refund where appropriate — can turn an angry customer into a loyal one. Some of the best reviews you will ever receive start as complaints that were handled well. Ask the customer, once the issue is resolved, whether they would consider updating their review. Many will, happily.

## Turn Good Reviews Into Marketing

A good review sitting on Google helps people who search for you. A good review reused in your marketing helps people who have never heard of you. Use your best reviews actively, with the customer's permission.

Screenshot WhatsApp testimonials and post them on your Instagram stories and in a "Reviews" highlight. Add one strong testimonial to your WhatsApp Business catalogue description. Put three short quotes on your website homepage, with the customer's first name and area — "Adaeze, Lekki" feels real, while an anonymous quote feels invented. Use a strong review in a Facebook or Instagram advert: an advert that opens with a real customer's words often performs better than one that opens with your own claims.

When you post a testimonial, keep the customer's words intact. Do not rewrite them into perfect marketing language. The small imperfections are what make them believable. Blur or remove phone numbers and other private details before you post a screenshot.

Ask permission before you share a WhatsApp message publicly. A simple "Can I share your kind words on our Instagram page?" is enough. Most customers say yes and feel pleased to be featured. Tag them if they are comfortable with it — that often brings their friends to your page.

Use reviews in your sales conversations too. When a new customer hesitates on price, a short message — "Here is what Mrs Okafor said after her last order" with a screenshot — can close the sale faster than another discount. Social proof answers the question "Will this work for me?" better than any promise you can make yourself.

## How Many Reviews Do You Need, and How Fast?

There is no magic number, but there are useful targets. Aim for your first 10 Google reviews in your first month of asking. That is enough to look credible. Aim for 30 within three months, and 50 within six months if you serve dozens of customers a week. After that, steady growth matters more than speed. Five new reviews a month, every month, looks healthier than 40 reviews in one week followed by silence for a year. Google notices recency: a business with recent reviews looks active, while one whose last review is a year old looks possibly closed.

Do not buy reviews. Services that sell Google reviews use fake accounts, and Google removes them. Worse, your profile can be flagged or suspended. Paid reviews also teach you nothing and they put you at risk with customers who spot the pattern — dozens of five-star reviews with similar wording and no detail. One genuine review is worth more than twenty fake ones.

Do not offer a discount or free item in exchange for a positive review. You can offer a small thank-you to anyone who leaves honest feedback, but you must not condition it on a five-star rating. Google and other platforms prohibit incentivised positive reviews, and customers can tell when reviews were bought with a freebie.

Focus instead on volume of asks. If you serve 100 customers a month and ask half of them, and one in five responds, that is 10 reviews a month. In six months you have 60. That is a realistic, honest path to a strong profile.

## A Simple Weekly Review Routine

Reviews grow when collecting them is a routine, not a campaign you remember once a quarter. Set aside 30 minutes every Friday for this routine:

1. List the customers you served this week who were happy. Your staff, your delivery notes and your WhatsApp chats will tell you who they are.
2. Send a review request to each of them, with the link, personalised with their name.
3. Reply to every new review from the week, positive and negative, within 48 hours.
4. Screenshot the best new testimonial and schedule it for your Instagram stories or WhatsApp status next week.
5. Note any complaint that appeared more than once and decide on one fix for next week.

Put this routine in your calendar as a repeating event. When it is scheduled, it happens. When it is left to memory, it does not.

Train your staff on one line to use at the happiest moment: "If you are happy with your order today, a Google review would really help us." Reward the team when you hit 50 reviews — a small bonus or shared lunch keeps everyone motivated.

## Measure What Reviews Are Doing for You

After three months, check whether reviews are working. Look at your Google Business Profile insights: how many people found you through search, how many called, and how many asked for directions. Compare the three months before you started asking for reviews with the three months after. Look at your WhatsApp enquiries: are new customers mentioning that they saw your reviews? Ask new customers how they found you and write the answer down. If "I saw your reviews on Google" starts appearing regularly, the system is paying for itself.

Reviews compound. Each one makes the next customer slightly more likely to trust you, to order, and to leave a review of their own. Start this week: set up your Google profile, save your review link, and ask your next five happy customers. In six months, your review profile will be doing selling for you while you sleep.
