Every evening across Nigeria, parents sit with their children's schoolwork and worry. A child is struggling with mathematics. Another is preparing for WAEC, NECO, JAMB or a common entrance examination. A third needs help with English, physics or chemistry. Many parents are willing to pay a good tutor to help — and that is why a home lesson and tutoring business is one of the most reliable small businesses you can start in Nigeria.

You do not need a shop, expensive equipment or a large loan. You need knowledge of your subjects, patience, good communication, and a way for parents to find and trust you. A single tutor with ten regular pupils can earn a steady monthly income; a tutoring business with several tutors can earn far more.

This guide covers everything: the subjects parents pay for, how to price your lessons, how to find pupils, how to teach well enough that parents renew every term, and how to grow from one tutor into a lesson agency.

## Understand the Tutoring Market in Nigeria

Education is one of the things Nigerian parents spend on even when money is tight. A parent who cannot afford many luxuries will still pay for a lesson teacher if they believe it will help their child pass an examination or get into a good school.

### Why parents hire home lesson teachers

Parents hire tutors for several clear reasons:

- **Examination preparation** — WAEC, NECO, JAMB, common entrance into secondary school, and university entrance examinations. Exam season is the busiest time for tutors.
- **Weak subjects** — a child who is failing mathematics, English or a science subject needs focused help that a crowded classroom cannot provide.
- **Homework support** — busy parents want someone to supervise homework and make sure it is done properly.
- **School admission** — parents preparing a child for admission into a competitive secondary school or university.
- **Adult learning** — adults who want to learn computer skills, a language, or prepare for professional examinations.
- **Special needs support** — children who learn at a different pace and need patient, individual attention.

### Who your pupils will be

Your pupils will range from primary school children to secondary school students, university students and even adults. Primary school pupils usually need help with English, mathematics and general subjects. Secondary school students need subject specialists, especially in mathematics, English, physics, chemistry, biology and economics. University students and exam candidates need focused coaching in specific subjects. Knowing your audience helps you decide which subjects to offer and how to teach them.

### The subjects in highest demand

The subjects parents pay most for include:

- **Mathematics** — the most requested subject at every level, from primary school to JAMB.
- **English Language** — essential for every examination and for school admission.
- **Physics, Chemistry and Biology** — the core science subjects for secondary school and JAMB.
- **Economics, Government and Literature** — popular among arts and social science students.
- **Computer skills and coding** — growing fast, especially for teenagers and adults.
- **French and other languages** — a smaller market, but with less competition.
- **Music and art** — for parents who want their children to develop a skill.

If you are strong in mathematics and English, you already have the two most marketable subjects. If you are strong in sciences, you can charge more, because good science tutors are harder to find.

## Decide What You Will Teach and How

### Teach what you know deeply

Only teach subjects you genuinely understand. A tutor who struggles with the subject will lose the pupil's confidence quickly, and the parent will notice. It is better to be excellent in two subjects than average in six. If you want to add a subject, study it properly first and practise teaching it to a friend or family member.

### Choose your format

There are several ways to deliver lessons, and you can combine them:

- **One-on-one home lessons** — you visit the pupil's home. This is the classic model, and parents pay the most for it.
- **Lessons at your home or a small centre** — pupils come to you. This saves travel time and lets you teach several pupils in one place.
- **Group lessons** — two to five pupils learning together. You earn more per hour, and parents pay less per child.
- **Online lessons** — teaching through WhatsApp video, Zoom or Google Meet. This works well for older students and for subjects like English and mathematics. Online lessons let you teach pupils in other cities and other states.

### Decide your age group

Teaching a seven-year-old is very different from teaching a seventeen-year-old preparing for JAMB. Younger children need patience, games and short, lively sessions. Older students need clear explanations, past questions and exam technique. Choose the age group you enjoy teaching most, and build your reputation there.

## Work Out Your Startup Costs

A tutoring business has some of the lowest startup costs of any business in Nigeria.

### A realistic starting budget

- **Textbooks and past question booklets:** ₦30,000 to ₦80,000 for the subjects you teach. Buy current textbooks and past questions for WAEC, NECO and JAMB.
- **Teaching materials:** ₦20,000 to ₦50,000 for notebooks, a whiteboard or a small marker board, pens and printed worksheets.
- **Phone and data:** you probably already have a phone, but budget for regular data for WhatsApp, online lessons and research.
- **Transport:** ₦30,000 to ₦60,000 per month if you travel to pupils' homes. Choose pupils in areas you can reach easily at first.
- **Branding:** ₦20,000 to ₦50,000 for a simple flyer, business cards and a WhatsApp Business profile.
- **A laptop (optional but useful):** ₦150,000 to ₦300,000 for preparing worksheets, running online lessons and keeping records.

You can start a tutoring business with as little as ₦50,000 to ₦100,000 if you already have textbooks and a phone. That low barrier is part of what makes tutoring such a good business for graduates, teachers and anyone with strong subject knowledge.

### Invest in your own learning

Set aside money for your own development: new textbooks, online courses, and past question booklets for the examinations your pupils take.

## Set Your Prices Properly

Pricing is one of the most important decisions you will make. Price too low and you will be exhausted and underpaid; price too high without a reputation and parents will not call.

### Research prices in your area

Ask other tutors discreetly, check tutoring adverts online, and note what lesson agencies charge. Prices vary by city, by subject and by level. In Lagos, a one-on-one home lesson for a primary school pupil often costs between ₦20,000 and ₦50,000 per month for two or three sessions a week, while secondary school and exam preparation lessons cost more, often between ₦40,000 and ₦100,000 per month. Science subjects and JAMB preparation command the highest fees. In smaller cities, prices are lower, but so are transport costs.

### Price per session or per month?

Most Nigerian parents prefer monthly payment: a fixed fee for a set number of lessons per week. Monthly payment gives you predictable income and reduces the hassle of collecting money after every lesson. Agree the schedule in writing — for example, three lessons per week, one and a half hours each — and collect payment at the start of the month, before lessons begin.

### Charge for your value, not just your time

Your price should reflect your qualifications, your results and your experience. A tutor with a track record of pupils passing JAMB can charge more than a beginner. A tutor who provides worksheets, past questions and progress reports is offering more than a tutor who simply shows up. When you raise your prices, explain the value: your materials, your preparation, your availability and your results.

### Offer a trial lesson

A free or discounted first lesson lets the parent and the pupil see how you teach before committing. A trial lesson that goes well often turns into a regular booking. Keep trials short — one session is enough — and follow up with the parent afterwards to discuss what you observed and how you can help.

## Find Pupils and Fill Your Timetable

### Start with your network

Tell everyone you know that you teach. Friends, neighbours, church members, colleagues and former classmates are your first source of pupils. Parents trust recommendations from people they know. Offer your first few pupils a fair price and do an excellent job — their parents will tell other parents.

### Use WhatsApp and Facebook well

Create a WhatsApp Business account with your subjects, your prices, your location and your qualifications. Post your teaching tips, success stories and available slots in your status regularly. Join local community and parenting groups on WhatsApp and Facebook, and share useful educational content — not just adverts. Parents notice a tutor who genuinely helps, and they remember the name when they need a lesson teacher.

### Register with lesson agencies and platforms

Several tutoring agencies and online platforms connect tutors with parents. Registering with them can bring you pupils, especially when you are starting out. The agency usually takes a commission or a registration fee, so read the terms carefully. Do not depend on agencies alone — build your own direct clients too, so you are not paying commission forever.

### Partner with schools

Private schools, nurseries and after-school centres often need extra tutors or recommend tutors to parents. Introduce yourself to school owners and teachers in your area. Offer to help with after-school classes or exam revision sessions. A school that trusts you can send you pupils for years.

### Distribute flyers in the right places

Simple, well-designed flyers in estates, churches, mosques, schools and supermarkets can bring enquiries. Your flyer should state your subjects, your levels, your location and your phone number clearly. Keep it simple and professional — a flyer full of spelling errors will not convince a parent to trust you with their child's education.

### Use Google Business

Set up a Google Business Profile so that parents searching for a tutor in your area can find you, see your subjects and read reviews. Add photos of your teaching materials and your workspace, and ask happy parents to leave a review.

## Teach Well Enough That Parents Renew

Getting a pupil is only the beginning. Keeping that pupil — and getting referrals — depends on how well you teach.

### Prepare for every lesson

Never arrive unprepared. Know what you will teach, bring your materials, and have exercises ready. Preparation shows in the quality of your lesson, and parents notice when their child is learning something new each time.

### Assess the pupil first

Before you start regular lessons, find out where the pupil stands: what they know, what they struggle with, and what the parent wants to achieve. A short assessment — a few questions, a look at their school books — helps you plan. Share your plan with the parent so they know what to expect.

### Make lessons engaging

Children learn best when they are interested. Use simple examples from everyday life, ask questions, let the pupil solve problems, and praise progress. For younger children, use games, drawings and short activities. For older students, use past questions and exam techniques. A lesson that feels like a punishment will not last — the pupil will dread it and the parent will cancel.

### Give feedback to parents

After each lesson, or at least once a week, tell the parent what was covered, how the pupil performed, and what needs more work. A short WhatsApp message is enough. Parents who see progress and feel informed will keep paying and will recommend you. Consider a simple monthly progress report for long-term pupils — it makes you look professional and helps parents see the value of their money.

### Track results

Keep a record of your pupils' test scores and examination results. When a pupil passes JAMB, improves from a fail to a pass, or gains admission into a good school, that result is your best advertisement. With the parent's permission, share success stories — they are more convincing than any flyer.

## Manage the Business Side

### Keep proper records

Record every pupil, their subjects, their schedule, their fees and their payments. A simple notebook or a phone spreadsheet works well at the start. Track your income and your expenses — transport, textbooks, data and materials. At the end of each month, work out your profit. If you teach ten pupils at ₦40,000 per month, that is ₦400,000 in income — but you must subtract your costs to know your real profit.

### Collect payment on time

Agree payment terms before lessons start: payment at the beginning of the month, by transfer, before the first lesson. Be polite but firm about late payment. A parent who owes you for two months is not a client — they are a debt. If a parent consistently pays late, you may need to pause lessons until payment is made.

### Register your business

Register your business name with the CAC. It protects your brand, lets you open a business account, and is necessary if you want to grow into a lesson centre or agency. Keep your business money separate from your personal money from the start.

### Protect yourself and your pupils

Always teach in a safe, visible setting. If you teach at a pupil's home, make sure a parent or guardian is present or nearby. If you teach at your own home or centre, keep the space open and professional. Set clear boundaries about contact outside lessons, and communicate with parents, not with children, about schedules and payments.

## Grow From One Tutor to a Lesson Agency

Once your timetable is full, you have a choice: raise your prices, or grow by adding other tutors.

### Hire other tutors

When you have more pupils than you can teach, recruit other qualified tutors to work under your brand. Vet them carefully: check their qualifications, test their subject knowledge, and observe a trial lesson. Pay them fairly and on time. Your brand's reputation depends on their work, so set clear standards for preparation, punctuality and parent communication.

### Open a small lesson centre

A small centre — a rented room or a flat with a few classrooms — lets you run group lessons and after-school classes for many pupils at once. Group lessons earn more per hour than one-on-one lessons, and a centre gives your business a visible, professional home. Start small, with one or two rooms, and grow as demand increases.

### Offer exam preparation classes

Holiday lessons and intensive exam preparation classes for WAEC, NECO and JAMB are very profitable. A six-week JAMB preparation class with twenty students can earn as much as several months of home lessons. Plan these classes well in advance, advertise early, and deliver real results.



Your brand is your name, your results and your reputation. Keep your promises, communicate well, and deliver results. Parents talk to each other — a tutor with a good name in an estate or a church will never lack pupils.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Teaching subjects you do not know well** — pupils and parents will find out quickly.
- **Arriving unprepared** — a tutor who improvises every lesson loses credibility fast.
- **Charging too little** — low prices lead to exhaustion and resentment, not success.
- **No payment terms** — lessons without agreed payment lead to unpaid work and arguments.
- **Poor communication with parents** — parents who do not know what is happening will not renew.
- **Taking on too many pupils** — an overloaded tutor teaches badly and burns out.
- **Ignoring results** — if a pupil is not improving, change your approach or be honest with the parent.
- **No records** — without records, you cannot tell which pupils have paid or how much you earn.

## Final Thoughts

A home lesson and tutoring business in Nigeria is one of the most honest ways to earn a living from what you know. Parents will always invest in their children's education, and a tutor who teaches well, communicates clearly and delivers results will always have work.

Start with the subjects you know best, find your first pupils through your network, price your lessons fairly, and teach every lesson as if the parent is watching — because in a way, they are, through their child's progress. Pick up your textbooks, prepare your first lesson plan, and start teaching this week.
