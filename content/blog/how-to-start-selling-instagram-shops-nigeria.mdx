Instagram is one of the biggest markets in Nigeria, and it never closes. Every day, millions of Nigerians scroll through Instagram looking for hair, shoes, food, skincare, gadgets, and clothes. Many of them buy directly from the small businesses they find there, without ever visiting a website or a physical shop. If you sell a product that people can see and want, Instagram can become your shop, your showroom, and your best salesperson, all in one place.

Selling on Instagram does not require a big budget. It requires a business account, good photos, clear prices, honest captions, and the discipline to reply to customers quickly. This guide walks you through each step, from setting up your page properly to taking payments and delivering orders across Nigeria.

## Set Up Your Page Like a Real Shop

Before you post a single product, set up your Instagram page so that a stranger who lands on it in three seconds understands what you sell, where you are, and how to buy. Most sellers lose customers in those first three seconds, because their page is confusing.

Switch to a business or creator account. It is free, and it gives you useful tools: contact buttons, insights that show who views your page, and the ability to run adverts later. Go to your settings, choose "Switch to professional account", and pick the category that fits your business, such as "Shopping and retail", "Beauty salon", or "Food and beverage". A professional account also lets you add a WhatsApp button, which is how most Nigerian customers will contact you.

Choose a clear username. Your username should be your business name, or as close to it as possible. If your business is called Ada's Cakes, a username like @adascakeslagos is good. Avoid usernames with many numbers and underscores, such as @adas_cakes_1234_lagos, because customers cannot remember them and will not find you again. If your exact name is taken, add your city or one useful word, and keep it short.

Write a bio that sells. You have only 150 characters, so use them well. Say what you sell, where you are based, and how to order. For example: "Luxury hair, wholesale and retail | Lagos | Nationwide delivery | Order on WhatsApp". Add your location, your opening hours, and a link. Instagram allows one link in your bio, so use it well: link to your WhatsApp number, your catalogue, or your website. A link that opens WhatsApp directly is the best choice for most Nigerian sellers, because customers are comfortable ordering there.

## Take Photos That Make People Want to Buy

On Instagram, your photos are your shop window. Customers cannot touch your product, so the photo must do all the work. A good photo can sell a ₦25,000 dress in minutes. A bad photo can make the same dress look like it costs ₦5,000.

Use natural light. The best light is daylight near a window or outdoors in the morning, before the sun is harsh. Avoid yellow indoor bulbs, which change the colour of your product. A customer who orders a red dress and receives an orange one will complain, and the cause is often your lighting. Take photos in daylight and your colours will be honest.

Keep the background clean. A plain wall, a white bedsheet, or a clean table works better than a busy room. The product should fill most of the frame, and nothing else should compete with it. If you sell food, use a clean plate and a plain surface. If you sell clothes, hang them on a plain wall or lay them flat on a bed. A clean background makes your product look more expensive.

Show the product in use. A flat photo of a shoe tells a customer what it looks like. A photo of someone wearing the shoe tells her how it will look on her. A photo of a cream jar tells a customer what it is. A photo of a hand applying the cream tells her how it feels. Use a mix: one clear product shot, one photo of the product being used, and one close-up that shows the quality and the detail. Customers buy the feeling, not just the object.

Be honest with your photos. Do not use another seller's photos, and do not edit your photos so much that the product looks different from what the customer will receive. Using a supplier's glossy photo when your own stock looks different is one of the fastest ways to get complaints and refund requests. Take your own photos of your own stock, even with a phone. Customers trust real photos more than perfect ones, and they will not be disappointed when the parcel arrives.

## Price Clearly and Honestly

One of the biggest mistakes Nigerian Instagram sellers make is hiding their prices. "DM for price" frustrates customers, wastes your time answering the same question, and makes people suspect the price changes depending on who is asking. Customers who see no price assume the product is expensive, and they move on to a seller who shows it.

Put the price on the post or in the caption. Write it clearly: "Dress: ₦18,500. Sizes: 8 to 16. Available in blue and black." A customer who knows the price can decide in seconds. A customer who must send a message and wait for a reply may lose interest before you answer. Clear prices also attract serious buyers and reduce time-wasters, because the people who message you already know what they are paying.

Price for profit, not just for sales. Before you set a price, add up your real costs: the cost of the product, the cost of bringing it to Nigeria or buying it from your supplier, packaging, delivery to the customer, and your time. Then add your profit. A dress that costs you ₦10,000 to buy and ₦2,000 to deliver should not sell for ₦12,000, because you have earned nothing. Many sellers in Nigeria underprice their products because they are afraid customers will not buy, and then they wonder why the business never grows. Customers will pay a fair price for a good product, especially when they can see the quality in your photos.

State your delivery fee separately and early. Say it in the caption or in your highlights: "Delivery in Lagos: ₦2,000 to ₦3,500. Outside Lagos: ₦3,500 to ₦6,000." Do not hide the delivery fee until the customer is about to pay, because that is when most orders are abandoned. A customer who knows the total cost from the start is a customer who completes the order.

## Take Payments Without Losing Money

Payment is where many Instagram sales fail. A customer who wants to buy but cannot pay easily will give up. Make payment simple, safe, and clear.

Offer bank transfer, because it is still the most common way Nigerians pay small sellers. Put your account name and number in your bio highlights and in your order confirmation message. Make sure the account name matches your business name, because customers check it. A transfer to a personal name that does not match the business makes customers nervous, especially first-time buyers.

Add a payment link for customers who prefer to pay with a card. Paystack and Flutterwave both let you create a free payment link for your products, which you can share on WhatsApp or in your bio. A customer who clicks the link can pay with her card, her bank transfer, or USSD, and you receive the money in your account. Payment links also protect you, because the money goes through a proper system with a record. Set up a Paystack or Flutterwave account, create a payment link for each product or for your whole catalogue, and share it with every customer who orders.

Keep a record of every payment. For each order, write down the customer's name, what she ordered, how much she paid, the date, and the payment reference. A simple Google Sheet works well. This record protects you when a customer claims she paid but you have no record, and it helps you track which products earn the most money. Never accept payment without confirming it in your bank app or your Paystack dashboard before you dispatch the order.

## Turn Followers into Customers

Followers are not customers. A page with 20,000 followers and no sales is a hobby. A page with 2,000 followers and steady orders is a business. The difference is how you turn attention into orders.

Make it easy to order. Every post should tell the customer exactly what to do next: "Send us a DM or WhatsApp 0803 123 4567 to order." Repeat it in your captions, your stories, and your highlights. A customer who must work out how to buy will not bother. A customer who sees a clear instruction will follow it.

Create a highlights section that works like a shop. Instagram highlights stay on your profile permanently, so use them well. Create highlights for: your price list, how to order, delivery information, customer reviews, and available items. A new visitor can read your highlights in two minutes and know everything she needs to buy. Update them every week so they never show products you no longer have.

Use stories to sell every day. Stories are where the real selling happens, because they appear at the top of your followers' screens and they feel personal. Post new arrivals, show yourself packing an order, share a customer's testimonial, run a poll asking which colour customers prefer, or show a countdown to a restock. A story that shows a product selling out creates urgency: "Only 3 pieces left in size 12" is a powerful message.

Reply to messages fast. On Instagram, a customer who sends a DM expects a reply within an hour during business hours. If you take six hours to reply, she has already bought from another seller. Set up saved replies for your most common questions: prices, delivery fees, availability, and how to order. Saved replies let you answer in seconds. Check your message requests folder too, because messages from people who do not follow you land there, and many sellers miss orders sitting in that folder.

Build trust with testimonials and proof. Post screenshots of happy customers, photos of packed orders, and videos of deliveries being handed over. When a customer sends you a kind message, ask if you can share it. Real proof from real customers is the strongest advert on Instagram, because new customers are always asking themselves one question: "Is this seller real, and will I receive what I paid for?"

## Growing Without Spending a Fortune

You can grow an Instagram shop without paying for adverts, if you are consistent and useful. Start with organic growth, and add paid adverts only when you know what already works.

Post content that helps, not only content that sells. If you sell skincare, post a short video about how to care for skin in the harmattan. If you sell hair, post a video about how to maintain a wig. If you sell food, post a recipe or a packing video. Helpful content gets shared, and shares bring new followers who already trust you. Aim for a balance: for every three selling posts, post one helpful or entertaining one.

Use hashtags that your customers actually search for. Tags like #LagosVendor, #AbujaVendor, #NigerianFashion, #LagosHairVendor, and #MadeInNigeria help customers find you. Use a mix of popular tags and specific ones. Ten relevant hashtags are enough; thirty random ones look desperate and do not help. Also tag your location on your posts, because customers often search for sellers in their own city.

Collaborate with small creators. You do not need a celebrity with a million followers. A micro-influencer in Lagos with 5,000 real followers who match your customers can bring you more sales than a big name whose audience is scattered. Send a creator your product, let her try it honestly, and agree on what she will post. Start with creators who already buy from you or who already talk about products like yours.

Run a small advert when you are ready. Instagram adverts let you reach people in a specific city, of a specific age, with a specific interest. Start with a small budget, such as ₦5,000 for three days, and promote your best-selling product to people in Lagos or Abuja. Watch the results in your insights: how many people saw it, how many clicked, and how many messaged you. If an advert brings orders, increase the budget slowly. If it brings only likes and no orders, change the photo or the price before you spend more.

## Avoiding Scams and Protecting Your Business

Instagram selling in Nigeria attracts scammers on both sides, so protect yourself and your customers. Never share your bank login, your OTP, or your account password with anyone, including someone who claims to be from Instagram or from your bank. Instagram will never ask for your password in a DM. A "customer" who asks you to click a link to receive payment is almost certainly trying to steal from you.

Protect your customers too. Post your real account details only in your highlights and in private messages, never in a public comment where a scammer can copy them. Warn your customers on your page: "We only have one account number, shown in our highlights. Do not pay any other account claiming to be us." Scammers create fake pages that copy your photos and trick your customers into paying them. Check Instagram regularly for pages using your name and photos, and report them immediately.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need a website to sell on Instagram in Nigeria?

No, you can start with Instagram and WhatsApp alone. Many successful Nigerian sellers run their whole business this way. A website becomes useful later, when you want customers to browse and order without messaging you, and when you want to appear on Google. Start with Instagram, and add a website when your sales are steady.

### How much money do I need to start selling on Instagram?

You can start with as little as ₦50,000 to ₦100,000 if you buy a small amount of stock and use your phone for photos. Your main costs are stock, packaging, and delivery. Avoid borrowing a large amount to buy stock before you have tested whether your products sell. Start small, sell what you have, and grow with your profits.

### How do I get my first customers?

Start with people who already know you: friends, family, church members, and colleagues. Ask them to follow your page, share your posts, and give honest feedback. Post in WhatsApp groups you belong to, and tell people what you sell. Your first ten customers will usually come from your own network. Treat them well, ask them to recommend you, and your second ten will come from their recommendations.

### What should I do when a customer says my price is too high?

Stay calm and polite. Explain the value briefly: the quality, the size, the delivery, and what is included. Do not argue or reduce your price out of fear. Some customers will still walk away, and that is fine. A customer who wants the cheapest price is not your customer. Focus on the customers who value what you offer and can afford it.

### Can I sell on Instagram if I have a full-time job?

Yes, many sellers start this way. Set clear hours in your bio, such as "Orders processed 6 pm to 9 pm weekdays, 9 am to 6 pm on Saturdays", and use saved replies and a WhatsApp Business away message. Pack orders in the evening and dispatch them in the morning. As sales grow, you can decide whether to go full time.
