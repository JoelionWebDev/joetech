You have a good product, a fair price, and customers who love it — but only the people who already follow you on Instagram or WhatsApp know you exist. Jumia and Konga change that. They are Nigeria's largest online marketplaces, visited by millions of shoppers every month who are already searching for phones, fashion, electronics, home items, beauty products, and groceries. When you list there, your product appears next to big brands, in search results for people you could never reach on your own.

Selling on a marketplace is not the same as selling on WhatsApp or Instagram. There are fees, rules, delivery systems, and ratings that can help you or hurt you. Sellers who understand the system make steady sales. Sellers who list carelessly lose money on fees, get bad ratings for late delivery, and give up after a month.

This guide walks you through selling on Jumia and Konga in Nigeria, step by step: how to open an account, how to list products that sell, how to price for fees and delivery, and how to handle orders, returns, and payouts without losing money.

## Jumia vs Konga — Which One Should You Start With?

Jumia and Konga are both marketplaces, but they are not identical. Understanding the difference helps you choose where to start, and whether to sell on both.

**Jumia** is the larger marketplace in Nigeria by traffic and by the number of shoppers. It has a wider range of categories, a large logistics network, and a well-known brand that many Nigerians trust for online shopping. It also has more sellers, which means more competition in popular categories like phones, fashion, and electronics. Jumia's seller centre, its delivery system, and its advertising tools are more developed, but its rules and fees can feel complex for a new seller.

**Konga** is smaller than Jumia, but it has a loyal customer base, a strong presence in electronics and home appliances, and a retail network that includes physical stores in some cities. Konga's seller process can feel simpler for some categories, and competition may be lower in certain niches. Its traffic is lower overall, so a seller may get fewer orders than on Jumia, but the orders they do get can be just as profitable.

The honest answer for most Nigerian sellers is: start with one, learn the system, then add the second. If you sell fashion, beauty, or general consumer goods, start with Jumia for its traffic. If you sell electronics, home appliances, or products where Konga's audience is strong, consider starting there. Once your listings, photos, and pricing are working on one platform, copying them to the second is much easier than starting both at once and making mistakes on both.

You can also sell on both at the same time once you are confident, but you must manage stock carefully. A product that sells out on Jumia and Konga on the same day, when you only have one unit left, will lead to a cancelled order and a bad rating on one platform. Keep your stock counts honest and update them daily until you have a system that syncs them.



## Step 3: Create Listings That Sell — Photos, Titles, and Descriptions

On a marketplace, your listing is your shop window, your salesperson, and your signboard all at once. Customers cannot touch the product, cannot ask you a question in person, and are comparing your listing with ten others on the same page. Your listing must be clear, honest, and professional.

**Photos — the most important part:**

- Use at least four photos per product: front, back, side or detail, and the product in use or in its packaging.
- Photograph in daylight, near a window, on a clean, plain background. A white or light-coloured background works well for most products.
- Show the true colour. Do not use heavy filters that make a brown shoe look black or a red dress look orange. Customers who receive a different colour will return the product and leave a bad review.
- Show scale where it matters. A bag next to a phone, a shoe next to a ruler, a bottle next to a hand — these help customers understand size.
- Do not use photos taken from another seller, from Google, or from a brand's website if you are not authorised to use them. Use your own photos of the actual product you are selling.

**Titles — clear and searchable:**

- Use the format customers search for: Brand (if genuine) + Product + Key Feature + Size or Colour. For example: "Men's Leather Sandals — Brown, Size 43" or "Ankara Midi Dress — Size 12, Blue and Yellow".
- Include the size, colour, and material where relevant. Do not stuff the title with words like "best", "cheap", "original", or "hot sale" — these do not help search and can look untrustworthy.
- Be honest. If it is not a branded product, do not claim a brand name. If it is a size 12, do not list it as size 10 to 14.

**Descriptions — answer the questions before they are asked:**

- Start with a short summary: what it is, who it is for, and why it is good.
- List the key details in bullet points: material, size, colour, what's included, care instructions, and any warranty.
- State the delivery information clearly if the platform lets you: how long dispatch takes, and whether the product is in stock in Lagos or will be shipped from elsewhere.
- Include measurements where relevant, especially for fashion. Nigerian sizes vary by maker, so a size chart in centimetres or inches reduces returns.
- Write in clear, simple English. Avoid long paragraphs, avoid slang that confuses, and avoid copying another seller's description word for word.

**Category and attributes:**

- Choose the most specific category available. A dress should be in Women's Dresses, not just Women's Fashion. A phone case should be in Phone Cases, not just Phone Accessories. The right category helps the right customers find you.
- Fill in every attribute the platform asks for: size, colour, material, brand, model. Attributes help customers filter search results, and listings with complete attributes rank higher.

Take your time with your first five listings. They are your test. If they get views but no orders, the photos, price, or description may need work. If they get orders, you have a template you can copy for the next fifty products.

## Step 4: Understand Fees, Delivery, and Payouts Before You Price

This is the step that separates profitable marketplace sellers from sellers who work hard and lose money. You must understand every charge that will be deducted from your selling price before you set that price.

Fees vary by category and by platform, and they can change, so always check the current fee schedule in your seller dashboard. In general, expect these types of charges:

- **Commission:** A percentage of the selling price, charged by the marketplace for listing and selling your product. This varies by category — electronics may be lower, fashion may be higher — and it is deducted automatically.
- **Shipping or logistics fee:** The cost of delivering the product to the customer, which may be charged to you, to the customer, or shared. On some plans, the marketplace handles delivery through its own logistics network. On others, you arrange delivery yourself or drop the product at a hub. Know who pays what before you list.
- **Payment processing fee:** A small percentage deducted when the customer pays by card or through the platform's payment system.
- **Value Added Tax (VAT):** Where applicable, VAT may be charged on fees. Check how this is shown in your dashboard so you are not surprised.
- **Advertising fees (optional):** If you choose to promote a listing to appear higher in search results, you will pay for clicks or for a sponsored placement. This is optional, and you should only use it once you know which products sell well organically.

Payouts do not arrive the day an order is placed. The marketplace holds the customer's payment until the order is delivered and the return window has passed, then pays you on a scheduled payout cycle — often weekly or twice a month, depending on the platform and your seller status. This means you need cash flow to buy stock, pay for packaging, and cover delivery costs before the payout arrives. Do not spend money you have not yet received, and keep a simple record of orders, payouts, and fees so you know exactly what you earned.

Read the fee schedule and the payout schedule carefully before you list your first product. If anything is unclear, contact seller support and ask for a written explanation. A seller who understands fees prices correctly. A seller who guesses at fees prices wrong.

## Step 5: Price for Profit After Fees — Do the Maths

Use this formula for every product: Selling Price must cover True Cost + Marketplace Fees + Delivery Cost + Profit.

Here is a worked example. You buy a pair of sandals for ₦8,000. Packaging costs ₦500. Your share of delivery or logistics cost is ₦1,500. The marketplace commission and payment fees together are 15% of the selling price. You want ₦3,000 profit per pair.

If you price the sandals at ₦15,000, the fees are 15% of ₦15,000 = ₦2,250. Your total cost is ₦8,000 + ₦500 + ₦1,500 + ₦2,250 = ₦12,250. Your profit is ₦15,000 − ₦12,250 = ₦2,750. That is close to your target, and it may be acceptable.

If you price the same sandals at ₦13,000 because a competitor does, the fees are ₦1,950. Your total cost is ₦8,000 + ₦500 + ₦1,500 + ₦1,950 = ₦11,950. Your profit is only ₦1,050 — and one return or one damaged delivery could wipe it out entirely.

Do this calculation for every product before you list it. If the profit after fees is too low, you have three honest choices: find a cheaper supplier, increase the price and improve the listing to justify it, or do not list that product on the marketplace and sell it only on WhatsApp where fees are lower. Listing a product at a loss to gain visibility only works if those customers then buy profitable products from you — and on a marketplace, they often buy from the next seller instead.

Also check competitor prices on the platform itself, not just on Instagram. Search for your product on Jumia or Konga, note the prices of the top five listings, and note their ratings, delivery times, and number of reviews. A seller with 200 good reviews can charge more than a new seller with no reviews. As a new seller, you may need to price slightly lower to win your first orders and earn your first reviews — but never so low that you lose money. Your first ten reviews are worth a small discount. Your first hundred sales at a loss are not.

## Step 6: Manage Orders, Delivery, and Returns Like a Professional

Once orders start coming in, speed and communication matter more than anything else. Marketplaces track how quickly you confirm and dispatch orders, how often you cancel, and how often customers return products. These numbers affect your seller rating, and your rating affects whether customers see your listings.

Follow this routine for every order:

1. **Check orders twice a day.** Morning and evening, at least. An order left unconfirmed for a day may be cancelled automatically, and a cancellation hurts your rating.
2. **Confirm and prepare quickly.** Pack the order the same day if possible, and no later than the next business day. Use clean packaging, include the correct product, size, and colour, and add a small thank-you note if you can. A correct order, well packed, is the best defence against returns.
3. **Use the marketplace logistics system correctly.** If the platform collects from you, have the order ready at the agreed time and place. If you drop off at a hub, go early and keep the receipt. If you arrange your own delivery, use a reliable rider and share the tracking information promptly. Late delivery is one of the most common reasons for bad reviews in Nigeria, where traffic and distance can delay even a good rider.
4. **Communicate through the platform.** Reply to customer messages on the platform promptly and politely. Do not ask customers to complete the transaction on WhatsApp outside the platform — this violates marketplace rules and can lead to suspension. Keep the sale on the platform where it is protected for both you and the customer.
5. **Handle returns calmly.** Returns happen, even for good sellers: wrong size, changed mind, damaged in transit. Follow the platform's return process, accept the return if it is valid, and inspect the product when it comes back. A polite, fast return process can turn an unhappy customer into a repeat customer. Arguing about a return in public messages will damage your rating more than the return itself costs you.

Keep a simple order log: order number, product, customer location, date ordered, date dispatched, date delivered, and any issue. This log helps you spot patterns — for example, that deliveries to Abuja take two days longer than deliveries in Lagos, or that one product is returned more often than others because the size chart is unclear.



## A 7-Day Plan to Start Selling on Jumia and Konga

- **Day 1:** Choose your first five to ten products. Check that they are easy to deliver, easy to photograph, and profitable after fees.
- **Day 2:** Gather your documents — ID, bank account details, business name, and pickup address. Start your seller registration on one platform.
- **Day 3:** Photograph your products in daylight, on a clean background, from several angles. Write clear titles and descriptions, and work out your prices after fees.
- **Day 4:** Complete your seller profile, set your delivery options, and explore the seller dashboard so you know where orders and payouts appear.
- **Day 5:** List your first five products. Double-check every title, price, photo, and stock count before you publish.
- **Day 6:** Share your marketplace store link on your WhatsApp status, Instagram, and Facebook. Tell your existing customers you are now on Jumia or Konga, and ask them to check your listings.
- **Day 7:** Check your dashboard for views and orders. Reply to any messages promptly, prepare any orders the same day, and note what you need to improve in your listings.

After thirty days, review your sales, fees, returns, and ratings. After ninety days, decide whether to add more products, try a small advert, or open an account on the second platform. The goal is not to list everything at once — it is to build a small, profitable, well-rated store that grows steadily.

## Final Word

Jumia and Konga are not magic. They are marketplaces — large, busy markets where millions of Nigerians come to shop, and where the sellers who win are the ones with clear listings, honest prices, fast delivery, and good service. The platforms give you reach you could never buy on your own. Your job is to give customers a reason to choose your listing, and then to deliver exactly what you promised.

Start with one platform, five good products, and a clear understanding of fees. List well, price honestly, dispatch fast, and earn your reviews one order at a time. Do that consistently, and the marketplace can become a steady, growing part of your business — alongside your WhatsApp, your Instagram, and your shop.
