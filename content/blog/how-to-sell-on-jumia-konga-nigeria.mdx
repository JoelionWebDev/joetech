Every day, millions of Nigerians open Jumia or Konga to buy phones, clothes, food items, electronics, beauty products and home goods. They search, they compare, and they buy from sellers they have never met, because the marketplace gives them confidence: the product is listed clearly, the price is shown upfront, and there is a system for delivery and for complaints. For a small business owner, that is an enormous opportunity. Instead of finding every customer yourself on WhatsApp and Instagram, you can put your products in front of shoppers who are already looking to buy.

Selling on a marketplace is not the same as selling on social media, and that is exactly why it works. On social media, you have to attract attention first and then persuade someone to buy. On Jumia or Konga, the customer arrives already intending to buy — your job is to be the seller they choose. This guide walks through how to start: opening a seller account, choosing what to sell, creating listings that convert, pricing for profit, handling delivery and returns, and growing from your first few orders into a steady stream of sales.

## Start by Understanding How the Marketplaces Work

Before you register anywhere, take time to understand what Jumia and Konga actually do for you, and what they expect from you in return. Both are online marketplaces: they provide the website, the shoppers, the payment system and the delivery network, while you provide the products. When a customer buys your product, the marketplace collects the payment, you prepare and hand over the product, and the marketplace pays you after the order is delivered — minus its fees. You do not need your own website, your own delivery riders or your own payment system to start. That is the core advantage for a small business.

The two platforms are similar but not identical. Jumia is the larger of the two, with a wider range of categories and a very large customer base across Nigeria. Konga is also well established, with a strong presence in electronics, phones and everyday goods, and its own delivery and logistics network. Many sellers list on both platforms at the same time, because the extra work of managing a second store is small compared with reaching a second audience. You do not have to choose one forever; you can start with one, learn how marketplace selling works, and add the second when you are comfortable.

Both platforms charge fees, and you must understand them before you price your products. Seller commissions and fees vary by category and by platform, and they can change, so always check the seller centre on the platform's own website for the current rates before you set your prices. There may also be fees for delivery, for advertising your products inside the marketplace, and for certain services. The right approach is to work out your total cost for each product — what you paid for it, packaging, your share of delivery, and the marketplace fees — and then set a price that still leaves you a profit. A product that sells well but loses money on every order is worse than a product that sells slowly but profitably.

## Open Your Seller Account and Set Up Your Store

Opening a seller account is free on both platforms, and the process is similar. You will need a valid email address, a phone number, a form of identification, and your bank account details, because that is where your payments will go. You may also be asked for business registration documents, but many individual sellers can start as sole traders, so do not let the absence of a CAC certificate stop you from beginning — check the current requirements in the seller centre and register your business properly as you grow.

Choose your store name carefully, because it is the name customers will see on every product you list. Use your business name if you have one, and keep it short, clear and easy to remember. Avoid names that are hard to spell or that look like another well-known brand. Fill in your store profile completely: a short description of what you sell, your location, and your contact details where asked. A complete profile looks professional and trustworthy, and a half-empty profile makes shoppers hesitate, especially when they are choosing between your product and a similar one from another seller.

## Choose the Right Products to Sell

Not every product sells well on a marketplace, and choosing the right ones is half the battle. The best products for Jumia and Konga share a few qualities: people search for them often, they are easy to describe and photograph, they are not too fragile or too large to deliver, and they leave you a reasonable profit after fees and delivery costs. Everyday items — phone accessories, beauty products, fashion items, kitchen goods, baby items, fitness products — tend to do well because shoppers buy them repeatedly and search for them constantly.

Start with products you already know. If you already sell Ankara fabrics, hair products, sneakers or phone cases on WhatsApp, those are your first marketplace listings, because you already know your suppliers, your prices and your customers' questions. Do not start by buying a large stock of an unfamiliar product just because someone told you it sells well. Order a small quantity first, list it, see how it performs, and buy more only when the orders prove the demand is real. This small-test approach protects you from being stuck with stock that does not sell.

Use the marketplace itself as your research tool. Search for the product you want to sell and study what appears: how many sellers offer it, what prices they charge, how many reviews the top listings have, and what customers complain about in the reviews. If a product has hundreds of sellers all charging nearly the same low price, it will be hard to stand out unless you can offer something different — better quality, a bundle, faster dispatch, or a variation nobody else offers. If a product has few sellers and steady demand, that gap is your opportunity. Read the negative reviews on competing listings carefully: they tell you exactly what customers are unhappy about, and if you can fix that problem — better packaging, honest descriptions, accurate sizes — you can win those customers.

## Create Listings That Make Shoppers Choose You

Your listing is your shop window, your salesperson and your shop assistant all in one. On a marketplace, customers compare listings side by side, so small differences decide who gets the order. A good listing has four parts: a clear title, good photos, an honest description, and the right price.

Write titles that say exactly what the product is, in the words customers actually search for. A title such as "Men's Leather Wallet — Brown, 8 Card Slots" will be found and understood far more easily than a clever or vague title. Include the brand if there is one, the colour, the size, and the key feature. Do not stuff the title with unrelated popular words to attract searches — marketplaces penalise misleading titles, and customers who click and find the wrong product will not buy anyway.

Photos matter more than anything else in a listing. Take clear photos in daylight against a plain background, showing the product from several angles and, where relevant, showing it in use — a shoe being worn, a bag carried, a blender on a kitchen counter. Include a photo that shows the size, and a photo of exactly what is inside the package, so the customer knows what to expect. Use your phone camera; it is good enough if the light is good and the lens is clean. Never copy another seller's photos or use photos from the internet that do not show your actual product — customers will notice the difference when the item arrives, and the bad review that follows will hurt every other product in your store.

Write the description as if you are answering the customer's questions before they ask them. What is it made of? What size is it? What is in the box? How should it be used or cared for? Be honest about what the product is and is not. An honest description that sets the right expectation produces satisfied customers and good reviews; an exaggerated description produces returns, complaints and a damaged seller rating. Fill in every specification field the platform offers — size, colour, material, weight — because shoppers use those filters to find products, and a listing with blank fields will not appear in their results.

## Price for Profit, Not Just for Sales

Pricing on a marketplace is where many new sellers make their biggest mistake: they set the lowest price they can, win a few orders, and then discover they have lost money on every one. Low prices attract buyers, but they attract the wrong kind of attention if the numbers do not work. Before you set any price, work out your real cost per order: the cost of the product, packaging materials, your share of delivery, and the marketplace commission and fees at their current rates. Then add the profit you need. If the final price is higher than competing listings, do not panic — compete on something else, such as better photos, a bundle, or faster dispatch, rather than by cutting your profit to nothing.

Discounts and promotions inside the marketplace can bring a flood of orders, especially during big sales events such as Black Friday. Join these events when you are ready, with enough stock and a price that still leaves a profit. Do not join a promotion you cannot fulfil — running out of stock in the middle of a sale damages your rating and wastes the opportunity. Keep a simple record of what each product costs you and what it earns you after fees, and review it every month. Products that consistently earn little or nothing should be repriced, bundled, or removed, and your time should go to the products that actually make money.

## Handle Orders, Delivery and Returns Professionally

When an order comes in, speed and care decide whether that customer becomes a repeat buyer. Check your seller dashboard at least twice a day, confirm new orders quickly, and prepare them for dispatch within the time the platform requires. Package each product carefully: a sturdy box or bag, protective wrapping for fragile items, and the product exactly as described in the listing. A well-packaged order tells the customer you are a serious seller before they even open it, and it prevents the damage that causes returns and bad reviews.

Delivery on Jumia and Konga usually runs through the platform's own logistics or its approved partners. You will usually drop your parcels at a designated point or have them collected, and the platform handles the journey to the customer. Learn how this works in your area before your first order — where the drop-off point is, what time collections happen, and how to print or attach the delivery label. Keep your own record of every order you hand over, with the order number and the date, so you can answer questions if a customer says their order has not arrived. If a delivery is delayed, message the customer through the platform to explain; customers are far more patient when they are kept informed than when they are left wondering.

Returns are part of marketplace selling, and how you handle them shapes your reputation. Accept that some orders will be returned — wrong size, changed mind, damaged in transit — and handle each one politely and promptly through the platform's process. Never ask a customer to cancel a return outside the platform, and never argue with a customer in messages. A calm, professional response to a return often earns you a good review anyway, while a rude response guarantees a bad one. After each delivery, encourage satisfied customers to leave a review, because reviews are the currency of the marketplace: listings with more good reviews get shown to more shoppers, and shoppers trust them more.

## Grow From Your First Orders to a Steady Marketplace Business

Your first orders matter more than your fiftieth. In the early days, your store has no reviews and no history, so shoppers are taking a chance on you. Make those first orders perfect: accurate listings, careful packaging, fast dispatch, and a polite message to the customer after delivery asking how they found the product. A handful of honest five-star reviews in your first month will do more for your store than any amount of advertising, because they prove to the next hundred shoppers that you can be trusted.

Once you have reviews and steady orders, grow deliberately. Add new products in the same category before jumping into something completely different, so your store builds a reputation in one area. Watch your dashboard for the numbers that matter: which products sell most, which get the most views but few orders, where customers abandon their carts. A product with many views and few orders usually has a problem with its price, its photos or its description — fix the listing before you spend money promoting it. A product that sells steadily deserves more stock and perhaps a small advertising boost inside the marketplace, which can push it to the top of search results for its category.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need a registered business to sell on Jumia or Konga?**

Not always to start — individual sellers can often register as sole traders with identification and bank details. Check the current requirements in each platform's seller centre, because they can change. Registering your business formally is still worth doing as you grow, since it builds trust and opens up more opportunities.

**How much does it cost to open a seller account?**

Opening a seller account is free on both platforms. You pay fees only when you sell — seller commissions and fees vary by category and platform, so check the seller centre for the current rates before you price your products. There may also be optional costs, such as advertising your listings inside the marketplace.

**How and when do I get paid?**

The marketplace collects payment from the customer, and after the order is delivered, the money is paid into the bank account you registered, minus the platform's fees. Payment timing depends on the platform's settlement schedule, which is shown in your seller dashboard. Always check your dashboard rather than guessing when money will arrive.

**What happens if a customer returns a product?**

Returns go through the platform's official process. You will be notified, and the product is usually returned to you. Handle returns politely and promptly, and learn from them — a pattern of returns for one product usually means the listing, the size guide or the packaging needs fixing.

**Can I sell on both Jumia and Konga at the same time?**

Yes, many sellers do. Selling on both gives you access to two audiences. Just make sure you can manage stock across both platforms, because selling the same last item on both at once is the most common mistake new sellers make.
