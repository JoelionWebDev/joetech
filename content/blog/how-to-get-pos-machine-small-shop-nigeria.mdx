A customer walks into your shop, picks items worth 25,000 naira, and then says, "I don't have cash, can I pay with my card?" If you have no POS machine, you have three bad choices: send the customer to find an ATM, ask them to transfer and wait for a network that may be slow, or watch them leave the goods on the counter and walk out. Each of those choices loses you a sale. A POS machine removes that problem. The customer taps or inserts a card, enters a PIN, and the payment is done in seconds.

For a small shop in Nigeria, a POS machine is no longer a luxury for big supermarkets. Pharmacies, provision stores, boutiques, phone shops, restaurants, salons and filling stations all use them. Customers expect them. A shop with a working POS looks serious and ready for business. A shop without one looks behind the times, even if the goods are good and the prices are fair.

This guide explains how to get a POS machine for your small shop in Nigeria, step by step. You will learn which providers to consider, what documents you need, what the machine costs, how to set it up, how to use it safely every day, and what to do when payments fail or the network is poor.

## Do You Really Need a POS Machine?

Before you apply, check whether a POS will actually help your shop. Look at your customers for one week. Count how many ask to pay by card, how many ask to transfer because they have no cash, and how many leave without buying because payment was difficult. If card requests happen every day, a POS will pay for itself quickly. If they happen once a month, transfers may be enough for now.

Think about your average sale. Shops that sell higher-value items, such as phones, electronics, furniture or wholesale goods, benefit most from a POS, because customers rarely carry that much cash. Shops that sell small daily items, such as bread, recharge cards or sachet water, may find that transfers and cash cover most sales, although a POS still helps on busy days.

Consider your location and your network. A POS needs a mobile network or Wi-Fi to process payments. If your shop is in an area with very poor network, a POS will struggle, and you will need a provider whose machine works well on the networks available where you are. Ask neighbouring shop owners which provider works best on your street. Their experience is often more useful than any advert.

Also think about who will use the machine. If you have staff, they must be trained and trusted. A POS in the hands of an untrained or dishonest staff member creates problems. If you run the shop alone, the decision is simpler: you control the machine, the PIN and the daily records.

## Choose the Right POS Provider

Several providers offer POS machines in Nigeria, including banks and fintech companies such as Moniepoint, Opay, Palmpay, Kuda and traditional banks. Each has its own charges, machine types and support. Do not pick the first one you hear about. Compare at least two or three before you decide.

Start with charges. Ask for the full list: the cost of the machine, whether it is a purchase, a lease or a caution deposit, the charge per transaction, and any daily or monthly fees. Some providers charge a percentage of each sale, often with a cap. Others charge a flat fee. Ask for examples in plain naira: "If a customer pays 10,000 naira, how much do I receive?" That single question makes comparison easy.

Ask about settlement time. Settlement is when the money from a card payment becomes available in your account. Some providers settle the same day. Others settle the next day. For a small shop that needs cash flow to buy stock, faster settlement matters. Ask clearly, and get the answer in writing where possible.

Ask about the machine itself. Some machines are small and light, with a touchscreen and a long battery life. Others are larger, with a physical keypad and a receipt printer. A machine with a printer is useful if customers expect a paper receipt. A machine with good battery life matters if your area has frequent power cuts. Ask how long the battery lasts and how the machine connects: by SIM, Wi-Fi or both.

Ask about support. When a payment fails or a machine stops working, you need help quickly. Ask how to reach support, whether there is a phone number, a WhatsApp line or an in-app chat, and how long repairs or replacements take. A provider with an agent near your shop is often better than one with a fine advert but no local help.

Finally, check the account the money goes to. Some providers settle into their own wallet or business account, from which you then transfer to your bank. Others settle directly into your bank account. Understand the flow before you sign up, so you know where your money sits at the end of each day.

## Documents You Will Need

Getting a POS usually requires identity and business documents. Having them ready before you apply saves days of back and forth. The exact list varies by provider, but most ask for similar items.

You will need a valid means of identification, such as your National Identity Number, voter card, driver licence or international passport. You will need a passport photograph and a clear photo of yourself, often taken in the provider app. You will need your Bank Verification Number, which links your identity across Nigerian banks. You will need a phone number and email address that you control.

For the business, you may need your business name, your shop address, and the type of goods or services you sell. Some providers ask for a utility bill as proof of address. If your business is registered with the Corporate Affairs Commission, have your registration documents ready. If you are not registered, many fintech providers will still give you a POS as a sole trader, using your personal details. Ask the provider what applies to you before you start, so you do not waste a trip.

You may also be asked for a recent bank statement, especially by traditional banks. This helps them confirm that your business is active. If you do not have one, ask whether a statement from your fintech account or a sales record will do instead. Some providers are more flexible than others.

Keep copies of everything you submit. Take photos of your documents and store them safely on your phone and in your email. Never hand your original ID to a stranger who promises to process your POS. Apply through the official app, a bank branch or a verified agent you know and trust. If someone asks for a large upfront fee to "fast-track" your machine, walk away. That is a common scam.

## How to Apply and Get Your Machine

The application process depends on the provider. With many fintech providers, you apply inside the app. Download the official business app, create an account, complete your identity verification, and look for the option to request a POS. Fill in your business details, your shop address and your expected sales. Submit the request and wait for approval. The provider may call you to confirm your details or to arrange delivery.

With a traditional bank, you usually visit a branch. Ask for the POS or merchant services desk. A staff member will give you a form and a list of documents. Fill in the form carefully, attach the documents, and submit them. The bank will review your application and may visit your shop to confirm that it exists and is trading. That visit is normal. Make sure your shop is open and your signboard is visible on the day they come.

With an agent, the process is often faster, because the agent helps you fill the form and submit it. Use only an agent you know, or one recommended by a trader you trust. Ask the agent to show their identification and their link to the provider. Do not pay the agent in cash without a receipt. The cost of the machine and any deposit should be clear before you pay anything.

Delivery times vary. Some machines arrive in a few days. Others take one or two weeks, especially if the provider is short of stock or your area is far from their office. Ask for a realistic date, and follow up politely if the date passes. When the machine arrives, check that it is new or in good working order, that it powers on, and that it is registered in your business name. Do not accept a machine that is already registered to another business.

## Set Up Your POS the Right Way

When your machine arrives, do not rush to use it on a customer. Spend thirty minutes learning it first. Charge it fully. Insert the SIM if it uses one, or connect it to your shop Wi-Fi. Sign in with the details the provider gave you, and confirm that the business name on the screen is yours.

Practise a transaction. Ask a family member or staff member to pay a small amount, for example 100 naira, with a card. Watch the steps: enter the amount, ask the customer to insert or tap the card, let them enter their PIN, and wait for the approval message. Check that the receipt prints or appears, and check that the payment appears in your app or account. That single practice run prevents confusion when a real customer is waiting.

Set the machine up in a fixed place on your counter, where customers can see the screen and reach the keypad comfortably. Keep the charger nearby. Keep the machine away from water, dust and direct sun. If your shop is open to the street, keep the machine where it cannot be grabbed quickly. At closing time, lock it away with your cash box.

Train every staff member who will use it. Show them how to enter an amount, how to handle a tap payment, how to reprint a receipt, and how to check the last transaction. Teach them the golden rule: never ask for a customer's PIN, never look at the PIN while it is typed, and never release goods until the machine shows an approved message. Write these rules on a small card near the till until everyone knows them by heart.

## Using the POS Every Day

A good daily routine keeps POS payments smooth and safe. Start the day by checking that the machine is charged and has network signal. If the signal is weak, move the machine near a window or a spot where the network is stronger, or switch between SIM and Wi-Fi if the machine allows it.

For each sale, tell the customer the total before you enter it. Enter the amount carefully and show the screen to the customer to confirm. A wrong amount entered in a hurry is a common mistake. If you enter the wrong amount, cancel the transaction before the customer enters their PIN, and start again. Do not try to correct it after approval.

Let the customer insert, tap or swipe their own card, and let them enter their own PIN. Stand back slightly while they type. Wait for the machine to show "Approved" or "Successful" before you hand over the goods. Give the customer their receipt, or offer to send it by text where the machine allows it. Keep your own copy of the receipt, or check the transaction in the app.

If a payment fails, stay calm. Read the message on the screen. If it says network error, wait a few seconds and try again. If it says insufficient funds, tell the customer politely and ask if they have another card or want to transfer instead. If it says the card was declined, do not keep retrying. Ask for another payment method. Repeated retries waste time and can worry the customer.

At the end of the day, settle and reconcile. Print a summary or check the total in the app. Write down the POS total, the transfer total and the cash total. Add them together. Compare the POS total with the money that arrived in your account. If a payment is missing, check the individual transactions on the machine before you leave. A missing payment is much easier to find on the same day than a week later.

## Charges, Settlement and Keeping Your Money Safe

Know your charges. POS charges in Nigeria are often a small percentage of each transaction, sometimes with a maximum cap. The exact rate depends on your provider and may change. Check the current rate in your app or with your provider, and do the maths for your typical sales. If you sell an item for 5,000 naira and the charge is 50 naira, you keep 4,950 naira. Build that cost into your prices where you can, but do not add a surprise surcharge at the till without telling the customer first. Hidden surcharges annoy customers and can breach your provider rules.

Settlement times matter for cash flow. Some providers settle POS payments the same day. Others settle the next working day. Ask your provider, and plan your stock purchases around it. If you need money on Saturday to buy stock on Monday, a provider that settles on Monday morning may leave you short. Keep a small buffer in your business account for those gaps.

Keep the machine and the account safe. Change any default PIN or password the provider gives you. Do not share your merchant login with anyone who does not need it. Keep the machine in a safe place at night. If the machine is lost or stolen, report it to your provider immediately so the account can be blocked. Keep the provider support number saved in your phone, not only on a paper in the shop.

Watch for fraud. Never let a customer take the machine out of your sight to enter a PIN elsewhere. Never accept a card that looks damaged or tampered with. If a customer rushes you, insists you skip the approval message, or tries to distract you during a payment, slow down and follow the steps. Scammers rely on busy traders being rushed. A calm, step-by-step process is your best protection.

Keep your own records. A simple book with date, amount, card type and approval code for each POS sale is enough. It helps you settle disputes, track your sales, and see whether the POS is bringing in more money than it costs. At the end of the month, add up the POS totals and compare them with the charges you paid. That tells you whether the machine is worth keeping, and whether you should ask your provider for a better rate as your sales grow.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does a POS machine cost in Nigeria?
The cost varies by provider. Some charge a one-time purchase price, others ask for a refundable deposit or lease fee. Always ask for the full cost and the charge per transaction before you apply.

### How long does it take to get a POS machine?
It often takes a few days to two weeks, depending on the provider and stock. Fintech apps are often faster than traditional banks. Ask for a realistic delivery date when you apply.

### Can I get a POS without a registered business?
Yes, many fintech providers give a POS to sole traders with personal ID and a Bank Verification Number. Banks may ask for more documents, so check with your provider first.
