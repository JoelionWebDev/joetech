If you sell on WhatsApp, Instagram, or Facebook in Nigeria, you already know the hardest part of the sale is often not convincing the customer. It is getting paid. A customer says, "I want it," you send your account number, and then you wait. Sometimes the money comes in ten minutes. Sometimes it comes the next day. Sometimes it never comes at all, and the customer quietly disappears.

Payment links fix most of that problem. A payment link is a simple web link that takes your customer straight to a secure checkout page. They open the link, see what they are paying for and how much it costs, and pay with a card, a bank transfer, or a USSD code. You get a notification the moment the payment succeeds, and the money lands in your business account according to your payment provider's settlement schedule.

You do not need a website to use payment links. You do not need a developer. If you can create an account on Paystack or Flutterwave and copy a link into WhatsApp, you can start collecting payments properly today. This guide walks you through the whole process, with real Nigerian examples, real fees, and the mistakes that cost small businesses money every week.

## What Is a Payment Link?

A payment link is a unique web address connected to a specific product, service, or amount. When a customer clicks it, they land on a checkout page that already has the price filled in. They enter their name, email address, and phone number, choose how they want to pay, and complete the payment.

Think of it like a digital invoice that also collects the money. Instead of sending your account number and asking the customer to send you a screenshot as proof, you send one link. The payment provider confirms the payment for you, records who paid, and sends both of you a receipt.

For example, imagine you sell Ankara dresses in Lekki for ₦45,000 each. You create a payment link called "Ankara Dress — Size 12" with the price set at ₦45,000. When a customer on Instagram asks for the price, you send the link in the chat. She taps it, pays with her GTBank card, and you receive a notification saying the payment was successful. No account number shared in a public comment section. No waiting for a screenshot. No guessing whether the alert you received is real.

Payment links work for products and services. A caterer in Abuja can create a link for a ₦250,000 event deposit. A makeup artist in Ikeja can create a link for a ₦35,000 bridal trial. A tutor in Enugu can create a link for a ₦20,000 monthly lesson fee. A fashion vendor can create one link per product, or one general link where the customer enters the amount they want to pay.

## Why Payment Links Work So Well in Nigeria

Nigerian customers are already comfortable paying online, but they are also careful. Many have been scammed before, or they know someone who has. A payment link from a recognised provider like Paystack or Flutterwave feels safer than sending money to a random personal account number shared in a chat.

First, the checkout page shows your business name, the product name, and the exact amount. The customer knows exactly what they are paying for before they enter any card details. That clarity builds trust, especially for first-time buyers who found you through an advert or a referral.

Second, payment links support the payment methods Nigerians actually use. Most links accept Nigerian debit cards from banks like GTBank, Zenith Bank, Access Bank, First Bank, and UBA. They also accept bank transfers, where the customer is given a temporary account number to transfer into, and USSD, where the customer dials a code on their phone. That matters because not every customer is comfortable typing card details online, and some customers only have a basic phone or a weak internet connection.

Third, payment links remove the manual work from your side. You no longer need to check your bank app ten times a day, match alerts to orders, or chase customers for screenshots. The payment provider does the matching for you. Each successful payment comes with the customer's name, email, phone number, and the product they paid for. When you have ten orders in a day, that record-keeping saves you hours and prevents costly mix-ups.

Fourth, payment links help you sell outside your immediate circle. A customer in Port Harcourt can pay a vendor in Lagos without either of them visiting a bank. A customer in Abuja can pay for a digital product at midnight while you are asleep, and you wake up to a confirmed order. That is the difference between a business that only sells when you are online and a business that sells around the clock.

## Paystack vs Flutterwave: Which Should You Choose?

Paystack and Flutterwave are the two most popular payment providers for Nigerian small businesses, and both offer payment links. You do not need to choose only one forever, but it helps to start with one and learn it well.

Paystack is known for a clean, simple dashboard and fast setup for Nigerian businesses. Its payment links can be created from the dashboard in a few minutes. Paystack supports cards, bank transfers, USSD, and bank accounts. Its standard Nigerian transaction fee is 1.5 percent plus ₦100 for local card transactions, capped at ₦2,000. Always check the current fee page inside your dashboard before you set your prices, because fees change from time to time.

Other options exist too. Moniepoint, Opay, and Kuda offer business payment tools, and some banks now offer payment links through their merchant services. The steps in this guide focus on Paystack and Flutterwave because they are the most widely used, but the principles are the same everywhere: create the link, set the price, share it, and confirm payment through the provider, not through a screenshot alone.

## How to Create a Payment Link on Paystack

Follow these steps to create your first Paystack payment link. The dashboard layout may change slightly over time, but the flow stays the same.

1. Go to the Paystack website and create an account. Enter your business name, your email address, and a strong password. Confirm your email address by clicking the link Paystack sends to your inbox.
2. Complete your business profile. Add your business name, the type of business you run, your business address, and your phone number. Upload your identification document and enter your BVN when asked. Add your bank account details carefully, double-checking the account number digit by digit.
3. Wait for verification. Paystack usually reviews new accounts quickly, but it can take a few hours or a full business day. You will receive an email when your account is approved and ready to accept live payments. Do not try to share a test link with customers before approval, because live payments will not go through.
4. Log in to your Paystack dashboard and look for Payments or Payment Links in the menu. Click the button to create a new payment link.
5. Enter the details of your product or service. Give the link a clear name, such as "Ankara Dress — Size 12" or "Bridal Makeup Trial — Ikeja". Enter the price in naira, for example ₦45,000. Add a short description so the customer knows exactly what they are paying for. If the product has limited stock, set the quantity available so the link stops working when you sell out.
6. Choose the settings that fit your business. You can set the link to accept a fixed amount, or allow the customer to enter any amount if you take deposits. You can add a redirect page that sends the customer to a thank-you page or your WhatsApp number after payment. You can also set the link to expire on a certain date, which is useful for event tickets or limited-time offers.
7. Save the link and copy it. Paystack will show you the link address, which usually looks like paystack.com/pay/your-business-name. Copy it and save it somewhere safe, such as a note on your phone.
8. Test the link yourself before you share it with a customer. Open the link in your browser, check that the product name, price, and business name are correct, and confirm that the checkout page loads properly on a phone. If anything looks wrong, edit the link in the dashboard and test it again.

Your first link might take fifteen minutes because you are learning the dashboard. Your second link will take three minutes. After a week, creating a link will feel as routine as posting a product photo.

## How Much Do Payment Links Cost?

Payment links are free to create. You do not pay a monthly subscription just to have links. You pay a fee on each successful transaction, and the fee is deducted automatically before the money reaches your account.

Bank transfer payments sometimes carry a different fee from card payments. USSD payments may also carry a small additional charge. International card payments cost more than local card payments, usually around 3 to 4 percent, because of cross-border processing costs. You should build the fee into your pricing. If your profit margin is thin, a 1.5 percent fee matters. For example, if you sell a product for ₦20,000 and your profit is only ₦2,000, a ₦400 fee takes one-fifth of your profit. Some businesses add a small amount to the price to cover the fee. Others absorb the fee because the convenience brings in more sales than the fee costs. Work out your numbers, test both approaches, and choose the one that keeps your business profitable.

Settlement is the time it takes for the money to move from your payment provider into your bank account. Paystack and Flutterwave usually settle within one business day for Nigerian bank accounts, though the exact timing depends on your bank and the day of the week. A payment received on a Friday evening may settle on Monday morning. Plan your cash flow with that delay in mind, especially if you need to buy stock or pay a supplier quickly.

## How to Share Payment Links So Customers Actually Pay

Creating the link is only half the job. The other half is sharing it in a way that makes paying easy and obvious. Share the link in the same conversation where the customer asked about the product. If a customer asks for the price on WhatsApp, do not reply with the price alone and wait. Reply with the price, a short confirmation of what is included, and the payment link in the same message. For example: "The Ankara dress is ₦45,000, including delivery within Lekki. You can pay securely here: [your link]. Once you pay, send me your size and delivery address."

Use a short, clear message with the link. Customers are more likely to pay when they do not need to ask three follow-up questions first. Include the product name, the price, what is included, and what happens after payment. Keep the message short enough to read on one phone screen.

Add payment links to your Instagram bio, your WhatsApp Business catalogue, and your Facebook page. WhatsApp Business lets you add a product catalogue with prices and descriptions. You can paste the payment link into the product description or send it when a customer asks about that product. For Instagram, you cannot put a clickable link in a regular post caption, but you can put a link in your bio and tell customers to tap the link in your bio. You can also send the link by direct message when a customer asks.

For repeat customers, save the link as a quick reply in WhatsApp Business. That way you can send the same link in two taps instead of searching for it every time. Create a quick reply for each of your best sellers, with the product name, price, and link already written. Send a polite reminder if a customer says they will pay later but does not pay within a few hours. A simple message works well: "Hello, just checking in. Your dress is still reserved for you until this evening. Here is the payment link again if you are ready: [link]." One reminder is good customer service. Three reminders in one hour feels like pressure, so keep it to one or two follow-ups at most.

## Tracking Payments, Refunds, and Failed Payments

Export your transactions weekly into a spreadsheet or a simple notebook. Record the date, the customer name, the product, the amount paid, and the fee charged. At the end of the month, you will know exactly how much you earned, how much you paid in fees, and which products sold best. That information helps you decide what to restock, what to stop selling, and where to spend your advertising money.

If you create professional invoices for your customers, a payment link can sit alongside the invoice. For example, you can create an invoice with your business details and add the payment link at the bottom so the customer can pay immediately after reading it. Failed payments happen, even when everything is set up correctly. A customer's card may be declined because their bank flagged the transaction, because they entered the wrong card details, or because they do not have enough money in the account. When a payment fails, ask the customer to check that they entered the correct card number, expiry date, and CVV. A single wrong digit is enough to decline a payment. Ask them to try a different payment method on the same link, such as bank transfer or USSD, instead of a card. Suggest they try again in a few minutes, or contact their bank if the card keeps failing.

Refunds are part of doing business. If you need to refund a customer, do it from your payment provider dashboard, not by sending money from your personal account. Refunding through the dashboard creates a proper record, returns the money to the original payment method, and protects you if the customer later claims they were not refunded. Both Paystack and Flutterwave let you refund a transaction from the transaction details page.

Disputes happen when a customer tells their bank they did not authorise a payment or did not receive the product. When a dispute is raised, your payment provider will ask you for evidence: the order details, the delivery receipt, the chat history, and the payment confirmation. Keep those records for every order. Take a photo of the parcel before you send it, keep the waybill number from the dispatch rider, and save the delivery confirmation message from the customer.

## Conclusion

Payment links are one of the simplest upgrades a Nigerian small business can make. They cost nothing to create, they work without a website, and they solve the daily problems of slow payments, fake alerts, and messy records. A customer who can pay in two minutes is more likely to complete the purchase than a customer who has to copy an account number, open their bank app, send the money, take a screenshot, and wait for you to confirm it.

Start with one provider, create links for your five best sellers, and test them with real customers this week. Share the link in the same message as the price. Check your dashboard before you deliver. Follow up politely when a payment is abandoned. Keep clean records, build the fee into your prices, and protect your account with two-factor authentication. The businesses that grow fastest in Nigeria are not always the ones with the biggest adverts. They are often the ones that make it easiest for customers to pay. A payment link does exactly that. Set yours up today, and let your customers pay you while you focus on delivering great products and service.
