A customer buys from you once, on Instagram or WhatsApp, and then disappears. You have no way to reach them again except by hoping they remember you. That is how most Nigerian small businesses lose money — not because their product is bad, but because they only ever sell to each customer once.

Email newsletters fix that. An email list is an audience you own. Instagram can change its algorithm, WhatsApp can limit your broadcasts, and a social media account can be suspended without warning. Your email list stays with you. When you send a newsletter, it lands in your customer's inbox, and you can remind them about your business, your new products, and your offers — as often as you like, for almost nothing.

Yet most Nigerian small businesses either ignore email completely or send one random email and give up. This guide shows you how to do it properly: how to build a list, what to send, how often to send it, and how to measure whether it is actually bringing in sales.

## Why Email Works for Repeat Sales

Repeat customers are the foundation of a profitable business. It costs far less to sell to someone who already trusts you than to find a brand-new customer through adverts. A skincare customer who buys a ₦15,000 cream every six weeks is worth far more over a year than ten one-time buyers who never return.

Email is the cheapest way to stay in touch with those customers. Sending an email to 500 people costs next to nothing. Compare that to running Instagram adverts in Lagos, where a modest campaign can easily cost ₦20,000 to ₦50,000 a week, or to WhatsApp broadcasts, which are limited in number and often end up ignored or reported as spam.

Email also reaches people at the right moment. A well-timed email on a Friday morning — payday week, or the start of the month — can turn into orders by the afternoon. And unlike a social media post that vanishes from the feed in hours, an email sits in the inbox until the person opens it, deletes it, or acts on it.

## Step 1: Build Your List the Right Way

Your email list is only valuable if it is made of real people who want to hear from you. Buying a list of email addresses is a waste of money and a fast way to get your emails marked as spam. Build your own list, slowly and honestly.

Start with the customers you already have. Every time someone buys from you on WhatsApp or Instagram, ask for their email address. Make it easy: "Can I add your email so I can send you our new arrivals and subscriber-only offers?" Most happy customers will say yes.

Then add a signup form to every place a customer can find you:

- Your website, on the homepage and at checkout.
- Your Instagram bio link, using a simple landing page.
- Your WhatsApp Business catalogue or automated greeting.
- Your physical shop, with a small sign or a notebook at the counter.
- Your market stall or pop-up stand, with a simple paper form.

Give people a reason to join. A clear incentive works far better than "join our newsletter." Offer something specific and valuable: 10 percent off their first order, a free delivery on their next purchase, a useful guide, or early access to a new collection before it goes on sale to the public. Keep the signup form short. Name and email address is enough. Every extra field you add reduces the number of people who complete the form. You can learn more about your subscribers later, through surveys and purchase history.

Make sure you have permission. Only email people who asked to be emailed. Add a clear unsubscribe link to every email, and honour it immediately. This is not just good manners — it protects your sender reputation, so your emails keep landing in the inbox instead of the spam folder.

## Step 2: Choose a Simple, Affordable Email Tool

You do not need an expensive platform to start. Several email tools have free plans that cover a small business comfortably.

MailerLite and Brevo both offer free plans for up to 1,000 subscribers, with templates and basic automation. Mailchimp's free plan covers 500 subscribers. ConvertKit is popular with creators. If you already use a website platform, check whether it has built-in email — many WordPress sites use a plugin, and Shopify has its own email tool.

When choosing, check three things: the free subscriber limit, whether the tool lets you send automated welcome emails, and whether it shows you open and click rates. Those three features cover most of what a small business needs for the first year.

As your list grows past 1,000 subscribers, expect to pay. Prices typically start around $10 to $20 a month — roughly ₦15,000 to ₦30,000 depending on the exchange rate — and rise with your list size. That is still far cheaper than most advert spend, and it is easy to justify once the emails start generating sales.

Set up your sender details carefully. Send from a real name and a business email address on your own domain, such as hello@yourbusiness.com.ng, not a free Gmail address. Emails from a proper domain look more professional and are less likely to be flagged as spam. Add your business address and an unsubscribe link to the footer of every email, as the law in many countries requires, and as good practice demands everywhere.

## Step 3: Send a Welcome Email That Sets the Tone

The welcome email is the most important email you will ever send. It goes out automatically the moment someone joins your list, when their interest is highest, and it usually gets the highest open rate of any email you send.

A good welcome email does three things. First, it delivers whatever you promised — the discount code, the guide, or the early access link. Do this immediately, in the first lines, so the subscriber trusts you. Second, it tells the subscriber what to expect: how often you will email, and what kind of content they will get. Third, it invites a reply. Ask a simple question, such as "What are you shopping for right now?" or "Reply and tell me your skin type." Replies help your emails land in the inbox, and the answers give you useful information about your customers.

Keep the welcome email short and warm. Write it like a message from a real person, not a corporation. Sign it with your name. A short welcome series of two or three emails works even better than a single message: the first delivers the incentive, the second tells your story and shows your bestsellers, and the third offers a gentle nudge to buy, with a deadline on the discount.

## Step 4: Decide What to Send Each Week

The biggest reason small businesses stop sending newsletters is that they do not know what to write. Solve that with a simple rotation of email types, so you never face a blank page.

Here are seven email types that work for Nigerian businesses:

1. **New arrivals and restocks.** Show the new product, state the price, and link to WhatsApp or your website to order. Keep it visual and short.
2. **Offers and promotions.** A discount, a bundle, free delivery, or a buy-one-get-one deal. Use these sparingly — no more than one in every four emails — or customers will only buy when there is a discount.
3. **Useful tips.** Teach something related to your product. A hair vendor explains how to keep a wig looking new. A food business shares a recipe using its products. A real estate agent explains how to verify land documents.
4. **Customer stories.** Share a testimonial, a photo, or a short story about a happy customer. Ask permission first, and keep it genuine.
5. **Behind the scenes.** Show how a product is made, how orders are packed, or what a day in your shop looks like. People love seeing the real business behind the brand.
6. **Seasonal emails.** Tie your message to payday, school resumption, wedding season, Ramadan, Easter, or Detty December. Timely emails feel relevant and get opened.
7. **Win-back emails.** For customers who have not bought in a while, send a friendly "We miss you" email with a small incentive to return.

Plan your emails a month ahead, in a simple calendar. Pick one main email per week, plus an occasional second email when you have a genuine reason — a launch, a restock, or a limited offer. One good email a week beats four rushed ones.

## Step 5: Write Subject Lines People Actually Open

The subject line decides whether your email gets opened or deleted. It is the most important sentence you will write.

Good subject lines are short, specific, and honest. Aim for under 50 characters so they display fully on a phone. Use the subscriber's name occasionally, but do not overdo it. Create curiosity without lying — never write "Your order has shipped" when it has not, and never use false urgency like "Closing forever" for a routine sale. Nigerians have seen those tricks before, and they destroy trust.

Subject lines that work:

- "Your size is back in stock (only 8 left)"
- "10% off ends tonight — your code inside"
- "3 mistakes ruining your skin this harmattan"
- "New arrivals: Ankara styles from ₦18,000"
- "We saved you a slot for Saturday"

Test two versions of a subject line when your tool allows it. Send version A to half your list and version B to the other half, then use the winner for the rest. Over time you will learn what your audience responds to. Some audiences love direct price subject lines; others prefer curiosity. Your own data is the best guide.

Avoid words that trigger spam filters: "FREE!!!" in capitals, excessive exclamation marks, and phrases like "make money fast." Write like a person, not a spam bot, and your emails will reach the inbox more reliably.

## Step 6: Design Emails That Are Easy to Read on a Phone

Most of your subscribers will read your emails on their phones, often on MTN or Airtel data, sometimes with a slow connection. Design for that reality.

Keep the layout simple: one column, a clear headline, one main image, short paragraphs, and one obvious button or link. Do not cram five products and three offers into one email — pick one main message and one call to action. If the reader has to scroll through a wall of text, they will close the email.

Use images, but keep them small so the email loads quickly. Compress images before uploading them, and always add alt text so the message still makes sense if the image does not load. Use a font size of at least 16px for body text, so it is readable without zooming.

Every email needs a clear call to action, and it should be obvious what happens when the reader taps it. "Shop the new collection" should go to the collection, not your homepage. "Order on WhatsApp" should open a WhatsApp chat with a pre-filled message. Make buying as easy as possible — every extra step loses customers.

## Step 7: Use Automation to Sell While You Sleep

Automation is where email marketing becomes powerful. Instead of sending every email manually, you set up sequences that go out automatically when a customer does something.

The four automations every Nigerian small business should set up:

**1. The welcome series.** Three emails over the first week after signup, as described above. This turns a new subscriber into a first-time buyer.

**2. The abandoned cart email.** If you sell through a website, email customers who added items to their cart but did not complete the purchase. A reminder after a few hours, and a second one the next day, can recover a surprising number of sales. Mention the exact product they left behind, and make it easy to complete the order, including payment on delivery if you offer it.

**3. The post-purchase follow-up.** A few days after delivery, send a thank-you email and ask for a review. A week later, suggest a related product. A skincare customer who bought a cleanser might need a moisturiser next. A customer who bought a dress might like matching accessories.

**4. The win-back sequence.** If a customer has not bought in 60 or 90 days, send a short series: a friendly check-in, a useful tip, and finally a small offer to come back. Some customers just forgot about you, and a reminder is all it takes.

These automations run in the background, day and night, and they are the closest thing to passive income a small business can get from marketing.

## Step 8: Segment Your List for Better Results

Not every subscriber wants the same thing. A customer in Lagos who buys women's fashion does not want emails about men's shoes. A customer who has bought three times does not need the same welcome offer as a brand-new subscriber.

Segmentation means dividing your list into smaller groups and sending each group more relevant emails. Start with three simple segments:

- **New subscribers** who have not bought yet. Send them your bestsellers, social proof, and a first-order incentive.
- **Repeat customers** who have bought before. Send them early access, loyalty offers, and new arrivals in the categories they already buy.
- **Inactive subscribers** who have not opened an email in three months. Send them a re-engagement email, and remove them from your list if they still do not respond. A smaller, engaged list performs better than a large, cold one.

You can also segment by location — Lagos customers may want same-day delivery information that Abuja customers do not need — and by product interest, based on what each person clicked on or bought. Most email tools make this easy once you start tagging subscribers by their behaviour.

## Step 9: Measure What Matters

After every email, check four numbers in your email tool:

1. **Open rate:** the percentage of subscribers who opened the email. Below 15 percent suggests a problem with your subject lines or your list quality. Above 25 percent is strong for a small business list.
2. **Click rate:** the percentage who clicked a link. This shows whether your content was interesting enough to act on.
3. **Unsubscribe rate:** the percentage who left your list. A small number after each email is normal. A spike means that email annoyed people — look at what you sent.
4. **Sales:** the number of orders or WhatsApp enquiries that came from the email. Ask new customers how they heard about an offer, or use a unique discount code in each email so you can trace sales back to it.

Do not obsess over vanity numbers. A high open rate means nothing if nobody buys. Track the sales, and keep doing more of whatever produces them.

## A Simple 30-Day Plan to Start This Month

You can go from no email list to a working newsletter in thirty days:

- **Days 1 to 3:** Choose your email tool, set up your sender address, and create a signup form with a clear incentive.
- **Days 4 to 7:** Add the form to your website, your Instagram bio link, and your WhatsApp Business profile. Ask your last twenty customers for their email addresses.
- **Days 8 to 10:** Write your welcome email and set it to send automatically.
- **Days 11 to 14:** Plan your first month of weekly emails using the seven email types above.
- **Days 15 to 21:** Send your first newsletter. Keep it simple: one product, one story, one offer.
- **Days 22 to 30:** Check your open and click rates, reply to every response, and plan the next month.

At the end of the month, you will have a list, a routine, and real data about what your customers respond to. That is more than most of your competitors have, and it compounds. A list of 200 engaged subscribers today can be 1,000 in a year, and those 1,000 people are customers you can reach any time, without paying for adverts.
