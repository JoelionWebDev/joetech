Almost every Nigerian business owner who has tried Facebook advertising has done the same thing first: they posted a picture, tapped the blue Boost button, picked a small amount of money, and hoped for the best. Sometimes it works. Often it does not. The money disappears, a few likes arrive, and no orders follow. The problem is not that Facebook advertising does not work in Nigeria. The problem is that the Boost button is a blunt tool. It gives you almost no control over who sees your ad, what you are asking them to do, or how your money is spent.

Ads Manager is the proper tool behind that button, and it is free to use. It looks intimidating the first time you open it, with its campaigns, ad sets, and columns of numbers, but it is simply a more honest version of the same job. It lets you choose exactly what you want — messages on WhatsApp, visits to your website, or sales — and show your ad only to the people most likely to respond. For a small business in Lagos or Abuja with a modest budget, that control is the difference between wasting money and making it back.

This guide walks you through your first ad, step by step, in plain language. You will learn how to set up Ads Manager, how to add a payment method that works in Nigeria, how to choose an objective and an audience, how to write an ad that Nigerians actually respond to, and how to read your results so you know whether to keep spending or stop. You do not need a marketing degree. You need a clear offer, a small test budget, and the patience to learn from your first campaign.

## What Ads Manager Is, and Why the Boost Button Costs You More

Ads Manager is the dashboard where Facebook's real advertising happens. The Boost button is a shortcut sitting on top of it. When you boost a post, Facebook makes most of the decisions for you: it picks a broad audience, it optimises for likes and reactions, and it hides the details of what your money actually did. That is convenient, but convenience is expensive. You can easily spend money showing your ad to people who will never buy from you — people in the wrong city, the wrong age group, or with no interest in what you sell.

Ads Manager gives you the controls that boosting hides. You can choose a specific goal, such as getting WhatsApp messages instead of likes. You can target people in a particular area, such as Lekki, Ikeja, or Abuja, within a particular age range, with particular interests. You can test two different pictures against each other and see which one brings cheaper messages. You can set a daily budget, a start date, and an end date, and you can stop the ad at any time. None of that is possible from the Boost button.

None of this means you should never boost a post. If a post is already performing well organically and you want more people to see it, a small boost can help. But for a business that wants orders, enquiries, and sales — not just likes — Ads Manager is the tool to learn. Think of it this way: boosting is like telling a dispatch rider "go to Lagos". Ads Manager is like giving him the full address. The second one costs the same fuel and arrives at the right door.

## Set Up Your Ad Account and Payment Method

Before you create an ad, you need three things in place: a Facebook Page for your business, a Meta Business Suite account, and an ad account with a payment method. If you already have a Facebook Page, you are most of the way there. Open business.facebook.com on your phone's browser or on a laptop, sign in with the Facebook account that manages your Page, and follow the setup to create your business account. Add your Page to it, and add yourself as an admin. If a friend or staff member helps with your marketing, you can add them later with limited access, so they can run ads without owning your Page.

Next, create your ad account inside Business Suite. Give it your business name, set your time zone to West Africa Time, and set the currency to naira. Setting the currency to naira matters, because it means your budgets and bills appear in the money you actually use, and you avoid confusion about conversion. You can also set an account spending limit — a cap on how much the account can spend in total — which is a useful safety net while you are learning.

Then add a payment method. Most Nigerian businesses pay for Facebook ads with a naira debit or credit card from their bank, and Meta accepts cards from Nigerian banks for naira billing. Add the card in Ads Manager under Billing and Payments, and make sure the card has enough balance and is enabled for online payments. Some banks block online dollar transactions by default, so if your card is declined, check with your bank before assuming the problem is with Facebook. Start with a small test budget you can afford to lose — the cost of a few plates of food, not your rent money. Your first campaign is a lesson, not a gamble.

## Choose Your Goal and Your Audience

Every ad in Ads Manager starts with an objective — the result you want. For most Nigerian small businesses, the best first objective is Messages, because it sends people into a WhatsApp or Messenger chat with you, and a chat is where Nigerian customers like to ask questions, negotiate, and place orders. If you sell through a website, Traffic or Sales may suit you better. If you run an event, you might choose Engagement. The important thing is to match the objective to what you actually want. If you want orders, do not optimise for likes. Likes do not pay for stock.

After the objective, you build your audience. This is where most first-time advertisers waste money, by targeting "everyone in Nigeria". Nigeria is a huge country. Your ad should reach the people who can actually buy from you. Start with location: pick your city and, if you deliver locally, narrow it to the areas you serve, such as Lekki, Surulere, or Wuse. Then set an age range that matches your buyers. A vendor selling luxury wigs will target differently from a vendor selling school shoes.

Then add interests. If you sell fitness products, target people interested in fitness, gyms, and healthy living. If you sell children's clothes, target parents. Keep your audience focused but not tiny: an audience of a few hundred thousand people in your city is a healthy size for a first test. If the audience is too small, your ad will struggle to spend its budget; if it is too broad, your money will scatter. Also consider language and placement. Most Nigerian customers browse Facebook on their phones, so your ad must look good on a small screen. Let Meta show your ad on Facebook and Instagram together at first, then check later which one brought cheaper results.

## Create Your First Campaign Step by Step

Here is the practical sequence, using the Messages objective as the example. Open Ads Manager, tap Create, and choose Messages as your campaign objective. Give the campaign a clear name you will understand later, such as "Weekend Hair Sale — WhatsApp — November". Good names matter, because in a month you will have several campaigns, and "Campaign 1" tells you nothing.

Next comes the ad set, where you set the audience, the budget, and the schedule. Choose WhatsApp as the messaging destination, since most Nigerian customers prefer it. Set your daily budget — a modest amount you can sustain for at least three days, because Facebook needs time to learn who responds to your ad. Set your start date to today and your end date to three or four days later. A short test of three days gives you enough data to judge, without letting a bad ad run for a week unnoticed. Select your location, age, and interests as described above, and leave the detailed targeting simple for your first run.

Finally, build the ad itself. Upload one clear photo or a short video of your product — your real product, not a picture from the internet. Write a short headline and a primary text that states the offer, the price, and what to do next. Before you publish, use the preview to see how the ad looks on a phone. Check the spelling, the price, and the WhatsApp number. Then publish, and wait. Do not edit the ad in the first day, even if the early numbers worry you. Facebook needs time to find your audience, and constant editing resets that learning.

## Write Ad Copy and Creative That Nigerians Respond To

Nigerian customers are quick to scroll past adverts that look like adverts. Your ad must earn its first two seconds. Start your primary text with the benefit, not your business name. "Bone straight hair, 20% off this weekend, free delivery in Lekki" is stronger than "Welcome to Ada's Hair Empire, your number one plug for quality hair". The first sentence tells the reader what they get. The second tells them nothing they care about.

Keep the text short and honest. State the product, the price, the delivery area, and the next step. If delivery is free only in Lagos, say so. If the price is for one size, say which size. Customers who feel misled by an ad will not buy, and they will remember the experience. Use simple language, the way you would talk to a customer in your shop. A little pidgin or local flavour is fine if it matches your brand, but keep the important facts — price, location, and contact — in clear English so nobody misunderstands.

For the creative, use your own photos or a short video. A short video of the product in use — hair being installed, food being served, a room being cleaned — usually outperforms a still photo, because movement catches the eye. Keep videos under thirty seconds, with the product visible in the first three seconds. Add captions if someone is talking, since many people watch with the sound off. Avoid cluttered images with too much text. Facebook may also show your ad to fewer people if the image is crowded with words, so let the picture sell and let the text explain.

## Read Your Results and Stop Wasting Money

After your ad has run for two or three days, open Ads Manager and look at the numbers. The columns can feel overwhelming, so focus on four: Amount Spent, Results, Cost Per Result, and Link Clicks. Results tells you how many WhatsApp conversations your ad started. Cost Per Result tells you what each conversation cost you. If you spent ₦5,000 and got 25 conversations, each one cost ₦200. Whether that is good depends on your business — if one in five conversations becomes a sale, and your profit per sale is more than the cost of five conversations, the ad is working.

Judge an ad by conversations and sales, not by likes or reach. An ad with thousands of likes and no messages is a failed ad for a business that wants orders. Also check which people responded: their age, their area, and whether they came from Facebook or Instagram. That information helps you improve your next ad. If Instagram brought cheaper conversations, put more of your next budget there.

Set a simple rule before you start: if an ad spends your test budget and brings no messages at all, turn it off and change one thing — the picture, the headline, or the audience — then test again. If it brings messages but they do not turn into sales, the problem may not be the ad. It may be your reply speed, your prices, or your product photos on WhatsApp. Do not keep pouring money into an ad that is not working, hoping it will fix itself. And when an ad works well, increase the budget gradually, not all at once. A sudden large increase can confuse the system and raise your costs. Small, steady steps are how small budgets grow into reliable advertising.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much should I spend on my first Facebook ad in Nigeria?

Start small, with an amount you can afford to lose — enough to run for three days at a modest daily budget. The point of a first campaign is to learn what works: which picture, which audience, and what a WhatsApp conversation costs you. Once you know those numbers, you can decide how much to spend next. Never borrow money or use your business float to fund a first test.

### Why is my ad not bringing any messages?

Check four things: your objective, your audience, your creative, and your offer. If the objective is set to likes instead of messages, Facebook will bring you likes. If the audience is too broad or in the wrong city, the right people never see it. If the picture is dull or the text is unclear, people scroll past. And if the price is hidden or the offer is weak, people will not bother to start a chat. Change one thing at a time and test again.

### Can I run Facebook ads with a naira debit card?

Yes. Meta accepts naira cards from Nigerian banks for naira billing, as long as the card is enabled for online payments and has enough balance. If your card is declined, check with your bank first — some banks restrict online payments by default. Make sure your ad account currency is set to naira so your budgets and bills appear in the money you actually use.

### Should I use the Boost button or Ads Manager?

Use Ads Manager when you want orders, messages, or sales, because it gives you control over the objective, the audience, the budget, and the results. The Boost button is fine for giving a popular post a little extra reach, but it hides the controls that make advertising profitable. If you are serious about advertising as a business, learning Ads Manager is worth the afternoon it takes.

### How long should I let my first ad run before judging it?

Give it at least three days at a steady daily budget before you judge it. Facebook needs time to find the people most likely to respond, and the first day is often the weakest. Do not edit the ad during this learning period, because editing resets it. After three days, check your cost per message and your sales, then decide whether to continue, adjust, or stop.

## What to Do Next

First, open Meta Business Suite today and make sure your Page, your ad account, and your payment method are all set up, with your currency set to naira and a small spending limit in place. Do the boring setup now, so your first campaign is not delayed by a declined card or a missing verification.

Second, choose one product and one clear offer, and prepare a single good photo or short video of it, plus three lines of copy: the benefit, the price, and the call to action. Do not run your first ad for your whole catalogue. One product, one offer, one audience.

Third, launch a three-day test campaign with the Messages objective, sending people to WhatsApp, and write down what each conversation costs you and how many become sales. That single number — your cost per sale — is the most important thing your first ad will teach you, and it will guide every ad you run after it.
