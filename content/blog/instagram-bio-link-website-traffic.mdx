Your Instagram page might be getting likes, comments, and new followers, yet your website traffic barely moves. That gap is common, and it usually comes down to two small things: a bio that does not tell people why they should click, and a link that does not give them a clear reason to leave the app.

Instagram gives you very little space to send people elsewhere. You get a short bio and one clickable link. That is all. So those two elements have to work hard. This guide shows you how to write a bio that makes the click obvious, how to set up a link page that keeps visitors moving, and how to check whether any of it is actually working for a Nigerian business.

## What your bio must do in five seconds

A person who lands on your profile is deciding, almost instantly, whether you are worth following and whether your link is worth tapping. Your bio has to answer three questions in that short window: what you do, who you do it for, and what they should do next.

Start with a clear line about what you offer. Avoid vague phrases such as "we do everything" or "your one-stop shop." A tailor in Surulere, a bakery in Lekki, or a cleaning company serving Abuja should say exactly that. Clarity beats cleverness here. If a stranger cannot tell what you sell from your bio, they will not click through to find out.

Next, name who you serve. A short phrase such as "for busy professionals in Lagos" or "for small businesses across Nigeria" helps the right person feel seen and helps everyone else move on. That is a good thing. You want clicks from people who are likely to buy, book, or enquire, not clicks from everyone.

Finally, give one instruction. "Tap the link to book," "See prices on our website," or "Order from the link below" tells people what to do. Without that instruction, many visitors will simply scroll away, even if they liked your posts.

## Write the bio line by line

Think of your bio as four short lines, each with a job to do.

Line one is your promise. Say what you help people achieve, not just what you sell. A fitness coach might write "Home workouts for busy mums in Lagos" instead of "Fitness coach." A web designer might write "Websites that turn visitors into customers" instead of "Web developer." The promise gives people a reason to care.

Line two is proof or detail. This could be your location, your main service, or how you work. For example, "Lekki, Lagos | Delivery across Nigeria" or "Wedding cakes baked to order." Keep it factual. Do not claim awards, rankings, or results you cannot back up.

Line three is your call to action. This is the line that points to the link. Make it match what the link actually leads to. If the link opens a booking page, say "Book a consultation below." If it opens a shop, say "Shop the new collection below." A mismatch between the instruction and the destination is one of the fastest ways to lose a click.

Line four is practical information. A WhatsApp number, an email address, or your opening hours can sit here, but only if you actually monitor that channel. An unanswered WhatsApp number in a bio does more damage than no number at all.

Keep the whole bio short enough to read on a phone screen without tapping "more." If it takes effort to read, most people will skip it.

## Set up a link page that does not waste the click

Instagram allows one link, so most businesses use a link-in-bio page: a simple page with several buttons. Done well, it routes people to the right place. Done badly, it becomes a menu of ten buttons that nobody taps.

Start by deciding what one action matters most right now. For most small businesses, that is either booking, ordering, or enquiring. Put that button first and make it the most visible. Everything else is secondary.

Then add only two or three more buttons, at most. Useful options include your main service page, your price list or catalogue, and your WhatsApp chat link. A link to your homepage can also work, but only if your homepage makes the next step obvious. Sending someone to a homepage with no clear button is like inviting them into a shop and then walking away.

Write button labels as actions, not page names. "Book a free consultation" works better than "Services." "See this week's menu" works better than "Website." "Chat with us on WhatsApp" works better than "Contact." People tap buttons that tell them what they will get.

Check the page on your own phone before you publish it. Tap every button and confirm it opens the right page. A broken link, a page that loads slowly on mobile data, or a checkout page that asks for too much information will undo all the work your bio just did.

## Match your posts to your bio

Your bio and link do not work alone. Every post and story that mentions your link should tell people exactly what they will find when they tap it. "Link in bio" on its own is weak, because it gives no reason to click. "Tap the link in bio to see the price list" is stronger. "Tap the link to book Saturday's last two slots" is stronger still, because it gives a reason and a sense of timing.

Consistency matters beyond Instagram too. If your Instagram, WhatsApp, and website all tell a different story about what you offer, visitors get confused and leave. ## How to know if it is working

You cannot improve what you do not measure, but you do not need complicated tools to start. Most link-in-bio tools show how many people tapped each button. Your website analytics show how many visitors came from Instagram. Compare those two numbers with what you were getting before you changed your bio.

Look for three simple signs. First, are link taps increasing after you rewrite the bio? Second, are visitors from Instagram spending time on your site, or leaving immediately? Third, are those visitors taking the action you wanted, such as booking, ordering, or sending a message? If taps are up but nobody acts, the problem is probably on the landing page, not the bio. If taps are flat, the problem is probably the bio itself or the way your posts point to it.

## FAQ

### Should my Instagram link go straight to my website or to a link-in-bio page?

It depends on what you want visitors to do. If you have one clear action, such as booking or ordering, link straight to that page. If you have several useful destinations, a simple link-in-bio page with three or four buttons works better. Avoid link pages with ten or more buttons, because choice overload makes people tap nothing.

### How often should I update my Instagram bio?

Update it whenever your main offer, location, or contact details change, and review it at least once every few months. A bio that mentions a closed offer or an old phone number quietly loses you enquiries. A short monthly check is enough for most businesses.

### Do I need a separate link tool, or can I just link my homepage?

You can link your homepage directly, and for some businesses that is the best option. A link tool is useful when you want to offer a few clear choices, such as booking, a price list, and WhatsApp, without rebuilding your homepage. Whatever you choose, make sure the first thing a visitor sees tells them what to do next.

### Why do people visit my profile but not click my link?

Usually one of three reasons: the bio does not make the benefit of clicking clear, the call to action does not match the link destination, or your posts do not give a specific reason to click. Fix the bio first, then make sure your posts point to a specific offer rather than just saying "link in bio."

Your Instagram bio is small, but it is the bridge between your social media audience and your website. Write it clearly, point it at one useful action, and keep it in step with what you are actually offering. Do that, and your followers will start becoming website visitors, and your website visitors will start becoming customers.
