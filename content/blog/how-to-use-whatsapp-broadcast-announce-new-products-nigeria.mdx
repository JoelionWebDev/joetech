When a Nigerian small business launches a new product, the first place customers hear about it is often WhatsApp. Not a billboard, not a radio advert, not a sponsored post. A short message, a clear photo and a price, sent straight to a customer's phone, can sell out a new Ankara design, a new skincare set or a new food pack in a single day.

WhatsApp Broadcast is the tool that makes this possible. Unlike a WhatsApp group, a broadcast sends a message to many people at once, but each person receives it as a private message from you. They cannot see each other, they cannot reply to the whole list, and you can talk to each buyer one by one. It feels personal, and in Nigeria, personal selling works.

This guide explains how to use WhatsApp Broadcast to announce new products in Nigeria, step by step. You will learn how to build a list of people who actually want your messages, how to write an announcement that gets replies, when to send it, and how to turn those replies into paid orders without annoying your customers.

## 1. What WhatsApp Broadcast Is, and What It Is Not

WhatsApp Broadcast lets you send the same message to many contacts at once, without creating a group. On WhatsApp Business, you can create a broadcast list, add contacts, and send text, photos, videos, voice notes, documents and links to everyone on the list in one tap.

It is not a group. In a group, everyone sees everyone else and anyone can reply to all. In a broadcast, each recipient sees only your message, and their reply comes to you alone. That privacy is important for customers who do not want their number shared with strangers.

## 2. Set Up WhatsApp Business Properly First

Before you send your first broadcast, set up WhatsApp Business correctly. It is free, and it makes your business look professional and trustworthy.

Download WhatsApp Business from the Play Store or App Store, or switch your existing business number to it. Use a dedicated business number if you can, separate from your personal number, so work messages do not mix with family chats. Many Nigerian sellers use a second SIM or an eSIM for this.

Fill in your business profile completely. Add your business name, a clear logo or product photo, your address or area, for example Lekki, Lagos or Wuse, Abuja, your working hours, your email, and a short description of what you sell. Add your catalogue with photos, prices and descriptions of your products, so customers can browse without asking for a price list every time.

Set up greeting and away messages. A greeting message can say: "Hello, welcome to Ada's Cakes. Thank you for messaging us. Please tell us what you would like to order, and we will reply shortly." An away message can say when you will reply, for example after 8 p.m. or on Sunday. These small touches make customers feel attended to, even when you are busy.

## 3. Build a Broadcast List of People Who Want Your Messages

A broadcast list is only as good as the people on it. A list of 50 people who love your products will outsell a list of 250 random numbers every time.

Start with your existing customers. These are people who have bought from you, enquired about a product, or asked you to keep them updated. Add them to a list only if they have shown interest. A good first list might have 30 to 80 people. That is enough to test your announcement and get real feedback.

Ask new customers to save your number and opt in. After a sale, send a short message: "Thank you for your order. Please save this number as Ada's Cakes, so you can receive our new product updates and special offers. Reply YES if you would like to join our broadcast list." When they reply YES, add them. When they do not reply, do not add them to a sales list.

Use your WhatsApp Status, Instagram and Facebook to grow the list. Post a Status that says: "New product launching on Friday. Save our number and send YES to get the first look and a launch discount." You can also add a short link or QR code on your Instagram bio that opens a WhatsApp chat with a pre-filled message like "Hi, I want new product updates."

Keep your lists segmented. Do not put everyone on one list. Create separate lists for different products or locations, for example "Skincare Lagos", "Skincare Abuja", "Wholesale buyers", and "VIP customers". A customer who buys baby clothes does not want messages about men's shoes, and a wholesale buyer does not want retail prices.

## 4. Plan Your Product Announcement Like a Launch

A good broadcast announcement is planned, not rushed. Treat a new product like a small launch, even if you are a one-person business.

Decide what is new and why it matters. Is it a new flavour, a new colour, a new size, a better formula, or a limited edition for Christmas or Sallah? Customers need a reason to care. "New product" alone is not enough. "New: our handmade shea butter now comes in a 500ml family size, with a pump, for busy mums" is a reason.

Choose a launch date and time. In Nigeria, many customers check WhatsApp in the morning before work, at lunchtime, and in the evening after 7 p.m. Test different times and note when you get the most replies. Avoid sending very early in the morning or late at night, unless your customers expect it.

Prepare your photos and video before you send anything. Take clear photos in good daylight, on a clean background, showing the product from the front and in use. A short 15 to 30 second video of the product being used, opened or worn is even better. Blurry photos and dark videos kill sales, no matter how good the product is.

Set your price and your offer in advance. Decide the launch price, whether delivery is included, how long the offer lasts, and how customers should order. For example: "Launch price ₦12,000 instead of ₦15,000, until Sunday only. Pay by transfer to confirm your order. Delivery in Lagos ₦2,000."

## 5. Write a Broadcast Message That Gets Replies

Your message has one job: to make the customer reply. A long, boring announcement will be ignored. A short, clear and exciting one will get orders.

Start with a personal greeting and the news. Use the customer's name if you can, or a warm general greeting. For example: "Hello, good morning. We have something new for you." Then say what the product is in one sentence.

Show the benefit, not just the feature. Do not only say "new 500ml body butter". Say "new 500ml body butter that keeps your skin soft all day, even in harmattan, with a pump so nothing is wasted." Customers buy benefits.

Include the price and how to order. Many Nigerian sellers hide the price and say "DM for price". That reduces replies, because customers do not want to ask and be embarrassed. State the price clearly, state the launch offer, and tell them exactly what to do: "Reply ORDER to buy now, or tap here to chat."

Keep it short. A broadcast message should fit on one phone screen. Aim for 80 to 120 words, plus one or two photos or a short video. If you need more detail, send a catalogue link or a PDF, and let the customer ask for more.

Here is a sample announcement you can adapt:

"Hello, this is Ada from Ada's Cakes. Our new Christmas fruit cake is ready. Rich, moist, with real fruits and nuts, in a beautiful gift box. Launch price ₦18,000 instead of ₦22,000, until Sunday. Only 20 boxes available. Reply ORDER and we will send payment details. Delivery in Lagos from Friday."

That message is short, specific, urgent and easy to act on. It tells the customer what it is, why it is special, what it costs, how long the offer lasts, and what to do next.

## 6. Send, Track and Follow Up Without Spamming

Sending the broadcast is only half the work. How you follow up decides how many sales you make.

Send at a time when customers are likely to read and reply. For many Nigerian businesses, 10 a.m. to 12 noon and 7 p.m. to 9 p.m. work well. Test both and track which gets more replies. Send the announcement once, not three times in one day. If someone does not reply, wait at least two days before a gentle reminder.

Watch the blue ticks and replies. On WhatsApp Business, you can see who received and read your message. If many people read but few reply, your price, photos or offer may need work. If few people even receive it, many have not saved your number, and you need to ask them to save it.

Reply fast. When a customer replies "How much?" or "Is it available?", answer within minutes if you can, and within an hour at most during working hours. Use quick replies in WhatsApp Business for common questions, so you do not type the same answer fifty times.

Follow up with people who showed interest but did not buy. A polite message after two days works: "Hello, thank you for your interest in our new cake. We have five boxes left at the launch price until Sunday. Would you like to reserve one?" Do not send this more than once, and do not pressure anyone. If they say no, thank them and move on.

## 7. Use Status, Groups and Catalogue Together

Broadcast works best when it is part of a wider WhatsApp system, not the only tool you use.

Use WhatsApp Status to warm up your audience before the broadcast. Post a teaser two days before launch: a close-up photo with the words "Something new is coming on Friday." Post another teaser the day before: "Tomorrow, 10 a.m. Save our number so you do not miss it." By launch day, customers are already expecting your message.

Link WhatsApp to Instagram and Facebook. Add your WhatsApp link to your Instagram bio, your Facebook Page, and your flyers. When a new customer clicks it, your greeting message should ask what they need and invite them to join your broadcast list for new products.

## 8. Avoid Spam, Blocks and Account Limits

WhatsApp protects its users from spam, and it will limit or ban accounts that send too many unwanted messages. Protect your account by following the rules.

Only message people who have saved your number and agreed to receive updates. This is the most important rule. If you buy a list of numbers and broadcast to strangers, many will report you, and WhatsApp may restrict your account. Build your list slowly, with permission, and it will last.

Do not send too often. For most small businesses, one broadcast a week is enough, plus one extra message for a major launch. Sending a broadcast every day, or several times a day, will make customers mute you, block you or delete your number. Quality beats frequency.

Do not send the same message to many lists at the same time in a rush. Space your sends by a few minutes, and keep each list under 256 contacts. If you have more customers, create several smaller lists and send to them one by one.

If a customer asks to stop receiving messages, remove them immediately and thank them. Keep a simple list of people who opted out, and never add them back without their permission. Respect builds trust, and trust builds sales.

## 9. Measure What Works and Improve Each Launch

You cannot improve what you do not measure. After each broadcast launch, spend 15 minutes reviewing what happened.

Count the basics: how many people received the message, how many read it, how many replied, how many ordered, and how much you sold. For example, if you sent to 120 people, 90 read it, 25 replied, and 12 ordered at ₦15,000 each, you made ₦180,000 from one broadcast. That is a result worth repeating.

Compare launches. Did a video get more replies than a photo? Did a morning send work better than an evening send? Did a launch discount of 20 percent sell more than a free gift? Keep notes in a notebook or Google Sheet, so your next launch is better than your last.

Ask customers what they thought. After delivery, send a short message: "Thank you for buying our new product. How did you like it? Is there anything we can improve?" Their answers help you improve the product, and their good reviews can be screenshotted, with permission, for your next announcement.

## 10. Real Examples for Nigerian Businesses

Examples make the method clear. Here are three simple launch plans you can copy.

A skincare seller in Lekki launches a new body butter. Two days before launch, she posts a teaser on Status. On launch day at 10 a.m., she sends a broadcast to her 85-person list with two photos, the price of ₦12,000, and a launch offer of free delivery in Lekki until Sunday. By evening, 18 people have replied and 9 have paid. She follows up the next day with the remaining interested customers, and sells 5 more. Total: 14 sales, ₦168,000 in revenue, from one broadcast and two Status posts.

A fashion seller in Onitsha launches a new Ankara dress design. She sends the broadcast to her VIP list first, at 7 p.m., with first access and a 10 percent discount for 24 hours. The next morning, she sends it to her general list at the full price. The VIP list feels special and buys first, and the general list still buys because the design is new and the photos are clear. She sells 20 dresses in three days, without spending money on ads.

Notice what these sellers have in common: a small, permission-based list, clear photos, a clear price, a deadline, and fast replies. You do not need a large list or a big budget. You need the right people and a clear message.

## 11. Grow From Broadcast to a Loyal Customer Base

Broadcast is not just for one launch. Used well, it builds a loyal customer base that buys from you again and again.

Reward repeat buyers. After a customer's third order, send a personal thank-you and a small loyalty offer, for example 10 percent off their next order or a free sample of a new product. Loyal customers are cheaper to keep than new customers are to find, and they refer friends.

Keep your list clean. Every three months, remove numbers that never read your messages, people who asked to opt out, and numbers that are no longer on WhatsApp. A clean list of 80 engaged customers is better than a messy list of 250 silent numbers.

Finally, combine broadcast with other channels as you grow. Use Instagram and TikTok to attract new customers, use broadcast to sell to them, and use a simple website or catalogue to show your full range. Broadcast is your direct line to customers who already trust you. Protect it, use it well, and it will keep selling for you long after a social media post has been forgotten.

## Conclusion

WhatsApp Broadcast is one of the simplest and most powerful sales tools for a Nigerian small business. It costs nothing, it reaches customers directly, and it feels personal in a way that a group message or a sponsored post never will.

To use it well, set up WhatsApp Business properly, build a list of people who have saved your number and asked for updates, and plan each product announcement like a small launch. Write a short, clear message with good photos, a clear price and one call to action. Send it at a good time, reply fast, and follow up politely with people who showed interest.

Do not spam, do not buy lists, and do not message people who have not agreed to hear from you. Send useful announcements, respect opt-outs, and measure what works so each launch gets better.

Your next product does not need a big advertising budget to sell. It needs the right message, sent to the right people, at the right time, on WhatsApp. Build your list today, prepare your photos and price, and send your next announcement with confidence. Your customers are already on WhatsApp, waiting to hear from you.
