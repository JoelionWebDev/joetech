There is a particular energy at a Nigerian trade fair that you cannot get from an Instagram page. The music, the crowds moving from booth to booth, the smell of food from the next stand, the bargaining, the laughter. Thousands of people walk past your products in a single day, people who came ready to buy. No advert can put that many potential customers in front of you that quickly.

But anyone who has exhibited at a fair in Lagos or Abuja will also tell you the other side of the story. Some vendors sell out by noon on the first day. Others sit behind their table for three days, watching the crowd walk past, and go home with most of their stock and a painful lesson about money spent on a booth that did not pay for itself. The difference is rarely luck. It is preparation.

Selling successfully at a trade fair is a skill, and it can be learned. This guide covers the whole process: choosing the right fair for your business, budgeting honestly so the fair makes money, setting up a booth that stops people in their tracks, selling to strangers face to face, collecting contacts you can follow up with, and turning a three-day event into customers who keep buying long after the fair is over.

## Why Trade Fairs Still Work in Nigeria

In a country where online shopping is growing fast, it is fair to ask whether trade fairs are still worth the money. For many Nigerian small businesses, the answer is yes, for reasons that have everything to do with how Nigerians like to buy.

First, Nigerians love to see, touch, and test a product before paying. A customer who has been unsure about your skincare product online will try a tester on the back of her hand at your booth and buy three bottles on the spot. A buyer who would never order a dress from a photo will try it on, feel the fabric, and pay immediately. A trade fair removes the doubt that stops online sales.

Second, a fair gathers your exact customers in one place. A food fair brings food lovers. A fashion fair brings fashion buyers. A made-in-Nigeria fair brings people who specifically want to support local brands. Instead of advertising to thousands of people and hoping the right ones see it, you are standing in a room full of them.

Third, fairs build trust fast. When a customer meets you in person, sees your products, tastes your food, or watches a demonstration, they believe in your business in a way a social media page cannot achieve. That trust turns into sales at the fair and orders on WhatsApp for months afterwards.

None of this means every fair is worth attending. The trick is choosing the right one, and that is where successful exhibitors start.

## Step 1: Choose the Right Fair

Not all trade fairs are equal, and the wrong fair can waste your money no matter how good your products are. Before you pay for a booth, do some research.

Start with the audience. Who attends this fair? A fair at the Lagos International Trade Fair Complex attracts a broad, price-sensitive crowd looking for bargains. A curated made-in-Nigeria exhibition at a hotel in Lekki attracts a smaller but higher-spending audience. A sector fair, such as a food and beverage expo or a beauty fair, attracts people who are already interested in what you sell. Match the audience to your product and your price. A luxury brand at a bargain fair will struggle, and a budget brand at a premium fair will look out of place.

Ask the organisers direct questions before you commit. How many visitors attended last year, and how many exhibitors were there? What kind of marketing are they doing to bring in buyers, not just exhibitors? Is the venue easy to reach, with parking and security? What exactly does the booth fee include: a table, chairs, electricity, a banner space? Get the answers in writing, ideally on WhatsApp or email, so there are no surprises.

Talk to vendors who exhibited at the previous edition. Ask them honestly: did you make your money back? Was the crowd serious buyers or just sightseers? Would you exhibit again? Vendors will usually tell you the truth, especially if you ask in person or in a vendors' WhatsApp group.

## Step 2: Budget Honestly Before You Book

A trade fair has more costs than the booth fee, and vendors who only budget for the booth are the ones who end up surprised. Build a full budget before you commit, and be honest about every line.

Your budget should include the booth fee, which in Lagos can range from around ₦50,000 for a small shared space at a community fair to ₦500,000 or more for a prime spot at a major exhibition. Add the cost of your banner, flyers, and any printed materials. Add transport for your stock and your team, and feeding for your team across the fair days. Add the cost of packaging and shopping bags. Add the cost of your stock itself, valued at what it cost you, not what you will sell it for. And add a small contingency, about 10 percent of the total, for the things you forgot.

Then set your sales target. A simple rule is that your total sales at the fair should be at least three times your total costs. If your full costs come to ₦200,000, you should aim to sell at least ₦600,000 worth of products. If that target looks impossible based on the fair's expected crowd and your prices, either negotiate a cheaper booth, share a booth with a complementary business, or skip this fair and wait for a better one.

## Step 3: Prepare Your Products and Prices

Decide what you will sell at the fair before you pack a single box. The fair is not the place to bring your entire catalogue. Bring your bestsellers, your most eye-catching products, and one or two items that are new or exclusive to the fair. A focused range of fifteen to twenty products sells better than a crowded table with a hundred items nobody can choose between.

Price for the fair deliberately. Many customers come to a fair expecting a deal, so a small fair discount or a fair-only bundle can be a strong draw. For example, buy two get one free, or a bundle price that saves the customer ₦2,000 compared with buying items separately. But protect your margin. Work out the profit on every fair price before the day, and never agree to a discount on the spot that you have not already calculated. It is easy to say yes to a bargaining customer in the excitement of the moment and realise later that you sold at a loss.

Prepare a special fair offer that gives people a reason to buy now instead of later. It could be a discount that applies only at the fair, a free gift with every purchase above ₦10,000, or a product that is only available at the fair. Make the offer clear on your banner and your price list, so customers understand it without asking.

## Step 4: Design a Booth That Stops People

At a fair, you have about three seconds to catch a visitor's attention as they walk past. Your booth has to do that job before you say a word.

Start with a clear, readable banner. Your business name should be big enough to read from several metres away, and it should say what you sell, not just your brand name. "Ada's Kitchen: Fresh Cakes and Small Chops" tells a passer-by far more than "Ada's Kitchen" alone. Add your WhatsApp number in large digits, because many visitors will photograph your banner and contact you later.

Use height and colour. A flat table with products laid out in a row is invisible in a crowded hall. Use crates, stands, or shelves to create levels, so products at the back are visible above the ones at the front. Use a bright tablecloth in your brand colour, and keep the table tidy. A cluttered booth signals a disorganised business.

Put your best product at eye level and at the front edge of your table, where hands can reach it. People buy what they can touch. If you sell food, offer free samples. A small taste costs you almost nothing and converts browsers into buyers better than any speech. If you sell skincare, have testers open and ready. If you sell clothes, hang a few pieces up so people can see the fit and the fabric instead of digging through a pile.

Light matters, especially at indoor venues where the hall lighting is poor. A small rechargeable LED light aimed at your products can make your booth look brighter and more professional than your neighbours'. Keep your walkway clear and your personal belongings out of sight. A booth that looks welcoming invites people in; a booth with bags and food containers on the display table pushes them away.

## Step 5: Attract Visitors and Start Conversations

A beautiful booth with a silent vendor behind it will not sell. Your job at the fair is to talk to people, and the way you start those conversations decides how many stop and how many buy.

Stand up, smile, and greet people as they pass. Do not sit behind your table scrolling through your phone. A simple "Good afternoon, would you like to taste our meat pie?" or "We have a fair discount today, come and see" works better than a hard sell. Nigerians respond to warmth and friendliness. A vendor who is cheerful and confident will outsell a vendor with better products but a closed face.

Use a demonstration whenever you can. A live demo stops a crowd. A food vendor cooking on the spot, a makeup artist doing a quick makeover, a fashion vendor styling an outfit on a customer, or a tech vendor showing how a product works will gather onlookers, and onlookers become buyers. If your product can be demonstrated, build your day around it.

Collect contacts from everyone who shows interest, even those who do not buy. A simple notebook, a Google Form on a tablet, or a QR code that opens your WhatsApp chat will do the job. Offer a small reason to leave a contact: a chance to win a hamper, a discount code for their next order, or early notice of your next fair. Every contact you collect is a future customer, and the follow-up after the fair is where much of the real money is made.

## Step 6: Sell, Take Payment, and Keep Records

When a customer decides to buy, make the purchase easy and fast. Have your prices written clearly on a price list, so there is no confusion and no long negotiation over every item. If you are willing to negotiate, know your lowest price for each product in advance, and never go below it. A polite "That is the best I can do at the fair price" is a complete answer.

Accept as many payment methods as you can. Many fair customers will pay by bank transfer, so have your account number printed on a card and ready to show, and confirm each payment before handing over the goods. If you use a point-of-sale terminal, bring it fully charged with a backup power bank, and test it before the fair opens. If you accept payment through a Paystack or Flutterwave link, have it ready on your phone. Cash still matters at fairs, so bring enough change in small notes, keep cash in a secure pouch or a small cash box, and count it regularly.

Give every buyer a receipt or a written note with your business name, WhatsApp number, and what they bought. A simple branded bag with your number on it does the same job. This is not just good service; it is how a one-time fair buyer finds you again on Monday morning.

Keep a sales record as you go. A notebook or a Google Sheet on your phone is enough. Record each sale: the product, the quantity, the amount, and the payment method. At the end of each day, count your cash, reconcile your transfers, and compare your sales against your target. This record tells you what is selling, what is not, and whether the fair is paying for itself while there is still time to adjust.

## Step 7: Follow Up After the Fair

The fair ends, but the selling does not. The vendors who make the most money from a trade fair are usually the ones who follow up properly in the week after it. Studies of trade fairs consistently show that most sales to new contacts happen after the event, not during it, because many visitors need time to think, talk to a partner, or wait for payday.

Start your follow-up within 48 hours, while the fair is still fresh in people's minds. Send a short, personal WhatsApp message to every contact you collected: "Hi Ada, it was lovely meeting you at the Lagos Trade Fair. Thank you for stopping by our booth. The shea butter cream you liked is still available, and I can deliver to Lekki this week. Would you like me to reserve one for you?" Keep it short, friendly, and specific. Mention the product they looked at, so they remember you.

Do not send the same broadcast to everyone. A generic broadcast feels like spam and gets ignored. A personal message that mentions what they saw gets answered. If you collected a hundred contacts, send ten personal messages a day for ten days. It takes discipline, but it works.

Track your results. How many contacts did you collect? How many replied to your follow-up? How many bought after the fair? How much did you sell in total, during the fair and in the two weeks after, compared with your total costs? Write the numbers down. They will tell you whether this fair is worth doing again, and what to change next time.

## Mistakes That Waste Your Booth Fee

The first mistake is choosing a fair for the wrong reasons, such as a cheap booth fee or because a friend is exhibiting. A cheap booth at a fair your customers do not attend is still wasted money. Choose by audience, not by price.

The second is arriving unprepared. Vendors who arrive late, with no banner, no price list, and no change, spend the first day fixing problems instead of selling. Prepare everything the day before, pack the night before, and arrive early enough to set up calmly before the gates open.

The third is sitting down and waiting. A trade fair rewards vendors who stand, greet, demonstrate, and invite. If you wanted to sit quietly, you could have stayed at home. Your energy is part of your display.

The fourth is having no way to take payment or no change. A customer who is ready to pay and cannot is a customer you will never see again. Test every payment method before the fair, and bring more change than you think you need.

The fifth is collecting no contacts and never following up. If every visitor who does not buy simply walks away, you have thrown away half the value of the fair. Always have a notebook, a form, or a QR code ready, ask for the contact, and message them within 48 hours.

## Conclusion

A trade fair can be one of the best investments a Nigerian small business makes, or one of the most expensive lessons. The difference is preparation. Choose a fair your customers actually attend. Budget for every cost, not just the booth. Bring a focused range of products priced with your profit intact. Build a booth that stops people, greet them warmly, demonstrate what you sell, and collect a contact from every interested visitor. Then follow up personally, quickly, and consistently.

Do that, and a three-day fair becomes more than three days of sales. It becomes new customers, new wholesale enquiries, new stockists, and new fans of your brand who will buy from you long after the fairground is empty. Your next trade fair is an opportunity waiting to be used well. Prepare for it like a professional, and it will pay you back like one.
