Your website gets visitors, but your phone stays quiet. Sound familiar? For many Nigerian businesses, the problem is not traffic. It is the gap between a visitor reading your site and that visitor actually reaching you.

Here is the good news: most of your visitors already have a preferred way to contact you, and it is probably WhatsApp. They use it every day. They trust it. They know how to send a message on it without thinking. If your website makes WhatsApp the easiest next step, more visitors will take it.

This guide shows how to do that properly, without plastering your site with pop-ups or turning every page into a sales pitch.

## Why WhatsApp works for Nigerian businesses

Think about how your own customers behave. A form on a website asks for a name, an email, a phone number, and a message, then shows a thank-you page and silence. The customer does not know when, or even whether, anyone will reply. A phone call feels more direct, but many people hesitate to call a business they have not spoken to before, especially outside working hours.

WhatsApp sits in the middle. It feels informal and low-pressure, yet the message lands directly with the business. The customer can write at midnight, attach a photo of what they want, and continue the conversation the next morning. The business can reply with text, voice notes, photos, prices, or a location pin, all in one thread.

That is why so many enquiries in Nigeria already happen on WhatsApp, even for businesses whose websites were built around a contact form. The smart move is to design for the behaviour your customers already have, rather than forcing them into a channel they do not enjoy.

## Step 1: Make WhatsApp visible, but not aggressive

Your WhatsApp contact option should be easy to find on every page, without blocking the content people came to read.

A floating WhatsApp button, usually at the bottom corner of the screen on mobile, is the standard approach for a reason: it stays visible as the visitor scrolls, and one tap opens a chat. Keep it small, use the familiar green WhatsApp look, and make sure it does not cover important buttons or text on small screens.

In addition, place a clear WhatsApp link or button in the places where a visitor is most likely to decide:

- At the top of your homepage, next to your phone number.
- At the end of each service page, after the visitor has read what you offer.
- On your contact page, as the first option, before or alongside the form.
- Inside blog posts, only where it genuinely fits, such as at the end of a helpful article.

## Step 2: Pre-fill the message for them

A blank chat window is a small barrier. The visitor has to decide what to write, and some people will close the window rather than compose a first message, especially if they are unsure what to ask.

A pre-filled message removes that hesitation. When the visitor taps your WhatsApp button, the chat can open with a message already typed, such as: "Hello, I found your business on your website and I would like to enquire about your services."

You can go further on individual service pages. A catering page can pre-fill: "Hello, I would like to enquire about catering for an event." A school page can pre-fill: "Hello, I would like to find out about admission." The visitor can edit the message before sending, but they no longer face a blank screen.

Keep the pre-filled message short, polite, and general. Do not pre-fill personal details or make it sound like a sales script.

## Step 3: Tell visitors what happens next

People message more readily when they know what to expect. On the page near your WhatsApp button, or in your first automated reply, set a simple expectation.

For example, a short line on your contact page can say: "Message us on WhatsApp and we will reply during working hours." If you have a WhatsApp Business account, you can set a greeting message and an away message that explains your working hours and when the customer can expect a reply.

This matters because an enquiry that gets no reply feels worse than no enquiry at all. A short acknowledgement, even an automated one, keeps the conversation alive until a real person can respond.

## Step 4: Reply like a human, and reply fast

The fastest way to lose a WhatsApp enquiry is to reply slowly, or with a wall of copied text that ignores what the customer actually asked. Reply as quickly as your working hours allow, and start by answering their actual question: if they asked about price, explain how pricing works rather than sending a long brochure; if they asked about availability, answer that first, then offer the next step.

A good reply pattern looks like this:

1. Greet the customer by name if they gave it.
2. Answer their question directly, in short sentences.
3. Offer one clear next step, such as a visit, a call, a booking, or a payment link.
4. Ask one follow-up question if you need more detail, such as the date, the quantity, or the location.

Avoid sending long voice notes as a first reply, and avoid asking for too much information at once. The customer chose WhatsApp because it feels easy. Keep it that way.

## Step 5: Turn the chat into a sale, gently

Not every WhatsApp enquiry becomes a customer, and that is normal. Your job is to guide the conversation without pressure.

When a customer shows interest, move the conversation forward with small, natural steps. Share a photo of the product or a short video of your work. Confirm the details in writing so both sides agree. Send payment details only after the customer has confirmed what they want. After a purchase or booking, follow up once to check that everything went well, and thank them.

One thing to avoid: do not add customers to broadcast lists or groups without their permission. A single unwanted broadcast can damage trust faster than a slow reply.

## Mistakes that kill WhatsApp enquiries

Watch out for these common problems:

- A WhatsApp button that links to a personal number with no business profile, no photo, and no description.
- A button that opens WhatsApp but goes nowhere because the number is typed incorrectly.
- A chat that is answered days later, or never answered at all.
- A first reply that is a long, generic sales message instead of an answer.
- A website that hides WhatsApp behind a form, forcing visitors to fill in details before they can message.

Test your own button from your phone: check that the chat opens, the pre-filled message appears, and the number is correct. A broken WhatsApp link is worse than no link, because it promises an easy conversation and then fails.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I replace my contact form with WhatsApp?

No. Keep both. Some customers prefer forms, especially for formal enquiries, and a form gives you a written record in your email. WhatsApp should be the easiest option, not the only option.

### Do I need WhatsApp Business, or is regular WhatsApp enough?

WhatsApp Business is better for a business, because it lets you set a business profile, greeting and away messages, quick replies, and labels for chats. It is a separate app, and you can move your existing number to it.

### What should my pre-filled message say?

Keep it short and neutral, such as: "Hello, I found your business on your website and I would like to make an enquiry." On service pages, you can mention the service, but do not pre-fill personal details or a long sales pitch.

### How fast should I reply to a WhatsApp enquiry?

As fast as your working hours allow. If you cannot reply immediately, set an away message that says when you will reply, and then keep that promise. A quick acknowledgement is far better than silence.

## The bottom line

Turning website visitors into WhatsApp enquiries is not about clever tricks. It is about removing friction: a visible button, a pre-filled message, a clear expectation, and a fast, human reply.

Make WhatsApp the easiest next step on your website, test it yourself, and treat every chat as a real conversation, not a lead to be processed. Do that consistently, and your website will start doing what it was built to do: bringing customers to you.
