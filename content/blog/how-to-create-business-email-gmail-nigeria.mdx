Tolu runs a catering business in Lekki. She has a domain, toluscatering.com.ng, a website, and an Instagram page with four thousand followers. Every quote she sends goes out from toluscatering2021@gmail.com, because that is the address she has used since she started. A hotel manager in Ikoyi received one of those quotes last month, for ₦850,000 worth of food for a conference, and chose a competitor instead. When Tolu asked a friend at the hotel what happened, the friend was honest. "Your food is better. But your email looked like a side hustle. The other caterer wrote from her own domain."

## The Two Roads: Workspace and Send-As

Google Workspace is the paid road. You pay Google every month for each person who needs an inbox, and Google gives you a real mailbox on your own domain. Your address becomes tolu@toluscatering.com.ng, and it is a proper address, with its own storage, its own login, and its own place in Google's system. When someone writes to it, the email lands in that mailbox. When you reply, it goes out from that address, and nothing about Gmail shows anywhere. It is clean, it is professional, and it is the standard that corporate clients expect.

The free road is called send-as. You keep your free Gmail account, and you tell Gmail to send emails that look like they came from your domain. Your customers see tolu@toluscatering.com.ng in their inbox. Behind the scenes, though, the mail is still travelling through your free Gmail account, and the replies still come back to your Gmail inbox unless you set up forwarding as well. It works, and for a one-person business watching every naira, it is a sensible place to start. But it is a costume, not a new identity, and costumes slip.

Prices change with the dollar, so check Google's current price before you pay. As a rough guide, the basic Workspace plan has commonly cost around ₦9,000 to ₦15,000 per user per month when billed in naira through a card. That is per person, so one inbox costs that amount, and a second staff member doubles it. A domain itself costs roughly ₦10,000 to ₦25,000 a year from a Nigerian registrar, depending on the name. Send-as costs nothing beyond the domain, which is why so many businesses start there.

## What MX Records Actually Are, in Plain Words

Every guide about custom domain email mentions MX records, and almost none of them explains what they are. Here is the plain version. Your domain name is like the name of your shop. The internet needs to know where to deliver letters addressed to that shop. MX records are the directions. They are a few lines of text, stored where your domain lives, that say: email for toluscatering.com.ng should go to Google's servers, or to Zoho's servers, or wherever your mailbox lives.

When you sign up for Google Workspace, Google hands you five MX records. They look like strange sentences, with numbers and server names. You do not need to understand them. You need to copy them, exactly as Google wrote them, into the DNS settings of your domain, which is the control panel your registrar gave you when you bought the domain. If that sentence made your eyes glaze over, here is the practical version: send the records to your registrar's support team or your website developer, and ask them to add them. It takes them a few minutes, and it is a normal request. They do it every week.

Until the MX records are correct, emails to your new address will bounce or vanish. That is normal, and it is not a reason to panic. After the records are added, they need time to spread across the internet, usually a few minutes to a few hours, and occasionally up to a full day. Send a test email from your personal Gmail to your new address, and reply to it. If the reply arrives, the road is open.

There are three more records that matter once the mail is flowing, and they are the reason many Nigerian businesses watch their proposals land in spam. SPF, DKIM, and DMARC are authentication records. In plain words, SPF is a list that says which services are allowed to send email for your domain. DKIM is a digital signature that proves the email really came from you. DMARC is an instruction that tells the receiving server what to do with mail that fails those checks. Google gives you the exact values to copy. You do not need to understand the cryptography behind them. You need to copy them into the same DNS settings, and then send a test email to a corporate address, a friend at a bank or a hotel, and ask whether it landed in the inbox or the spam folder. Businesses that skip these three records are the ones whose ₦850,000 proposals sit unread in a spam folder.

## Setting Up Send-As Without Tripping Over It

If you choose the free road, here is how it works in practice. First, you need a mailbox on your domain that actually receives mail. That usually means signing up for a free plan somewhere, such as Zoho Mail, and pointing your MX records at Zoho. Then you open Gmail, go to Settings, then Accounts and Import, then "Send mail as", and add your business address. Gmail will ask you to prove you own the address, usually by sending a code to it. You open the Zoho mailbox, find the code, paste it into Gmail, and the link is made.

From that day on, when you write an email in Gmail, you can click the From field and choose your business address. That click is the whole trick, and it is also where the whole trick falls apart, as we will see shortly.

## Sending as Your Business Address, Every Single Time

The most common mistake in this whole story is not technical. It is human. It is replying from the wrong address. You are in a hurry. A customer writes to your business address. You hit reply, and Gmail replies from your personal address, because that is the account you were signed into, or because you forgot to change the From field. The customer now has two addresses for you, and the next time she writes, she will use the personal one, because it is the one that answered her. Three months later, your business history is scattered across two inboxes, and you are searching both every time a customer asks about an old quote.

The fix is a setting and a habit. In Gmail's Settings, under Accounts and Import, there is an option that says "Reply from the same address the message was sent to". Turn it on. It tells Gmail to answer a business email from the business address automatically, without you having to remember. Then build the habit. Before you press Send on any business email, look at the From line. It takes one second. Do it every time, even when you are sure. The one time you do not check is the time a ₦850,000 proposal goes out from your personal address.

If you have staff, the rule is stricter. Give each person their own address, and never let two people share one login. With Workspace, that is easy. You create sales@, support@, and accounts@, and each person signs in as themselves. When someone leaves, you change the password and keep the address, so the customer history stays with the business. With send-as, sharing one Gmail login between three people is a slow disaster. Someone will reply from the wrong address, someone will delete a message, and you will have no way to know who did it.

## Signatures That Do the Selling for You

A signature is the small block of text at the bottom of every email, and it is the cheapest branding your business will ever buy. A good signature has your name, your role, your business name, your phone number, your WhatsApp number, your website, and your address, in that order, and nothing else. No jokes. No quotes. No picture that is two megabytes and makes your email look like a flyer.

In Gmail, you write your signature under Settings, then General, then Signature. Write one for your business address, and make it the default for that address. That way, every email you send as your business carries the same signature, and every email you send as yourself carries your personal one, or none at all. Customers who receive a quote with a full signature can call you, WhatsApp you, and visit your website without asking for anything. Customers who receive a quote with no signature have to reply and ask for your number, and some of them will not bother.

## The Mistakes That Give You Away

The first mistake is the one this article keeps returning to, because it is the one that happens most often. Your customer sees your gmail address. It happens when you reply from the wrong account, when you forward a business email from your personal address, or when you send a quotation as an attachment from the wrong inbox. Each time it happens, the customer learns that your business address is decoration, and your real address is a free Gmail account. The fix is the setting above, and the habit of checking the From line before every send.

The next mistake is never checking the inbox. A professional address that nobody reads is worse than a personal address you actually answer. A customer who emails you on Monday and hears nothing until Thursday will assume you are not serious, and will buy from someone else. If you cannot check email every day, set an auto-reply that says when you will respond and gives your WhatsApp number for urgent enquiries. An auto-reply that is honest buys you time. Silence buys you nothing.

## Moving From Send-As to Workspace When You Are Ready

There will come a day when send-as is no longer enough. Maybe a corporate client asked why your emails say "sent via Gmail". Maybe you hired two staff and you need three inboxes. Maybe you are tired of checking two systems every morning. The move is simpler than most people fear, because your domain and your address stay exactly the same. Only the service behind them changes.

Tolu, the caterer from the start of this article, made the move in a single weekend. She kept her Gmail app, because Workspace is Gmail, and her customers never noticed a change. What they noticed, over the next month, was that her quotes arrived from toluscatering.com.ng, her replies came from the same address, and her signature carried her phone number and her website. She won two hotel contracts in the following quarter. She will tell you the food won them. Her friend at the hotel will tell you the email helped. Both of them are right. Your product still has to be good. But when it is, an email address that carries your own domain makes sure the customer takes you seriously enough to find out.
