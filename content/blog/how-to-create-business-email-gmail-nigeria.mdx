When a customer in Lagos receives a quote from info@yourbusiness.com.ng, they treat it differently from the same quote sent from cutebabygirl2020@gmail.com. That is not snobbery — it is trust. A professional email address tells a customer that your business is real, that you take it seriously, and that you will still be there next month when they need after-sales support. For a Nigerian small business competing with bigger companies, that first impression matters, and it costs far less than most people think.

Gmail is the easiest place to start, because most Nigerians already use it, it works well on cheap Android phones and slow MTN or Airtel data, and its spam filtering is strong. There are two main ways to use it for business: a free Gmail address that sounds professional, such as yourbusinesslagos@gmail.com, and a custom domain email — yourname@yourbusiness.com.ng — that still runs on Gmail through Google Workspace. This guide explains both, with real costs in naira, so you can choose what fits today and upgrade later.

## Why a Professional Email Matters More Than You Think

Your email address appears on your business card, your Instagram bio, your WhatsApp profile, your invoices, and every proposal you send. It is often the first thing a corporate client, a hotel, or a government office sees. A free personal address with a nickname, numbers, or a joke makes a bad first impression before they even read your message.

A professional address also protects you. When you hire staff, you can give each person their own address — sales@, support@, accounts@ — and if someone leaves, you keep the address and the customer history. With a personal Gmail address, everything is tied to one person, and losing access can mean losing your customer emails and invoices.

Deliverability is another reason. Emails from a custom domain are less likely to land in spam, especially with the authentication records covered below, and a corporate inbox in Lagos or Abuja is more likely to open a branded address than a generic one — particularly for a proposal worth hundreds of thousands of naira.

Finally, a professional email makes you look organised: an automatic signature carries your branding on every email, and filters can sort orders, invoices and bank alerts into folders automatically.

## Option 1: A Professional Free Gmail Address

If you are just starting out and you do not yet own a domain name, a well-chosen free Gmail address is a good first step. It costs nothing, takes ten minutes to set up, and is far better than using a personal address with a nickname.

Choose a name that is clear and easy to spell over the phone. Use your business name if it is available, such as joetechlagos@gmail.com or greenfarmsng@gmail.com. If that is taken, add a word like "ng", "lagos", "official" or "support" rather than random numbers. Avoid dots and underscores where you can, because they are easy to mishear. Say the address out loud as if you were giving it to a customer on a call in a noisy market — if it is hard to spell, pick a simpler one.

Here is how to create it:

1. Go to mail.google.com or open the Gmail app on your phone and tap "Add another account", then "Create account".
2. Choose "For my personal use" if this is a sole business, or "For work or my business" if Gmail offers that option on your screen. Either path creates a Gmail address; the wording changes from time to time.
3. Enter your first and last name — use your real name or your business name in the name fields, because this is what recipients see.
4. Choose your username carefully. This becomes your email address. Check the spelling twice before you continue.
5. Create a strong password with at least twelve characters, mixing letters, numbers and a symbol. Do not use your birthday, your phone number, or "password123".
6. Add a recovery phone number and a recovery email address. This is how Google helps you get back in if you forget your password. Use a number you control.
7. Turn on two-step verification as soon as you are signed in. Go to your Google Account, then Security, then "2-Step Verification", and follow the prompts. This is the single most important security step in this whole guide.

Once created, add your logo as your profile photo, fill in your signature under Settings with your name, business, phone, WhatsApp and website, turn on "Undo Send" for 30 seconds, and create labels for Orders, Invoices, Suppliers and Customers.

Use this address consistently. Put it on your flyers, your Instagram bio, your Google Business Profile and your invoices. Do not keep replying to business enquiries from your personal address, or customers will save the wrong one and you will lose track of orders.

## Option 2: A Custom Domain Email with Google Workspace

A custom domain email looks like info@yourbusiness.com.ng or hello@yourbusiness.ng. It is the professional standard, and it runs on Gmail through Google Workspace, Google's paid business version of Gmail. You keep the familiar Gmail inbox, but your address carries your own domain.

First, you need a domain name. If you do not own one yet, you can buy a .com.ng or .ng domain from a Nigerian registrar or an international one for roughly ₦10,000 to ₦25,000 per year, depending on the name and the registrar. A .com is often in a similar range. Choose a short name that matches your business, and buy it for at least two years if you can afford it, so you do not lose it by forgetting a renewal.

Google Workspace pricing changes with exchange rates, so check the current price in naira before you pay. As a rough guide, the basic Business Starter plan has commonly cost around ₦9,000 to ₦15,000 per user per month when billed in naira through a card, though the exact figure depends on Google's current pricing and the dollar rate. That is per user, so one inbox costs that amount, and adding a second staff member doubles it. If that is too much right now, the free Zoho Mail option in the next section is a sensible alternative, and you can move to Google Workspace later.

Here is how to set up Google Workspace:

1. Go to workspace.google.com and click "Get started". Enter your business name, the number of employees, and your country, Nigeria.
2. Enter your domain name when asked. If you already own the domain, choose "I have a domain". If not, Google will offer to help you buy one, but buying from a Nigerian registrar first usually gives you more control.
3. Create your first user. This is usually you, and it becomes your main address, such as info@yourdomain.com.ng. Choose a strong password and save it safely.
4. Verify that you own the domain. Google will ask you to add a small TXT record to your domain's DNS settings, or to upload a file to your website. This sounds technical, but your domain registrar's support team can do it for you in a few minutes if you send them Google's instructions.
5. Add the MX records Google gives you to your domain's DNS settings. MX records tell the internet where your email should be delivered. Until these are correct, emails to your new address will not arrive. Again, your registrar or your website developer can add them for you.
6. Wait for the records to take effect. This usually takes a few minutes to a few hours, and occasionally up to 24 hours. Do not panic if the first test email does not arrive immediately.
7. Sign in at mail.google.com with your new address, send a test email to your personal Gmail, and reply to it. Check that your signature appears and that the reply arrives.

Once it works, set up the extra records that improve deliverability: SPF, DKIM and DMARC. Google Workspace gives you the exact values to add in your DNS settings. SPF tells other servers which services are allowed to send email for your domain. DKIM adds a digital signature to your emails. DMARC tells receiving servers what to do with emails that fail those checks. You do not need to understand the cryptography — you just need to copy the records Google gives you into your DNS settings, and ask your registrar for help if you get stuck. Businesses that skip these records are more likely to have their proposals land in spam.

## Option 3: A Custom Domain Email with Zoho Mail for Free

If Google Workspace is too expensive for now, Zoho Mail offers a free plan that gives you a custom domain email without a monthly fee. Many Nigerian small businesses start here, including businesses that later move to Google Workspace when they can afford it.

Zoho's free Forever Free plan has historically allowed a small number of users on one domain, with webmail access and a mobile app. Check Zoho's current terms before you sign up, because free plans change. The setup is similar to Google Workspace: sign up at zoho.com/mail, enter your domain, verify ownership by adding a TXT record, add Zoho's MX records to your DNS, and create your user. The interface is different from Gmail, but the concepts are the same.

Zoho's free plan has limits, such as less storage and no IMAP or POP access on the free tier in some versions, which means you may need to use Zoho's own app or webmail rather than the Gmail app. For a one-person business that mainly sends quotes and replies to customers, that is usually fine. When you grow, you can pay for a Zoho plan or move to Google Workspace, and your domain and your email address stay the same — only the service behind it changes.

Whichever service you choose, set up a real mailbox — not a forwarding trick to your personal Gmail, which breaks when you reply from your personal address.

## Setting Up Your Business Email on Your Phone

Most Nigerian business owners read email on their phones, so set up your new address properly on the device you actually use. On Android, open the Gmail app, tap your profile photo, tap "Add another account", choose Google for a Gmail or Google Workspace address, and sign in. For a Zoho address, choose "Other" or download the Zoho Mail app from the Play Store and sign in there.

On iPhone, open Settings, then Mail, then Accounts, then "Add Account". Choose Google for Gmail or Google Workspace, and sign in. For Zoho, choose "Other" and enter the server settings Zoho gives you, or download the Zoho Mail app from the App Store.

Turn on notifications, but manage them. Set your email app to notify you for important emails, and check the rest twice a day — once in the morning and once before you close. Replying to a customer within an hour during business hours is a competitive advantage in Nigeria, where many businesses reply the next day. You do not need to be online all night. Set business hours in your signature or your auto-reply, and stick to them.

Keep your inbox tidy: archive emails you have dealt with, star orders waiting for payment, and check email twice a day — morning for new orders, evening to archive what is done.

## Writing Professional Emails That Get Replies

A professional address is only half the job. Use a clear subject line, such as "Quotation for 50 branded T-shirts — Joetech", greet the person by name, keep the first paragraph short, and use bullet points for prices and dates. End with a clear next step, such as "Please confirm by Friday so we can reserve your date." Send quotations as PDFs with clear file names, proofread the numbers before you send, and turn on Gmail's "Undo Send" for 30 seconds.

## Security: Protecting Your Business Email from Hackers and Scams

Business email is a target for scammers in Nigeria and abroad, because a hacked email account can be used to send fake invoices and steal payments. Protect yours from day one.

Turn on two-step verification. This is the single most effective step you can take. Even if someone learns your password, they cannot sign in without the code from your phone. Use an authenticator app such as Google Authenticator if you can, because it works even when your network is down. Keep backup codes in a safe place, not in your email inbox.

Use a strong, unique password for your email. Do not reuse the password you use for Facebook, Instagram or your bank app. If one service is breached, the others fall too. A password manager, even the free one built into your phone or browser, is better than writing passwords in a notebook or reusing the same one everywhere.

Never share your password or your verification code with anyone, including someone who claims to be from Google, Gmail, or your bank. Google will never ask you for your password or your code by email or phone. If you receive an email saying your account will be closed unless you click a link, do not click it. Go directly to mail.google.com or your Google Account page and check there.

Be careful with payment changes: if a supplier emails new account details, call them on a number you already have before sending money — scammers hack supplier inboxes and send fake details.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

The first mistake is using one address for everything. Do not use your business email to sign up for betting sites, random newsletters, or personal shopping. Every signup increases your spam and your risk. Keep a separate personal address for personal use, and keep your business inbox clean.

The second mistake is never checking the inbox. A professional address that nobody reads is worse than a personal address you actually answer. Customers who email you and hear nothing for three days will assume you are not serious, and they will buy from someone else. If you cannot check email daily, set an auto-reply that says when you will respond and gives your WhatsApp number for urgent enquiries.

The third mistake is sending large attachments without warning. Do not email a 25MB video to a corporate client and wonder why it bounced. Compress large files, or upload them to Google Drive and send a link with permission set to "Anyone with the link can view". Test the link yourself before you send it.

The fourth mistake is forgetting to renew your domain. If your domain expires, your custom email stops working, your website goes down, and someone else can buy your domain name. Set your domain to auto-renew, keep your card details up to date with your registrar, and set a calendar reminder a month before the renewal date.

The fifth mistake is letting a former staff member keep access. When someone leaves, change the password, remove their access, and forward their old address to you for a month so you do not miss customer emails. Do this on their last day, not a month later.

## Your Checklist: From Zero to Professional Email This Week

Day one: decide between a free professional Gmail address, Google Workspace, or Zoho Mail. If you own a domain, choose the custom domain option. If you do not, start with a professional free Gmail address and buy a domain when you can.

Day two: create the account, turn on two-step verification, add your logo, and write your signature with your name, business, phone and website.

Day three: add your new address to your Instagram bio, WhatsApp business profile, Google Business Profile, flyers and invoices. Send a test email to a friend and ask them to reply, so you can check that sending and receiving both work.

Day four: set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC if you use a custom domain, or ask your registrar or developer to do it. Send a test email to a corporate address and check that it lands in the inbox, not in spam.

Day five: write three email templates and save them as drafts or Gmail templates — one for a quotation, one for an order confirmation, and one for a follow-up on an unpaid invoice. Templates save you time and keep your wording consistent.

By the end of the week, you will have an email address you can put on a proposal to a bank, a hotel, or a government office without apologising for it — a small investment for a change that affects every customer interaction. Your product still has to be good, but when it is, a professional email makes sure the customer takes you seriously enough to find out.
