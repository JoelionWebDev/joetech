Walk into a busy market in Lagos, a shop in Abuja or a salon in Port Harcourt and you will hear the same question many times a day: "Can I transfer?" Customers in Nigeria now expect to pay by transfer, card or POS, and a business that only takes cash loses sales every day. People do not always carry cash. They carry a phone, and they want to pay quickly and leave with a confirmation on their screen.

Moniepoint has become one of the most common ways Nigerian small businesses accept those payments. It is known for fast transfers, a business account with an account number in your business name, a POS machine option, and a mobile app that shows payments as they arrive. For a small shop, a food vendor, a pharmacy, a salon or a trader, it offers a simple way to receive money without the queues and paperwork that used to come with a traditional bank account.

This guide explains how to accept payments with Moniepoint in your small business, step by step. You will learn how to open a business account, how to receive transfers, how to use the app and POS together, what charges to expect, how to handle failed or delayed payments, and how to keep clean daily records so your money is easy to track.

## What Moniepoint Offers a Small Business

Moniepoint is a financial service built for businesses in Nigeria. At its centre is a business account that comes with an account number, usually in your business name, that customers can transfer to from any Nigerian bank or fintech app. When a customer sends money to that number, you get a notification in the Moniepoint app, and the money appears in your business account.

The app lets you check your balance, see a list of transactions, send money to suppliers, pay bills, buy airtime and data, and transfer money to your other bank accounts. You can also create separate accounts or sub-accounts for different branches or purposes, which helps if you run more than one shop or want to keep savings apart from daily sales.

Moniepoint also offers POS machines for businesses that take card payments in person. A customer can tap or insert a card, enter a PIN, and the payment goes to your Moniepoint account. The POS works alongside transfers, so customers can choose the method that suits them. For businesses that sell online or on WhatsApp, Moniepoint offers payment links and transfer options that let a distant customer pay without coming to the shop.

One reason many traders prefer Moniepoint is speed. Transfers to a Moniepoint account often arrive quickly, and the app usually confirms them in seconds. That matters at a busy till. A customer who has paid wants to leave. A seller who can confirm payment in seconds keeps the queue moving and avoids arguments about whether money has arrived.

## Open Your Moniepoint Business Account

Opening a Moniepoint business account starts with the app. Download the Moniepoint Business app from the Google Play Store or the Apple App Store. Make sure you download the business version, not a personal wallet app, and check that the developer name matches Moniepoint. Avoid downloading the app from links sent by strangers on WhatsApp.

Open the app and choose to create a business account. You will be asked for your phone number and email address. Use a phone number you control and an email you check regularly, because Moniepoint will send verification codes and important notices there. Create a strong PIN and keep it private. Never share your PIN, password or one-time codes with anyone, including someone who claims to be Moniepoint staff. Real staff will never ask for them.

You will then enter your business details: your business name, the type of business, and your address. Use the name customers know you by. If your business is registered, use the registered name. If you trade under a personal name, use that name consistently. Inconsistent names cause problems later, especially when customers compare the account name on their transfer screen with the name on your shop sign.

Next comes identity verification. You will usually need a valid means of identification, such as a National Identity Number, voter card, driver licence or international passport, and a clear photo of yourself. Follow the instructions in the app to take the photo in good light, with your face fully visible and no cap or dark glasses. You may also be asked for a utility bill or other proof of address, and for business documents if your business is registered. Have these ready before you start, so the process does not stall halfway.

After you submit your details, Moniepoint reviews them. Approval times vary. Some accounts are approved quickly, while others take longer if a document is unclear or a detail does not match. If your application is delayed, check the app for a message explaining what is missing, correct it, and resubmit. Do not pay anyone who promises to speed up approval for a fee. That is a common scam pattern.

Once approved, you will receive your business account number. Test it with a small transfer from your own personal account, for example 100 or 500 naira, and confirm that it arrives and that the account name shown is correct. Only then should you print it on a banner, share it with customers or add it to your WhatsApp catalogue.

## How to Receive Transfer Payments Correctly

Transfers are the most common Moniepoint payment method for small businesses, so it pays to handle them properly. Display your account number clearly at your till, on your counter and on your WhatsApp profile or catalogue. Write the account name exactly as it appears, and the bank name as Moniepoint MFB. Customers copy what they see, so a faded sign or a wrong digit causes failed transfers and delays.

When a customer says they have transferred, ask to see the confirmation screen on their phone. Check three things: the amount, the account name, and the time. Then check your own app for the credit alert. Do not release goods based on a screenshot alone, because screenshots can be faked. Wait for your own notification or check your balance in the app. This takes seconds, and it protects you from fake transfer scams that target busy traders in Lagos markets and elsewhere.

Set a clear rule for your staff. Only the person at the till confirms payments. Helpers should not release goods on a customer's word alone. Write the rule down, explain it to every staff member, and repeat it during busy periods when the temptation to rush is strongest. One fake transfer can wipe out a day of profit.

For WhatsApp and Instagram orders, send your account details in a short message with the total amount, including any delivery charge. Ask the customer to send a screenshot of the confirmation and their delivery address in the same message. That keeps the order, the payment proof and the address together in one chat, which makes packing and delivery much easier. Keep a record of each order in a notebook or a simple spreadsheet, with the date, customer name, items, amount and payment status.

If a customer transfers the wrong amount, handle it calmly. If they overpaid, offer to refund the difference or to keep it as credit for their next purchase, and let them choose. If they underpaid, show them the balance and ask them to complete it before you release the goods. Stay polite and clear. A calm response keeps the customer, even when the payment went wrong.

## Using Moniepoint POS and Card Payments

A POS machine helps you serve customers who want to pay by card. If you already have a Moniepoint business account, you can request a POS through the app or through a Moniepoint agent. You will be asked about your business type, your location and your expected sales volume. Be honest. A POS that sits unused in a drawer helps nobody, while a POS in a busy shop earns its keep quickly.

When the machine arrives, learn how to use it before your first customer. Practise a sale with a small amount, check that the receipt prints or appears on screen, and learn how to reprint a receipt and check the last transaction. Teach every staff member who will use it. A POS that nobody knows how to operate is worse than no POS at all.

Keep the machine charged. POS machines run on battery, and a dead machine at the busiest hour of the day loses sales. Charge it overnight, and keep the charger in the shop, not at home. If your area has frequent power cuts, charge the machine whenever power is available, and consider a small power bank as a backup. Keep the machine in a safe place when the shop is closed, and do not leave it on the counter overnight.

Explain the process to customers. Tell them the amount before they insert or tap their card. Let them enter their own PIN, and never ask for their PIN or look at it while they type. If a card payment fails, check the message on the screen. A common message is a network problem. In that case, wait a moment and try again. If it fails twice, ask the customer to try a transfer instead, or to use another card. Do not keep retrying a failed card many times, because repeated attempts can confuse the customer and hold up the queue.

Reconcile POS sales at the end of each day. The app and the machine both keep records. Compare the total on the POS with the total in your app and with your cash sales for the day. If the numbers do not match, check the individual transactions before you close. It is much easier to find a missing payment on the same day than a week later.

## Charges, Settlement and Getting Your Money Out

Every payment service charges fees, and Moniepoint is no different. Charges can change, so check the current fees inside the app before you set your prices. As a guide, transfers into your Moniepoint account are often free or low cost, while POS transactions and transfers out may carry a small charge. Read the fee list in the app, and ask Moniepoint support if anything is unclear. Never rely on what another trader told you months ago, because fees change.

Settlement means the money becomes available in your account. Moniepoint transfers usually settle quickly, often within seconds or minutes. POS payments may settle the same day. Check your app to confirm that each payment has actually landed. Do not assume that a customer receipt means the money is in your account. The receipt shows the attempt. Your app shows the result.

You can move money out of your Moniepoint account to your personal bank account or to a supplier. Transfers out may attract a charge, so plan them. Instead of moving small amounts many times a day, move money once or twice a day, or keep a working balance in Moniepoint for daily supplier payments and move the rest to your main bank at the end of the day. That reduces charges and keeps your records simpler.

Keep a float for change and for refunds. A customer who pays by transfer and returns a faulty item will expect a refund. Having a small refund float in your Moniepoint account lets you refund quickly without a trip to the bank. Agree a refund rule in advance: what can be returned, within how many days, and whether the refund is cash, transfer or exchange. Write it down and display it where customers can see it.

## Keep Daily Records and Reconcile Every Evening

Good records turn Moniepoint from a payment tool into a business tool. At the end of each day, write down three figures: cash received, transfers received, and POS sales. Add them together for the day total. Compare that total with the money in your till and in your app. If they match, your day is clean. If they do not match, find the difference before you go home.

Use the app transaction list as your source of truth for electronic payments. Each entry shows the date, time, amount, sender name where available, and the type of payment. If a customer claims they paid yesterday, search the list for that date and amount. That settles most disputes in seconds.

Keep a simple sales book, even if you use the app. A paper book or a spreadsheet with date, item, amount, payment method and customer name is enough. It helps you see which products sell best, which days are busiest, and whether your business is growing. At the end of the month, add up the daily totals. That monthly figure tells you what you actually earned, not what you remember earning.

Separate business money from personal money. This is one of the hardest habits for small business owners, and one of the most important. Pay yourself a fixed amount each week or month, and leave the rest in the business account. When business and personal money mix in one account, it becomes impossible to know whether the business is making a profit. A Moniepoint business account helps here, because it keeps business receipts apart from your personal spending.

Back up your records. Take a photo of your sales book page each week, or export your transactions from the app where the option exists. Store the backup on your phone and in your email or cloud storage. If your phone is stolen or damaged, your records should not disappear with it.

## Handle Failed Payments, Disputes and Scams

Payments sometimes fail, and how you handle a failure shapes whether the customer returns. If a customer transfers money but you do not receive it, first check your app and your transaction list. Ask the customer to check their own bank app to confirm the transfer was successful and that the account number was correct. If the money left their account but did not reach yours, the problem is usually with the sending bank or the network. Ask the customer to contact their bank, and keep a note of the date, amount and reference number. Stay in touch with the customer and update them. A customer who feels ignored will not return.

If a POS payment fails but the customer is debited, tell the customer to keep the receipt or screenshot, and advise them to contact their bank if the money does not return. Debited but failed payments are usually reversed automatically, but the timing varies by bank. Do not refund from your own pocket until you have confirmed that you did not receive the money. Refunding a payment you never received means you lose twice.

Watch for scams. The most common scam is a fake transfer alert: a customer shows a text message that looks like a bank alert, but no money arrives. Always confirm in your own app. Another scam is a customer who claims to have paid twice and asks for a refund of the second payment, when only one payment was made. Check your transaction list before you refund anything. A third scam targets new POS owners: someone offers to help set up the machine and asks for your PIN or login details. Never share them. Moniepoint staff will not ask for your PIN.

If you suspect fraud, report it through the app or Moniepoint support channels, and keep screenshots and transaction details. Report quickly, because delays make it harder to trace the money. Also warn your staff, so the same trick does not work on them the next day.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need a registered business to open Moniepoint?
No. You can open an account as a sole trader with your personal ID, but keep your business name consistent. Registered businesses may be asked for extra documents.

### How fast do Moniepoint transfers arrive?
Transfers often arrive within seconds. Always confirm in your own app before releasing goods.

### Can I use Moniepoint without a POS machine?
Yes. Transfers to your business account number work without a POS. Add a POS later if many customers want to pay by card.
