Your website looks good. The colours match your brand. The logo is in the right place. You invested in design. So why are visitors not converting?

The answer is almost always in the user experience — the invisible layer that determines whether visitors find what they need quickly and easily, or get frustrated and leave. You can have the most beautiful website in Lagos, but if the user experience is broken, your conversion rate will suffer.

Here are the most common UI/UX mistakes we see on Nigerian business websites — and how to fix each one.

## Mistake 1: Unclear Value Proposition Above the Fold

The first thing visitors see when they land on your website should tell them exactly what you do and why they should care. Instead, most Nigerian business websites lead with a generic hero image and a vague tagline.

**Examples of weak value propositions:**
- "Welcome to Our Website"
- "Your Trusted Partner for Excellence"
- A generic stock photo with no text

**Examples of strong value propositions:**
- "Get Your Lagos Business Online in 2 Weeks — Website Packages from ₦350,000"
- "Professional Bridal Makeup in Lekki — Book Your Free Consultation"
- "Same-Day Delivery in Lagos — Order by 2 PM, Get It by 7 PM"

**The fix:** Within 3 seconds of landing on your homepage, a visitor should know: what you offer, who it is for, and what to do next. Write your value proposition in plain language. Test it by showing it to someone who does not know your business — can they explain what you do after 5 seconds?

## Mistake 2: No Clear Call to Action

Many Nigerian business websites treat their homepage like a brochure — lots of information about the company, but no clear direction on what the visitor should do next.

**Examples of weak CTAs:**
- "Click here"
- A non-clickable phone number
- No CTA at all

**Examples of strong CTAs:**
- "Book a Free Consultation"
- "Get Your Quote Today"
- "Browse Our Menu"
- "Shop Now — Free Delivery in Lagos"

**The fix:** Every page of your website should have a primary action you want visitors to take. That action should be stated clearly in a button that stands out visually. Use action-oriented language that tells the visitor exactly what will happen when they click.

## Mistake 3: Poor Mobile Experience

Over 70% of Nigerian web traffic comes from mobile devices. If your website is not optimized for mobile, you are alienating the majority of your potential customers.

**Common mobile issues:**
- Text too small to read without pinching
- Buttons too close together (users tap the wrong one)
- Horizontal scrolling required
- Images that do not resize properly
- Forms that are impossible to fill out on a phone
- Pop-ups that cover the entire screen and are hard to dismiss

**The fix:** Test your website on actual mobile devices (not just the desktop browser resize tool). Common phones in Nigeria — Tecno, Infinix, Samsung A-series, iPhone — should all display your site properly. Every element should be usable with a thumb on a 6-inch screen.

At Joetech, we design mobile-first — we build for the smallest screen first, then expand to larger screens. This ensures the mobile experience is never an afterthought.

## Mistake 4: Slow Load Times

We covered this in detail in our [website speed guide](/blog/website-speed-impact-sales), but it bears repeating in a UX context. Slow load times are the fastest way to destroy user experience. Every second of delay increases bounce rate and decreases conversions.

Mobile users in Nigeria are especially sensitive to speed because of network conditions. A site that loads in 2 seconds on fibre may take 8 seconds on 4G in a crowded area of Lagos.

**The fix:** Compress images, enable caching, use a CDN, minimize scripts, and upgrade hosting if needed. Aim for under 3 seconds on 4G.

## Mistake 5: Confusing Navigation

If visitors cannot find what they are looking for within 3 clicks, they will leave. Nigerian business websites often suffer from navigation that makes sense to the business owner but confuses customers.

**Common navigation problems:**
- Too many menu items (10+ options in the main nav)
- Unclear category names ("Resources" instead of "Blog" or "Guides")
- No search functionality
- Buried contact information
- Inconsistent navigation across pages

**The fix:** Limit your main navigation to 5-7 items. Use clear, descriptive labels. Put your most important pages first. Include a search bar for content-heavy sites. Ensure your contact information is visible from every page — preferably in the header or a sticky bar.

## Mistake 6: Missing or Weak Trust Signals

Nigerian consumers are understandably cautious about online transactions. If your website does not inspire trust, they will not convert.

**Essential trust signals:**
- Professional design (first impression of credibility)
- Clear contact information (address, phone number, email)
- About page with real people and real stories
- Testimonials from real customers (with names and photos if possible)
- Case studies or portfolio work
- Social proof (number of customers served, years in business)
- Secure checkout indicators (padlock icon, payment logos)
- Privacy policy and terms of service

**The fix:** Audit your website for trust signals. Visit it as if you were a potential customer seeing it for the first time. Would you trust this business with your money? If not, add the missing trust elements.

## Mistake 7: Complicated Forms

Forms are where many conversions die. A contact form should be simple and fast. Instead, many Nigerian business websites have forms that ask for too much information.

**Bad form practices:**
- 10+ required fields
- Asking for information you do not need (why do you need their fax number?)
- No clear indication of what happens after submission
- No confirmation message or email
- Forms that do not work on mobile
- No autofill support
- No error messages that actually help

**The fix:** Only ask for essential information. For a contact form, name, email, phone, and message are usually enough. Show a clear success message after submission. Send a confirmation email. Test your forms regularly to ensure they work.

## Mistake 8: Poor Readability

Your content might be excellent, but if it is hard to read, nobody will read it.

**Common readability problems:**
- Light grey text on white background (low contrast)
- Font size under 16px for body text
- Line height too tight
- Paragraphs that go on forever without breaks
- No subheadings, bullet points, or visual breaks
- Justified text that creates uneven spacing

**The fix:** Body text should be at least 16px on mobile and 18px on desktop. Use high contrast (dark text on light background). Break up long paragraphs. Use subheadings every 200-300 words. Use bullet points for lists. Keep line length to 50-75 characters.

## Mistake 9: No Mobile Click-to-Call

Many Nigerian customers prefer to call rather than fill out a form. If your phone number is not clickable on mobile, you are making it harder for customers to reach you.

**The fix:** Make your phone number a clickable link using the tel: protocol. On mobile, tapping the number should open the phone dialer with your number pre-filled. This seems obvious, but many websites still miss it.

## Mistake 10: Auto-Playing Videos and Audio

Auto-playing videos with sound are one of the most disliked UX elements on the web. They are especially problematic on mobile, where they consume data and interrupt the user's experience.

**The fix:** Never auto-play videos with sound. If you have a video on your homepage, let users choose to play it. If you must auto-play (for a hero video, for example), keep it muted and add captions.

## Mistake 11: Too Many Choices (Hick's Law)

Hick's Law states that the time it takes to make a decision increases with the number and complexity of choices. When visitors land on your homepage and see 10 different options, they may choose none and leave.

**The fix:** Reduce cognitive load. Prioritize one primary action per page. Remove unnecessary elements. Use progressive disclosure — show the most important information first, and let users click through for details if they want them.

## Mistake 12: Ignoring the "What's in It for Me?" Question

Visitors do not care about your company history or your awards (at least not initially). They care about what you can do for them. Many Nigerian business websites lead with "About Us" information instead of customer benefits.

**The fix:** Reframe your content from customer-centric perspective. Instead of "We are a leading web development agency with 5 years of experience," try "Get a professional website for your Lagos business in 2 weeks — starting from ₦350,000."

## How to Audit Your Own Website's UX

Here is a simple UX audit you can do yourself:

1. **The 5-Second Test** — Show your homepage to someone who has never seen it. Give them 5 seconds. Ask them: What does this business do? Can you find their phone number? What should you click next?
2. **The Click Test** — Ask someone to complete a specific task (find a product, find your address, fill out the contact form). Watch them without helping. Where do they hesitate? Where do they make mistakes?
3. **The Mobile Test** — Perform the same tasks on a phone. Is it easy? Are buttons tappable? Is text readable without zooming?
4. **The Form Test** — Fill out every form on your website yourself. Does it work? Is the confirmation clear? Do you receive the notification?
5. **The Speed Test** — Run your site through Google PageSpeed Insights. If your mobile score is under 70, speed is hurting your UX.
