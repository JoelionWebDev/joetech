Your app is live on the App Store and Google Play. Users are downloading it. Congratulations. Now the real work begins.

Many first-time app founders believe that launch day is the finish line. The reality is that launch day is the starting line. An app requires ongoing maintenance for as long as it is live. Neglecting maintenance leads to security vulnerabilities, compatibility issues, poor performance, and eventually, users abandoning your app.

This guide explains what app maintenance involves, why it matters, and how to budget for it.

## Why Apps Need Ongoing Maintenance

Operating system updates happen constantly. Apple and Google release major OS updates annually and minor updates every few months. Each update can introduce changes that affect your app. A feature that worked perfectly on iOS 17 may break on iOS 18. Your app needs to be updated to remain compatible.

Device hardware evolves. New phone models with different screen sizes, camera configurations, and sensors are released regularly. Your app needs to support these new devices to reach the widest possible audience.

Bugs are discovered after launch. No amount of testing catches every bug. Users will encounter issues in real-world conditions that your testing team missed — edge cases, network conditions, device configurations, and usage patterns you did not anticipate. Regular updates fix these bugs.

Security vulnerabilities emerge. New security threats are discovered regularly. Your app's dependencies may have vulnerabilities that need patching. User data protection requires ongoing attention.

Third-party APIs change. Payment gateways, mapping services, and other third-party services update their APIs over time. When an API your app depends on changes, your app must be updated to match.

User feedback drives improvements. Your early users will request features, suggest improvements, and highlight pain points. Responding to this feedback keeps users engaged and improves your app's ratings.

## Types of Maintenance

Bug fixes address issues discovered after launch. Some bugs are critical — they crash the app or block core functionality. Others are minor — visual glitches or edge cases. Prioritize fixes based on impact.

Compatibility updates ensure your app works with new OS versions and devices. These are typically required annually when Apple and Google release major OS updates.

Performance optimization improves app speed, reduces crashes, and optimizes battery usage. As your user base grows, performance issues that were not noticeable with small numbers of users become apparent.

Security updates patch vulnerabilities. When a security issue is discovered in your code or in a third-party library, an update must be released promptly. Security updates are non-negotiable.

Feature updates add new functionality based on user feedback and business needs. These updates keep your app competitive and engaging.

Server maintenance includes monitoring server performance, scaling infrastructure to handle growing traffic, applying security patches to servers, and managing database performance and backups.

## How Much Maintenance Costs

A common rule of thumb is to budget 15-20% of the initial development cost per year for maintenance. If your app cost ₦5,000,000 to build, budget ₦750,000 — ₦1,000,000 per year for ongoing maintenance.

This covers critical bug fixes and compatibility updates, server hosting and infrastructure costs, third-party API usage fees (payment gateways, SMS, email, mapping), security monitoring and updates, and minor feature updates.

Major feature additions are separate projects with their own budgets. The maintenance budget covers keeping your app running, not building significant new functionality.

## What Happens Without Maintenance

Without regular maintenance, your app becomes incompatible with new OS versions. Users on the latest iOS cannot install or use your app. Reviews drop and users leave.

Security vulnerabilities go unpatched. If a vulnerability is discovered and exploited, user data may be breached. The reputation damage can destroy your app's future.

Performance degrades over time. The app becomes slower, crashes more frequently, and drains more battery. Users uninstall and leave negative reviews.

Your app gets outdated. Competitors release new features and better designs while your app stagnates. Users switch to alternatives that feel more modern and capable.

## Planning for Maintenance

Include maintenance in your budget from the start. Many founders spend their entire budget on development and have nothing left for maintenance. Plan for the full lifecycle.

Build a relationship with your development team for ongoing support. A team that knows your codebase can fix issues faster and more reliably than a new team. Negotiate a maintenance retainer or hourly rate before the project ends.

Set up monitoring and analytics from launch. Track crash reports, user behaviour, and performance metrics. Data-driven maintenance decisions are more effective than reactive ones.

Prioritize maintenance tasks. Not all issues need immediate attention. Create a triage system: critical issues (app crashes, security vulnerabilities) fixed within 24-48 hours, important issues (performance degradation, broken features) fixed within 1-2 weeks, and minor issues (visual polish, non-critical bugs) fixed within the next release cycle.
