A flyer is often the first thing a Nigerian customer sees before deciding whether your business is worth their money. It appears on a WhatsApp status in Lekki, in a Facebook group in Abuja, on an Instagram story in Port Harcourt, or taped to a shop window in Ikeja. In those few seconds, the customer decides: does this look like a serious business, or does it look like a scam?

That decision matters more in Nigeria, where customers have been burned by fake vendors, failed deliveries and businesses that disappear after payment. A clean, professional flyer builds trust before you say a word. Canva makes that level of design possible without hiring a designer, and the free version is enough for most small businesses.

This guide shows you exactly how to use Canva to design business flyers that sell in Nigeria. You will learn which size to pick, how to choose colours and fonts that Nigerians respond to, what to write, what to leave out, how to add your prices in naira, how to make one flyer work on WhatsApp, Instagram and print, and the common mistakes that kill sales.

## Why Your Flyer Matters More Than Your Product Photo

Many Nigerian sellers post a plain photo of their product with a caption like "Available, DM for price" and wonder why nobody orders. The problem is not the product. The problem is that the customer has no reason to trust you, no clear price, and no simple next step.

A good flyer solves all three problems in one image. It shows the product clearly, states the price or the starting price, gives one clear way to order, and looks organised enough that the customer believes you will deliver. When a customer in Surulere sees ten similar products on their timeline, the flyer that looks most professional usually gets the message first.

Consider two food vendors in Lagos. One posts a blurry photo under yellow light, with no price. The other posts a bright flyer with a clear photo, "Jollof Rice + Chicken — ₦4,500", a delivery note, and a WhatsApp number. The second vendor will get more orders even if the first cooks better food. Customers cannot taste food from a photo.

Your flyer is also your cheapest salesperson. It works while you sleep. It gets forwarded in WhatsApp groups, saved on phones, and shared by customers who like your work. A single strong flyer for a Christmas promo, a new stock arrival, or a weekend discount can bring in orders for days. A weak flyer gets scrolled past in under a second.

## Step 1: Choose the Right Flyer Size Before You Design Anything

The most common beginner mistake is designing in the wrong size, then watching Canva crop your text when you share it. Decide where the flyer will live first, then pick the size to match.

For WhatsApp status and Instagram stories, use 1080 by 1920 pixels. This is the vertical phone-screen size. It fills the whole screen, your text stays readable, and nothing gets cut off at the edges. In Canva, search for "Instagram Story" or create a custom size of 1080 by 1920 pixels. This is the size most Nigerian businesses should start with, because WhatsApp is where most of their customers are.

For Instagram feed posts and Facebook posts, use 1080 by 1080 pixels, the square format. Square posts sit well in the feed, they are easy to forward, and they print reasonably well at small sizes. Search for "Instagram Post" in Canva to get this size instantly.

For printed flyers to share in your shop, church, estate or market, use A5, which is 148 by 210 millimetres, or A4 if you need more space. A5 is the sweet spot for handouts in Lagos: big enough to read, small enough to carry, and cheap to print. In Canva, search for "A5" or "Flyer" and pick the A5 document size. Always download print files as PDF Print, not JPG, so the printer gets sharp quality.

A practical rule: design once in the vertical 1080 by 1920 size for WhatsApp and stories, then duplicate the design in Canva and resize it to square for Instagram and Facebook. Canva Pro has a one-click Magic Resize tool, but free users can duplicate the design and adjust the layout manually in a few minutes. Do not try to stretch one size into another. Text will distort and your flyer will look careless.

## Step 2: Set Up Your Brand Kit, Even on the Free Plan

Consistency is what makes a small business look like a big brand. If your flyers use a different colour and font every week, customers will not recognise you when they scroll past. If your colours stay the same, they start to recognise your posts before they even read your name.

Canva Pro has a built-in Brand Kit where you save your logo, colours and fonts. If you pay for Pro, set it up on day one. Add your logo, pick two main colours and one accent colour, and choose one heading font and one body font. Every flyer after that takes half the time because you are not choosing colours from scratch.

Free users can get the same result with a simple system. Create a folder in Canva called "My Brand" and save one master flyer template inside it. Each time you need a new flyer, duplicate that template instead of starting from a blank page. Keep a note on your phone with your exact colour codes. For example, write down "Blue #0B5FFF, Yellow #FFC107, Dark text #111111" and reuse those codes every time.

Choose colours that fit your business and stand out on a phone screen. Food businesses do well with warm colours like orange, red and yellow, because they suggest appetite and energy. Fashion and beauty brands often use black, gold, pink or deep purple for a premium feel. Tech, logistics and finance businesses suit blue, navy and teal, because those colours suggest trust. Whatever you pick, use high contrast: dark text on a light background, or white text on a dark background. Light grey text on a white background may look soft on your laptop, but it will be unreadable on a phone in bright Lagos sunlight.

Pick at most two fonts per flyer. Use one bold font for the headline and one clean, simple font for the smaller details. Good free pairings in Canva include Montserrat Bold for headlines with Open Sans for body text, Poppins Bold with Lato, or Bebas Neue for a big sale headline with Montserrat for the details. Avoid script fonts for prices and phone numbers. A customer should never have to squint to read how much something costs or how to reach you.

## Step 3: Pick a Template, Then Make It Yours

Canva has thousands of flyer templates. Templates are a starting point, not a finished design. The fastest way to design well is to pick a template with a strong layout, then replace everything that makes it generic.

Start by searching Canva for your business type plus "flyer". Try "food flyer", "fashion sale flyer", "real estate flyer", "salon flyer" or "church flyer". Filter for the size you need. Choose a template where the headline is big, the main image is large, and there is clear space for a price and a contact detail. Ignore templates that are crowded with decorative shapes, because you will need that space for your own information.

Once you pick a template, change the colours to your brand colours, swap in your own product photo, replace the headline with your own words, and delete any element that does not serve your goal. Many templates include fake phone numbers, fake addresses and placeholder text like "Lorem ipsum". Leaving any of that in your flyer is one of the fastest ways to lose trust. A customer who sees a London address on a Lagos business flyer will assume you copied it from the internet.

Replace stock photos with your own photos wherever possible. Customers in Nigeria want to see the actual product they will receive, not a perfect studio photo of something similar. Take your product photos in daylight, near a window or outside, on a clean background. Wipe the camera lens, hold the phone steady, and take several shots so you can pick the sharpest one. Upload the best photo to Canva and place it as the largest element on the flyer. If you must use a Canva stock photo, choose one that closely matches what you actually sell, and never use a stock photo of a product you do not stock.

## Step 4: Write a Headline That Stops the Scroll

Your headline is the most important line on the flyer. Most people read only the headline before deciding to keep looking or scroll past. A good headline promises a clear benefit, names the product, or states an offer the customer can understand in three seconds.

Weak headlines say nothing specific. "Welcome to our store", "Quality products available", "Best deals in town" and "We sell all kinds of items" tell the customer nothing. They could belong to any business. Strong headlines are specific: "Fresh Catfish Pepper Soup — Delivered Hot in Lekki", "Ready-to-Wear Gowns from ₦18,000", "2-Bedroom Flats in Ajah from ₦2.5 Million Per Year", "Braids, Wigs and Frontal Installation — This Weekend Only".

Use numbers and prices in headlines when you can, because numbers catch the eye. "50% Off Selected Wigs This Weekend" is stronger than "Huge Discount on Wigs". "Buy 2 Get 1 Free" is stronger than "Special Offer Available". "Free Delivery in Ikeja Today" is stronger than "We Deliver Anywhere".

Keep the headline short: five to nine words is ideal. Make it the largest text on the flyer, in bold, with strong contrast. Place it at the top or in the centre, where the eye lands first. Do not put your business name as the headline unless your name is already famous. Customers care about what they get first; your business name belongs in a smaller line at the top or bottom.

Below the headline, add one supporting line that answers the customer's next question. If the headline promises a price, the supporting line can explain what is included. If the headline promises a service, the supporting line can name the location or the delivery area. For example: headline "Home Cleaning from ₦25,000", supporting line "Lekki, Ajah and Victoria Island — Book Today, We Come Tomorrow". That pair gives the customer enough information to decide in seconds.

## Step 5: Add Prices, Location and One Clear Way to Order

Nigerian customers hate the phrase "DM for price". Many will not message you at all if the price is hidden, because they assume it will be expensive, or they have been embarrassed before by a price far above their budget. Putting the price on the flyer filters out people who cannot afford it and attracts people who are ready to buy. It also saves you hours of answering the same question in your inbox.

Show the price clearly, in naira, with the ₦ symbol. Write "₦15,000" rather than "15k", because "15k" is informal and can be misread. If you sell sizes or packages, show the starting price and the range: "From ₦12,000" or "₦12,000 – ₦25,000 depending on size". If you offer a discount, show the old price crossed out next to the new price: "₦30,000 ₦22,000". That contrast makes the deal feel real.

Add your location or delivery area in one short line. "Based in Lekki, deliver nationwide" is enough. "Available in Abuja and Lagos" is enough. Customers need to know whether you can serve them before they message you. If you deliver nationwide, say so. If you only deliver in Lagos, say so clearly, so you do not waste time on orders you cannot fulfil.

Give one clear call to action, not three. "Order on WhatsApp: 0803 123 4567" is a clear instruction. "Call or WhatsApp 0803 123 4567 to order" is also clear. What does not work is listing your WhatsApp number, Instagram handle, Facebook page, email address and shop address all in the same small space. The customer will read none of them properly. Pick the channel where you actually reply fastest — for most Nigerian small businesses, that is WhatsApp — and make that number big and easy to read.

Make your phone number easy to copy and dial. Use spaces to break it into groups, like 0803 123 4567, rather than a long unbroken string. If you use a WhatsApp link, shorten it and test it yourself before posting. Click the link from another phone, or ask a friend to click it, and confirm it opens a chat with your business. A broken link on a flyer that has already been shared in ten groups is a lost weekend of sales.


## Mistakes That Make Even a Good Flyer Fail

The first mistake is crowding. Beginners try to fit the full price list, ten products, three phone numbers and a long testimony on one flyer. The result is unreadable. One flyer should sell one offer. If you have five products, make five flyers, or make one flyer for your best seller and let customers ask about the rest.

The second mistake is low-quality images. A blurry photo, a screenshot of another screenshot, or a photo with a busy background makes your business look careless. Always use the original photo, not a version downloaded from WhatsApp, because WhatsApp compresses images. Keep your originals in a folder on your phone or Google Drive.

The third mistake is spelling errors and wrong prices. Before you post, read the flyer out loud, check every digit in the phone number, and confirm the price matches what you will actually charge. Ask one other person to check it. A wrong number on a flyer shared in many groups is difficult to correct.

The fourth mistake is forgetting the expiry date on an offer. If a discount ends on Sunday, write "Offer ends Sunday, 30 November" on the flyer. Without a date, customers will message you weeks later expecting the same price, and you will either lose money or disappoint them.

## How to Print Flyers in Lagos Without Wasting Money

Start small. Print 50 to 100 copies of an A5 flyer first and see how they perform. In many Lagos print shops, 100 copies of a full-colour A5 flyer cost roughly ₦25,000 to ₦45,000 depending on paper quality, while a single-sided A4 poster costs more. Ask for a sample print before you pay for the full run, and check the colours under normal light. Colours on screen are often brighter than colours on paper.

Share printed flyers where your customers already gather: your shop counter, estate gates with permission, church notice boards, salons, gyms and offices. A printed flyer works best when it points the customer back to WhatsApp, so always include the number.

## A Simple Weekly Flyer Routine That Brings Orders

You do not need a new design every day. Pick one day a week to make your flyers. On Monday, decide the offer for the week: a new arrival, a discount, or a best seller. Design the vertical version first, duplicate it for Instagram, and export both as JPG for social media and PDF for print if needed. Post the vertical version on WhatsApp status in the morning and evening, when people check their phones before work and after work. Share the square version on Instagram and Facebook, and send it to your broadcast list and loyal customers.

## Your Flyer Checklist Before You Post

Run through this list every time: is the headline specific and easy to read in three seconds, is the price shown in naira, is there one clear way to order, is the phone number correct, is your business name and location included, is the main photo your own and sharp, and is there an expiry date if it is an offer? If you can answer yes to all seven, post it. If not, fix the weak point before you share it.

Canva will not sell your product for you, but it removes the excuse that good design is too expensive or too difficult. With the right size, your own colours and fonts, a specific headline, a clear price and one way to order, a flyer made on a phone in Lekki can compete with a flyer made by an agency. Start with one offer, design it well, post it where your customers already are, and let the orders tell you what to design next.
