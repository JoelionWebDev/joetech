You do not need a website to sell online in Nigeria. Thousands of Nigerian businesses — fashion sellers in Lekki, food vendors in Surulere, phone accessory traders in Computer Village, skincare brands in Abuja — make their money every day without owning a website at all. They sell on WhatsApp, on Instagram, on Jumia and Konga, and in Facebook groups. Their customers find them, pay them, and receive their goods, all without a single web page.

A website is useful, and you should probably get one as your business grows. But it is not the first step, and waiting until you can afford one is a mistake. The first step is to sell where your customers already are, with the tools you already have: your phone, your WhatsApp, and your social media accounts. This guide shows you how to do that properly — how to set up each channel, how to price and present your products, how to take payments safely, how to handle delivery, and how to grow from a few sales a week to a steady online business.

## Start with WhatsApp Business

WhatsApp is the most important selling tool in Nigeria. Almost every Nigerian with a smartphone uses it daily, and many customers prefer to order on WhatsApp than on a website, because they can ask questions, negotiate, and talk to a real person before they pay. WhatsApp Business is the free app that turns your WhatsApp into a shop, and it is where your online selling should start.

Set up your profile properly. Your business name should be clear and searchable. Your profile photo should be your logo or a clear photo of your best product. Your description should say what you sell, where you are based, and how to order, in one or two lines. Add your address, your business hours, your email, and your website if you have one. A complete profile makes you look real, and real is what customers pay for.

Build your catalogue. This is the heart of selling on WhatsApp Business. Add every product you sell, with a clear photo, a name, a price, and a short description. Include the sizes, colours, or variations available. Keep the prices up to date. When a customer asks what you have, you can send them the catalogue link instead of typing a long list, and they can browse and choose at their own pace. A catalogue with good photos sells more than a catalogue with blurry, dark photos, so take your product photos in daylight, against a clean background, with the product filling the frame.

Use labels to manage your orders. Create labels such as New Enquiry, Order Placed, Paid, Packaged, Delivered, and Follow Up. Label every chat as it moves through the process, so you always know who has paid, who is waiting for delivery, and who needs a follow-up message. When you have fifty chats a day, labels are the difference between a business and a mess.

Set up quick replies for the messages you send most often: your price list, your delivery fees, your bank details, your location, and your delivery times. Set a greeting message for new customers and an away message for when you are closed. Post your products on your WhatsApp status every day — new arrivals, restocks, discounts, and customer reviews. Your status is free advertising to everyone who has your number, and it is seen by people who already know you, which makes them more likely to buy.

Share your WhatsApp link everywhere. WhatsApp gives you a short link in the form wa.me followed by your number. Put it in your Instagram bio, your Facebook page, your flyers, and your email signature. You can also print it as a QR code on your flyers and packaging so customers can scan it with their phone camera and start chatting with you in seconds. ## Sell on Instagram

Instagram is where Nigerians discover products. A customer who has never heard of you can find your page through a hashtag, a reel, or a friend's share, and decide to buy within minutes. To sell on Instagram, you need a business or creator account, which is free to set up in your profile settings. A business account gives you insights about your followers, a contact button, and the ability to run adverts.

Your page is your shop window, so treat it like one. Your bio should say what you sell, where you deliver to, and how to order, with your WhatsApp link as the link in bio. Your profile photo should be your logo. Your highlights should include your price list, your delivery information, your reviews, and your most popular products. A new visitor should understand your business in five seconds.

Post consistently, at least three to five times a week. Mix your content: product photos, short videos of the product in use, behind-the-scenes clips of you packing an order, customer reviews, and simple tips related to your products. Reels get the most reach on Instagram, and a short video of a dress being worn, a meal being plated, or a product being unboxed will reach far more people than a still photo. You do not need a professional camera — a clean phone camera, good light, and a steady hand are enough.

Use hashtags that your customers actually search for, such as #LagosVendor, #AbujaFashion, #MadeInNigeria, and hashtags for your specific product. Use your location tag on every post, because many customers search for sellers in their own city. Reply to every comment and every direct message quickly — Instagram rewards accounts that respond fast, and customers buy from sellers who answer.

Run small adverts when you are ready. Instagram adverts can start from as little as ₦1,000 to ₦2,000 a day, and you can target people in Lagos, Abuja, or any city, by age and interests. Start with a small budget, test one product, and see what happens before you spend more. A good advert is a clear photo or video, a simple message, and a call to action that sends people to your WhatsApp.

## Sell on Facebook and in Facebook Groups

Facebook still matters in Nigeria, especially for customers over thirty, for community buying, and for people outside Lagos and Abuja. Create a Facebook Page for your business, separate from your personal profile. Fill in your about section, your contact details, and your WhatsApp number, and post your products regularly, just as you do on Instagram.

Facebook Groups are a powerful and underused selling channel. There are groups for buying and selling in almost every Nigerian city, for mums, for students, for specific estates, and for specific products. Join the groups where your customers are, read the rules before you post, and share your products there a few times a week. Do not spam. Post good photos, clear prices, and your WhatsApp number, and answer questions politely. A single good post in a large, active group can bring you dozens of orders.

Facebook Marketplace is another free channel. List your products there with clear photos and honest prices, and buyers in your area will find you. It works especially well for phones, electronics, furniture, fashion, and household items. Respond quickly to enquiries, because Marketplace buyers message several sellers at once and buy from the one who replies first.

## Sell on Jumia and Konga

Marketplaces like Jumia and Konga bring you customers you could never reach on your own. Millions of Nigerians shop on these platforms, and they trust them because of buyer protection, reviews, and reliable delivery. Selling on them costs you a commission on each sale, but it also gives you visibility, credibility, and access to customers across the country.

To sell on Jumia, go to the Jumia seller centre, create an account, and provide your business details, your identification, and your bank account. You will need to list your products with photos, descriptions, and prices, and Jumia will review your listings before they go live. You will also need to keep your stock up to date and ship orders quickly, because Jumia tracks your performance and penalises sellers who cancel orders or ship late.

Konga works in a similar way. Create a seller account, list your products, and fulfil orders through Konga's logistics or your own delivery. Konga also offers seller training and support, which is useful if you are new to marketplaces.

Marketplaces work best for products that are easy to ship and easy to describe: fashion, electronics, beauty products, home goods, and packaged food. They work less well for fresh food, custom-made items, or products that need a lot of explanation before a customer buys. Use them as one channel among several, not as your only channel, because the commission and the rules are outside your control. Keep your own WhatsApp and Instagram customers too, so that your business does not depend on any single platform.

## Other Places Nigerians Buy and Sell

Do not ignore the smaller channels. TikTok is growing fast in Nigeria, and short videos of your products can reach thousands of people for free. Post the same videos you make for Instagram Reels on TikTok, and add your WhatsApp link to your bio. Twitter, now called X, works well for some products, especially tech, fashion, and food, and it is a good place to build a personal brand around your business.

Classified sites such as Jiji are popular for used goods, electronics, cars, and property, and they are free to list on. If you sell second-hand items, refurbished phones, or furniture, Jiji is worth your time. Nairaland, Nigeria's largest forum, has buy-and-sell sections where traders have sold for years, although you should read the rules carefully and build a reputation before you post.

Offline still counts. A stall in a market, a table at a trade fair, or a pop-up at an event can bring you customers who then follow you online and keep ordering on WhatsApp. The best online sellers in Nigeria are often the ones who also show up in person.

## Take Payments Safely

Payment is where many new online sellers lose money, so get it right from the start. The most common payment method in Nigeria is a bank transfer to your business account. Always give your customers your business account details, not your personal account, and always confirm payment before you ship. Ask the customer to send a screenshot of the transfer, and check your own bank app or SMS alert to confirm the money arrived before you send the goods.

Payment links make this easier. Paystack and Flutterwave both let you create a payment link for a product, which you can send to a customer on WhatsApp. The customer pays with a card, a transfer, or a USSD code, and you receive a confirmation. Payment links reduce the back-and-forth of sharing account numbers, and they look professional. Both services charge a small fee per transaction, usually around 1.5 percent, which is worth it for the convenience and the records they give you.

Cash on delivery is popular with customers, but it is risky for sellers. You pay for delivery, and the customer may refuse the goods or not be available. If you offer cash on delivery, charge a delivery fee upfront, or use a dispatch service that collects payment for you. For high-value items, always take a deposit or full payment before you ship.

Watch out for fake payment alerts. Scammers send fake bank alerts and fake transfer screenshots to trick sellers into releasing goods before payment arrives. Never release goods based on a screenshot alone. Always check your own bank balance or your bank's SMS alert. If a customer pressures you to send goods quickly because they "already paid", slow down and verify. A genuine customer will wait for you to confirm.

## Handle Delivery Like a Professional

Delivery can make or break an online business in Nigeria. Customers remember a late or damaged delivery far longer than they remember a good product. Set clear delivery expectations: tell the customer the fee and the delivery time before they pay, and keep your promise.

For deliveries within your city, use a dispatch rider. In Lagos, a bike delivery costs roughly ₦1,000 to ₦3,000 depending on distance, and it usually arrives within a few hours. Build a relationship with two or three reliable riders in your area, agree on prices in advance, and give them clear addresses and phone numbers for each delivery. If you deliver yourself for nearby orders, plan your route and communicate your arrival time to the customer.

For deliveries to other states, use a courier or a logistics company. Services such as GIG Logistics, DHL, and several local couriers deliver across Nigeria in one to three days. Compare prices, and choose based on reliability, not just the cheapest option. Pack your goods properly: use bubble wrap for fragile items, sturdy boxes, and tape that holds. A product that arrives broken is a refund, a bad review, and a lost customer.

Consider offering free delivery above a certain order value, such as free delivery for orders over ₦50,000 in Lagos. It encourages customers to buy more, and the extra sales often cover the delivery cost. Be clear about which areas are covered and what the delivery time is, so there are no surprises.

## Build Trust Without a Website

A website builds trust, but it is not the only way. Without one, you need to work harder to prove you are real and reliable. Post customer reviews and testimonials on your WhatsApp status and Instagram regularly, with the customer's permission. Share photos and videos of orders being packed and delivered. Show your face occasionally, so customers know there is a real person behind the business. Answer questions honestly, including about what you do not have in stock. A seller who says "that size is sold out, it will be back on Friday" is trusted far more than one who promises everything and delivers nothing.

Keep your promises. If you say an order will arrive on Tuesday, make sure it does. If there is a delay, tell the customer before they ask. If a product is faulty, replace it or refund it quickly. A single honest refund can turn an angry customer into a loyal one, while a refused refund can turn one customer into a dozen lost ones through bad reviews.

## Grow from a Few Sales to a Steady Business

Once you are selling regularly, start thinking about growth. Keep a record of every sale: what sold, who bought it, how much you earned, and what it cost you. At the end of each week, look at the numbers. Which products sell best? Which channel brings the most orders? Where do your customers live? The answers tell you where to focus.

Reinvest your profits. Buy more stock of your best sellers, improve your product photos, print better packaging, and save towards a website. A simple website, when you are ready, will let customers browse and order without messaging you, and it will make your business look more established. Until then, your WhatsApp catalogue and your Instagram page are your shop, and they are enough.

Collect your customers' contact details, with their permission, and keep in touch. Send a message when new stock arrives, when you have a discount, or during festive seasons. A customer who bought once is far more likely to buy again than a stranger is to buy for the first time, so your existing customers are your most valuable asset. Treat them well, and they will bring their friends.

Start small, sell where your customers already are, and let your reputation do the work. You do not need a website to start selling online in Nigeria. You need good products, clear prices, honest service, and the discipline to reply, deliver, and follow up. The website can come later. The sales can start today.
