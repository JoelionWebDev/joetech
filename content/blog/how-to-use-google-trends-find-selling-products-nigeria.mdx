Every trader has stocked a product that refused to sell. The goods sit on the shelf, the money sits in them, and each week they gather dust while the fast-selling items run out. The painful part is that the mistake was avoidable. Customers were already telling the market what they wanted, through the searches they typed into Google every day. Google Trends is a free tool that lets you listen to those searches before you spend money on stock.

Google Trends shows how often people search for a word or topic, how interest changes over time, and where in Nigeria that interest is strongest. It does not tell you exact sales figures, and it does not guarantee that a product will sell in your shop. But it does show you direction: whether interest is rising or falling, whether a product is seasonal, and whether people in Lagos search for it differently from people in Kano or Port Harcourt. For a small business owner choosing what to stock, that direction is valuable.

This guide explains how to use Google Trends to find products Nigerians want to buy. You will learn how to open the tool, how to read its charts, how to compare products, how to spot seasons and regions, and how to combine what you find with what you already know about your own customers.

## What Google Trends Is and What It Is Not

Google Trends is a free website from Google, at trends.google.com. You do not need to pay, and you do not need a special account to use the basic features. You type a word or phrase, such as "solar inverter" or "human hair wigs", choose Nigeria as the location, and Google shows you a chart of search interest over time. The chart uses a scale from 0 to 100. A score of 100 means the highest point of interest in the period you chose. A score of 50 means half that peak. A score of 0 means very little interest at that time.

That scale is important to understand. Google Trends shows relative interest, not the number of searches. A score of 80 for one product and 40 for another does not mean the first product had twice as many searches in absolute terms across all of Google. It means the first term was twice as popular as the second within the comparison you made. Used carefully, that comparison still tells you which product attracts more attention.

Google Trends also does not show you who is searching, why they are searching, or whether they have money to buy. A spike in searches for a product can mean people want to buy it, but it can also mean people are curious after a news story, a celebrity post or a viral video. A search for "how to repair a phone screen" is different from a search for "buy phone screen in Ikeja". The first person wants knowledge. The second wants to buy. Learning to tell those apart is a key skill in using the tool.

Treat Google Trends as one source of evidence, not the whole answer. Combine it with what you see in your shop, what customers ask you for, what sells in your market, and what suppliers tell you about prices. When the trend data and your own experience point the same way, you can stock with more confidence. When they disagree, investigate before you spend big money.

## Open Google Trends and Set It to Nigeria

Open your browser on your phone or laptop and go to trends.google.com. The page will load with a search box at the top. Type the product or topic you want to check. Use the words your customers would use. Nigerians often search in simple English: "solar panel price in Nigeria", "wedding dress Lagos", "air fryer", "school bag". Start with a simple term, then try variations.

After you search, check the filters. Set the location to Nigeria. You can also set it to a state if you want a closer look, although state-level data is not always available for smaller topics. Set the time range. For a general view, try the past twelve months. For a seasonal product, try the past five years, so you can see the pattern repeat each year. For a new product, try the past ninety days to see recent movement.

For most product research, the "All categories" setting is fine. If results look messy, try the Shopping category to focus on buying intent.

Set the search type to Web Search to start. You can also try YouTube Search, which is useful if you sell products people learn about through videos, such as beauty products, gadgets or fashion. A product that is rising on YouTube as well as on web search has a stronger signal than one rising in only one place.

Write down what you see with the date, so you can compare it later. Keep a small note with the products you have checked.

## Read the Interest Over Time Chart

The main chart is the first thing you see. It shows a line moving up and down over time. Read it slowly. A steady upward line over months suggests growing interest. A flat line suggests stable demand. A downward line suggests interest is fading. A sharp spike suggests a sudden event, such as a festive season, a price change or a viral moment.

Do not be fooled by a single spike. Open the chart and look at what happened before and after the spike. If interest jumped for one week and then fell back to normal, it was likely a short-lived event. If interest jumped and stayed higher, something has changed in the market. For example, searches for solar products in Nigeria rose and stayed higher as fuel prices and power supply problems made alternatives more attractive. That is a different signal from a one-week spike after a celebrity mentioned a product.

Look for seasons. Many products in Nigeria follow a clear calendar. Searches for raincoats and umbrellas rise during the rainy season. Searches for Christmas clothes, hampers and decorations rise from October to December. Searches for school items rise before school resumption in September and January. Searches for fans and air conditioners rise in hot months. If you can see the season in the chart, you can time your stock: buy early, sell during the peak, and avoid being left with stock after the season ends.

Compare the same product over five years. If the peak comes at the same time every year, you have found a reliable season. If the peak moves or disappears, the market is changing. A product that peaked every December for four years but not in the fifth year may have lost its appeal, or customers may have switched to a new alternative.

## Compare Products Before You Choose

Comparing products is where Google Trends becomes most useful for a trader. You can add up to five terms and see their lines on the same chart. Type your first product, then use the compare option to add others. For example, a phone accessories seller might compare "wireless earbuds", "power bank" and "phone case". A fashion seller might compare "ankara dress", "lace fabric" and "ready to wear gown". A food seller might compare "chin chin", "plantain chips" and "groundnut".

Read the comparison with care. The product with the highest line attracts the most search interest, but that does not always mean it is the best product to stock. A product with very high interest may also have very high competition. Every trader in your market may already sell it, and prices may be squeezed. A product with moderate but steady interest and less competition may earn you more profit.

Also compare the words people use. Nigerians may search for "power bank" far more than "portable charger", even though both mean the same thing. They may search for "human hair" more than "wig", or "men sneakers" more than "male trainers". Use the more popular term in your shop signs, your WhatsApp catalogue and your product descriptions, because that is the language your customers already use.

Try comparing a product with its price search. For example, compare "solar inverter" with "solar inverter price in Nigeria". If the price version is rising faster, buyers are moving from curiosity to purchase. They want to know what it costs, which suggests they are closer to buying. That is a stronger buying signal than a general search alone.

## Use Rising Searches and Related Queries

Below the main chart, Google Trends shows related topics and related queries. These are gold for a small business owner. Related queries show the exact phrases people search alongside your product. If you search for "air fryer", related queries might include "air fryer price in Nigeria", "air fryer for sale in Lagos" or "best air fryer brand". Those phrases tell you what buyers care about: price, location and brand.

Pay attention to queries marked as breakout. A breakout query is one that has grown very fast recently. It may be a new brand, a new model or a new use for a product. For example, a breakout query for a particular phone model or a particular style of dress can signal a trend before it reaches your market. If you spot it early, you can stock it before your competitors do.

Related topics show broader ideas connected to your search. If you search for "skincare", related topics might include particular ingredients, brands or routines. Those topics can inspire new products to add to your range, or new services to offer. A salon owner who sees rising interest in a particular hair treatment can add that treatment before customers start asking for it elsewhere.

Use related queries to write better product descriptions. If customers search for "long lasting" or "original" alongside a product, those words matter to them. Include them honestly in your descriptions where they are true. Do not claim a product is original if it is not. False claims bring refunds and bad reviews. Use the language to describe real qualities: the size, the material, the warranty, the delivery area.

## Check Interest by State and Region

Google Trends shows a map of Nigeria with interest by state. This section is useful if you sell to more than one state, or if you deliver across the country. A product may be very popular in Lagos but quiet in other states, or it may be strong in the north and weak in the south. That pattern tells you where to advertise and where to focus your delivery.

Click on a state to see its ranking. The ranking is relative to other states, based on the share of searches in that state, not the total number of searches. A small state can rank high if a large share of its searches are for that product. That still matters, because it shows concentrated interest. If you sell online and deliver nationwide, a high-ranking state outside Lagos may be a market you have ignored.

Use the regional data to plan your adverts and your WhatsApp broadcasts. If interest in your product is strongest in Lagos, Abuja and Port Harcourt, focus your adverts there first. If a product is popular in Kano and Kaduna, consider whether your delivery partner can reach those cities reliably and what delivery will cost. A product that sells well in a state you cannot deliver to is not a good product for you, however strong the trend looks.

Regional data also helps you understand local language and taste. A fashion item popular in the south-west may differ from one popular in the north. A food product popular in one region may need different packaging or a different size in another. Visit the market, talk to customers from those areas, and check what they actually buy before you order stock for a new region.

## Spot Seasons and Time Your Stock

Timing is one of the biggest advantages Google Trends gives you. Many Nigerian businesses lose money by buying seasonal stock too late or holding it too long. A trader who buys Christmas lights in December pays a higher price and competes with everyone else. A trader who buys in September, when interest is just starting to rise, often gets a better price and sells through the peak.

Use the five-year chart to find your season. Note the month when interest starts to rise, the month it peaks, and the month it falls. Plan your buying two to four weeks before the rise begins. That gives you time for delivery, especially if your supplier is abroad or in another state. Plan a clearance or a discount for the weeks after the peak, so you are not left with stock that will sit for a year.

Watch for products that are rising for a different reason: a change in policy, a new technology or a shift in lifestyle. Searches for work-from-home equipment rose when more Nigerians started working remotely. Searches for water filters, solar lights and rechargeable fans rose as power supply problems continued. These are not seasons. They are shifts. A shift that lasts for months is worth investing in more heavily than a season that lasts for weeks.

Be careful with fads. A fad rises very fast, peaks sharply and falls just as fast. A product tied to a viral dance, a meme or a single celebrity outfit is often a fad. If you buy heavily at the peak, you may be left with stock nobody wants a month later. Buy a small test quantity first. If it sells quickly, reorder. If it slows down, stop. Test small, scale what works.

## Turn Trend Findings Into a Stock Decision

Finding a trend is not the same as making money from it. Turn what you learn into a simple decision checklist before you spend money.

First, confirm demand in your own market. Visit your market, ask three or four fellow traders whether the product is moving, and ask your regular customers whether they would buy it and what price they expect. If the trend says yes but your customers say no, trust your customers. They are the ones who will pay.

Second, check supply and cost. Find out the wholesale price, the minimum order, the delivery cost and the delivery time. Calculate your selling price and your profit after transport, packaging and any POS or transfer charges. A product with strong demand but a thin profit margin may earn less than a quieter product with a better margin. Do the maths on paper before you order.

Third, start small. Order a test quantity, enough to see whether it sells but not so much that a failure hurts you. Display it well, tell your WhatsApp contacts, and post about it. Track how fast it sells in the first week. If it sells out, reorder quickly. If it moves slowly, discount it early rather than waiting for it to expire or go out of fashion.

Fourth, watch your competitors. If every shop on your street already sells the product, your edge must come from price, quality, service or convenience. Can you offer faster delivery, better packaging or a warranty? Can you bundle the product with something else? A trend alone does not give you an advantage. Your service does.

Fifth, keep checking the trend. Set a monthly reminder to re-check your best-selling products on Google Trends. If interest starts to fall, reduce your next order. If a new related product starts to rise, test it. Markets move, and the trader who watches the movement stays ahead of the trader who stocks the same goods year after year.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Google Trends free to use in Nigeria?
Yes. Google Trends is free at trends.google.com. You do not need to pay or install anything to check search interest for a product.

### Does high search interest mean a product will sell?
Not always. High interest can mean curiosity as well as buying intent. Check related queries for price and buying words, and confirm demand with your own customers.

### How far back should I check a trend?
Check the past twelve months for a general view, and the past five years for seasonal products, so you can see whether the pattern repeats each year.

3. Note the season, the top states and the related queries.

5. Order a small test quantity first, and reorder only if it sells fast.
