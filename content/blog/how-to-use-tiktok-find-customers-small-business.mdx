If you think TikTok is only for teenagers dancing, you are missing where a large part of Nigeria now spends its attention. Millions of Nigerians open TikTok every day, and many of them discover new businesses there — a tailor in Yaba, a food vendor in Lekki, a phone repair shop in Ikeja. They did not find those businesses through an advert. They found them through short, honest videos that showed up on their screen.

TikTok can work for a small business, but it works differently from Facebook or Instagram. This guide explains how it actually works, what to post, and how to turn viewers into customers — without hype and without spending a fortune.

## Why TikTok Works for Small Businesses

TikTok's main feed, the For You Page, shows people videos based on what they watch and enjoy — not only on who they follow. That is the big difference. On other platforms, a new business page with few followers struggles to be seen. On TikTok, a good video from a brand-new account can reach thousands of people, because the platform tests every video with a small audience first and shows it to more people if they watch, like, and comment.

That gives small businesses a genuine chance. You do not need a large following or a big budget to be discovered. You need videos that people actually watch to the end.

## Set Up Your Account Properly

Before you post anything, set up a business account. It is free. In your profile settings, switch to a business account and choose the category that fits your business. A business account gives you access to analytics — which videos people watch, who your viewers are, and when they are online — and that information is what helps you improve.

Your profile should make it obvious what you sell and where you are. Use your business name, a clear profile photo — your logo or a good photo of your product — and a short bio that says what you offer and where you are based. For example: "Handmade Ankara dresses | Lagos | Nationwide delivery | Order on WhatsApp". Add the link to your WhatsApp or website so people can reach you in one tap.

## What to Post: Five Video Ideas That Work

You do not need to dance, and you do not need to be funny if that is not your style. These five kinds of videos work for almost any small business.

### 1. Show the work

Film what you do. A baker filming bread coming out of the oven, a tailor showing a dress taking shape, a barber giving a clean fade — process videos are some of the most watched content on TikTok, because people enjoy watching skilled work. Keep it short, 15 to 45 seconds, and let the work speak.

### 2. Show the product in real life

Instead of a still photo, show the product being used — a bag being packed for delivery, a skincare product being applied, a chair being delivered to a customer's home. Real use builds more trust than any studio photo.

### 3. Answer a question your customers ask

Think of the question you hear most often — "How much is delivery to Abuja?", "Do you have this in size 42?", "How long does it take to make?" — and answer it in a 30-second video. These videos are useful, and useful videos get saved and shared, which helps them reach more people.

### 4. Show before and after

Before-and-after videos are powerful for services: a dirty car becoming clean, an empty room becoming a furnished space, a plain face with makeup applied. The transformation is the hook, and the hook is what makes people watch to the end.

### 5. Tell the story of your business

People buy from people they like. A short video about why you started, what a day in your business looks like, or a mistake you learned from can build a connection that no product photo can. Keep it honest and simple — you do not need a script, just talk to the camera like you would talk to a customer.

## How to Make a Video That Gets Watched

You do not need expensive equipment. Your phone, good light, and clear sound are enough. Film near a window or outdoors during the day, and speak clearly. Here are the details that make the difference.

**Hook the viewer in the first two seconds.** The first frame and the first words decide whether someone keeps watching or swipes away. Start with the most interesting part — the finished product, the surprising result, the bold claim — not with "Hello, welcome to my page."

**Keep it short.** Most small business videos do best between 15 and 60 seconds. Say one thing, show it clearly, and stop. A video that drags loses viewers, and TikTok notices when people leave early.

**Add captions.** Many people watch without sound, so add text on the screen that carries the main message. TikTok can generate captions automatically, and you can edit them.

**Use sounds and trends carefully.** Trending sounds can help a video reach more people, but only if the sound fits your video. Forcing your product into an unrelated trend looks awkward. Use trends when they genuinely fit, and ignore them when they do not.

**Post regularly.** Aim for three to five videos a week rather than one perfect video a month. TikTok rewards consistency, and every video is another chance to be discovered. You will learn faster from ten simple videos than from one video you spent a week perfecting.

## Turning Viewers Into Customers

Views are not customers. Here is how to turn attention into orders.

First, make it easy to contact you. Your bio link should go to WhatsApp or your website. In your videos, say it out loud: "Send us a message on WhatsApp to order." People will not search for your contact details — you have to point them to it.

Second, reply to comments quickly, and reply as a video when a comment asks a good question. A video reply is a whole new piece of content, and it shows followers that you listen.

Third, use TikTok's analytics to learn what works. After a few weeks, look at which videos got the most views, the most saves, and the most comments. Make more of that kind of video. Stop making the kind that nobody watches.

Finally, never buy followers. Bought followers do not watch, do not buy, and they damage your account's performance, because TikTok sees that your followers ignore your videos and stops showing them to real people. Growth on TikTok is slower than the promises you see online, but real followers who found you through your videos are worth far more than a big number on your profile.

## Mistakes to Avoid

- **Copying big brands.** Your advantage is being real and close to your customers. A small business pretending to be a corporation loses the very thing that makes it interesting.
- **Only posting adverts.** If every video is "buy this, buy this", people will scroll past. Mix in useful, entertaining, and behind-the-scenes videos, and let the selling happen naturally.
- **Inconsistent posting.** Posting five videos in one week and then nothing for a month teaches the platform — and your audience — to forget you.
- **Expecting overnight results.** TikTok can bring customers, but it is a steady channel, not a lottery ticket. The businesses that win are the ones that keep showing up.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need to show my face on TikTok?

No. Many successful business accounts never show the owner's face — they show hands doing the work, the product, or the finished result. Showing your face can build trust faster, but it is not required. Start with what you are comfortable with.

### How many followers do I need before TikTok brings customers?

There is no magic number. Because TikTok shows videos to people who do not follow you, even an account with a few hundred followers can get orders from a video that reaches the right people. Focus on making useful videos, not on chasing a follower count.

### Should I pay to promote my TikTok videos?

Not at first. Learn what works organically, and only consider paid promotion once you know which kind of video brings you real enquiries. Paying to promote a video that does not work organically is usually a waste of money.

### What if my business is not visual?

Every business has something visual: the work, the tools, the result, the customer, the place. A lawyer can answer common legal questions on camera. An accountant can explain tax deadlines in 30 seconds. The visual part is you, talking to the camera — and that is enough.

## Start With One Video Today

You do not need a strategy document or a content calendar to begin. Film one short video of your work today — the product, the process, or an answer to a common question — and post it. Then post another one tomorrow. That is how every successful small business TikTok account started.
