Nobody wakes up, opens TikTok, and thinks, "Today I will discover a new vendor." Discovery happens sideways. A woman in Enugu is searching for "asoebi styles 2026" at midnight because her cousin's wedding is in three weeks. A student in Ibadan is reading the comments under a wig video, looking for someone who delivers to Oyo State. A man in Abuja watches a duet where a cleaner reacts to a stained sofa, and suddenly he is thinking about his own sofa.

Your customers are already on TikTok, already looking. The question is whether you show up in the places where their looking happens. There are four of those places, and each one works differently: search, the comments under other people's videos, duets, and trends.

## Search: The Customers Who Already Want to Buy

Search is the most overlooked way customers find businesses on TikTok, and it is the one closest to a sale. When someone types "catering Lekki" or "braids Abuja" or "iPhone repair Ikeja" into the TikTok search bar, they are not browsing. They have a problem, a budget, and a deadline. They are the best kind of visitor a small business can get.

TikTok search works on words: the words in your captions, the words you say out loud in the video, the words on the screen, and the words in your bio. A video of a beautiful cake with the caption "love this" will never be found by someone searching "birthday cake Lekki". The same cake with the caption "3-layer birthday cake Lekki ₦45,000 — delivery across Lagos" will.

Say the search words out loud in the first five seconds. TikTok reads the audio, and it matches what people type. A phone repairer in Ikeja, Chidi, starts his videos with the same line every time: "iPhone screen replacement in Ikeja, ₦35,000, thirty minutes, while you wait." He says it plainly, in his own voice, before he shows any repair. When someone in Lagos searches "iPhone screen Ikeja", his videos are the ones that surface — because he used the words his customer used.

Cover the searches your customers actually type, not the ones you wish they typed. Nobody searches "premium luxury hair solutions". People search "bone straight wig Lagos", "how much is frontal installation", "wig vendor near me". Sit down with the questions your WhatsApp customers already ask you and make one short video answering each.

Your bio is a search result too. "Adaeze — Hair vendor, Lagos. Nationwide delivery. WhatsApp to order" will be found. "Queen of slay, God's favourite, CEO of vibes" will not, no matter how many followers it has. Put your product, your city, and your service in the bio, in plain words.

Search rewards patience. A video answering "how much is delivery to Ibadan" can keep bringing customers for months, long after the day it was posted. The For You Page forgets you by tomorrow, but search remembers you for as long as people keep asking the question.

## Comments: Walking Into Rooms Full of Buyers

Every popular video in your niche is a room full of your customers, and the comments section is the door. A video of a Lagos food vendor with 200,000 views might have three thousand comments, and a good share of those comments are people asking where to buy, how much it costs, and whether the vendor delivers to their area. Those people are buyers in motion. They have already shown interest in exactly what you sell.

Commenting your way into their feeds works, but only if you behave like a guest, not a hawker. The sellers who get customers from comments do three things.

First, they comment where their customers already gather. A skincare seller in Lekki spends fifteen minutes every morning reading the comments under the biggest skincare videos in Nigeria — not to sell, but to see what people are asking. When ten people ask about acne on oily skin, she knows what video to make that afternoon.

Second, they answer questions instead of advertising. "We sell this too, check my page" gets ignored, and worse, it makes people distrust you. "For oily skin, use the gel version, not the cream — the cream sits on top and causes the breakouts. I had the same problem for a year" gets read, liked, and remembered. One helpful answer does more for your business than fifty "check my page" comments, because the helpful answer proves you know your product.

Third, they reply to comments on their own videos like a shopkeeper, not a celebrity. A comment that says "How much?" is a customer standing at your counter. Answer with the price, the delivery cost, and the next step — in public, so the next forty people with the same question can read it without asking. Reply fast, within the hour if you can.

There is a line, and it matters. Never spam another seller's comments with your prices. Never argue with a competitor in their comments. People can smell desperation, and on TikTok it travels fast. Comment like someone who knows their craft and likes talking about it. The customers come to that person on their own.

## Duets: Borrowing Someone Else's Audience Honestly

A duet puts your video side by side with someone else's. Done well, it is the fastest honest way to stand in front of an audience that already cares about your niche. Done badly, it looks like clinging to someone else's fame.

The duets that bring customers are reactions, not copies. A furniture maker in Onitsha, Obinna, duets videos of badly made chairs — wobbling legs, peeling varnish — and shows, on his side of the screen, how he builds the same chair properly. He never names the other seller. He never insults anyone. He just shows the difference between cheap work and good work. People who were watching a chair video are, by definition, people thinking about chairs. Some of them tap through to his profile and order.

A caterer in Abuja, Halima, duets videos of wedding food spreads and adds her own commentary on portion sizes — how many guests a tray actually feeds, what a realistic budget looks like. Brides planning weddings watch those duets, because they are planning the same thing.

Duet the question, not the person. When a popular creator asks "why is this wig so expensive?", duet it with your answer: the hair grade, the length, the construction, the price breakdown. When someone posts a video of a room before cleaning, duet it with your after. When a customer posts a video review of your product — good or bad — duet it with a thank-you or a fix. A duet of a real customer's video is worth ten of your own adverts, because it is proof from someone who paid.

Ask permission when the video belongs to a small creator like you. A quick "I loved your video, can I duet it?" costs you nothing and has started more than one collaboration between Lagos sellers. And never duet a competitor's video just to poach their customers in front of them. The audience sees it, the competitor sees it, and the only person it embarrasses is you.

## Trends: Riding the Wave Without Drowning in It

Trends are the loudest way to be discovered on TikTok, and the easiest way to look foolish. A sound goes viral, a dance takes over, a phrase is suddenly everywhere. Jump on it well and thousands of strangers see your business in a day. Jump on it badly and thousands of strangers see your business making a fool of itself.

The rule is simple: the trend serves your product, never the other way round. A trending sound over a video of you packing orders, with the price and your location on the screen, is a trend working for you. A trending dance with your product held up at the end like an afterthought is a trend using you. Viewers can tell the difference in two seconds, and they punish the second kind with a scroll.

Use trends that fit your voice. A tailor in Aba who is naturally funny can ride a comedy sound and make it his own. A quiet, serious pharmacist in Port Harcourt who forces himself into the same comedy sound looks uncomfortable, and discomfort is not persuasive. Your customers buy from the person you actually are, not the person the trend wants you to be.

Use trends fast or not at all. A trend has about three to five days before it goes stale. If you see a sound on Monday, film your version on Monday. By Friday it is a joke about people still doing it.

And know which trends to skip. If a trend is built on mocking customers, mocking other sellers, or making light of something serious, skip it. A video that gets views by embarrassing someone will bring you an audience you do not want and a reputation you cannot easily wash off. The trend will be gone in a week. The screenshot of what you did will not be.

## Putting the Four Together

These four ways of being found do different jobs, and a business that uses only one of them is leaving customers on the table.

Search brings you the buyers who already know what they want. Answer their questions in plain words, say the search terms out loud, and your videos keep working for months.

Comments put you in rooms full of buyers who are already interested in your niche. Answer helpfully, reply fast on your own videos, and never hawk in someone else's space.

Duets let you stand next to audiences that already care, by reacting with skill and honesty. React to the question, not the person, and let your work do the talking.

Trends give you sudden reach, but only when the trend fits your product and your voice. Use them fast, use them sparingly, and skip the ones that would embarrass you next month.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need a lot of followers for search to work?

No. Search does not care about your follower count. It cares whether your video matches the words someone typed. A new account answering "how much is delivery to Enugu" can outrank a big account that never said those words.

### Is commenting on other people's videos rude?

It depends entirely on what you write. A helpful answer is welcome. An advert is not. If your comment would annoy you on your own video, do not post it on someone else's.

### How do I know which trends are worth joining?

If you can explain in one sentence how the trend connects to your product, join it. If the connection takes a paragraph to explain, skip it. Your viewers will not wait for the paragraph.

## Start Where Your Customers Already Are

Open TikTok right now and type your product and your city into the search bar. Look at what comes up, and what does not. The gap between those two lists is your work for this month: the questions nobody has answered, the searches with no good video, the comments full of buyers nobody is talking to.

Then pick one of the four — search is the best place to start — and make one video for it today. One honest answer, in your own voice, with the words your customer would type. That is how customers find you. Not by accident, and not by luck. By showing up in the exact place they were already looking.
