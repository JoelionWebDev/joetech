Most of your customers in Nigeria read text messages. They may ignore an email, miss a social media post, or run out of data, but a short SMS usually reaches their phone and gets read quickly. That is why bulk SMS is still one of the most useful tools for a Nigerian small business, even in a time of WhatsApp and Instagram.

Bulk SMS means sending the same short message to many customers at once through a bulk SMS provider. You can use it to announce new stock, share a price change, remind customers about an appointment, send a payment reminder, invite people to a sale, or thank buyers after they purchase. Done well, it brings customers back to your shop. Done badly, it annoys people and wastes money.

This guide explains how to use bulk SMS properly in Nigeria. You will learn how to build a clean list of customers who want to hear from you, how to choose a provider, how to write messages that get replies, when to send them, and how to measure whether your money is working.

## Start With a Clean List of Real Customers

Bulk SMS only works if you send to the right people. Sending messages to random numbers is wasteful, and it can damage your business. People who never bought from you and never asked for updates will see your message as spam. They will ignore it, block your sender name, or complain.

Build your list from real customers. Collect phone numbers when a customer buys, books a service, or asks for information. Ask for permission in a simple way: "Can we send you updates on new stock and offers by SMS?" Most customers will say yes if they like your shop. Write the number down correctly, with the full Nigerian format, and add the customer's name if you can. A list with names is far more useful than a list of numbers alone.

Keep your list organised. Separate buyers from people who only asked for a price. Separate customers in Lagos from customers in Abuja if you deliver only in one city. Separate regular customers from one-time buyers. That way, you can send a message about a sale in Lekki only to customers who can actually come to Lekki. Sending a message to the wrong group wastes money and teaches customers to ignore you.

Clean your list regularly. Remove numbers that bounce, numbers that belong to people who asked you to stop, and duplicate numbers. If a customer changes their number, update it. A clean list of 500 real customers who want your messages is worth more than a bought list of 5,000 strangers who never asked to hear from you.

## Choose a Bulk SMS Provider Carefully

You need a provider to send bulk SMS in Nigeria. The provider gives you a dashboard or a website where you upload your list, write your message, and send it to many numbers at once. Some providers also let you send through an app or connect to your own website.

Compare providers on five things: price per SMS, delivery rate, sender name, ease of use, and customer support. Price matters, but the cheapest provider is not always the best. A provider that charges very little but delivers only half your messages is more expensive in real terms than a provider that charges a little more and delivers almost all of them.

Ask about the delivery rate and the delivery report. A good provider will show you how many messages were delivered, how many failed, and why they failed. That report helps you clean your list and check whether your money was spent well. If a provider cannot show delivery reports, be careful.

Ask about sender names. Many Nigerian providers let you register a sender name, such as your business name, so customers see "Joetech" or "Adaeze Stores" instead of a random number. A sender name builds trust and helps customers recognise your message. Registration may take a few days and may need your business details. Start that process early, before a big sale.

Test with a small amount first. Buy a small bundle of SMS units, send a test message to your own phone and to a few staff numbers, and check that it arrives quickly and looks correct. Only buy a large bundle after the test works. Keep your login details safe, and do not share your provider account password with staff who do not need it.

## Write Short Messages That Get a Reply

An SMS is short. Most bulk SMS messages in Nigeria are limited to about 160 characters per unit, and longer messages cost more because they are split into two or three units. That limit is actually helpful. It forces you to be clear and direct.

Start with the most important information. Do not start with a long greeting. Start with the offer, the new stock, or the reminder. For example: "New Ankara fabrics now in stock at Adaeze Stores, Balogun Market. From ₦8,000 per yard. Visit us today or call 0803 123 4567." That message tells the customer what is new, where to find it, the price, and what to do next, all in a few lines.

Include one clear action. Tell the customer exactly what to do: visit the shop, call a number, reply to the message, or click a link. Do not give three different actions in one message. One action is enough. If you want customers to call, put the phone number in the message. If you want them to visit, put the shop address or area in the message.

Use the customer's name when you can. A message that starts "Dear Mrs. Okafor, your order is ready for collection" feels personal and is more likely to be acted on than a message that starts "Dear customer." Personalisation takes a little more work, but it improves replies, especially for appointment reminders and order updates.

Send a test message to yourself before you send to your full list. Check the spelling, the price, the phone number, and the shop name. A mistake in a bulk message goes to hundreds of people at once, and it is hard to correct. A one-minute check can save you from a costly error.

## Send at the Right Time and Not Too Often

When you send matters as much as what you send. A good message sent at the wrong time will be ignored. A message sent too often will make customers ask you to stop.

For most Nigerian small businesses, the best times to send marketing SMS are late morning and early afternoon, when people are awake, active, and able to act. A message sent at 10 a.m. about new stock can bring customers to your shop the same day. A message sent at 2 a.m. will annoy people and may be forgotten by morning. Avoid sending late at night unless it is an urgent reminder about an early appointment the next morning.

Do not send too often. For most shops, one or two marketing messages a week is enough. A daily message, even with a good offer, quickly becomes noise. Customers will stop reading, and some will ask to be removed. Save your messages for real news: new stock, a sale, a price change, a reminder, or an important update. If you have nothing useful to say, do not send anything that week.

Separate marketing messages from service messages. A payment reminder, an appointment reminder, or an order update is a service message. Customers expect those, and they are happy to receive them. A sales message is different. Do not mix them. Send service messages when they are needed, and send marketing messages only when you have something worth sharing.

Respect customers who ask you to stop. If a customer replies "STOP" or asks you not to send messages again, remove their number immediately and do not send to them again. Keeping them on your list will damage your reputation and may cause your sender name to be blocked. A customer who opts out today may still buy from you tomorrow if they feel respected.

## Use SMS for Reminders, Orders, and Payments

Bulk SMS is not only for advertising. Some of the most valuable uses are simple service messages that save you time and prevent lost sales.

Use SMS to remind customers about appointments. A salon in Lekki, a clinic in Surulere, or a tailor in Aba can send a reminder the day before an appointment: "Dear Mrs. Eze, this is a reminder of your appointment tomorrow at 10 a.m. at Blessing Salon, Lekki. Please call 0803 123 4567 if you need to reschedule." That one message reduces missed appointments and keeps your day organised.

Use SMS to tell customers when an order is ready. A customer who has paid for a dress, a cake, or a phone repair does not want to keep calling to ask if it is ready. A short message when the order is ready saves phone calls and brings the customer back quickly. It also shows that your business is organised and reliable.

Use SMS for payment reminders. If a customer owes a balance, a polite reminder is better than an angry phone call. For example: "Dear Mr. Ade, this is a friendly reminder that your balance of ₦25,000 for the chairs is due on Friday. Thank you for your patronage. Adaeze Stores." Keep the tone polite, include the amount and the date, and thank the customer. If you send payment reminders by SMS, you can create a clear invoice first with Joetech's free invoice tool at /tools/invoice-generator.

Use SMS to confirm deliveries. When a dispatch rider is on the way, a short message to the customer with the rider's name and phone number helps the customer prepare to receive the goods. That reduces failed deliveries and wasted trips, especially in Lagos traffic.

## Measure What Works and Stop What Does Not

Bulk SMS costs money, so you need to know whether it is bringing customers back. You do not need complex software to measure it. You need a simple system and a habit of checking it.

Track replies and visits. After you send a message about a sale or new stock, note how many customers visit, call, or reply in the next two days. Compare that with a normal week when you did not send a message. If a message brings in many customers, note what you sent and when you sent it. If a message brings in almost no one, note that too, and change the wording, the timing, or the offer next time.

Use a simple code or a specific offer in each message so you can tell which message worked. For example, if you send "Show this SMS for a 5 percent discount on Ankara fabrics this weekend," you can count how many customers show the message. That tells you directly how many sales came from that SMS. Without a code, it is hard to know whether customers came because of the SMS or for another reason.

Check your delivery reports. If many messages fail to deliver, your list needs cleaning. If messages are delivered but no one replies, your message may be unclear, your offer may not be attractive, or you may be sending to the wrong group. Do not keep sending the same message that gets no response. Change one thing at a time: the offer, the timing, or the audience, and see what improves.

Calculate your cost per reply. If you spend ₦10,000 on SMS and get 50 replies or visits, your cost is ₦200 per response. If you spend the same amount and get only five replies, your cost is ₦2,000 per response, and something needs to change. That simple calculation helps you decide whether to spend more on SMS or to try another channel, such as WhatsApp broadcast or a Facebook post.

## Avoid Common Mistakes That Waste Money

Many small businesses waste money on bulk SMS because of simple mistakes. Avoiding those mistakes can save you more than finding a cheaper provider.

Do not send to people who never asked to hear from you. Bought lists and random numbers produce very low replies and high complaints. Build your own list, even if it grows slowly. A small list of real customers is better than a large list of strangers.

Do not send long messages. A message that runs to three units costs three times as much as a one-unit message. Keep your message short, clear, and focused on one offer. If you need to explain more, send the short SMS and invite customers to call or check your WhatsApp for details.

Do not send without a sender name if you can register one. A message from a random number looks like spam. A message from your business name looks like a real business. Take the time to register your sender name properly.

Do not forget to include your business name and a way to contact you. A customer who receives a good offer but does not know which shop sent it cannot act on it. Always include your shop name, and include a phone number or address when it helps the customer find you.

Do not send the same message to the same people many times. If a customer did not respond to the first message about a sale, sending the same message three more times will not help. It will annoy them. Send once, wait, and send a different message later if you have new information.

## What to Do Next

1. Build your list from real customers who agree to receive SMS updates, and organise it by location and buying history.
2. Compare two or three bulk SMS providers on price, delivery rate, sender name, and delivery reports, then test with a small bundle.
3. Register your business sender name so customers recognise your messages instead of seeing a random number.
4. Write one short message with a single offer and a single action, and test it on your own phone before sending to your list.
5. Send marketing messages once or twice a week at a sensible time, and send service messages such as reminders and order updates when they are needed.
6. Use a simple code or offer in each message so you can count replies and visits and calculate your cost per response.
7. Clean your list every month, remove numbers that bounce or opt out, and save your best-performing messages to reuse.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does bulk SMS cost in Nigeria?**
The price per SMS varies by provider and by the size of the bundle you buy, often a few naira per message. Buy a small bundle first to test delivery, compare providers on delivery rate as well as price, and calculate your cost per reply so you know whether the spend is bringing customers back.

**Do I need permission to send bulk SMS to customers?**
Yes, in practice you should only send to customers who bought from you or asked for updates. Ask for permission when you collect a number, and remove anyone who asks you to stop immediately. Sending to random numbers wastes money and damages trust.

**What is a sender name, and why does it matter?**
A sender name is your business name shown instead of a phone number when the SMS arrives. It helps customers recognise your message, builds trust, and reduces the chance that your message is seen as spam. Most providers can register a sender name for you, but it may take a few days.

**How long should a bulk SMS be?**
Keep it to about 160 characters when you can, because longer messages are split and cost more. Include the offer, the price if relevant, your business name, and one clear action, such as calling a number or visiting your shop.

**When is the best time to send bulk SMS?**
For most shops, late morning and early afternoon work well, when customers are awake and able to act. Avoid late-night messages unless it is an urgent reminder. Send marketing messages once or twice a week, and send service messages such as appointment reminders when they are needed.

**How do I know if my bulk SMS is working?**
Use a simple code or offer in each message, count how many customers reply, call, or visit after you send it, and check your provider delivery report. If messages are delivered but few people respond, change the offer, the timing, or the audience, and test again.
