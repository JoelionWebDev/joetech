Think about the last time you bought something online. You saw a photo, then you read the description. That short piece of writing decided whether you bought or kept scrolling. The same thing happens with your customers every day. Your product description is the salesperson who talks to every customer, even while you are asleep — and most small business owners write it as an afterthought.

A good product description does three things: it tells the customer exactly what they are getting, it shows them why it will make their life better, and it answers the questions that would otherwise stop them from buying. This guide shows you how to write descriptions like that, whether you sell on Instagram, WhatsApp, Jumia, Konga or your own website.

## Start With the Customer, Not the Product

The biggest mistake in product descriptions is listing features while ignoring what the customer actually cares about. Features describe the product. Benefits describe what the product does for the buyer. Customers buy benefits.

Take a handbag. "Made from genuine leather, 30cm wide, with an inner pocket" is a list of features. "A sturdy leather bag that fits your laptop, your purse and your lunch — and still looks smart at the office or at owambe" is a benefit. The customer is not buying leather and measurements. She is buying a bag that carries her whole day and looks good doing it.

Before you write, ask yourself three questions. Who is buying this? What problem does it solve for them? What would they worry about before paying? A mother buying baby clothes worries about the fabric irritating her baby's skin. A student buying a power bank worries about whether it will actually charge his phone twice. A bride buying aso-ebi worries about the colour matching the picture. Write your description to the person and their worry, not to a general audience.

Use the words your customers use. If your customers say "soft" instead of "premium-grade textile", write "soft". If they ask "will it fade after washing?", your description should say "colour stays bright after washing". Listen to the questions people already ask you on WhatsApp and in your comments — those questions are the outline of your best description.

## Give the Facts Customers Need to Say Yes

Benefits win attention, but facts close the sale. A customer who cannot find the size, the material, the colour options or the delivery time will not buy — they will ask, wait, and often forget. Put every important detail in the description so the customer can decide without messaging you.

For most products, include these basics: what it is and what it is made of, the size or dimensions, the colours available, what is included in the package, and how it should be cared for. A dress needs size, length, fabric and whether it stretches. A wig needs length, density, lace type and whether it can be bleached. A food item needs weight, ingredients and expiry information. A gadget needs battery life, what it works with, and what is in the box.

Be specific instead of vague. "High quality" means nothing — every seller says it. "Made from 100% cotton that stays soft after many washes" means something. "Big size" means nothing; "fits laptops up to 15.6 inches" means something. Specific details build trust because they show you actually know your product.

And be honest. If a dress runs small, say "runs small — order one size up". If a colour looks slightly different on different phone screens, say so. Honest descriptions reduce returns and complaints, and customers who feel they were told the truth come back. If you are writing a [quotation that wins the job](/blog/how-to-send-quotation-that-wins-the-job), the same rule applies: clear, honest details beat vague promises every time.

## Write Like You Talk, and Keep It Short

Nobody wants to read a long essay about a pair of slippers. Your product description should be easy to scan on a phone, because that is where most of your customers will read it. Keep it short: two to four sentences of description, then a short list of the key details as bullet points.

Write in the same voice you use when a customer walks into your shop. If you are friendly and warm in person, be friendly and warm in writing. Read your description out loud — if it sounds stiff or strange, rewrite it until it sounds like you talking to a customer. Avoid heavy grammar and big words; simple, clear English sells better than fancy English.

Use short paragraphs and bullet points. Online shoppers scan before they read. A wall of text gets skipped; a few short lines and a clear bullet list gets read. Put the most important thing first — the main benefit and the price — because many customers will never read past the first two lines.

## Add Social Proof and Remove the Fear of Buying

Customers trust other customers more than they trust sellers. That is why reviews and real photos matter so much. If a customer has told you they love a product, ask if you can quote them in the description: "My customers say this is the softest fabric they have ever worn" is far more persuasive than you saying it yourself.

Real photos from customers using your product are even better. A photo of a real customer wearing your dress or using your product tells a new buyer "this is real, and it works". Ask happy customers to send you photos, and feature them alongside the description where your platform allows it.

Then remove the reasons a customer might hesitate. State your delivery areas and time clearly: "Delivered in Lagos within 24–48 hours, nationwide in 3–5 working days." State your payment options and your exchange or return policy honestly. A customer who knows exactly what happens after they pay is far more likely to pay. If your price is higher than competitors, your description is where you explain why: better material, longer lasting, handmade, or comes with a guarantee. Never apologise for your price — justify it.

## Use Words That Help Customers Find You

If you sell on a website or a marketplace like Jumia, your description also needs to help customers find the product through search. Think about what a customer types into the search box: "red Ankara gown size 12", "wireless earbuds with long battery", "handmade shea butter for dry skin". Those phrases should appear naturally in your title and your description.

Use your product title wisely. A title like "Dress" helps nobody. A title like "Red Ankara Midi Gown with Pockets — Size 12" tells the customer and the search engine exactly what it is. Then repeat the key phrases once or twice in the description in a natural way — not stuffed in awkwardly, but written as a person would write it.

The same applies to your WhatsApp catalogue and Instagram captions. When customers search inside a platform, clear titles and descriptions are what surface your product. Vague names and empty descriptions are invisible, no matter how good the product is.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long should a product description be?

Long enough to answer the customer's questions, short enough to read on a phone. For most products, two to four sentences plus a bullet list of key details is ideal. Expensive or technical products deserve a little more explanation, because the buyer needs more reassurance before spending more money.

### Should I include the price in the description?

Yes, almost always. Hiding the price forces every customer to message you before they can even decide, and many will simply move on to a seller who shows a price. Showing the price filters in serious buyers and saves you hours of answering "how much?" messages every day.

### Can I copy a product description from another seller or from the manufacturer?

You can use the manufacturer's facts — the size, material and specifications — but write the description in your own words. Copied descriptions make your shop look lazy, and on search engines they make your page invisible because the same text already exists elsewhere. Your own words are also what make your shop sound like you.

### What if my product is similar to many others on the market?

Then your description must explain the difference. Is your material better? Is it handmade? Does it come with a guarantee, faster delivery, or better packaging? Is it made in Nigeria? Find the one or two things that are genuinely different about your product and put them at the top of the description. If there is truly no difference, compete on trust: clear details, honest photos, good reviews and reliable delivery.

Good product descriptions are not about clever writing — they are about clear thinking. Know your customer, show the benefit, give the facts, write like you talk, and remove the fear of buying. Do that for every product, and your descriptions will start selling while you sleep.
