A customer on Instagram buys because she likes your page, your photos and your vibe. A customer on Jumia buys because she searched for "Oraimo power bank 20000mAh", filtered by price, opened five listings that looked identical, and picked one. Those are two different buyers, and the words that win the first one will lose the second one.

Marketplace selling is a search game. Your listing competes with dozens of identical products, side by side, sorted by price and rating. The buyer cannot ask you a question before deciding — she can only read. If your title is vague, your description is empty, or your delivery details are missing, she closes your listing and opens the next one. It takes her three seconds.

This guide is about writing listings for Jumia, Konga and Jiji that survive that three-second test: titles that get found, descriptions that answer the buyer's real questions, and details that build enough trust for a stranger to pay a stranger.

## The title does the finding

On a marketplace, the title is not a headline. It is a search query. Buyers type what they want — brand, product, size, colour — and the platform shows listings whose titles match those words. A title that says "Original Quality Power Bank" will not be found by someone searching "Oraimo power bank 20000mAh". A title that says "Oraimo OPB-P204D 20000mAh Power Bank, Fast Charging, Black" will.

Write your title like a buyer searching. Put the brand first, then the product, then the key detail: model, capacity, size or colour. "Nike Air Max SC Men's Sneakers, White, Size 43". "Tecno Spark 20, 8GB RAM, 256GB Storage, 5000mAh Battery". "Ankara 6 Yards Cotton Fabric, Blue and Gold Print". Do not waste title space on words like "original", "quality", "best price" or "hot sale" — buyers do not search for those, and the platform's ranking does not reward them. And never put a phone number or a WhatsApp link in the title. The platforms remove it, and repeated attempts can get your shop penalised.

Check your title by searching for your own product as a buyer would. If your listing does not appear on the first two pages, your title is missing the words buyers actually type. Fix the title before you touch anything else.

## The spec table buyers actually read

After the title, the buyer scrolls to the specifications, and this is where most Nigerian sellers lose the sale. A listing that says "Size: M, Colour: Blue, Material: Good" tells the buyer nothing. A listing that says "Size: M (chest 96cm, length 68cm), Colour: Royal Blue, Material: 100% cotton, Machine washable, Made in Nigeria" tells her everything she needs to decide without messaging you.

Write the specifications as a plain list, and include the details buyers ask about most. For clothes: size chart with actual measurements in centimetres, fabric, stretch, and whether the colour in the photo is the exact colour. For wigs: length in inches, density, lace type, whether it can be bleached or dyed, and whether a wig cap is included. For phones and gadgets: battery capacity, RAM, storage, what is in the box, and whether a charger is included. For food items: weight, ingredients, expiry date or shelf life, and how it is packaged. For shoes: sizes available, material, sole type, and whether they run small or true to size.

Be honest about what is not included. If the phone comes without a charger, say so. If the wig does not come with a cap, say so. A buyer who discovers a missing item after delivery will return the product and leave a one-star review, and one bad review on a marketplace costs more sales than ten good descriptions earn. Honest gaps in the description are forgiven. Surprises after delivery are not.

## Photos and words must agree

On marketplaces, the photo sells and the description confirms. If they disagree, the buyer trusts neither. A dress photographed in bright sunlight that arrives in a duller shade will be returned. A power bank photographed next to a phone for scale, described as "large capacity", that arrives small will be returned. Returns on Jumia and Konga are not just a lost sale — they damage your seller rating, and a damaged rating pushes your listings down in search.

Photograph the real product, in daylight, against a plain background, from the front, the back, and one close-up of the detail that matters — the fabric, the label, the port, the stitching. Include one photo of the product next to something familiar for scale, like a hand or a phone. And write the description to match exactly what the photos show. If the photo shows a blue dress, the description must say blue, not "blue/black". If the photo shows the box contents, list those contents in the description, item by item.

## Answering the "is it fake?" fear

The biggest fear of a Nigerian marketplace buyer is not the price. It is the fake. Everybody has heard a story about an "original" product that turned out to be a copy, and that fear makes buyers hesitate on every listing that looks too cheap or too vague.

Your description is where you calm that fear. If the product is genuine, say how the buyer can verify it: serial number, scratch-off verification code, original packaging, receipt. If it is a good-quality alternative and not the original brand, say so plainly — "high-quality replica" is honest, "original" on a replica is a lie that will be discovered at delivery. Mention your seller rating and how long you have sold on the platform. Mention your return policy in plain words: "If the product is not as described, you can return it within 7 days."

Do not write "100% original guaranteed" in capital letters with five exclamation marks. Genuine sellers do not need to shout. Specific, checkable facts build more trust than promises: the model number, the verification code, the exact contents of the box.

## Price, delivery and returns in plain words

Buyers abandon listings over unclear costs. State the delivery reality in the description, even though the platform shows a fee. "Delivery to Lagos takes 1 to 3 working days. Delivery to Abuja takes 2 to 4 working days. Delivery fees are calculated at checkout." A buyer who knows when her order will arrive is a buyer who completes the purchase. A buyer who is unsure will wait, and waiting usually means never.

Say what happens if something goes wrong, in one sentence: "If your order arrives damaged or different from the description, you can return it within 7 days for a refund or replacement." Do not copy the platform's legal text. Write it the way you would say it to a customer in your shop. Plain words build trust. Legal words build suspicion.

And never write "chat me on WhatsApp for a better price" in a marketplace listing. The platforms penalise sellers who try to move buyers off the platform, and the buyers who do message you there are often the ones who will haggle the price to nothing and then disappear. Keep the sale on the platform. That is where the buyer is protected, and where your rating grows.

## Before and after: the same power bank

A weak listing says: "Original Oraimo Power Bank, 20000mAh, fast charging, best quality, order now!!!"

A strong listing says: "Oraimo OPB-P204D 20000mAh Power Bank, 22.5W Fast Charging, USB-C and Dual USB-A, Black. Battery: 20000mAh lithium polymer. Charges a phone 4 to 5 times. Full recharge in about 6 hours with a fast charger. In the box: power bank, USB-C cable, user guide. Charger not included. Delivery to Lagos: 1 to 3 working days. 7-day return if the product is not as described."

Same product. Same price. The second listing gets found by the buyer searching "Oraimo 20000mAh", answers every question she would have asked, and gives her no reason to open the next seller's page. The first listing gets found by nobody and trusted by nobody.

## Your listing checklist

Before you publish any listing, check five things. One: does the title contain the brand, the product and the key detail a buyer would search for? Two: does the description list the real specifications with measurements, not vague words? Three: do the photos show the exact product described, including the box contents? Four: does the description say what is not included, and how long delivery takes? Five: would you buy this product from this listing yourself, without messaging the seller? If the answer to any of them is no, fix it before you publish.

Marketplace buyers are not browsing for fun. They came to buy something specific, and they will buy it from the listing that makes the decision easiest. Make yours that listing: findable, factual, honest, and complete. The sellers who win on Jumia, Konga and Jiji are rarely the ones with the lowest price. They are the ones whose listings a buyer can trust in three seconds.
