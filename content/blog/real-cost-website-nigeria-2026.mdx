Ask five web designers in Nigeria what a website costs and you will get five wildly different prices — from "₦50,000, message me" to "₦2 million, minimum." Some of that gap is quality. A lot of it is that nobody tells you the real number, including the costs that arrive after the site is "finished."

This is the honest 2026 breakdown. You get real Naira ranges for every option — DIY, no-code, freelancer, agency, and fully custom — plus the hidden costs most people forget: hosting, domain, maintenance, content, security, and AI. You get a table showing what you actually get at each tier, when each option makes sense, and how AI has genuinely reshaped website pricing in 2026.

## The Short Answer First

For a typical Nigerian SME, "how much is a website?" has a realistic three-part answer:

1. **A basic, professional website from a good freelancer: ₦150,000–₦400,000.**
2. **The same from an agency: ₦350,000–₦800,000.**
3. **Plus yearly running costs you must budget: ₦30,000–₦120,000** for domain, hosting, email, security, and updates.

If the number you have been quoted is wildly outside these ranges, you are either getting an amazing deal, getting ripped off, or being sold much more than you need. The rest of this guide helps you tell which.

## Option 1 — DIY Website (as low as ₦0 per year)

You build it yourself with a free or cheap website builder. Total cash cost can be near zero for the first year.

**What you actually get:** a simple site you control completely, with an acceptable design that looks like other builder sites. Good for a shop owner who just wants a linkable information page they can update themselves.

**The hidden cost is time.** A first-timer can spend 2–4 days getting a decent site up, then half a day a month keeping content fresh. For a business owner whose time is worth money, this is often not free at all.

**Ranges:** paid builder plans run roughly ₦5,000–₦25,000/month (₦60,000–₦300,000/year). Free versions are genuinely free but come with platform branding and serious limits.

## Option 2 — No-Code / Semi-Professional (₦50,000–₦200,000)

In 2026, no-code builders and AI mean a freelancer — or a patient owner — can build a genuinely professional site without writing a line of code. This is the cheapest service tier you can buy, and it is legitimately good now.

**What you actually get:** a clean, mobile-first business website: several pages, professional typography and colors, proper responsiveness on African phones, and a working WhatsApp or contact form. Typically built on a modern no-code or AI-generated platform with professional templates.

**When it makes sense:** a service business, small shop, or one-pager where you need to look professional today but do not need custom software. The limitation is the shared template look and limited customisation — your site will resemble other sites built on the same tool.

**The tip:** a good freelancer near this price uses AI to keep quality high, so you get a proper result at the lower tier — the same workflow covered in our [AI landing page guide](/blog/build-high-converting-landing-page-ai-2026).

## Option 3 — Professional Freelancer (₦150,000–₦400,000)

This is the sweet spot for most Nigerian SMEs. A competent, modern freelancer builds a fully custom, mobile-first website — usually with AI assistance for speed, which you benefit from directly.

**What you actually get:**
- A custom design and build matched to your brand, not a generic template.
- Fast, mobile-first performance built for Nigerian data realities.
- Integration for WhatsApp, contact forms, and a payment gateway if you sell.
- Care and testing on real phones, plus after-sales support.

**When it makes sense:** you need a professional online presence you trust to represent your business, with a result you can point to with confidence. For most small and medium businesses, this is the right tier.

## Option 4 — A Small Agency (₦350,000–₦800,000)

At this level you are no longer buying "a website" — you are buying a managed project with deliverables: discovery, design, extra pages and features, content, testing, and a documented handover. That process is what the extra Naira pays for.

**What you actually get:** a documented project with project management, a more thorough design process, polished extras, SEO groundwork, and reliable support and a dedicated point of contact. The extra you pay is for the process, the accountability, and the safety net.

**When it makes sense:** you value a managed project, need many pages or ongoing work, want SEO built in, or prefer one reliable partner over a solo freelancer. For a simple site, a good freelancer can deliver equal quality for less.

## Option 5 — Fully Custom / Web Application (₦1 million+)

When your website stops being a website and becomes a software product — a dashboard, an online booking system, a marketplace, a membership portal — you are in custom development territory.

**What you actually get:** custom login and user accounts, a database, payments, admin panels, and complex business logic. Nothing off-the-shelf does what you need, and the developer is building genuinely new software, not assembling templates.

**The honest note:** only pay for custom development once you have validated the need. ## Hidden Costs That Surprise Everyone

The build quote is only the beginning. These are the costs most owners forgot when they budgeted:

**Domain (₦15,000–₦25,000/year):** your address online — a `.com.ng` or `.ng` is more expensive than a `.com` but reads as Nigerian and local. A `.com` is cheaper and generic.

**Hosting (₦20,000–₦60,000/year):** the server space that makes your site load. Cheap shared hosting can hurt performance and reliability; modern Git-based hosting is often better value in Nigeria.

**Professional email (₦10,000–₦30,000/year):** `you@yourbusiness.com` instead of a free Gmail address — a major trust signal for Nigerian customers.

**SSL and security (often included, sometimes ₦10,000/year):** the padlock and encryption in the browser. Most modern hosting includes free SSL; paid extras add a security layer for sensitive sites.

**Content and photography (₦20,000–₦100,000+):** you will need copy and photos, and this small cost makes or breaks a website. A great design with weak copy and blurry photos looks unprofessional.

**Maintenance and updates (₦5,000–₦30,000/month or a retainer):** plugin and security updates, monitoring, and content tweaks. The number one reason Nigerian websites die is zero maintenance.

**SEO setup (₦30,000–₦100,000 one-off, then monthly for growth):** technical setup so your site can rank in Google. Most owners forget this until the site is built and nobody finds it.

When a "cheap" ₦80,000 website is quoted, none of the above is included. Compare that honestly against the ₦150,000–₦400,000 option that covers your running costs, and the higher tier is not as "expensive" as it first sounds.

## When Each Option Actually Makes Sense

- **Choose DIY** if you need a linkable page cheaply and have time to spare — you are trading time for money.
- **Choose no-code** if you want a good-looking business site fast at a one-time low cost and can accept standard features.
- **Choose a freelancer** if you need a strong, trustworthy, mobile-first site with solid after-sales — the default for most SMEs.
- **Choose an agency** if you need a managed, designed, multi-page build and want one reliable working relationship.
- **Choose a custom / web app** only when your business needs a specific software feature no standard option provides.

The mistake most owners make is aiming for the top tier when they need the middle. Scope the need before the budget.

## How AI Changed the Cost Structure in 2026

AI is the main reason website prices are shifting — and it changes what you pay for:

**Build time has collapsed.** With AI workflows (the ones in our [AI web development guide](/blog/ai-powered-web-development-workflow-2026)), a site that took a freelancer 2–3 weeks now ships in days. That lowers some quotes directly.

**The fee moved to craft, not labour.** You now pay for taste, strategy, strong copy, and the last 20% of polish — the parts AI cannot hand over. That is where the value sits.

**Custom is cheaper to approach.** An AI-assisted developer can build feature prototypes and dashboards at a fraction of the old price, bringing some web-app builds into SME range.

**The floor is lower, but the quality gap is real.** Basic sites are genuinely more affordable than before, but AI also makes it easy to ship mediocre ones fast. The differentiator for a good provider is now strategy, speed, accuracy, and reliability — not simply hours billed.

The practical takeaway: because AI made the labour cheap, do not shop purely on price. You will find ₦80,000 sites and ₦800,000 sites — the value difference now sits almost entirely in thinking, craft, and what happens after launch.

## Common Pitfalls (And How to Avoid Them)

1. **Accepting a quote without a full scope.** Get every element — pages, forms, mobile, WhatsApp, content, and the first year of maintenance — itemised in writing so there are no surprises.
2. **Forgetting the recurring costs.** Domain, hosting, and email renew yearly. Budget them, or the site can quietly go offline when a renewal lapses.
3. **Paying for scope you do not need.** For a startup, test the market with a catalogue site or WhatsApp flow before paying for custom payments or a web app.
4. **Choosing the cheapest build.** A site that is not mobile-first or secure is not a deal — it silently costs you customers. Judge value, not the lowest number.
5. **No support plan.** The cheapest builds often break and nobody updates them. Ask who maintains it and for what, and set the arrangement up front.
6. **Cheap, unreliable hosting.** Untrusted hosting is a Nigerian support nightmare. Use a host with proper deployment and support, even if it costs a little more.

## FAQ

### How much does a website really cost in Nigeria in 2026?

A basic business site is ₦150,000–₦400,000 from a freelancer, ₦350,000–₦800,000 from an agency, and ₦50,000–₦200,000 no-code. Add ₦30,000–₦120,000 per year for domain, hosting, and email.

### Is a free DIY website good enough?

For a hobby, test, or bridge, yes — but for a client-facing business, usually not. A free DIY site often reads as unprofessional and limits your growth. It is enough to start; replace it once your business makes it worthwhile.

### Why do different designers quote such different prices?

Differences in scope, quality, turnaround, and reliability. Two designers can legitimately quote ₦150,000 and ₦800,000 for a similar-looking site because the cost sits mostly in design taste, copy, security, and what happens after launch.

### Is AI making websites cheaper or more expensive?

Both. The floor (a basic site) is cheaper because build time collapsed. But value has moved to design quality, copy, and conversion — which still cost. You get more for the same Naira, but good craft is never free.

### Do I need the agency package?

Only if you want scale, complex features, or an ongoing managed relationship. For effectively every small business, a really good freelancer is the better cost trade-off for a professional site.

## The Bottom Line

For a typical Nigerian business, a good website is fully achievable for ₦150,000–₦400,000 from a freelancer, plus a few tens of thousands a year to run it. You do not need agency prices most of the time, and you do not need to DIY either if your site represents your business.

Scope it right, budget the hidden costs, and use a professional who uses AI well to keep the price fair and the quality high. When you pay for a custom build, you are paying for craft and reliability — the things AI does not hand over automatically.
