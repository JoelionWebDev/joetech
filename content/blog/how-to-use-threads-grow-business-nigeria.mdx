# How to Use Threads to Grow Your Business in Nigeria

Threads is Meta's text-based social app, linked to Instagram. It feels like a mix of X and Instagram, but with a calmer, more conversational style. People go there to share short thoughts, ask questions, and join discussions. For a Nigerian small business, that is a useful opportunity. You can talk directly to potential customers, show your knowledge, and build trust without spending money on adverts.

Many business owners in Lagos and Abuja have not tried Threads yet, which is exactly why it is worth trying now. The platform is still growing in Nigeria, so good posts can reach many people. This guide explains how to set up your Threads profile, what to post, how to grow followers, and how to turn conversations into sales on WhatsApp and in your shop.

## What Is Threads and Why It Matters

Threads was launched by Meta as a companion to Instagram. You sign in with your Instagram account, and you can follow the same people you follow there. Posts are short, usually a few sentences, and people reply in threads, which are chains of connected posts. You can also share photos, short videos, and links.

For businesses, Threads matters because it rewards conversation. Unlike Instagram, where a beautiful photo is the main thing, Threads rewards clear, useful, and honest writing. A fashion seller can explain how to style an outfit. A caterer can share a tip about planning a party. A web designer can explain why a business needs a website. Each post is a chance to prove you know your work.

Threads also connects with Instagram, so your Instagram followers can find you easily. When you post on Threads, some of your Instagram followers may see it. That cross-over helps a small business grow on two platforms at once, without doing twice the work. In Nigeria, where many customers are already on Instagram and WhatsApp, Threads is a natural next step.

## Step 1: Set Up Your Business Profile Properly

Start by downloading the Threads app from the Play Store or App Store, or open threads.net on your computer. Sign in with the Instagram account for your business. If you use a personal Instagram account for business, consider creating a separate business Instagram account first, so your Threads profile stays professional.

Choose a clear profile photo. Use your business logo, or a clear photo of you if you are the face of the business. A blurry photo or a random picture will make people scroll past. Next, write a short bio that says what you do, who you help, and where you are based. For example: "We make ready-to-wear dresses in Lagos. Nationwide delivery. Order on WhatsApp." That tells a visitor everything they need in one line.

Add a link to your WhatsApp, your website, or your Instagram shop. Threads lets you add one main link in your bio. Use the link that leads most directly to sales, which for many Nigerian businesses is WhatsApp. If you have a website, you can mention it in your posts as well. Finally, check your privacy settings. A public profile is best for a business, because anyone can see your posts and follow you. A private profile limits your reach.

## Step 2: Understand How Threads Works

Threads posts are short, with a limit of 500 characters per post. That is about three or four sentences. You cannot write a long essay in one post. Instead, you write a short post, then add replies to it to continue the thought. This is called a thread, and it is a good way to share a longer tip or story in small, readable pieces.

People can like, repost, quote, and reply to your posts. A repost shares your post with the reposter's followers. A quote lets someone add their own comment above your post. Replies are where conversations happen. The more replies a post gets, the more Threads shows it to other people. That is why asking a question at the end of a post often helps it spread.

The feed shows posts from people you follow and posts Threads thinks you will like. It also shows trending topics. Spend a few days reading Threads before you post a lot. Notice what kind of posts get replies in Nigeria, what tone people use, and what topics your customers care about. Learning the culture of the platform first will make your own posts feel natural, not like adverts dropped into a conversation.

## Step 3: Find Your Voice and Your Topics

Your voice is the way you write. On Threads, a friendly, direct voice works best. Write the way you talk to a customer in your shop in Lekki or at a market stall in Abuja. Short sentences help. A little Pidgin can help too, if that is how your customers speak. Avoid stiff, formal language that sounds like a company memo.

Pick three or four topics you will post about regularly. These are your content pillars. For a skincare business, they might be skincare tips, new products, customer results, and behind-the-scenes posts. For a restaurant, they might be new dishes, cooking tips, customer reviews, and special offers. For a web design agency, they might be website tips, client work, pricing advice, and business lessons.

Stick to those topics most of the time. When you post about the same topics again and again, people start to see you as an expert in that area. If you post about everything, from football to politics to your products, people will not know what you stand for. You can still share a personal post now and then, but keep your main focus on the topics that help your customers and your business.

## Step 4: Write Posts That People Reply To

The best Threads posts start a conversation. A post that only says "Buy our shoes, ₦35,000" will get little attention. A post that says "What is the hardest part of buying shoes online in Nigeria? For me, it is sizing. What about you?" will get replies, because it asks people for their experience. Once people reply, you can join the conversation and mention your product naturally.

Use simple formats that work well on Threads. A short tip works well: "Tip: wash your Ankara with cold water and mild soap, so the colour lasts longer." A short story works well: "A customer in Ikeja ordered a dress on Monday and wore it to a wedding on Saturday. She sent us photos. This is why we love what we do." A question works well: "Lagos or Abuja, which city has the best small chops?" A list works well, if you keep it short and split it across replies.

Always end with a reason to respond. Ask a question, invite an opinion, or ask people to share their experience. Do not end every post with "DM to order." That feels like a sales pitch. End most posts with a conversation, and end some posts with a clear sales message. A good balance is about four useful or fun posts for every one direct sales post. That way, people enjoy following you and do not feel they are only being sold to.

## Step 5: Post Consistently and at the Right Times

Consistency matters more than volume. Posting two or three times a day on Threads is a good target for a small business, because posts are short and quick to write. That may sound like a lot, but a post can be one sentence and a question. You do not need a long post every time. If two or three times a day feels too much, start with one good post a day and build up.

The best times to post in Nigeria are usually early morning, around 7am to 9am, when people check their phones before work, and evening, around 6pm to 9pm, when people are home and relaxing. Lunchtime, around 12pm to 2pm, can also work well. Test different times and watch which posts get the most replies. Your audience may be different. Students, office workers, and market traders all use their phones at different times.

Plan your posts in advance to save time. Spend thirty minutes on a Sunday evening writing five or six posts for the week. Save them in your phone notes. Then post one or two each day, and add a fresh post when something interesting happens in your business, such as a new product arriving or a customer sending a good review. Planning ahead keeps you consistent, even on busy days when you have many orders to fulfil.

## Step 6: Grow Your Followers the Honest Way

Grow your followers by being useful and by engaging with others. Follow people in your niche, such as other Lagos fashion sellers, food bloggers, or business owners in your area. Reply to their posts with thoughtful comments, not just "Nice." A good reply adds something to the conversation and makes people curious about who you are. When they tap your profile, they should see a clear bio and useful posts.

Join conversations about topics your customers care about. If someone asks where to find a good tailor in Lekki, and you are a tailor, reply helpfully. If someone asks about the best way to start a small business in Nigeria, share what you have learned. People remember helpful replies and often follow the person who gave them. This is slow, honest growth, and it builds a following that actually cares about your business.

Avoid buying followers or using bots. Fake followers do not buy anything, they do not reply, and they make your account look suspicious to real customers. Threads and Instagram can also limit accounts that use fake growth tools. It is better to have 500 real followers in Nigeria who might buy from you than 10,000 fake followers who never engage. Focus on real people, real conversations, and real value.

## Step 7: Use Photos, Videos and Links Well

Threads is mainly a text app, but photos and videos can make your posts stand out. A photo of your product, a short video of you packing an order, or a before-and-after photo can stop someone scrolling. Use clear, bright photos taken in good light. You do not need a professional camera. A clean phone photo is enough if the product is the focus and the background is tidy.

Share a photo when it adds to the post. If you write about a new dress, show the dress. If you write about a meal, show the meal. Do not add a random photo just to fill space. One good photo is better than four blurry ones. Short videos also work well on Threads, especially videos under thirty seconds that show your product in use or your team at work.

Use links carefully. Threads allows links in posts, but posts with links sometimes reach fewer people, because the platform wants people to stay in the app. If you want people to order, it is often better to write the post, get replies, and then share your WhatsApp link in a reply or in your bio. You can also say "Link in bio" or "Send us a DM for the link." This keeps your main post clean and encourages people to talk to you first, which often leads to a sale.

## Step 8: Turn Conversations into Sales

Threads is not a shop, so sales usually happen after a conversation. When someone replies to your post and shows interest, reply quickly and warmly. Answer their question, then invite them to continue on WhatsApp. For example: "Yes, we have it in size 42. Send us a message on WhatsApp and we will send you photos and the delivery details." That moves the conversation to a place where you can close the sale.

Keep track of people who ask about prices or availability. If someone says "How much?" reply with the price and the delivery cost to their area, if you know it. Do not hide your price when someone asks directly. Many Nigerian customers will move on if they have to ask three times before getting a price. Be clear, be polite, and be fast. A reply within an hour is good. A reply the next day may be too late.

Use direct messages on Threads or Instagram to follow up, but do not spam people. If someone showed interest but did not buy, you can send one polite follow-up message. If they do not reply, leave it. Chasing people too hard will damage your reputation. Instead, keep posting useful content, so they remember you when they are ready to buy. Many customers need to see your business several times before they trust you enough to pay.

## Step 9: Work with Other Nigerian Businesses

Collaboration can help you grow faster on Threads. Find businesses that serve the same customers but do not compete with you. A fashion seller can work with a makeup artist. A caterer can work with an event planner in Lekki. A web designer can work with a photographer. You can repost each other's posts, mention each other, or run a joint offer.

A simple collaboration is a shared post. For example, a baker and a gift shop can post together about a birthday package. Each business shares the post with its own followers, so both reach new people. Another simple idea is a short question-and-answer thread, where two business owners answer questions about their work. This shows your expertise and introduces you to a new audience.

You can also join Threads communities and trends in Nigeria. When a topic is trending, such as a public holiday, a football match, or a popular event in Lagos, join the conversation if it fits your business. A restaurant can post about a match-day food offer. A fashion seller can post about what to wear to a concert. Timely posts often get more attention, because people are already talking about the topic.

## Mistakes to Avoid on Threads

The first mistake is only posting adverts. If every post is "Buy now, ₦20,000," people will stop following you. Threads is a conversation platform, not a notice board. Mix useful posts, questions, and stories with your sales posts. The second mistake is copying posts from X or Instagram without changing them. Threads has its own style, and posts that feel copied often perform poorly. Rewrite them for Threads, in shorter, more conversational language.

The third mistake is ignoring replies. If someone takes time to reply to your post and you never answer, they will not reply again. Check your notifications at least twice a day and reply to comments, especially questions about your products. The fourth mistake is posting and disappearing. Threads rewards people who are present. Spend ten minutes a day reading other posts, liking good ones, and replying to people in your niche.

The fifth mistake is arguing or joining heated debates about politics or sensitive topics. As a business, your goal is to attract customers, not to win arguments. Stay neutral, stay polite, and focus on your business and your customers. If a conversation becomes hostile, step away. Your reputation is more valuable than a single debate.

## Your Threads Growth Checklist

Use this checklist to grow your business on Threads in Nigeria. First, set up a public business profile with a clear photo, a short bio, and a WhatsApp link. Second, pick three or four topics you will post about regularly. Third, post one to three times a day, in the morning and evening, with a mix of tips, questions, stories, and sales posts. Fourth, end most posts with a question that invites replies. Fifth, reply to comments quickly and move interested customers to WhatsApp. Sixth, engage with other Nigerian businesses and customers every day, with thoughtful replies. Seventh, share clear photos and short videos when they add to your post. Eighth, track which posts get the most replies and make more posts like those. Ninth, never buy followers, and never spam people with sales messages.

Threads is still early in Nigeria, and that is your advantage. Businesses that start now, post consistently, and talk to customers honestly will build an audience before the platform becomes crowded. Open Threads today, fix your profile, and write your first post. Ask your customers a simple question, share a useful tip, and reply to everyone who answers. Your next customer may already be on Threads, waiting for a business like yours to start a conversation.
