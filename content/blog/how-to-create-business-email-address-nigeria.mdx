Picture two businesses. One sends you a proposal from joetechventures@gmail.com. The other sends the same proposal from info@joetechventures.ng. Same words, same prices — but which one do you trust more? For most Nigerians, the answer is instant: the second one. A business email address with your own domain tells a customer, before they read a single word, that you are a real business, that you have invested in yourself, and that you are not going to disappear tomorrow.

Yet thousands of Nigerian small businesses still run everything through a Gmail or Yahoo address. It works, until it does not: a customer hesitates before paying ₦150,000 to a Gmail address; a corporate client asks for your "official email" and you have none; your Gmail account gets locked and your whole business goes silent.

Setting up a professional business email is easier and cheaper than most business owners think. You can do it free with Zoho Mail, or for a few thousand naira a month with Google Workspace. This guide walks you through every step — choosing your domain, picking a provider, setting up the technical records in plain language, and using your new email like a professional.

## Why a Business Email Matters

**Trust.** This is the biggest reason. In a market where online scams are common, customers look for signals that a business is genuine. A domain email is one of the strongest signals you can send. It shows you own a domain, which means you paid for something permanent. Scammers rarely bother.

**Professionalism.** When you email a corporate client from info@yourbusiness.ng, you look like a peer, not a hustler. It changes how people reply to you, how quickly they reply, and how seriously they take your prices.

**Control and continuity.** A Gmail address belongs to whoever created it. A domain email belongs to your business. When a staff member leaves, you can change the password, reassign the address, and keep every customer conversation.

**Deliverability.** Emails from a properly set up domain are less likely to land in spam than emails from free accounts, especially when you send to corporate addresses. If you have ever sent a proposal and heard nothing back, there is a real chance it went to spam.

**Branding.** Every email you send carries your domain name. Customers see it, remember it, and type it into Google later. It is free advertising in every message.

## Step 1: Get a Domain Name

Your business email needs a domain — the part after the @ sign. If you already have a website, you already have a domain. If not, you need to buy one.

**Choose the right name.** Keep it short, easy to spell over the phone, and matched to your business name. joetech360.ng is better than joetech-ventures-and-digital-solutions.com.ng. Avoid hyphens and numbers if you can; they are hard to dictate and easy to mistype.

**Pick the extension.** For a Nigerian business, .ng or .com.ng is a strong choice — it tells customers you are local and it is often cheaper than .com. A .com is the global standard and works well if you serve international customers. Either is fine; what matters is that you own it and it matches your brand.

**Where to buy.** You can buy a .ng domain from a Nigerian registrar such as WhoGoHost, SmartWeb, or DomainKing for roughly ₦3,000–₦8,000 per year, depending on the extension. A .com typically costs ₦15,000–₦25,000 per year from international registrars like Namecheap or Cloudflare. Buy from a registrar you can reach easily, keep the login details safe, and set the domain to auto-renew so you never lose it by accident.

**One warning:** buy the domain yourself, in your own name and your own account. Never let a developer or a friend buy it in their name. If that relationship ends, your domain — and your email — goes with them.

## Step 2: Choose Your Email Provider

You have four realistic options in Nigeria. Each has a cost, and each suits a different kind of business.

### Option A: Zoho Mail — Free, and the Best Starting Point

Zoho Mail offers a free forever plan for up to five users on one domain, with 5GB of storage per user and webmail plus mobile apps. For most small Nigerian businesses, this is the right choice: it costs nothing, it is reliable, and it looks fully professional. The setup takes about 30 minutes.

### Option B: Google Workspace — The Premium Choice

Google Workspace gives you Gmail with your own domain, plus Google Drive, Docs, Meet, and Calendar, for about $6–$7 per user per month (roughly ₦9,000–₦11,000 at current rates, billed in dollars). It is the most polished option and the easiest to use, because it is Gmail with your domain on it.

Choose this if you already live in Google's world, if you have staff who need shared drives and calendars, or if you email corporate clients who expect the Gmail experience. The cost adds up with several users, so start with one or two accounts and add more as the business grows.

### Option C: Microsoft 365 — Best for Office Users

Microsoft 365 Business Basic costs about $6 per user per month and gives you Outlook with your domain, plus Word, Excel, PowerPoint online and OneDrive storage. Choose it if your team already uses Word and Excel heavily, or if your clients are corporate organisations that run on Microsoft. It is slightly more complex to set up than Zoho, but the Office apps are a real advantage for businesses that produce documents and spreadsheets daily.

### Option D: Email From Your Web Host — Cheap but Limited

If your website is hosted with a company like WhoGoHost, Bluehost, or SiteGround, your hosting plan usually includes free email accounts on your domain. This costs nothing extra and is quick to set up from your hosting control panel.

The catch is deliverability and reliability. Hosting email sometimes lands in spam, storage is limited, and if your hosting goes down, your email goes down with it. It is acceptable for a brand-new business, but plan to move to Zoho or Google Workspace once you can afford it.

**Our recommendation:** start with Zoho Mail free. It is professional, reliable, and costs nothing. Move to Google Workspace when your business can comfortably afford ₦10,000 a month per user and you want the Gmail experience.

## Step 3: Set Up Your Email, Step by Step

We will use Zoho Mail as the example, because it is the free option most readers will choose. The steps are similar for Google Workspace and Microsoft 365.

**Step 1: Create your Zoho account.** Go to zoho.com/mail, choose the free plan, and sign up with your name and your existing email address. You will create an organisation — use your business name.

**Step 2: Add your domain.** In the Zoho admin panel, add the domain you bought. Zoho will ask you to prove you own it, usually by adding a small text record (called a TXT record) to your domain's DNS settings at your registrar. This sounds technical, but it is just copying a line of text from Zoho and pasting it into your registrar's DNS page. It takes two minutes, and Zoho's setup wizard walks you through it.

**Step 3: Add your MX records.** MX records are the signposts that tell the internet where your email should be delivered. Zoho gives you three MX records to add at your registrar, and you must delete any old MX records first — if you previously used your host's email, its records will conflict. Once saved, email starts arriving in Zoho, usually within a few hours, sometimes up to 24 hours.

**Step 4: Add SPF, DKIM, and DMARC.** These three records prove your emails are genuinely from you, and they are the difference between landing in the inbox and landing in spam:

- **SPF** lists the servers allowed to send email for your domain. Zoho gives you one line to add.
- **DKIM** adds a digital signature to every email you send. Zoho generates the record for you.
- **DMARC** tells receiving servers what to do with emails that fail the first two checks. Start with a simple policy that only monitors, then tighten it later.

You add all three as TXT records at your registrar, exactly as Zoho shows them. If this feels intimidating, ask your web developer to do it — it is a 15-minute job for anyone who has done it before, and it is worth doing properly.

**Step 5: Create your email addresses.** In Zoho, create your first user — for example, info@yourbusiness.ng for general enquiries. Then create the others you need: your own name (joel@yourbusiness.ng), sales@ for enquiries, support@ for customer issues. On the free plan you can have five users; each one is a separate inbox with its own login.

**Step 6: Connect your phone and computer.** Download the Zoho Mail app on your phone, sign in, and turn on notifications. You can also connect your domain email to the Gmail app or Outlook using the settings Zoho provides. Test it: send an email from your new address to your personal Gmail and check that it arrives in the inbox, not spam.

## Step 4: Choose the Right Addresses for Your Business

Do not create random addresses. Use a simple structure your customers can guess:

- **info@yourbusiness.ng** — general enquiries, the address on your website, flyers, and business cards.
- **sales@yourbusiness.ng** — quotes, orders, and price enquiries. Use this in proposals so sales conversations stay in one place.
- **support@yourbusiness.ng** — complaints, delivery issues, and after-sales help. Customers trust a business that has a support address.
- **hello@ or contact@** — a friendly alternative to info@, good for small creative businesses.
- **yourname@yourbusiness.ng** — your personal business address for direct conversations with clients and partners. Use your first name, not a nickname.
- **accounts@ or finance@** — invoices, receipts, and payment matters, if you handle enough volume to need it.

Avoid addresses that look unprofessional: no "bigboss@," no "cutehairvendor123@," no addresses with your birth year in them. And avoid using one person's name for the general inbox — when that person leaves, the address becomes useless.

For a one-person business, you need only two addresses to start: info@ for the public and yourname@ for direct conversations. You can add more as you grow.

## Step 5: Use Your Business Email Like a Professional

Setting up the email is half the job. Using it well is the other half.

**Write a proper signature.** Every email should end with a short signature: your name, your role, your business name, your phone number, your website, and your address if relevant. Example:

> Igono Joel
> Founder, Joetech
> 0803 000 0000 | info@joetech360.ng
> www.joetech360.ng | Lekki, Lagos

A signature makes every email look complete and gives the reader three ways to reach you. Set it up once in your email settings and it appears automatically.

**Answer within one business day.** A business email creates an expectation of a reply. If you cannot answer fully, send a short acknowledgement: "Thank you for your email. I will send you the quote by tomorrow morning." Silence is the fastest way to lose a customer who emailed three businesses and is waiting to see who replies first.

**Keep your inbox organised.** Create folders: Enquiries, Orders, Payments, Suppliers, Receipts. Move emails out of the inbox once dealt with. A clean inbox means nothing important gets buried.

**Use a separate address for subscriptions and sign-ups.** When you register on a new website or download a free resource, use a personal address, not your business one. This keeps your business inbox clean.

**Set up an auto-reply for busy periods.** If you travel or close for a holiday, set an out-of-office reply with the date you return and an alternative contact. A customer who gets an auto-reply waits patiently; a customer who gets silence assumes you have closed down.

**Back up important emails.** Download or forward critical contracts, payment confirmations, and agreements to a second location. Email providers rarely lose data, but a locked account or a forgotten password can cut you off at the worst moment. Keep your recovery email and phone number up to date.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Using a Gmail address on your business card and website.** It tells every customer you have not invested in your own brand. Fix it this week.
- **Letting someone else own your domain.** If your developer registered your domain in their name, transfer it to your own account now, while the relationship is good.
- **Skipping SPF, DKIM, and DMARC.** Without them, your emails may go to spam, and anyone can send fake emails pretending to be your business.
- **Sharing one login with all staff.** When everyone logs in as info@, nobody is accountable and a departing staff member can lock you out. Give each person their own address.

## What to Do If Your Emails Go to Spam

Even with a proper setup, your emails can sometimes land in spam. Here is how to fix it:

1. **Check your SPF, DKIM, and DMARC records.** Use a free checker like MXToolbox or Google's Postmaster Tools to confirm all three are present and correct. A missing or mistyped record is the most common cause.
2. **Ask the recipient to mark your email as "Not spam."** Once they do, future emails from you are more likely to reach their inbox.
3. **Avoid spam trigger words in subject lines.** Words like "FREE!!!", "urgent," "winner," and "make money fast" — especially in all caps — trigger filters. Write subject lines like a human, not a spammer.
4. **Do not send bulk emails from your business address.** Sending a promotion to 500 people from info@ will get you flagged. Use a proper email marketing tool like Mailchimp, Brevo, or Zoho Campaigns for bulk sends.
5. **Test before you send.** Before an important proposal goes out, send a test email to your own Gmail and Yahoo addresses and check where it lands.

## Final Word

A professional business email is one of the cheapest trust signals a Nigerian business can buy — free, in the case of Zoho Mail, and a few thousand naira a month at the premium end. It takes an afternoon to set up, it works for years, and it changes how customers, corporate clients, and partners see you from the very first message.

Start today. If you have a domain, set up Zoho Mail free this evening. If you do not have a domain, buy one this week — .ng domains cost less than a lunch in Lekki. Then put info@yourbusiness.ng on your WhatsApp bio, your Instagram bio, your business card, and your website. The next time a customer asks for your email, you will give them an address that sounds like a business they can trust — because it is.
