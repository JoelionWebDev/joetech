Lagos has no shortage of web developers. Open Instagram, Facebook, or LinkedIn, and you will find dozens of people advertising web development services. Prices range from ₦100,000 to ₦10,000,000, and everyone claims to build "world-class websites."

But not all developers are created equal. The web development industry in Lagos has a serious quality problem. "Briefcase developers" — people who learned HTML last month and now call themselves full-stack developers — are everywhere. They promise the world, deliver a half-finished template, and disappear when something breaks.

At Joetech, we have fixed more websites than we care to count that were originally built by someone else. The mistakes are almost always the same. Here is how to avoid them.

## Red Flag #1: They Cannot Show You a Portfolio

Every legitimate web developer has a portfolio. It might be small — everyone starts somewhere — but it exists. If a developer cannot show you at least 3-5 websites they have built, something is wrong.

**What to look for:** Live, functioning websites that you can visit. Not screenshots. Not Figma mockups. Real, deployed websites. Ask about their role in each project — were they the sole developer, part of a team, or just the designer?

**The excuse to watch for:** "My clients are confidential." While NDAs exist, any developer with real experience can show you at least some work. Offer to sign an NDA if needed. If they still cannot produce examples, walk away.

## Red Flag #2: They Promise Everything Cheap and Fast

Web development takes time. A proper business website takes 2-4 weeks from start to finish. A custom web application takes 8-16 weeks or more. If someone promises you a full e-commerce website in 3 days for ₦150,000, they are either lying or planning to deliver a templated mess.

**What to look for:** Realistic timelines and transparent pricing. A good developer will explain why the project takes the time it does and break down what each phase includes.

**What to ask:** "Walk me through your development process from start to finish." A legitimate developer will have a clear process: discovery, design, development, testing, deployment, and handover. If they cannot describe their process, they do not have one.

## Red Flag #3: They Do Not Ask About Your Business

If a developer starts talking about colours and fonts before understanding your business, run. A website is not an art project — it is a business tool. Before writing a single line of code, a professional developer should understand:

- What your business does
- Who your target customers are
- What you want customers to do on your website
- What your competitors are doing well and poorly
- Your budget and timeline constraints

**What to look for:** A developer who asks more questions than they answer in your first meeting. They should be curious about your business, your customers, and your goals.

**What to ask:** "What information do you need from me to give an accurate quote?" A thorough developer will ask about features, content, integrations, hosting preferences, and long-term maintenance needs.

## Red Flag #4: They Do Not Write Contracts

In Lagos, many web developers operate without formal agreements. Money changes hands via bank transfer, promises are made over WhatsApp, and when something goes wrong, there is no recourse.

**What to look for:** A written contract or statement of work that covers:

- Project scope and deliverables
- Timeline with milestones
- Payment schedule (tied to milestones, not arbitrary dates)
- Hosting and domain ownership details
- Maintenance and support terms
- Intellectual property rights (who owns the code, content, and design)
- Termination clauses

**What to ask:** "Can I see a sample contract?" If they do not have one, they are not a professional operation. We provide a detailed contract for every Joetech project, and we encourage clients to read it carefully and ask questions.

## Red Flag #5: They Host the Website on Their Personal Account

This is one of the most common problems we see. A developer builds a website and hosts it on their personal hosting account. When the client-developer relationship ends (and it will), the client loses access to their own website. We have seen businesses lose years of SEO progress, content, and customer data because their developer controlled the hosting and refused to transfer it.

**What to look for:** The developer should set up hosting in your name, with you as the account owner. They manage it, but you own it. Your domain should also be registered in your name with your email address.

**What to ask:** "Who will own the hosting account and domain?" The answer should always be "you will." If they hesitate, that is a major red flag.

## Red Flag #6: They Do Not Know About SEO

If your developer builds a website without SEO fundamentals, you will be invisible on Google. SEO is not an add-on — it is part of the build process.

**What to look for:** Developers should understand semantic HTML, proper heading structure, meta tags, image optimisation, page speed, mobile responsiveness, and structured data. They do not need to be SEO experts, but they need to build on a strong foundation.

**What to ask:** "What do you do for SEO as part of the build?" A good answer includes semantic HTML structure, fast hosting, image optimisation, meta tags, sitemap generation, and mobile-first design. A bad answer is "SEO is something you can add later."

## Red Flag #7: They Disappear After Delivery

The launch date is not the end — it is the beginning. Websites need ongoing maintenance: software updates, security patches, content changes, and technical support. Many developers deliver the site, collect payment, and are never heard from again.

**What to look for:** A clear post-launch support plan. Will they fix bugs for free for 30 days? What is their hourly rate for ongoing changes? How quickly do they respond to support requests?

**What to ask:** "What happens if something breaks at 9 PM on a Saturday?" The answer tells you everything about their commitment to client success.

## Green Flags: What to Look For Instead

Now that you know the red flags, here are the qualities of a developer you can trust.

### They Have Process and Systems

A professional developer does not wing it. They have a project management system (even if it is just a Trello board), communication guidelines, a development workflow, and quality assurance steps. They can describe exactly what happens at each stage of your project.

### They Communicate Clearly

You should never have to guess what is happening with your project. A good developer sends regular updates, responds to messages within 24 hours (usually much faster), and proactively communicates delays or changes.

### They Educate, Not Just Build

A great developer teaches you how to use your website. They provide training on updating content, explain basic SEO principles, and help you understand the tools you are using. They want you to succeed because your success is their best marketing.

### They Recommend What You Need, Not What Makes Them Money

If you need a simple 5-page website, a good developer will tell you that. They will not try to upsell you on a complex web application you do not need. Trustworthy developers turn down projects that are not the right fit for their skills or the client's needs.

## Questions to Ask Every Developer Before Hiring

Print this list and take it to your next developer meeting:

1. "Can you show me 3-5 live websites you have built, and what was your role on each?"
2. "Walk me through your process from start to finish."
3. "Who will own the domain, hosting, and code?"
4. "What do you do for SEO during the build?"
5. "What happens after launch — do you offer support and maintenance?"
6. "Can you provide references from past clients?"
7. "What is included in your quote, and what is not?"
8. "How do you handle revisions and changes during the project?"
9. "What is your policy on project delays?"
10. "Do you have a contract, and can I review it?"
