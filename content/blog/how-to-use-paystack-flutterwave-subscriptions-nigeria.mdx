Every month, the same dance. You run a small gym in Ikeja, a food subscription box in Lekki, an online course, or a cleaning service in Abuja. Every month, you send reminders on WhatsApp: "Good morning, your subscription is due." Some customers pay. Some say "I will send it tomorrow." Some never reply.

There is a better way. Paystack and Flutterwave both let you collect subscription payments automatically. Your customer signs up once, enters their card details, and the payment is collected on the same date every month without you sending a single reminder. Failed cards get retried automatically. You get a dashboard that shows who paid, who is overdue, and how much recurring revenue you can expect next month.

This guide walks you through setting up subscriptions with Paystack or Flutterwave, step by step, in plain language. No coding is required. By the end, you will know which platform fits your business, how to create your first plan, how to price it in naira, and how to handle failed payments and cancellations.

## What Are Subscription Payments, Really?

A subscription payment is an agreement: your customer authorises you to charge their card a fixed amount on a regular schedule — weekly, monthly, quarterly, or yearly — until they cancel. The customer does not need to remember to pay. You do not need to remember to ask.

In Nigeria, subscriptions already power many businesses you use every day: DSTV and GOtv, Netflix, Spotify, gym memberships, internet providers, and food boxes delivered weekly. The model works because it turns one-off sales into predictable income.

For a small business, predictable income changes everything. Instead of starting every month at zero, you start with the money your subscribers will pay. You can plan stock, pay staff, and invest in growth with confidence.

Subscriptions also change your relationship with customers. A one-time buyer can disappear. A subscriber is a relationship. They expect regular value, and in return they give you regular revenue.

## Paystack or Flutterwave: Which One Should You Choose?

Both platforms are excellent, both are built for Nigeria, and both support naira subscriptions. The right choice depends on your business.

**Choose Paystack if** you want the simplest setup and the cleanest dashboard. Paystack is owned by Stripe, and its subscription tools are straightforward: create a plan, share a payment link, and customers subscribe. Paystack supports Nigerian cards, bank transfers, USSD, and mobile money, and its dashboard makes it easy to see active subscribers, failed payments, and monthly recurring revenue at a glance.

**Choose Flutterwave if** you need to accept payments from customers outside Nigeria or you want more payment methods. Flutterwave supports cards from many countries, M-Pesa, mobile money, and bank transfers across Africa. If your customers include people in Ghana, Kenya, the UK, or the US, Flutterwave's international coverage is a real advantage.

**You can also use both.** Some businesses offer Paystack as the main option and Flutterwave as a backup for customers whose cards fail on one platform.

The steps are similar on both platforms, and where they differ, both versions are explained. If you are just starting, pick the one you already have an account with. If you have neither, start with Paystack.

## Before You Start: What You Need

Setting up subscriptions takes less than a day if you have the basics ready:

**A registered business or a sole trader setup.** Both Paystack and Flutterwave will ask for your business name, your CAC registration if you have one, and your BVN. A registered business name makes approval faster, but sole traders can also sign up with their personal details.

**A Nigerian bank account.** This is where your subscription money lands. Both platforms settle into Nigerian bank accounts, usually within one business day. Make sure the account name matches your name on the platform, or payouts can be delayed.

**A BVN and a valid ID.** Both platforms will ask for your Bank Verification Number and a government-issued ID — a national ID card, driver's licence, international passport, or voter's card. This is a regulatory requirement from the Central Bank of Nigeria. Have them ready before you start.

**A product or service worth subscribing to.** A subscription only works if the customer gets regular value. A weekly food box, a monthly beauty box, a gym membership, a cleaning service, an online course with new lessons, or a software tool all work. A product a customer buys once a year does not.

**A price in naira.** Decide your price before you create the plan. Think about what the customer gets, what it costs you to deliver, and what similar services charge. A price that is too low will not cover your costs. A price that is too high will scare customers away.

## Step 1: Create Your Account and Complete Verification

If you do not already have an account, go to paystack.com or flutterwave.com and sign up with your business email. Use an email you check every day — payment notifications and payout confirmations all go there.

After signing up, complete the verification steps:

1. Enter your business details: business name, what you sell, and your business address.
2. Add your bank account details for payouts.
3. Upload your ID and enter your BVN.
4. Wait for approval. This usually takes a few hours to two business days.

While you wait, you can explore the dashboard in test mode. Both platforms let you create plans and test the payment flow with test cards before you go live.

Use a strong, unique password and turn on two-factor authentication immediately. Your payment dashboard controls your business income.

## Step 2: Create Your First Subscription Plan

A plan is the product your customer subscribes to. It has a name, a price, and a billing frequency.

**On Paystack:**

1. Log in to your dashboard and go to the "Subscriptions" or "Plans" section.
2. Click "Create Plan."
3. Enter the plan name — for example, "Monthly Lunch Pack" or "Premium Gym Membership."
4. Enter the amount in kobo. Paystack stores amounts in kobo, so ₦25,000 is entered as 2500000. Double-check your zeros before saving.
5. Choose the billing interval: daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly, or yearly.
6. Add a short description of what the subscriber gets.
7. Save the plan. Paystack gives you a payment link for that plan, which you can share anywhere.

**On Flutterwave:**

1. Log in to your dashboard and go to "Payments" and then "Payment Plans."
2. Click "Create Payment Plan."
3. Enter the plan name, amount in naira, and billing frequency.
4. Add a description and, if you like, an image.
5. Save the plan. Flutterwave gives you a payment link for that plan too.

Create one plan per offering. A gym might have three plans: Basic ₦15,000 a month, Standard ₦25,000 a month, and Premium ₦40,000 a month. Keep the names clear — customers should understand what they are buying from the name alone.

## Step 3: Share Your Payment Link and Get Your First Subscribers

You do not need a website to start collecting subscriptions. The payment link is enough.

**Share it on WhatsApp.** Send the link to your broadcast list or your customer groups with a short message: "You can now subscribe to our monthly lunch pack and never worry about daily payments. Tap here to subscribe — ₦25,000 a month, cancel anytime."

**Add it to your Instagram bio.** Put the payment link directly in your bio, and add a Story highlight called "Subscribe" that explains the plans.

**Put it on your website.** If you have a website, add a "Subscribe" or "Pricing" page with your plans and the payment links. A clear pricing page builds trust and answers questions before customers ask them. If you need a simple way to share your payment and contact links in one place, a digital business card works well — Joetech's free digital business card tool at joetech360.ng/tools/digital-business-card lets you put all your links on one page.

**Offer it at the point of sale.** When a customer buys from you in person, mention the subscription. "You buy this every week — would you like to subscribe and save yourself the stress of paying each time?"

**Start with your best customers.** Your first subscribers should be the people who already buy from you regularly. They trust you and know your quality. Message ten of them personally and invite them to subscribe.

## Step 4: Price Your Subscription So It Works for Both Sides

Pricing is where most subscription businesses succeed or fail. Price too low and you lose money on every subscriber. Price too high and nobody signs up. Here is how to get it right.

**Start with your costs.** Add up everything it costs you to serve one subscriber for one billing period: ingredients, packaging, delivery, staff time, and platform fees. Both Paystack and Flutterwave charge a percentage on each payment — check the current fee on their website before you set your price. Your price must cover your costs plus a margin.

**Look at what customers already pay.** If a customer currently buys your product four times a month at ₦7,000 each, that is ₦28,000 a month. A subscription at ₦25,000 a month saves them ₦3,000 and saves you the stress of chasing four separate payments.

**Offer tiers.** Give customers a choice. A basic tier at a lower price, a standard tier with the most popular features, and a premium tier for customers who want more. Most customers will choose the middle tier.

**Consider an annual discount.** Offer twelve months for the price of ten, or a similar discount. Annual subscribers give you cash upfront. Just make sure you can deliver for a full year before you offer it.

**Test before you commit.** Launch with a small group — twenty or thirty customers — and watch what happens. If everyone signs up instantly, your price may be too low. If nobody signs up, it may be too high. Adjust after a month of real data, not guesswork.

## Step 5: Handle Failed Payments Before They Cost You Subscribers

Failed payments are the biggest silent killer of subscription businesses in Nigeria. A customer's card expires, their account is empty on debit day, or their bank blocks the transaction. If you do nothing, that subscriber is gone — and many will not tell you why.

**Understand why payments fail.** The most common reasons in Nigeria are insufficient funds, expired cards, bank transaction limits, and network problems during the debit attempt. None of these mean the customer wants to cancel.

**Both platforms retry automatically.** Paystack and Flutterwave both retry failed subscription payments automatically over several days. But do not rely on retries alone — many customers need a nudge.

**Message the customer quickly.** When a payment fails, send a polite WhatsApp message the same day: "Good morning! Your subscription payment for this month did not go through — it may be your card or your account balance. Please check and let us know, or update your card here: [link]. Thank you!"

**Give customers a way to update their card.** Both platforms provide a link where customers can update their card details. Share that link in your message.

**Set a grace period.** Do not cut off a subscriber the moment a payment fails. Give them five to seven days to fix the issue. A customer given a grace period feels respected, and most will pay.

**Track your failed payment rate.** Once a month, check how many subscription payments failed and how many were recovered. If many fail, look for a pattern: the same bank, the same day, or the same plan. Salary week — the last few days of the month — is often the worst time to debit, because accounts are empty. Consider debiting on the 1st or 2nd of the month instead, when salaries have just landed.

## Step 6: Manage Cancellations and Keep Subscribers Happy

Every subscription business has cancellations. The goal is not zero cancellations — that is impossible. The goal is to keep them low and learn from every one.

**Make cancelling easy.** Hiding the cancel button is the worst thing you can do. A customer who cannot cancel will dispute the charge with their bank instead, and that costs you money and can get your account flagged. Both Paystack and Flutterwave let customers cancel from their subscription page. Trust is what keeps subscribers, not traps.

**Ask why they are leaving.** When someone cancels, send a short message: "Thank you for being with us. Please, what made you cancel? Your answer will help us improve." Some will tell you the real reason — the price, the quality, the delivery, or simply that they no longer need it.

**Offer a pause instead of a cancellation.** Many customers cancel because of a temporary problem — they are travelling, or money is tight this month. Offer to pause for one month instead. A paused subscriber is far more likely to return than a cancelled one.

**Win back old subscribers.** Once a month, message customers who cancelled in the past: "We have added something new you might like — here is 10% off if you resubscribe this week."

**Keep delivering value.** The best retention strategy is not a trick. It is a good product, delivered on time, every time. Send subscribers a small surprise now and then — a free sample, an extra item, a thank-you message.

## Step 7: Track Your Numbers Every Month

Subscriptions give you numbers that one-off sales never do. Check them monthly, and they will tell you how healthy your business is.

**Monthly recurring revenue (MRR).** Add up what all your active subscribers pay each month. This is your baseline income. If your MRR is growing, your business is growing. If it is shrinking, something needs attention.

**Churn rate.** This is the percentage of subscribers who cancel each month. If you have 100 subscribers and 5 cancel, your churn is 5%. A high churn rate means something is wrong — price, quality, or delivery.

**Failed payment rate.** How many payments failed, and how many were recovered after retries and reminders?

**Average revenue per subscriber.** Total subscription revenue divided by the number of subscribers. If this number rises, your tiers or upsells are working.

Both Paystack and Flutterwave show these numbers in their dashboards. Set aside one hour on the first Monday of each month to review them, and write the numbers down so you can compare month to month.

## Common Mistakes Nigerian Businesses Make with Subscriptions

**Starting without a clear product.** A subscription needs a clear, repeatable promise. "Pay ₦20,000 a month and get something" is not a promise. "Pay ₦20,000 a month and get a weekly food box delivered every Friday" is a promise. Be specific.

**Forgetting platform fees.** Both platforms charge a fee on every transaction. If you forget to include it in your price, your margin disappears as you grow. Check the current fee schedule before you launch, and review it when fees change.

**Ignoring failed payments.** A failed payment is not a lost customer — until you ignore it. Message the customer the same day, every time.

**Overpromising.** Do not promise daily delivery if you can only manage weekly. Missed deliveries are the fastest way to lose subscribers.

## A Simple Launch Plan for Your First 30 Days

Here is a practical plan to go from zero to your first paying subscribers in a month.

**Week 1: Set up.** Create your Paystack or Flutterwave account, complete verification, and create your first plan. Test the payment link yourself to make sure it works.

**Week 2: Invite your best customers.** Message your ten most loyal customers personally. Explain the subscription, share the link, and offer a small founding-member discount — 10% off for the first three months.

**Week 3: Announce publicly.** Post on Instagram, TikTok, and WhatsApp Status. Explain what subscribers get, the price, and how to sign up. Share the payment link.

**Week 4: Review and adjust.** Check your numbers. How many subscribers signed up? Did any payments fail? What questions did customers ask most? Adjust based on what you learned.

Thirty days from now, you could have ten, twenty, or fifty subscribers paying you automatically every month.

## Start With One Plan and One Customer

You do not need a big launch, a website, or a team to start. You need one plan, one payment link, and one customer willing to subscribe. Create the account today and message your best customer tomorrow morning.

The businesses that win in Nigeria are not always the biggest. They are the ones that make it easy for customers to pay, and that keep showing up with value every single month.
