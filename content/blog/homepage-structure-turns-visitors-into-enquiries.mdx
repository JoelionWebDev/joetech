Imagine a potential customer finds your business online, clicks your website, and leaves within seconds. It happens every day, and it is rarely because the business is bad. It is usually because the homepage did not answer the visitor's questions fast enough.

Your homepage is not a brochure. It is a conversation. A visitor arrives with questions, and your page must answer them in the right order: What is this? Is it for me? Can I trust it? What do I do next? When a homepage answers those questions clearly, visitors stay, read, and enquire. When it does not, they go back to search results and try your competitor.

This guide covers the homepage structure that works for Nigerian service businesses, from salons and restaurants to law firms, schools, and hotels. Note that this is about homepages, not landing pages. A landing page is built around one offer and one action, while a homepage must introduce your whole business. If you want the landing page side of the story, read our guide on the [anatomy of a high-converting landing page](/blog/anatomy-high-converting-landing-page) to see how the two differ.

## What a visitor is really asking

Put yourself in the visitor's shoes. Someone in Lekki searches for a service, clicks your site, and in the first few seconds they are silently asking:

- Am I in the right place?
- Do you offer what I need?
- Are you credible and professional?
- How do I reach you or take the next step?

If your homepage opens with a vague slogan, a slideshow of stock photos, or a long story about your company history, none of those questions gets answered. The visitor has to work to understand you, and most people will not bother. Your job is to remove that work.

## The order that works

A homepage that converts usually follows this order from top to bottom:

1. A clear headline saying what you do and who you serve
2. Proof that you are real and trustworthy
3. Your services, described in plain language
4. How working with you works
5. One clear next step

You need the right information, in the right order.

## 1. A headline that says what you do

Your headline is the most important sentence on your website. It should tell a stranger, in one line, what your business does and who it is for. Avoid clever wordplay that only makes sense after someone already knows you.

Compare these two approaches. A vague headline says something like "Welcome to excellence." A clear headline says something like "Affordable bridal makeup and hairstyling in Lekki, Lagos." The second one tells the visitor immediately whether the page is relevant to them.

A good headline formula is simple: what you do, plus who you serve, plus where you serve them if location matters. Add one short supporting sentence underneath that explains the main benefit. For example, a catering business might add: "We cook, deliver, and serve for weddings and corporate events, so you can enjoy your day without worrying about the food."

## 2. Proof, right under the headline

Trust comes next, and it must come early. A new visitor does not know you, so give them reasons to believe you before you ask them for anything.

Useful trust signals include the number of years you have operated, the areas you serve, recognisable types of clients you work with, and clear contact details. A visible phone number or WhatsApp button, a real Lagos address, and links to your social pages all signal that a real business sits behind the website. A short list of services with prices left out is fine here; the point is credibility, not a full sales pitch.

## 3. Your services, in plain language

Next, show what you offer, using the words your customers use. A visitor should be able to scan your services in seconds and recognise their own need on the list.

Keep each service description to one or two short sentences focused on the outcome, not the technical process. Instead of writing about your internal methods, write about what the customer gets. For a cleaning company, "Deep cleaning for homes and offices in Lagos, including kitchens, bathrooms, and post-renovation cleanup" is far more useful than a paragraph about cleaning equipment.

If you offer many services, group them into a handful of clear categories and link each one to its own detailed page. A homepage that tries to explain everything becomes a wall of text that nobody reads.

## 4. How working with you works

Many visitors hesitate because they do not know what happens next. Will they need to pay immediately? How long does the service take? Do they need to visit your office first? Answer those worries with a simple three-step process.

For most service businesses, the steps look like this: reach out through the website or WhatsApp, discuss your needs and agree on the details, then receive the service on the agreed date. Write your own version in your own words, but keep it to three or four steps at most. When people can see the path ahead, they feel safer taking the first step.

## 5. One clear next step

Every homepage needs a clear call to action, and ideally just one main action repeated in a few places: at the top, in the middle, and at the bottom of the page. The action should match what your customers are ready to do. For many Nigerian service businesses, that action is sending a WhatsApp message, calling, or filling in a short enquiry form.

Avoid giving visitors five different buttons that compete for attention. If you ask them to call, chat, subscribe, download, and follow you all at once, they often do nothing at all. Pick the action that leads most directly to a conversation, make the button easy to find, and use direct words like "Chat with us on WhatsApp" or "Request a quote."

## Mistakes that push visitors away

Watch out for these common homepage mistakes:

- A headline that describes nobody and promises nothing specific
- Large images or videos that push the important information far down the page
- No phone number, WhatsApp link, or contact details above the bottom of the page
- Long paragraphs about the company before the visitor learns what you sell
- Outdated information, broken links, or photos that do not match the real business

Fixing even two of these often improves enquiries noticeably, because the problem was never traffic alone. It was a homepage that made visitors work too hard.

## A simple homepage checklist

Before you publish or redesign your homepage, check it against this list:

- Does the headline say what you do and who you serve?
- Can a stranger find your main services within seconds?
- Is there a trust signal near the top of the page?
- Is the next step obvious, with a working button or link?
- Does the page load quickly and read well on a phone?
- Is your contact information correct and easy to find?

If you answer no to any of these, fix that section first. Small, focused changes beat a complete redesign that never gets finished.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Should my homepage talk about my company history?**

A short line about your experience is useful, but history should not come first. Visitors care about their own problem before they care about your story. Put what you do, proof, and services first, and keep the fuller story for your about page.

**How long should a homepage be?**

Long enough to answer the visitor's questions, and no longer. Most service business homepages do well with a headline section, proof, services, a simple process, and a call to action. If the page feels endless, trim it and move detail to dedicated service pages.

**Do I need a slider or video at the top?**

No. Sliders and background videos often slow the page down and push your headline out of view. A single clear headline with one strong image usually performs better and loads faster, especially for visitors on mobile data.

**What is the difference between a homepage and a landing page?**

A homepage introduces your whole business to visitors who may need different things. A landing page focuses on one offer and one action, usually for visitors arriving from a specific advert or campaign. Both matter, but they do different jobs, which is why they need different structures.
