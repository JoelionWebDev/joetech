TikTok is no longer just a place for dance videos and comedy skits. In Nigeria, it has become a serious marketplace. Every day, sellers in Lagos, Abuja, Ibadan and Port Harcourt post short videos of wigs, sneakers, skincare, food, and phone accessories — and buyers order straight from those videos without ever leaving the app.

That is the power of TikTok Shop. A customer watches a 30-second video, taps the small shopping bag icon, sees the price, and checks out. There is no long chat, no "DM for price", and no lost customer who saw your video at midnight and forgot about it by morning.

But TikTok Shop also rewards sellers who do it properly and punishes those who cut corners. A shop with blurry photos, vague descriptions, late deliveries and no replies will get poor reviews, and TikTok will show its videos to fewer people. A shop with clear listings, honest videos, fast replies and reliable delivery will grow steadily, one video at a time.

This guide explains how to sell on TikTok Shop in Nigeria, step by step. You will learn what you need to register, how to set up your shop, how to list products that people actually buy, how to make videos that sell without a big budget, and how to handle delivery, payments and customer complaints.

## Why TikTok Shop Works for Nigerian Sellers

TikTok works because people discover products there by accident. On Instagram, a buyer usually searches for a shop they already know. On TikTok, a buyer who was watching comedy can stumble on your video about a ₦12,000 handbag, like what they see, and buy it in two minutes. That discovery is hard to get anywhere else for free.

The audience is also large and young. Millions of Nigerians open TikTok every day, many of them between 18 and 35, which is the age group that buys fashion, beauty, gadgets and food online most often. If your product suits that audience, TikTok is where they already spend their time.

Video builds trust faster than a photo. A photo can hide a bad wig, a small bag, or a faded fabric. A video shows the true colour, the real size, and how the product moves. When Blessing, a hair seller in Ikeja, started filming herself wearing each wig and shaking her head to show the movement, her orders rose sharply. Customers told her they bought because they could see the hair was real and full, not just a filtered photo.

TikTok Shop also keeps the whole sale in one place. The customer sees the video, the price, the delivery fee, and the reviews together. That removes friction. The fewer steps between "I like this" and "I have paid", the more sales you will make.

## What You Need Before You Register

Before you open the TikTok Seller Centre, gather your details so the process goes smoothly. You will need a TikTok account for your business, separate from your personal account. Use your business name, a clear profile photo of your logo or your products, and a short bio that says what you sell and where you deliver.

You will also need a valid means of identification, such as a National Identity Number slip, a voter's card, a driver's licence, or an international passport. TikTok uses this to verify that you are a real person. Use your real name as it appears on the ID, because a mismatch can delay approval.

You need a Nigerian bank account in your name or your business name, so TikTok can pay your sales into it. Make sure the account name matches your registration details. You will also need a phone number and an email address that you check regularly, because TikTok sends verification codes and order alerts there.

Prepare your shop details too. Decide your shop name, write a short description of what you sell, and choose your main product category. If you sell several things, pick the category you sell most. You can add other categories later.

Finally, prepare your first five products before you register. Take clear photos of each one in daylight, write down the price, the sizes or colours available, and the weight for delivery. Starting with five ready products is better than opening an empty shop and filling it later, because TikTok shows new shops with real listings to more people.

## How to Set Up Your TikTok Shop Step by Step

First, go to the TikTok Seller Centre on your phone or computer and sign up with your business TikTok account. Choose Nigeria as your country and select whether you are registering as an individual seller or a business. Most small sellers start as individuals and upgrade later.

Second, fill in your personal details exactly as they appear on your ID. Upload a clear photo of your ID and, if asked, a photo of yourself holding it. Do this in good light, with no glare, and make sure every corner of the ID is visible. Blurry uploads are the most common reason for delays.

Third, add your bank account details. Double-check the account number before you submit it. A wrong digit means your money will not arrive, and fixing it takes time. TikTok will usually send a small verification or ask you to confirm the account name.

Fourth, set up your shop profile. Add your shop name, logo, and a short description. Use the same name and logo as your Instagram and WhatsApp Business, so customers recognise you everywhere. Add your warehouse or pickup address — your shop in Lekki, your home in Surulere, or wherever you pack orders. TikTok uses this to calculate delivery and to know where riders will collect parcels.

Fifth, set your shipping options. TikTok works with logistics partners in Nigeria, and you can also arrange your own delivery for some orders. Check the delivery fees TikTok shows for Lagos, Abuja, and other states, and make sure they make sense for your products. A ₦3,000 delivery fee on a ₦5,000 item will kill sales, so think about free delivery for orders above a certain amount if your margin allows it.

Sixth, add your products. For each product, upload at least three clear photos, write a title that includes the product name and a key detail, and fill in the description honestly. Set the price, the stock quantity, and the available sizes or colours. Do not copy another seller's photos or description. Use your own, because customers will compare what arrives with what they saw.

Seventh, wait for approval, then publish your first video. Approval usually takes a short time if your documents are clear, but be patient and do not create a second account while you wait. Once approved, link your products to a video and publish. Your shop is live.

## List Products That People Actually Buy

A good listing answers every question a buyer might ask, so they do not have to message you before ordering. Start with a clear title. "Human Hair Wig, 22 Inches, Body Wave, Natural Black" tells a buyer far more than "Quality Wig". Include the size, material, colour, and length where they matter.

Your photos matter even more. Use your real product, not a supplier's photo from the internet. Photograph the front, the back, and a close-up of the material or detail. If you sell clothes, show them on a person or a mannequin, not crumpled on a bed. If you sell food, show the real portion in a real pack, not a picture from Google. Tunde, who sells sneakers in Yaba, photographs each pair from the side, the sole, and the inside label. His customers say the sole photo is what convinced them the shoes were genuine.

Write an honest description. State the material, the size range, what is included, and how to care for the product. If a dress runs small, say so and advise buyers to order a size up. Honesty like that reduces returns and bad reviews, which is worth far more than one extra sale.

Price your products carefully. Check what similar sellers charge for the same quality, then set your price to cover your cost, the TikTok commission, delivery packaging, and a profit. If your cost is ₦8,000, TikTok takes a commission, and packaging costs ₦500, selling at ₦9,000 leaves you almost nothing. Selling at ₦13,500 gives you room to run a small discount later and still profit. Do not set prices by copying the cheapest seller. That seller may be selling a lower quality product, or may be losing money without knowing it.

Keep your stock honest. If you have three pieces of a dress left, set the stock to three. Overselling — taking orders for products you do not have — leads to cancelled orders, angry customers, and a shop rating that drops fast.

## Make Short Videos That Sell

On TikTok, your video is your shop window, your salesperson, and your advert all at once. You do not need a studio or an expensive camera. Your phone, daylight, and a clean background are enough.

The first three seconds decide everything. Start with the product in use, a clear result, or a direct hook: "This ₦15,000 bag fits your laptop, your lunch, and your charger." Do not start with a long greeting or your logo. Viewers scroll past slow starts in a second.

Show the product honestly. Film it from different angles, show it being worn or used, and show the size next to something familiar, like your hand or a phone. If you sell a cooking pot, show food cooking in it. If you sell a dress, walk in it and sit in it. Customers want to know what life with the product looks like, not just what it looks like on a shelf.

Keep videos short, usually 15 to 45 seconds. One product, one clear message, one call to action: "Tap the orange bag to order" or "Link in the shop, only a few pieces left". Long videos that try to show ten products at once rarely sell any of them.

Post regularly. Aim for one to three videos a day when you are starting, using different angles for the same product: one video showing the colour, one showing how to style it, one answering a common question. You do not need a new product every day. A new angle on the same product can reach a new set of buyers.

Answer questions with videos. When a customer asks "Does this wig come in brown?", reply with a short video showing the brown version. When someone asks "Is this shoe true to size?", film yourself wearing your normal size. These reply videos are often the ones that sell most, because they answer a real doubt from a real buyer.

## Pricing, Delivery and Payments on TikTok Shop

TikTok takes a commission on each sale, and the rate can change, so check the current rate in your Seller Centre before you set your prices. Build that commission into your price from the start. Also budget for packaging: a sturdy bag, a box, or a bubble wrap for fragile items. A customer who receives a crushed cake or a torn dress will not order again, no matter how good your video was.

Delivery speed matters. TikTok shows buyers how fast you usually ship, and buyers choose faster sellers. Pack orders the same day when you can, or at least the next day. Keep a small stock of packaging at home — bags, boxes, tape, and a marker — so you are not running to the market for packaging each time an order comes in.

Use the delivery options TikTok provides where possible, because they come with tracking. A buyer who can track a parcel from Lekki to Enugu is calmer than one who has no idea where their order is. If you use your own dispatch rider for Lagos orders, share the rider's number and the expected delivery time with the buyer immediately after they order.

Payments go to your bank account through TikTok's settlement system, usually after the buyer confirms delivery or after a set number of days. Do not expect the money the same hour you ship. Plan your cash flow around that delay. Keep enough money aside to buy new stock and pay for packaging while you wait for settlements.

Handle complaints quickly and politely. If a customer says the size is wrong or the item arrived damaged, reply the same day, apologise, and offer a clear solution: a replacement, a refund, or an exchange. A polite, fast reply can turn an angry customer into a repeat buyer. A slow or rude reply becomes a one-star review that every future buyer will read.

## Common Mistakes New TikTok Sellers Make

The first mistake is using another seller's photos or videos. Customers will notice when the product that arrives looks nothing like the video, and TikTok can also penalise copied content. Always film your own stock.

The second mistake is hiding the price or writing "DM for price" on a TikTok Shop listing. The whole point of the shop is that the price is visible and the buyer can check out at once. A hidden price sends buyers to a competitor whose price is clear.

The third mistake is posting once and waiting. TikTok rewards consistency. One video a week is not enough to learn what your audience likes. Post daily for at least a month, watch which videos get orders, and make more videos like those.

The fourth mistake is ignoring comments and messages. A buyer who asks a question in your comments and gets no reply will buy from a seller who answers. Reply to comments every day, even with a short answer, and pin the most helpful answers to the top.


## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need a registered business to sell on TikTok Shop in Nigeria?

No, you can start as an individual seller with a valid ID and a bank account in your name. Registering your business with the CAC helps with trust and with opening a business bank account later, but it is not required to open a TikTok Shop as an individual.

### How much does it cost to open a TikTok Shop?

Opening a shop is free. You pay a commission only when you make a sale, plus the cost of your stock, packaging, and delivery. Check the current commission rate in the Seller Centre before you set your prices, because the rate can change.


### How do I get paid for my TikTok Shop sales?

TikTok settles payments to the bank account you registered, usually after the buyer confirms delivery or after a set settlement period. The money does not arrive instantly, so keep enough cash aside for new stock and packaging while you wait for settlement.

### Why are my videos getting views but no orders?

Views without orders usually mean the price, the photos, or the trust is missing. Check that your price is visible and fair, that your photos show the real product, and that your shop has reviews and clear delivery details. Try a video that shows the product in use and ends with a clear instruction to tap the shopping bag.

### What should I do if a customer complains about a product?

Reply the same day, apologise, and offer a clear solution, such as a replacement, a refund, or an exchange. Do not argue in public comments. A fast, polite response protects your shop rating and often turns a complaint into a repeat order.


## What to Do Next

1. Create a business TikTok account with your shop name, logo, and a short bio that says what you sell.
2. Gather your ID, bank account details, and clear photos and prices for your first five products.
3. Register in the TikTok Seller Centre, upload clear documents, and add your bank account carefully.
4. Set up your shop profile, shipping options, and delivery fees, then list your five products with honest titles and photos.
5. Film and publish your first product video, starting with the product in use in the first three seconds.
6. Post one to three short videos a day for a month, reply to every comment, and track which videos bring orders.
7. Pack orders the same day where possible, share tracking details, and reply to complaints the same day.
8. Review your numbers weekly, restock your best sellers, and add one new product at a time.
