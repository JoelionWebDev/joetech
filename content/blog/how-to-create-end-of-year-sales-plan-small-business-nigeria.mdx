For many Nigerian small businesses, December decides the year. Salaries, bonuses, parties, weddings, church events, travel and gift buying all come together in a few busy weeks. Customers who were quiet in October suddenly want clothes, food, hampers, shoes, phones, cakes and home items. If you plan well, you can sell more in December than in two normal months. If you do not plan, you will run out of stock, miss deliveries, or spend money on adverts that bring no sales.

An end-of-year sales plan is simply a written plan for what you will sell, to whom, at what price, and how you will reach them, from late November through early January. It does not need to be long or complicated. This guide shows you how to build one in a few hours, using your own sales records, your customers and a realistic budget.

## Start by Reviewing Last Year and This Year

Before you plan December, look back. What sold best last December? What sold slowly? Which customers bought the most? Which offers worked and which ones wasted money? If you kept records, check them. If you did not, ask yourself what you remember: the products people asked for repeatedly, the days you were busiest, and the items you had to turn customers away from because you were out of stock.

Also review this year so far. Which products have sold steadily from January to October? Which ones only sell at certain times? A product that sells well all year is a safe bet for December. A product that only sold once may not deserve a large December order. Use your own numbers, not another seller's success story. Your customers and your area are different.

Write down three lessons from last year. For example: "We ran out of children's shoes in week two", "Our hamper sold well but delivery was late", or "Our discount was too deep and profit was low". Those three lessons should shape this year's plan. A plan that ignores last year's mistakes will repeat them.

Set a simple sales goal. It could be a total amount, such as ₦500,000 in December sales, or a number of orders, such as 80 orders. Make the goal realistic for your size. A goal that is too high will push you to overstock. A goal that is too low will not push you to prepare. Base it on last December plus a small increase you can defend, such as 20 percent more if you now have more customers than last year.

## Choose Your December Products Carefully

December buyers buy gifts, party items, food and things that make a home feel festive. Choose products that fit that mood. For a fashion seller, that may be party dresses, shoes, children's outfits and matching family sets. For a food seller, it may be cakes, small chops, hampers and bulk rice or oil. For a cosmetics seller, it may be gift sets and skincare bundles. For a phone seller, it may be accessories and gift-ready phones.

Do not stock everything. Pick five to ten hero products that you will promote heavily, plus a few supporting items. Hero products should be items you know sell well, that you can source reliably, and that leave a good profit. Supporting items can be smaller, cheaper items that buyers add to an order, such as a scarf with a dress or a small gift box with a hamper.

Check your suppliers early. Talk to them in November about December stock, prices and delivery times. Ask what may run out, what needs a deposit, and how long delivery will take. Order your hero products early, before the December rush pushes prices up and slows transport. Keep a list of a second supplier for your top two products in case your first supplier runs out.

Plan for different budgets. Some customers will spend ₦50,000 or more on a gift. Others will spend ₦5,000 to ₦10,000. Offer a small gift option, a mid-range option and a premium option. A customer who cannot afford your premium hamper may still buy your small hamper and return next year for a bigger one. A range gives everyone a way to buy from you.

## Set Prices, Offers and a Profit Target

Decide your prices before December starts. Work out the cost of each product, including delivery, packaging and any discount you plan to offer. Then set a price that leaves a profit you can accept. A December sale that brings many orders but little profit is not a success. It is extra work for little reward.

Design one or two clear offers, not five confusing ones. A simple offer is easier to explain and easier for buyers to act on. Examples that work in Nigeria include: buy two, get free delivery within Lagos; a gift bundle at a small saving compared with buying each item alone; or a percentage discount on a specific product for a limited weekend. State the end date clearly and stick to it.

Avoid deep discounts on your bestsellers unless you have a clear reason, such as clearing old stock. A 50 percent discount sounds exciting, but it can wipe out your profit and train customers to wait for discounts. A smaller discount, such as 10 percent, plus free delivery or a small free gift, often protects profit while still attracting buyers.

Set a profit target as well as a sales target. For example: "Sell ₦600,000 worth of goods in December and keep at least ₦150,000 profit after costs." Track your costs daily during December so you know whether you are on track. If a product is selling fast but profit is low, raise the price slightly or reduce the discount. If a product is not selling, do not keep discounting it blindly; check whether the photos, the price or the audience is the problem.

## Plan Your Marketing Week by Week

December marketing works best when it starts early. Begin in mid-November with a teaser: "Our Christmas collection arrives next week." Then launch your main products in the last week of November. That gives buyers time to plan, order and receive delivery before Christmas. Waiting until 20 December to start marketing leaves too little time for delivery, especially for orders outside Lagos.

Plan your posts week by week. Week one can focus on gift ideas and your hero products. Week two can focus on party outfits, food and hampers. Week three can focus on last-order dates, delivery updates and what is still in stock. The final days before Christmas should focus on ready-to-deliver items and same-day pickup, not on items that need days to arrive. After Christmas, plan a short New Year offer for the last week of December.

Use the channels your customers already use: WhatsApp Status, Instagram, Facebook and Telegram. Post clear photos, prices and delivery dates. Share customer reviews and photos of packed orders. Use short videos to show a hamper being packed or a dress being worn. Keep your message consistent across channels so a buyer who sees you on Instagram and then on WhatsApp recognises the same offer.

Set a small advertising budget if you can afford it, and test it early. A small paid post in late November can tell you which product gets the most interest before you spend more in December. Do not spend your whole budget on one advert on one day. Spread it, test two different photos or captions, and put more money behind the one that brings real messages and orders, not just likes.

## Sort Out Stock, Cash Flow and Delivery

Stock is your biggest December risk. Too little stock means lost sales. Too much stock means cash tied up in items that do not sell after January. Order your hero products in good quantity, order supporting items in smaller quantity, and keep a simple stock sheet. Each morning in December, check what is left, what is selling fast, and what needs a reorder. Reorder early; suppliers also get busy in December.

Cash flow needs attention. You will spend money on stock before you make it back from sales. Do not spend all your cash on stock at once. Keep a reserve for delivery, packaging, fuel and small emergencies. If you offer pay on delivery, remember that you will pay the rider or supplier before the customer pays you. Track money daily in December, even if that is only ten minutes each evening with a notebook.

Delivery will be slower in December because roads are busier and couriers handle more parcels. Tell customers honest delivery times and set a last-order date for each area. For example, orders to Abuja or Port Harcourt may need to be placed by 18 December to arrive before Christmas, while Lagos orders may be accepted until 23 December. Share those dates in your posts and in your pinned messages.

Package December orders as gifts where appropriate. A clean box, a ribbon, a thank-you card and a neat label make a gift feel special and encourage the buyer to share a photo. For food hampers, use sturdy boxes and label fragile items. For clothes, use a neat bag and fold items carefully. Good packaging costs a little more, but it reduces damage and improves reviews.

Prepare for returns and complaints. A small number of December orders will have a wrong size, a late delivery or a damaged item. Have a clear, fair policy and apply it calmly. A customer who receives a quick replacement is more likely to buy again in January than a customer who is ignored. Keep a small reserve for replacements so a problem does not become a crisis.

## Prepare Your Team and Your Daily Routine

If you work alone, December will test your time. Write a daily routine: morning stock check, packing orders, replying to messages at set times, and an evening review. Use quick replies on WhatsApp Business for common questions about prices, delivery and last-order dates. That saves hours of typing the same answers.

If you have staff, brief them before December. Explain the hero products, the offers, the prices, the delivery dates and the return policy. Give each person a clear role: one person packs, one person replies to messages, one person handles delivery. Check their work daily in the first week. A small mistake in December, such as sending the wrong size, can create many complaints when order volume is high.

Set working hours you can sustain. December is busy, but working from 6 am to midnight every day will lead to mistakes. Choose hours, tell customers, and keep a short break each day. If you need extra help, hire a temporary assistant for packing or delivery for two or three weeks. Agree pay and hours in advance and train them on your basic process.

Look after yourself and your phone. Keep your phone charged, keep your bank app ready, and keep your order book up to date. A lost order in December is a lost sale and a disappointed customer. Use a simple spreadsheet or notebook and update it after each order, not at the end of the day when you are tired and may forget details.

## Sell to Old Customers First

Your existing customers are your easiest December sales. They already know you and trust you. In mid-November, message your regular buyers personally: "Our Christmas collection is ready. You bought the red dress last year; we now have it in green and blue." A personal message like that feels different from a general broadcast and often leads to an order.

Offer your loyal customers early access. Let them order before you announce a product to the public, or give them a small early-bird price for the first week. That reward makes them feel valued and gives you early sales that help you judge demand. If a product sells out with your loyal customers first, you will know to reorder before the public launch.

Ask for referrals. After a December delivery, ask a happy buyer to recommend you to a friend or family member who needs a gift. A referral in December is powerful because many people are buying gifts for others and need a trusted seller quickly. Make it easy by sharing your WhatsApp link or a simple flyer they can forward.

Follow up after delivery. A short message after a gift arrives — "I hope the hamper arrived well. Thank you for ordering" — opens the door for a review, a photo, or a New Year order. If the buyer is happy, ask if you can share their feedback. Social proof in December, when new buyers are cautious, can be the difference between a sale and a missed sale.

## Track Results Daily and Adjust Quickly

In December, do not wait until January to review your plan. Check three numbers each evening: how much you sold today, which products sold best, and how much stock is left. If a product is selling fast, reorder or promote it more. If a product is not selling after three days of promotion, change the photo, the caption or the offer. If messages are high but orders are low, check whether your price, delivery date or payment steps are confusing buyers.

Keep a simple daily log: date, total sales, number of orders, best-selling product, and any problem that came up. That log will be gold next year. It will tell you exactly when demand rose, which offer worked, and what to stock earlier. Without a log, you will rely on memory and repeat the same guesses next December.

Be ready to stop an offer that is not working. If a discount brings many messages but few payments, the discount may be attracting the wrong buyers or the product may not be right. Stop the offer, try a different product, or change the audience. A plan is a guide, not a prison. The best December sellers adjust quickly based on what their own customers do.

Watch your cash daily. December sales can look large on paper while your bank balance stays low because money is tied up in stock, unpaid deliveries or pay-on-delivery orders. Check your actual bank balance, not just your order total. If cash is tight, slow down new stock orders and focus on selling what you already have.


## Frequently Asked Questions

**When should I start planning for December sales?**
Start in late October or early November. That gives you time to review last year, talk to suppliers, order stock, and start marketing before the rush. Starting in mid-December leaves too little time for delivery and promotion.

**Should I offer discounts in December?**
A small, clear, time-bound offer can help, but avoid deep discounts that wipe out profit. A bundle, free delivery, or a small free gift often works better than a large percentage discount on your bestsellers.

**What if my supplier runs out of stock in December?**
Keep a second supplier for your top products, order early, and tell customers honestly if an item is sold out. Offer an alternative product or a restock date rather than promising delivery you cannot keep.

**How do I handle late deliveries in December?**
Tell customers honest delivery times and last-order dates before they pay. If a delivery will be late, message the customer before the agreed date, explain the delay, and offer a clear new date or a solution.

December rewards the seller who plans early, stocks wisely, prices fairly, markets consistently and delivers on time. Write your plan this week, even if it is only one page. Choose your hero products, set your prices and last-order dates, message your old customers, and start posting in November. Then track your sales daily, adjust quickly, and thank the customers who bought from you. A good December can pay for a slow January and give your business a strong start to the new year.
