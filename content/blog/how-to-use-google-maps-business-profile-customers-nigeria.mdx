A woman in Lekki wants her hair done this Saturday. She does not scroll Instagram for an hour. She opens Google Maps, types "hair salon near me", and looks at the first three results. She checks the photos, reads two or three reviews, checks the opening hours, and taps the phone number or the directions button. Within five minutes, she has chosen a salon. If your business is not on that list, or if your listing looks empty and untrusted, she will never find you — no matter how good your work is.

This is how millions of Nigerians now find local businesses: restaurants in Ikeja, pharmacies in Surulere, fashion shops in Abuja, hotels in Port Harcourt, mechanics in Yaba, and event halls in Lekki. Google Maps and Google Search have become the new yellow pages, the new signboard, and the new word-of-mouth recommendation, all in one place. And the tool that controls how you appear there is free. It is called Google Business Profile — formerly known as Google My Business.

This guide shows you how to set it up, verify it, optimise it, and use it to win customers in Nigeria, step by step, on the phone you already own.

## What Google Business Profile Is — And Why It Matters

Google Business Profile is the free listing that Google shows when someone searches for your business name, or for a type of business near them. It is the box on the right side of a Google search result, and the pin and card on Google Maps, that shows your business name, address, phone number, opening hours, photos, reviews, and a link to your website or WhatsApp.

When a customer searches "restaurant near me" in Lekki, Google does not show every restaurant in Lagos. It shows the ones that are close, relevant, and trusted. Relevance means your listing matches what the customer searched for. Distance means you are near them. Trust means your listing is complete, verified, has recent photos, and has good reviews. Your job is to make your listing as complete, relevant, and trusted as possible, so Google chooses you over the business down the road.

The profile matters for three reasons. First, it is free, and it puts you in front of people who are already ready to buy. A person searching "pharmacy open now in Surulere" is not browsing for fun — they need a pharmacy now. Second, it builds trust before the customer ever speaks to you. Photos, reviews, and accurate hours tell a stranger that you are a real business, in a real place, that other people have used. Third, it works while you sleep. Your listing is visible at midnight, on Sunday, and during a public holiday, even when your shop is closed. Customers can find your hours, your location, and your phone number at any time.

Many Nigerian businesses have a profile already, created automatically by Google or by a customer who checked in. If you have not claimed yours, someone else's information — or wrong information — may be showing. Claiming and correcting it is the first step.

## Step 1: Claim or Create Your Profile

Search for your business name on Google, on your phone or computer. If a profile appears on the right side of the search results or on Google Maps, tap it and look for a link that says "Own this business?" or "Claim this business". If no profile appears, go to google.com/business and create one.

You will need a Google account. Use the same Gmail you use for your business, not a personal account you may lose access to. If your business has a Gmail like joetechorg@gmail.com for business use, use that one, so your staff can help manage the profile later without using your personal email.

Enter your business information carefully:

- **Business name:** Use your real, exact business name, exactly as it appears on your signboard and your CAC registration if you have one. Do not add keywords like "Best Hair Salon in Lekki" to your name — Google can suspend listings that stuff keywords into the name. Your real name is enough.
- **Category:** Choose the category that best describes your main business. A salon should choose "Hair Salon", not "Beauty Salon" if hair is the main service. A restaurant should choose "Restaurant", and add secondary categories like "African Restaurant" or "Fast Food Restaurant" if they apply. The category is one of the strongest signals Google uses to decide when to show you, so choose it carefully.
- **Address:** Enter your exact address, including the street number, street name, area, and city. For example: "14 Admiralty Way, Lekki Phase 1, Lagos". If customers come to you, show the address. If you deliver and do not want customers visiting, you can hide the address and set a service area instead — for example, Lekki, Victoria Island, and Ikoyi. Be honest: a wrong address leads to lost customers and bad reviews from people who could not find you.
- **Phone number:** Use the number customers should call or WhatsApp. In Nigeria, this is usually a mobile number. Make sure it is a number you actually answer during business hours. A profile with a dead number is worse than no profile, because it wastes the customer's time.
- **Website:** Add your website address if you have one, for example your Joetech website. If you do not have a website yet, you can add your Instagram page or your WhatsApp link as a temporary step, but a proper website builds more trust and helps Google understand your business better.
- **Opening hours:** Enter your real hours, including any differences for Saturday and Sunday. Many Nigerian businesses close early on Sunday or open late on Monday. Enter the truth. Nothing frustrates a customer more than driving to a shop Google said was open, only to find it closed.

Take your time with this step. The information you enter here is what Google will show to every searcher, and correcting it later takes time.

## Step 2: Verify Your Business — The Step Most People Skip

Verification proves to Google that your business is real and that you are authorised to manage it. Until you verify, your profile will have limited visibility, and you cannot respond to reviews or post updates.

Google offers several verification methods in Nigeria, and the one you see depends on your business type and location. The most common methods are:

- **Postcard by mail:** Google sends a postcard with a verification code to your business address. This can take one to two weeks in Lagos, sometimes longer. When it arrives, enter the code in your profile dashboard. Keep the postcard safe until you have entered the code.
- **Phone or SMS verification:** Google calls or texts a code to your business phone number. This is faster when it is available, and it is the method most Nigerian businesses prefer when Google offers it.
- **Email verification:** Google sends a code to your business email address. This is available for some business types.
- **Video verification:** Google asks you to record a short video showing your shop front, your signboard, your business name, and the inside of your premises. This is becoming more common in Nigeria, and it is a good way to prove a real location quickly if you have a physical shop.

Follow the instructions Google gives you exactly, and do not try to verify a business you do not own or a location that does not exist. Google can suspend profiles for false verification, and recovering a suspended profile is slow and difficult.

If verification fails, check the common causes: the business name does not match your signboard, the address is incomplete, the phone number is not answered, or the category is wrong. Fix the information, wait a day, and try again. If you run a business from home and do not want your home address public, set a service area and hide the address — Google allows this for service-area businesses like plumbers, cleaners, and delivery services.

## Step 3: Complete Every Section — Google Rewards Complete Profiles

A half-finished profile is like a shop with no signboard and no lights on. Google ranks complete profiles higher, and customers trust them more. Go through every section in your profile dashboard and fill in what applies:

- **Description:** Write two or three short paragraphs about what you do, who you serve, and what makes you different. Use natural language and include the words customers actually search for: "hair salon in Lekki", "African restaurant in Ikeja", "event hall in Chevron". Do not stuff the description with a long list of keywords — write it for a human customer, not for a robot.
- **Services and products:** List your main services with prices where possible. A salon can list "Braids — from ₦15,000", "Wig installation — from ₦10,000". A restaurant can list its signature dishes. A hotel can list its room types and event hall capacity. Prices help customers decide before they call, and they reduce the number of enquiries that go nowhere because the price was not what the customer expected.
- **Attributes:** Tick the boxes that apply: "Wheelchair accessible", "Free Wi-Fi", "Outdoor seating", "Accepts walk-ins", "Family friendly". These small details help customers choose, and they help Google match you to specific searches like "restaurant with outdoor seating in Lekki".
- **Photos:** Add at least ten good photos, and keep adding more every month. See Step 4 below for what to photograph.
- **Questions and answers:** Google lets customers ask questions on your profile, and you can answer them — or you can add your own questions and answers in advance. Add the questions you hear most often: "Do you offer home service?", "What are your opening hours on Sunday?", "Do you have parking?" Answering them on the profile saves you from answering the same question on WhatsApp fifty times.

Completing the profile takes an hour or two, once. The benefit lasts for months and years, because Google keeps showing the profile to every relevant searcher.

## Step 4: Add Photos That Make Customers Choose You

Photos are the most powerful part of your profile. Customers decide in seconds, and a listing with bright, clear, recent photos will win over a listing with no photos or with one dark, blurry image from three years ago.

Photograph these things:

- **The outside of your shop:** The signboard, the entrance, and the street, so customers can recognise it when they arrive. Take the photo in daylight, from across the road, so the whole front is visible.
- **The inside:** Clean tables, comfortable chairs, well-arranged shelves, a tidy workspace. Customers want to see that the place is clean and professional.
- **Your products or your work:** Food on a plate, a finished hairstyle, a completed dress, a set-up event hall, a clean hotel room. Use natural light near a window where possible, and photograph the work at its best.
- **Your team at work:** A stylist braiding hair, a chef plating food, a staff member welcoming a customer. Real people doing real work builds more trust than stock photos from the internet.
- **Your menu, price list, or catalogue:** A clear photo of the menu or the price list helps customers decide before they visit.

Use your phone camera. You do not need a professional photographer to start, though a professional shoot once a year is a good investment if you can afford it. Take photos in daylight, clean the lens, hold the phone steady, and take several shots so you can choose the best one. Avoid filters that change the colour of food or hair — customers want to see the real thing.

Add new photos at least once a month. Fresh photos tell Google your business is active, and they tell customers that what they see is what they will get today, not three years ago. When you launch a new dish, a new style, or a new product, photograph it and add it to the profile the same day.

Never use photos that are not yours, and never use stock photos that show a different shop or a different city. Customers who arrive and find a different place will leave a bad review, and that review will stay on your profile for a long time.

## Step 5: Earn Reviews — And Reply to Every Single One

Reviews are the trust signal that matters most on Google. A business with twenty good reviews will be chosen over a business with no reviews, even if the second business is closer or cheaper. Reviews also help Google rank you higher, because they show that real customers use and like your business.

Ask for reviews, politely and at the right moment. The best time is immediately after a good experience: after a customer has eaten and is smiling, after a hairstyle is finished and the customer is admiring it in the mirror, after a delivery arrives in good condition. Say something simple: "Thank you so much. If you have a minute, a review on Google would really help our small business. I can send you the link on WhatsApp." Then send the link — Google gives you a short review link in your profile dashboard that you can share on WhatsApp.

Make it easy. Do not ask a customer to search for your business and find the review button themselves. Send the direct link. One tap should take them to the review form. If they need help, guide them: tap the link, tap the stars, write one sentence. Many customers are happy to help but will not bother if it takes more than a minute.

Never pay for reviews, and never ask friends who have never used your business to write fake reviews. Google detects fake reviews and removes them, and can suspend your profile. A fake review also hurts you when a real customer arrives and finds the experience does not match the review. Earn reviews honestly, one happy customer at a time.

Reply to every review, good and bad, within a day or two. Thank the happy customers by name, and mention something specific: "Thank you, Mrs Eze, we are so glad you loved the jollof rice. See you next weekend." For a bad review, stay calm, polite, and professional. Apologise for the experience, explain briefly what happened if you know, and invite the customer to contact you directly to fix it. Never argue in public, never insult the customer, and never ignore a bad review. Other customers read your replies, and a calm, caring reply to a complaint builds more trust than a perfect five-star rating with no replies at all.


## A 7-Day Plan to Get Your Profile Working

- **Day 1:** Search for your business on Google and Google Maps. Claim the profile if it exists, or create one if it does not.
- **Day 2:** Enter your correct name, category, address, phone number, website, and opening hours. Start the verification process.
- **Day 3:** Write your description, list your services with prices, and tick the attributes that apply.
- **Day 4:** Take and upload at least ten good photos — outside, inside, products, and team.
- **Day 5:** Add your most-asked questions and answers, and create your review link. Send it to five happy customers on WhatsApp.
- **Day 6:** Post your first update — a new product, an offer, or a behind-the-scenes photo. Reply to any existing reviews.
- **Day 7:** Check your Insights, note how customers are finding you, and set a weekly reminder to post and a monthly reminder to check reviews and photos.

After thirty days, check your Insights again. You should see more searches, more direction requests, and more calls. After ninety days, your profile should be bringing you a steady stream of new customers who found you on Google — customers you did not pay to reach.

## Final Word

Google Maps and Google Business Profile are not just for big companies. They are for the salon in Surulere, the restaurant in Ikeja, the hotel in Lekki, and the fashion shop in Abuja. They are free, they are used by millions of Nigerians every day, and they reward the businesses that are complete, honest, and active.

Claim your profile today, verify it, fill it in completely, add good photos, earn honest reviews, and post every week. The customers searching for what you sell are already on Google. Your job is to make sure that when they search, they find you — and they choose you.
