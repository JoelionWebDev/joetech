Walk into the back room of almost any small shop in Lagos and you will find it: the shelf of shame. Last season's dresses that never sold. Phone cases for models nobody uses any more. Skincare with three months left before expiry. Shoes in sizes 45 and 46 that nobody in the neighbourhood wears. It sits there, gathering dust, while the money you spent buying it sits there with it.

Old stock is not just clutter. It is cash frozen in physical form. Every naira locked in a product that is not selling is a naira you cannot spend on new stock that would sell.

A clearance sale is the fastest honest way to turn that dead stock back into cash. But a badly run clearance can do real damage: customers who learn to wait for discounts and never buy at full price again, a brand that starts to look cheap, and discounts so deep that you lose money on every sale. This guide shows you how to clear old stock properly — quickly, profitably, and without training your customers to devalue your business.

## First, Know Exactly What You Are Clearing

Before you announce anything, do a proper stock count. Go through your shop, your store room and your online listings, and write down every item that has not sold in 60 to 90 days. For each item, note the quantity, the size or variant, the price you paid for it and the price you have been selling it at.

Then sort the stock into three groups. Group A is stock that will sell with a small discount — popular items in the wrong colour, or items that sold well until a newer version arrived. Group B is stock that needs a deep discount — out-of-season items, odd sizes, slow sellers. Group C is stock that will probably never sell at any price — damaged goods, expired products, items for phone models that are now obsolete. Group C should not go into your clearance sale at all.

This sorting matters because it decides your pricing. Most small businesses make the mistake of putting one flat discount on everything — say, 50 percent off all old stock. That gives away margin on Group A items that would have sold at 20 percent off, and it still does not move Group B items that need 60 percent off. Price each group differently and you will recover far more money.

Also check expiry dates and condition honestly. A cream with two months to expiry can be sold at a discount with the date stated clearly. A cream that expired last month cannot be sold at any price. In Nigeria, selling expired cosmetics or food can destroy your reputation in a single WhatsApp group. When in doubt, leave it out.

## Set a Clear Goal and a Hard Deadline

A clearance sale without a goal drifts. Decide before you start what success looks like. Are you trying to recover a specific amount of cash — say, ₦500,000 to fund your next order? Are you trying to clear 80 percent of the old stock before a new season starts? Are you trying to free up shelf space before December? Write the goal down and give the sale a hard end date, usually 7 to 14 days.

A deadline does two things. First, it creates urgency for customers, which is the entire engine of a clearance sale. Second, it protects you from a sale that never ends. The most damaging pattern in Nigerian retail is the "clearance sale" that runs for six months. After the first two weeks, customers stop believing it is a sale at all. They assume the discounted price is the real price, and they will never pay your full price again.

## Price It to Move, Not to Punish Yourself

Pricing a clearance is a balance between recovering money and actually selling the stock. The goal is not to get back everything you spent — that is usually impossible on old stock. The goal is to recover as much as possible while moving the goods.

Start from your cost price, not your selling price. If you bought a dress for ₦8,000 and have been selling it at ₦15,000, a clearance price of ₦10,000 still gives you ₦2,000 profit and is a genuine 33 percent discount for the customer. A clearance price of ₦7,000 loses you ₦1,000 per dress but recovers ₦7,000 of cash that would otherwise be zero when the dress never sells. Both can be sensible, depending on the group.

Use tiered discounts so customers can see the value clearly. For example: Group A at 20 to 30 percent off, Group B at 40 to 50 percent off, and a "buy two, get one free" deal on the slowest items. Tiered pricing also gives you a story to tell: "Up to 50% off" is a stronger headline than a flat 30 percent, and it is honest if some items really are half price.

Beware the deep-discount trap. Discounts above 60 percent send a message: this product was never worth its price, or something is wrong with it. They also attract bargain hunters who will never become regular customers. Keep most discounts between 20 and 50 percent, and reserve anything deeper for genuine bundle deals or the final two days of the sale.

## Choose Your Channels and Prepare Them

Where you sell the clearance matters as much as the discount. Most Nigerian small businesses will sell mainly through WhatsApp and Instagram, and both need preparation.

On WhatsApp, use your broadcast list and your status. Post the clearance items on your status every day of the sale, two or three items at a time, with the old price, the new price and the quantity left. Scarcity is honest here — when there are only three pieces left in size 42, say so. Customers in Nigeria respond to real scarcity because they know from experience that good deals disappear. Also send a single broadcast message on the first day of the sale to your customers who have bought from you before. One message, not five. Repeated broadcasts during a sale annoy people and get your number reported.

On Instagram, create a simple, consistent look for the sale. Use the same background colour or the same "CLEARANCE" label on every post so followers recognise the sale instantly. Post a mix of single-product photos and short videos showing the items. Instagram Reels showing you packing clearance orders or walking through the available items perform well because they feel real and urgent. Pin the main sale post to the top of your profile for the duration of the sale.

If you sell on Jumia or Konga, check their rules before discounting. Both platforms run their own promotional campaigns, and listing your clearance items during a platform-wide sale can multiply your reach. But remember the platform takes a commission, so calculate your clearance price after the commission, not before it.

Your physical shop matters too. If you have a shop in Lekki, Ikeja, or anywhere with foot traffic, make the clearance visible. A clear sign outside, a dedicated clearance rack near the entrance, and price tags that show the old price struck through with the new price beside it. Customers who walk in for one item often leave with two when the clearance rack is easy to browse. Keep the clearance area tidy. A messy pile of discounted goods signals that the products are worthless; a neat rack signals that they are a bargain.

## Write Copy That Sells the Deal Honestly

The words you use decide whether people buy or scroll past. Good clearance copy is short, specific and honest. Bad clearance copy is vague and desperate.

Good copy says: "Clearance: Ankara midi dresses, was ₦18,000, now ₦12,000. Only 6 pieces left — sizes 10, 12 and 14. Delivery available in Lagos today. Send a DM to order." It tells the customer the product, the old price, the new price, what is left and how to buy.

Bad copy says: "Massive clearance sale!!! Everything must go!!! Cheapest prices ever!!!" It says nothing about what is actually for sale, and it sounds like every other desperate sale post on the timeline. Customers have learned to ignore it.

Use urgency, but keep it truthful. "Sale ends Sunday" is a real deadline if the sale genuinely ends on Sunday. "Only 3 left" is real scarcity if there are genuinely three left. Fake urgency is easy to spot — when a seller says "last piece" every week for a month, customers stop believing anything they say.

## Protect Your Brand While You Discount

This is the part most small businesses skip, and it is the part that matters most. A clearance sale is a tool, not an identity. If customers start to think of your business as "the cheap shop", you will struggle to sell anything at full price again.

First, separate the clearance from your main brand. Sell the old stock under a clear "Clearance" label, in a separate highlight on Instagram, a separate section on your website, or a separate rack in your shop. Your new arrivals stay at full price, presented as usual. The separation tells customers that the discount applies to old stock, not to your business.

Second, limit how often you run clearances. Two or three a year is reasonable for most small businesses. Four or more, and customers will simply wait for the next one. If you find yourself clearing stock every month, the problem is not your sale — it is your buying. You are ordering too much of the wrong things, and no amount of discounting will fix that.

Third, never discount your bestsellers or your new arrivals to create a clearance. A clearance is for stock that is not selling. Discounting your popular items trains customers to expect discounts on everything, and it throws away profit you did not need to give up.

## Run the Sale Day by Day

A clearance sale is not a single announcement. It is a short campaign that needs daily attention.

On day one, announce everywhere at once: WhatsApp status and broadcast, Instagram feed and stories, Facebook page, and a message to your previous customers. Lead with your strongest items — the ones with the best discounts that people actually want. First impressions decide whether people pay attention for the rest of the sale.

During the sale, post fresh content every day. Show what has sold out, which is social proof that the sale is real. Share screenshots of customer orders being packed. Each post gives people a reason to check back. But keep the volume sensible: two or three status updates a day, one Instagram post a day, and no more than one broadcast message in the whole sale after the opening announcement.

Track your numbers daily. How many items sold today? How much cash came in? Which items are moving and which are not? If a group of items is not moving after three days, the discount is not deep enough or the items are not being shown properly. Drop the price by another 10 percent or bundle the slow items with a popular item.

Handle orders fast. During a clearance, customers expect quick responses because they know the item may sell out. If you reply six hours late, the customer has usually bought something else. If you are running the sale alone, set specific hours for replying to messages and tell customers when you will respond.

Be firm on the rules. No holding items without payment. This is the single most important operational rule of a clearance sale in Nigeria. Customers will ask you to keep an item for them until salary day, until Friday, until their friend confirms. Every held item that is not paid for is an item you cannot sell to someone ready to pay now. The polite rule is simple: first to pay gets the item. State it in your announcement and stick to it. You will lose a few requests, but you will sell far more overall.

Payment should be before delivery or on pickup, never on credit. Accept bank transfers and payment links through Paystack or Flutterwave so you can confirm payment instantly. If you offer pay on delivery, confirm the customer is genuinely available before dispatching, because a failed delivery during a clearance costs you both the delivery fee and the sale.

## What to Do With What Is Left

Even a good clearance sale leaves something behind. Decide in advance what happens on the day after the sale ends, and do it immediately. Do not let leftover clearance stock drift back onto your main shelves at a confused price.

For items that are still good but simply did not sell, try a different channel. A dress that did not sell on Instagram might sell at a physical market stall in Yaba or at a pop-up. A bundle deal — three slow items for the price of two — can move stock that single-item discounts could not.

For items with a short shelf life, consider giving them away as free gifts with full-price purchases. A small body lotion or a sample-sized cream added to a customer's order costs you little and delights the customer. It is better than throwing the product away, and it introduces customers to a product they may buy at full price later.

For genuinely unsellable items, dispose of them properly and move on. Expired cosmetics, damaged electronics and torn clothing should be discarded. Keeping them in your store room "just in case" only clutters your space and your thinking. Record the loss in your books honestly — it is a lesson, not a failure, if you learn from it.

Most importantly, put your prices back to normal the day the sale ends. Remove the clearance signs, take down the pinned sale post, and stop mentioning discounts. The faster your shop returns to normal, the faster customers return to buying at full price. A sale that lingers is a brand that lingers in discount mode.

## Stop the Problem at the Source: Buy Smarter

A clearance sale fixes the symptom. Smarter buying fixes the cause. After every clearance, sit down and ask why the stock became old in the first place. The answer is usually one of four things.

You ordered too much. The most common cause. A supplier offered a better price for 100 pieces, you bought 100, and you only ever sell 40. Next time, order the smaller quantity at the higher unit price. The extra profit per unit is worthless if 60 units never sell.

You ordered the wrong sizes or variants. If you sell shoes, check which sizes your customers actually buy before ordering. Your past sales records are the best buying guide you have. Order more of what sold and less of what did not.

You followed a trend too late. By the time a trend reaches your supplier, it may already be fading with your customers. Buy small quantities of trendy items, test them fast, and only reorder if they sell within two weeks.

Set a simple rule for yourself going forward: review your stock every month, and flag any item that has not sold in 60 days. Act on it early, while a small discount still works, instead of waiting until the stock is so old that only a deep discount will move it. Monthly stock reviews take an hour and save you thousands of naira.

## Your Clearance Sale Checklist

- Count and sort your stock into groups A, B and C before announcing anything.
- Remove expired or damaged items from the sale entirely.
- Set a cash goal and a hard end date of 7 to 14 days.
- Price from your cost, with tiered discounts of 20 to 50 percent.
- Show the real original price beside the clearance price.
- Prepare your pictures, captions and price list in advance.
- Announce once, everywhere, on day one.
- Post fresh content daily, but do not flood your contacts.
- Insist on payment before holding any item.
- Track sales daily and adjust prices on items that are not moving.
- On the last day, announce the final hours honestly, then close the sale.
- The next morning, restore full prices and remove all sale signs.
- Review what did not sell and change how you buy next time.

Old stock is a normal part of doing business. Every retailer in the world, from a market stall in Onitsha to the biggest stores on the Island, deals with it. The businesses that survive are not the ones that never have old stock. They are the ones that clear it quickly, honestly and without damaging their brand, then buy smarter next time.
