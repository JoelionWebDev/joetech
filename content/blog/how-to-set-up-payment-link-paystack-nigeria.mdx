## The Payment Chase Every Nigerian Seller Knows

A customer orders a dress from your WhatsApp status. You send your account number. She says she will transfer. An hour passes. You message her: "Have you sent it?" She says the network is bad. Another hour passes. She sends a screenshot, but the screenshot is blurry, or it is for the wrong amount, or it turns out to be an old screenshot from a different purchase. By the time the money actually arrives, you have spent more time chasing the payment than you spent selling the dress.

This is the daily reality for thousands of Nigerian small businesses. Bank transfers work, but they are slow, they need manual confirmation, and they invite mistakes and dishonesty. A payment link changes all of that. Instead of sending an account number and waiting, you send a link. The customer taps it, pays with their card, bank transfer, or USSD, and you get an instant notification that the money has arrived. No screenshots, no guessing, no chasing.

Paystack is one of the most widely used payment companies in Nigeria, and its payment link feature is free to set up. You do not need a website, you do not need a developer, and you do not need a registered company to start. This guide walks you through creating your Paystack account, setting up your first payment link, sharing it with customers on WhatsApp and Instagram, handling the common problems, and using payment links to get paid faster every single day.

## What a Payment Link Is, and Why It Beats Bank Transfer

A payment link is a web address that takes a customer straight to a checkout page for one specific product or amount. You create the link once, and you can send it to as many customers as you like. Each customer who opens it sees the product name, the price, and a simple form where they enter their name, email, and payment details.

The biggest advantage is instant confirmation. The moment a customer pays through your link, Paystack sends you an email and a dashboard notification, and the money appears in your Paystack balance. You do not need to ask for a screenshot, and you do not need to log into your bank app to check. The payment either happened or it did not, and you know immediately.

The second advantage is professionalism. A checkout page with your business name looks far more trustworthy than a bare account number in a chat. Customers who have been scammed before, and many Nigerians have, are more willing to pay through a proper checkout page than by sending money to a personal account they cannot verify.

The third advantage is flexibility. Customers can pay by card, by bank transfer, by USSD, or through their bank app. If a customer's card fails, they can switch to transfer without messaging you. If they are on a phone with no banking app installed, USSD still works. You offer one link, and the customer chooses the method that suits them.

## Before You Start: What You Need

Setting up a Paystack payment link takes less than an hour if you have the right things ready. Gather them before you begin so you do not stall halfway through.

You need an email address for your business. This will be your Paystack login and the address where payment notifications are sent. Use a business email if you have one, or a dedicated Gmail address. Do not use an email you cannot access, because Paystack will send verification and payment alerts to it.

You need a Nigerian bank account in your name or your business name. This is where Paystack will settle your money. Paystack settles to Nigerian bank accounts, and you can choose daily or weekly settlement. Have your account number and the bank name ready. The name on the account must match the name you register with, or your verification may fail.

You need a valid means of identification. Paystack will ask for a government-issued ID, such as your National Identity Number, your voter's card, your driver's licence, or your international passport. Have it ready to upload or to enter. This is a standard requirement for financial services in Nigeria, and it protects both you and your customers.

You need your business details. Your business name, what you sell, and your business address or location. If you are a sole trader without a registered business name, you can still sign up as an individual. Paystack supports unregistered businesses and sole traders, which is one of the reasons it works so well for small sellers.

You also need a phone number that can receive SMS, because Paystack will use it to verify your account. Use a number you control and keep active.

## Step 1: Create Your Paystack Account

Open paystack.com in your browser and click Sign Up. Enter your business email address and choose a strong password. Paystack will send a confirmation email. Open it and click the confirmation link to activate your account.

Once you are signed in, Paystack will ask you to complete your business profile. Enter your business name, your phone number, and a short description of what you sell. Choose your business category from the list, such as retail, food, fashion, or services. Pick the closest match. This information appears on your checkout page, so write it clearly and honestly.

Next, add your bank account. Go to Settings, then Bank Accounts, and enter your account number and bank name. Paystack will verify the account name automatically in most cases. If the name does not match, double-check the number and try again. A single wrong digit is the most common cause of a failed verification.

Complete your identity verification. Upload or enter your ID details as requested. This step is sometimes called KYC, which stands for Know Your Customer. It is a legal requirement, and it usually takes a few hours to a day for Paystack to review. While you wait, you can still explore the dashboard and create test payment links, but you may not be able to receive live payments until verification is complete.

Switch your account from test mode to live mode when you are ready to accept real payments. In live mode, real money moves, so double-check your bank details before you switch.

## Step 2: Create Your First Payment Link

Creating a payment link takes about two minutes. From your Paystack dashboard, go to Payments, then Payment Links, and click Create Payment Link.

First, give the link a name your customers will recognise. "Ankara Midi Dress - 18,500" is clear. "Product 1" is not. The name appears on the checkout page, so it should tell the customer exactly what they are paying for.

Next, add a description. One or two sentences are enough. "Ankara midi dress, sizes 8 to 16, available in blue and green. Delivery within Lagos takes 24 hours." This reassures the customer and reduces questions.

Then set the price. Enter the amount in naira, without commas. If you charge 18,500 naira, type 18500. Check the amount twice before saving, because a wrong price on a live link can cost you money or embarrass you with customers.

Choose whether the link is a fixed amount or an open amount. A fixed amount is right for a product with a set price. An open amount lets the customer type in how much they want to pay, which is useful for donations, custom orders, or services where the price varies. For most products, use a fixed amount.

You can also set a redirect link, which is the page the customer sees after paying. If you have a thank-you page on your website, add it here. If you do not have a website, you can leave it blank and Paystack will show its own confirmation page, which is perfectly fine.

Save the link and copy the address. It will look something like paystack.com/pay/yourbusinessname. This is the link you will send to customers. Test it yourself before sharing it widely: open it on your phone, enter a small test amount if you set one, and confirm the checkout page looks right.

## Step 3: Share the Link Where Your Customers Already Are

A payment link only works if customers see it at the moment they are ready to pay. Share it in the places where your sales conversations already happen.

WhatsApp is the most important place. When a customer says "I want to order," reply with the link and a short message: "Thank you. You can pay here: paystack.com/pay/yourbusinessname. Once payment is confirmed, I will arrange delivery." Save this message as a quick reply in WhatsApp Business so you can send it in one tap every time.

Add the link to your WhatsApp status when you post a product. Instead of writing "DM to order," write "Tap the link to pay now" and include the payment link in the status caption or in the first reply. Customers who are ready to buy can pay immediately, even at midnight when you are asleep.

Put the link in your Instagram bio or in your story. Instagram does not allow clickable links in captions, but the link in your bio and the link sticker in stories both work. If you sell one main product, put its payment link in your bio. If you sell many products, create a separate link for each of your best sellers and rotate them.

If you have a website or a simple online catalogue, add a "Pay Now" button that goes to the payment link. You do not need a full online store. A single page with your products and a pay button for each one is enough to start taking orders while you sleep.

## Step 4: Understand Fees and Getting Your Money

Paystack charges a fee on each successful payment, and you should understand it before you start, so there are no surprises. For payments made by Nigerian cards, the fee is 1.5 percent of the transaction plus 100 naira, capped at 2,000 naira per transaction. For international cards, the fee is higher. These fees are deducted automatically before the money reaches your balance, so the amount you see in your dashboard is the amount you actually receive.

Set your prices with the fee in mind. If you sell a product for 10,000 naira and a customer pays by card, you will receive about 9,750 naira after the fee. For most small businesses, that is a fair price for instant confirmation, fraud protection, and proper records. Do not try to pass the fee to the customer as a separate charge unless you state it clearly upfront, because surprise charges damage trust.

Your money does not sit in Paystack forever. Paystack settles your balance to your bank account automatically, usually the next business day. You can choose your settlement schedule in the dashboard. Daily settlement is useful if you need cash flow quickly. Weekly settlement is fine if you prefer fewer transfers to reconcile.

## Step 5: Handle Failed Payments and Common Problems

Failed payments happen, and most of them are easy to fix if you stay calm and guide the customer.

The most common problem is a declined card. The customer's bank may have blocked the transaction, the card may have insufficient funds, or the customer may have entered the wrong details. Ask the customer to try a different payment method on the same link, such as bank transfer or USSD, rather than trying the same card again and again.

The second common problem is a customer who pays but does not receive confirmation. Ask them for the email address they used at checkout, then check your Paystack dashboard for the payment. If it is there, reassure the customer and proceed with the order. If it is not there, the payment did not go through, and the money will usually be reversed by their bank within a few days.

The third problem is a customer who says they paid but the money never arrives. Never rely on screenshots. Check your dashboard first. If there is no record of the payment, it did not happen, no matter what the screenshot shows. Politely explain that you will proceed once the payment appears in your system, and offer to resend the link.

The fourth problem is a dispute or a chargeback, where a customer asks their bank to reverse a payment. This is rare for small businesses, but it happens. Keep records of every order: the payment receipt from Paystack, the delivery confirmation, and the customer's messages. If a dispute arises, these records are your evidence, and Paystack will ask for them.

## Step 6: Use Payment Links to Get Paid Faster Every Day

Once your first link works, use payment links as your default way of collecting money, not as an occasional extra.

Ask for payment before you start work, not after. A tailor should collect a deposit through a payment link before cutting fabric. A caterer should collect payment before buying ingredients. A graphic designer should collect at least half before starting a design. Payment links make this easy, because you can send the link the moment the customer agrees, and the customer can pay in under a minute.

Send the link immediately when the customer says yes. Every hour of delay between agreement and payment is an hour in which the customer can change their mind, forget, or spend the money elsewhere. The link should go out within minutes of the customer confirming the order.

Follow up once if a customer does not pay. A single polite message the next day, such as "Good morning, here is the payment link again for your order. Once payment is confirmed, I will arrange delivery," is helpful. If they still do not pay, leave it. Chasing the same customer five times wastes your time and damages the relationship.

Create a separate link for each of your best-selling products, and name them clearly. When you post a product on your status, include its own link. This removes the step where the customer messages you, waits for a reply, and then pays. They can pay the moment they decide, which is when they are most likely to buy.

## A Simple 7-Day Plan to Start Collecting Payments by Link

Here is a one-week plan to move your business onto payment links.

Day one: create your Paystack account, complete your business profile, and add your bank account. Start your identity verification the same day.

Day two: while verification is running, create your first payment link for your best-selling product. Set the price, write a clear description, and test the checkout page on your phone.

Day three: check whether your verification is complete. If it is, switch the account to live mode. If it is still pending, be patient and check again tomorrow. Use the time to create payment links for your next three best-selling products.

Day four: save a quick reply in WhatsApp Business with your payment link and a short message. Send it to the next three customers who order, and note how quickly they pay compared with bank transfer.

Day five: add your main payment link to your Instagram bio and post it on your WhatsApp status alongside the product. Watch how many customers pay without messaging you first.

Day six: review the week. How many payments came through links, and how many came by transfer? How much time did you save on confirmations? Fix any product description or price that caused questions.

Day seven: set your prices with the Paystack fee in mind, confirm your settlement schedule, and make payment links your default. From now on, the link goes out first, and the account number is only a backup for customers who genuinely cannot use the link.

## Final Thoughts

Bank transfers will always have a place in Nigerian business, but they should not be your only way of getting paid. A Paystack payment link is faster, safer, and more professional, and it costs you nothing to set up. It removes the daily chase for screenshots, it confirms payments instantly, and it lets customers pay you at midnight, on a Sunday, or from another state without a single phone call.

Start with one link for your best-selling product. Send it to your next customer instead of your account number, and see how it feels. Once you experience a payment arriving with an instant notification and no follow-up messages, you will wonder why you waited so long to make the switch.

Your time is better spent making products and serving customers than chasing payments. Let the link do the chasing for you.
