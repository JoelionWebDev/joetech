Tunde sells phone accessories from a kiosk in Computer Village, Ikeja. Chargers, pouches, screen guards, earpieces — the small things every Lagos phone owner needs twice a year. After three years of trading without a registered name, he decided to make it official. He already had a name picked out and told to his regulars. Then a friend asked him one question: "Have you checked if that name is free?"

He had not. And that question is the whole of this article. Before you print a banner, open an Instagram page, or pay for a full registration, there is a smaller, cheaper step that comes first: search the name on the Corporate Affairs Commission portal, and if it is free, reserve it so nobody else takes it while you get ready. This article covers only that step — the search and the reservation. The full registration, with your ID, passport photograph and certificate, is a separate job, and it comes after this one.

## What a Name Reservation Actually Is

A reservation is the CAC holding a name for you. You tell the Commission, "I want to trade as this name," they check it against their register and their rules, and if it passes, they hold it for you for 60 days. During those 60 days, nobody else can reserve or register that name. After 60 days, if you have not used it to complete your registration, the hold lifts and the name goes back into the pool.

A reservation is not a registration. It does not give you a certificate, it does not let you open a business bank account, and it does not stop someone from using the same name on Instagram or WhatsApp. What it does is buy you time — two months to gather your documents and complete the registration without the fear that someone else will grab the name while you are getting ready.

It is also cheap, and it happens before you spend real money. The reservation fee is ₦500, paid on the CAC portal when you submit the application. Fees can change, so go by what the portal shows on the day. But the order of spending is what matters: ₦500 to hold the name now, rather than money on banners and a logo for a name the CAC would never have approved.

## Before You Touch the Portal: Have Two or Three Names Ready

Tunde's first name was "Tunde Phone Hub". Simple, honest, and exactly what he does. His second choice was "Tunde Gadgets Hub", in case the first was taken. His third was "Ikeja Phone Hub", in case both failed. Write yours down in order of preference before you log in. It saves you from making a panicked choice on the portal when your first name comes back as taken.

## Restricted Words: The Names the CAC Will Not Give You

Some words in a business name need permission from a regulator before the CAC will approve them. Others are refused outright because they suggest you are something you are not. The ones that catch small business owners most often:

- **Bank, Microfinance Bank, Mortgage, Insurance.** These suggest you handle other people's money. A name with "Bank" in it needs approval from the Central Bank of Nigeria. "Ikeja Microfinance Bank" from a man selling phone chargers will be refused.
- **University, Polytechnic, College, Institute.** These suggest an educational institution and need approval from the relevant education authorities.
- **National, Federal, Nigeria, Nigerian, State.** Words that suggest a government connection or a national body need special approval, and the CAC is strict about them.
- **Chamber of Commerce, Trust, Trustee, Cooperative, Society.** These suggest a professional body, a charity structure, or a regulated group. "Lekki Traders Cooperative" sounds friendly; to the CAC it sounds like a cooperative society, which is a different registration entirely.
- **Names of famous people or brands.** You cannot register "Dangote Phone Hub" or "Shoprite Supermarket" and expect it to pass. The CAC checks for names that are identical or confusingly similar to existing businesses, and it protects well-known names. Borrowing a famous name is not clever branding; it is a refusal letter.

There is a simple test before you fall in love with a name: say it out loud and ask, "Does this make me sound like a bank, a school, a government agency, or somebody else's company?" If the answer is yes, pick another name.

## Three Example Names: One Taken, One Restricted, One That Passes

Tunde sat with his three names written on a piece of paper. This is, near enough, what happened with each of them. Treat these as examples of how the check works, not as a live search — the register changes every day, and only a search on the portal on the day tells you the truth about a name.

**Name one: "Dangote Phone Hub" — refused.** Tunde liked it because Dangote is a name every Nigerian trusts. That is exactly the problem. The name borrows the reputation of one of the biggest business groups in the country, and the CAC will not let a phone accessories kiosk trade under a name that suggests a connection to that group. Lesson: never build your name on someone else's reputation.

**Name two: "Ikeja Microfinance Bank" — restricted.** Tunde's cousin suggested it as a joke — "you give credit to customers, so you are basically a bank." It is not a joke to the CAC. "Microfinance Bank" is a regulated term that needs approval from the Central Bank of Nigeria, and a phone accessories trader will not get it. Lesson: if your name contains a regulated word, you need the regulator's blessing first, and for most small businesses that blessing is not coming.

**Name three: "Tunde Gadget Hub" — passes.** His first choice, "Tunde Phone Hub", was already close to an existing registered name in the search results, so he went to his second choice. "Tunde Gadget Hub" is his own name, a plain trade word, and a plain noun. Nothing regulated, nothing borrowed, nothing that suggests a government body. The search showed no identical or confusingly similar name, and he reserved it. Lesson: your own name plus a plain description of what you sell is the safest name in Nigeria.

## The Search and Reservation, Click by Click

Here is the walkthrough as Tunde did it, on his phone. The portal is the CAC post-incorporation portal at postinc.cac.gov.ng, where name reservations live. The screens change from time to time, so follow the labels on the day.

First, open postinc.cac.gov.ng in your browser and create an account if you do not have one. You will need an email address you actually check and a phone number. The portal sends a confirmation to your email; open it and confirm before you go further.

Next, log in and look for "Name Reservation" on the dashboard. Click it, and choose the type of reservation — for a sole trader or small partnership, that is a business name reservation. The portal will ask for the proposed name. Type your first choice exactly as you want it registered, including the exact spelling. "Tunde Gadget Hub" is not the same as "Tunde Gadgets Hub" to the register, so type it the way you want it on your certificate one day.

Then the portal runs the availability search. This is the moment of truth: the system checks your name against every registered business name and flags anything identical or confusingly similar. If the name is free, the portal will say so and let you continue. If it is taken, it will tell you, and you go back, type your second choice, and search again. Do not try to sneak a taken name past the system by changing one letter — "Tunde Gadget Hubb" is still confusingly similar, and a human officer reviews these applications too.

Once a name shows as available, the portal asks for a short description of what the business does. Tunde wrote "retail of mobile phone accessories". Keep it plain and honest, and make sure it matches what you actually do.

Then you pay the reservation fee — ₦500 — through the payment options on the portal, by card or bank transfer. Pay only on the portal, into the account the portal shows you. Anyone who asks you to pay a reservation fee into a personal account is not helping you register a business. Keep the payment receipt and the transaction reference; screenshot them as well as saving the email.

After payment, submit the application. The CAC reviews it, and if the name passes, you receive an approval with an availability code. That code is the key to the next stage: you will enter it when you start the full business name registration. Write it down in your notebook and save the approval email somewhere safe.

## The 60 Days: What to Do, and What Happens If They Run Out

The day your reservation is approved, a clock starts: 60 days. That is your window to complete the full registration. Two months sounds like a long time. It is not, if you spend the first three weeks deciding whether to bother.

Use the window well. In the first week, gather what the full registration needs: your means of identification, a passport photograph, your signature, and your business address. In the second week, start the registration on the portal using your availability code. Leave the last two weeks as slack for the things that always go wrong — a photograph that gets rejected, a name spelling that needs correcting, a payment that needs confirming.

If the 60 days run out before you register, the reservation lapses and the name goes back into the pool for anyone to take. You can apply for a fresh reservation and pay the fee again, but there is no guarantee the name will still be free. Tunde put a reminder in his phone for day 45: "Register or lose the name."

## If Your Name Is Taken: Four Fixes That Actually Work

Most first choices are taken. That is normal, and it is not the end of the road. Here are four ways traders fix a taken name without losing what made it good:

Add your location. "Phone Hub" is taken; "Ikeja Phone Hub" or "Computer Village Phone Hub" probably is not.

Add a trade word. If "Tunde Phones" is gone, "Tunde Phone Accessories" or "Tunde Phone Repairs" may be free, and it tells customers more about what you do.

Use your full name. "Tunde Gadget Hub" is free where "Gadget Hub" is taken.

Change the structure, not the spelling. Adding "Enterprise", "Ventures" or "Stores" to the end of a name is a legitimate way to make it distinct. What does not work is changing one letter and hoping nobody notices. The CAC notices.

## What Reservation Does Not Do

Be clear about the limits, so the reservation does not give you false confidence. It does not register your business — you still need the full registration, the certificate, and then a business account and a Tax Identification Number. It does not protect your name on Instagram, WhatsApp, or TikTok. And it does not last: 60 days, then it is gone.

But as a first step, it is the smartest ₦500 a new business owner in Nigeria can spend. It answers the only question that matters before you spend real money: is this name mine to use? Tunde's answer was yes, for "Tunde Gadget Hub", for 60 days. He used 23 of them to finish the registration. The banner went up only after the certificate arrived.
