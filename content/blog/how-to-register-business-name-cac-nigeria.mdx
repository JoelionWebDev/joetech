Registering your business name with the Corporate Affairs Commission, known as the CAC, is one of the most important steps a Nigerian small business owner can take. It turns your business from an idea into a legal entity with a name that belongs to you, and it opens doors that stay closed to unregistered businesses: a business bank account, contracts with companies, access to loans and grants, and the right to stop another business from using your name. Many business owners put it off because it sounds complicated, but the process is straightforward once you understand the steps.

A business name registration is different from registering a company. A business name is the simplest form of registration in Nigeria, suited to a sole trader or a small partnership — one person, or a few partners, trading under a chosen name. A company registration, which creates a limited liability company, is a separate and more detailed process. This guide covers the business name route, which is where most Nigerian small businesses start. It explains how to choose a name, check that it is available, reserve it, and complete the registration with your details, your identification, your passport photograph, your signature, and your business address.

## Start by Choosing a Good Business Name

Your business name is the first thing customers hear, and it stays with you for years, so choose it carefully. A good business name is short, easy to spell, easy to pronounce, and easy to remember. When a customer hears it once, they should be able to find you again. Names that are too long, too clever, or too similar to another business cause confusion, and a confused customer is a customer who buys from someone else. Say the name out loud, write it down, and imagine it on a signboard, a WhatsApp profile, and an invoice — if it looks and sounds professional in all three places, it is a good name.

Let the name reflect what your business does without limiting you. A name that suggests food, fashion or digital services helps customers understand you at once, but avoid one so narrow it becomes a problem when you add a second service. Also consider your future online presence: a name that works as a website address and a social media handle will save you trouble later.

Before you fall in love with a name, check it in the places that matter. Search for it on Google and on social media to see whether another business is already using it, and think about whether customers might confuse the two. Check that the name is not misleading — a name that suggests you are a bank, a government agency, or a professional body when you are not will cause problems. And remember that the final decision on whether a name is acceptable belongs to the CAC, which checks every proposed name against its rules. Choosing two or three good names, in order of preference, gives you a fallback if your first choice is not available.

## Search the Name and Reserve It on the CAC Portal

The first formal step is to search for your chosen name on the CAC portal, to see whether it is already taken. The CAC keeps a public register of business names, and the search shows you whether an identical or very similar name already exists. Do this search before you spend money or print anything with the name on it, because a name that is already registered cannot be registered again. If your first choice is taken, try your second choice, or adjust the name — for example, by adding a word that describes your service or your location — and search again.

Once you find a name that is available, the next step is to reserve it. Reservation means the CAC holds the name for you for a limited period while you complete the registration, so nobody else can take it in the meantime. You apply for the reservation through the CAC portal, giving the proposed name and your details, and the CAC checks the name against its rules. Some names need special approval — for example, names that suggest a regulated activity — and the portal will tell you if that applies. If the name passes the check, the reservation is approved, and you receive a confirmation with an availability code.

Keep your reservation details safe, because you will need the availability code for the next stage. Do not delay after reserving: a reservation lasts for a limited time, and if you do not complete the registration within that period, the name becomes available to others again. Plan to move to the registration step promptly, while the name is still held for you. If you are unsure about any part of the portal, the CAC has help guides on its website, and accredited agents can assist you with the process if you would rather not handle it alone.

## Prepare Your Details, ID, Passport Photo, Signature and Address

Before you start the registration itself, gather everything you will need, because a complete application moves far faster than one that keeps stopping for a missing document. First, your personal details: your full name, date of birth, phone number, email address, and your residential address. You will also need the details of your business: the name you have reserved, the nature of the business — what you actually do, such as trading, catering, tailoring, or web design — and the business address where the business operates. The business address can be your shop, your office, or your home address if you work from home.

Second, your means of identification. The CAC accepts a valid national identity card, international passport, driver's licence, or voter's card. Have the document ready, and make sure the name on it matches the name you are registering with. If the names differ — for example, if your identity card uses a different spelling of your name — sort that out before you apply, because a mismatch will delay your registration.

Third, your passport photograph and your signature. You will need a clear passport photograph, usually in the standard passport size, with a plain background and your face clearly visible. Take it at a proper photo studio if you can, because a poor-quality photograph can be rejected. Your signature will also be captured as part of the registration, so practise signing clearly and consistently, in the same way you sign your bank documents. Finally, confirm your business address and keep a document that proves it, such as a utility bill or a tenancy agreement, in case it is requested. Having all of this ready before you log in to the portal turns the registration into a single smooth session.

## Complete the Business Name Registration Step by Step

With your name reserved and your documents ready, you can complete the registration on the CAC portal. Log in to the portal and start a new business name registration, entering your availability code from the reservation step. The portal will ask you to fill in the proprietor's details — your name, date of birth, contact details, and residential address — and the business details, including the reserved name, the nature of the business, and the business address. Fill in every field carefully, and double-check the spelling of names and addresses, because errors at this stage are difficult and slow to correct later.

Next, upload the required items: your passport photograph, your signature, and your means of identification. The portal will tell you the format and size it accepts for each upload, so prepare your files to match. If a file is rejected, check its size and format before trying again, and do not use a blurry photograph or a cropped image that cuts off part of your face or signature. The portal may also ask you to confirm your business address and the nature of your business from a list of options — choose the description that matches what you actually do, because this becomes part of your official record.

At the end of the application, the portal will show you the current fee for the registration, and you pay it online through the payment options provided. The fee is shown on the portal at the time of payment, and it is the amount the CAC currently charges for a business name registration, so you will see the exact figure before you pay. Keep the payment receipt and your application number, because you will need them to track the application. After payment, submit the application and wait for the CAC to review it. If everything is in order, the registration is approved and your certificate is issued.

## What Happens After Approval, and How to Use Your Certificate

When your registration is approved, the CAC issues your business name certificate, which is the official proof that your business name is registered. Download it from the portal and keep both a digital copy and a printed copy in a safe place. Check the certificate carefully as soon as you receive it: the business name, your name, the nature of the business, and the business address should all be exactly as you submitted them. If you spot an error, report it to the CAC promptly, because correcting a certificate later takes more time than fixing it at once.

Your certificate unlocks the next steps in building your business. Take it to your bank to open a business bank account in your business name. Use it when you apply for a Tax Identification Number, known as a TIN, from the tax authorities, which you will need if you plan to work with companies or government agencies. Show it to clients who ask for proof that your business is registered, and keep a copy with your business records. Many corporate clients in Lagos will not sign a contract with an unregistered business, so your certificate is often the difference between winning a job and losing it.

Your certificate also protects your name: once registered, another business cannot register the same name. But registration brings duties too — keep your records up to date, file the annual returns the CAC requires, and tell the CAC if your address or details change. A registered business with clean records is one banks, clients and partners can trust.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid When Registering

The most common mistake is choosing a name that the CAC will reject, and only discovering this after you have printed banners, opened social media accounts, and told your customers. Always search and reserve the name first, before you spend money on branding. A second common mistake is rushing the application with errors — a misspelt name, a wrong date of birth, or an address that does not match your documents. Read every field twice before you submit, and have someone else check it if you can, because a fresh pair of eyes catches errors you have stopped seeing.

A third mistake is using poor-quality uploads. A passport photograph taken in bad light, or a signature scanned so faintly that it is barely visible, can be rejected and send you back to the start. Take the photograph properly, sign clearly on plain white paper, and scan or photograph both carefully. A fourth mistake is paying the wrong person. The CAC registration is done through the official CAC portal, and the fee is shown on the portal at payment. Be careful of anyone who offers to register your business for an unusually low price, or who asks you to pay into a personal account — use the official portal, or an accredited agent you trust, and always keep your receipts.

Finally, do not register a business name and then forget about it. Some owners register, frame the certificate, and continue running the business exactly as before — same personal account, same informal records, no tax number. The certificate is valuable only when you use it: open the business account, get your tax number, put the registered name on your invoices and your website, and keep your records in order. Registration is the beginning of running a proper business, not the end of it.

## What Registration Means for Your Business Going Forward

Registering your business name changes how the world sees your business, and it should change how you run it too. Customers treat a registered business more seriously, suppliers are more willing to give you credit, and corporate clients can work with you without worrying about their own records. Use that trust well. Put your registered business name on your invoices, your receipts, your WhatsApp profile, your social media pages, and your website, exactly as it appears on the certificate. Consistency builds recognition, and recognition builds a brand.

Registration also brings responsibility. Keep proper records of your income and your expenses, file your annual returns with the CAC, and meet your tax obligations. These duties are part of running a registered business, and they protect you: a business with clean records can prove its income when it applies for a loan, can defend itself in a dispute, and can grow without fear of an unexpected problem from the authorities. If your business grows to the point where you want the protection of limited liability, or you want to bring in partners and investors, speak to a professional about converting to a company registration — your business name registration is a solid foundation to build on.

Most importantly, registration is a statement of intent: you are not running a side hustle for a few months — you are building a business that will last. That mindset changes your decisions, from better records to more professional customer service. Many successful Nigerian businesses started exactly where you are now, with a name, a registration, and a determination to do things properly. Take the step, use the certificate, and let your business grow on a proper foundation.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the difference between a business name and a company?**

A business name is the simplest registration, suited to a sole trader or a small partnership trading under a chosen name. A company, such as a limited liability company, is a separate legal entity with directors and shareholders, and it involves a more detailed registration. Most small businesses start with a business name.

**How long does the name reservation last?**

A reservation holds the name for a limited period while you complete the registration, so you should move to the registration step promptly. The portal will show the validity period of your reservation, and if it expires before you complete the registration, the name becomes available to others again.

**How much does it cost to register a business name with the CAC?**

The CAC portal shows the current fee at the time of payment, and you pay it online through the options on the portal. The fee is displayed before you pay, so you will see the exact amount for your application on the portal itself.

**Can I register a business name if I work from home?**

Yes. Your home address can serve as your business address if you operate from home, and you will give it as the business address in your application. Make sure the address you provide is one where you can receive official correspondence.

**What documents do I need for the registration?**

You need your personal details, a valid means of identification, a passport photograph, your signature, and your business address, along with the availability code from your name reservation. Have all of these ready before you start the application on the CAC portal.
