Cooking gas, also called LPG, has become the cooking fuel of choice for many Nigerian homes. Kerosene is smoky and costly, firewood is slow and dirty, and charcoal leaves soot everywhere. Gas is faster, cleaner, and easier to control. As more families switch to gas, and as more restaurants, hotels, and food vendors use gas every day, the demand for safe and reliable gas refills keeps growing. That makes a cooking gas business a practical opportunity, if you run it safely and honestly.

This is not a business to rush into without learning the rules. Gas is flammable, and a mistake can cause a fire that harms people and destroys property. The good news is that the rules are clear, the market is large, and a well-run gas retail shop can earn steady daily income. This guide explains how to start, what licences you need, how much it costs, how to stay safe, and how to build customers who return every week.

## Why a Cooking Gas Business Works in Nigeria

Almost every home that cooks needs fuel every day. A family that uses a 12.5kg cylinder may refill every two to four weeks, depending on how much they cook. A small family with a 6kg cylinder refills more often. Restaurants and food vendors refill even more frequently. That repeat demand is the strength of this business. You are not looking for one big sale. You are serving the same customers again and again.

Gas is also a product people cannot easily do without. When a cylinder finishes in the middle of cooking, the family needs a refill that same day. That urgency means a shop in a good residential area, with gas in stock and fair prices, will rarely lack customers. If you are open on time, honest with your scale, and polite, customers will choose you over a distant or unreliable seller.

The business can start at different sizes. You can start as a small retailer with a few cylinders and a dispensing scale, or as a larger plant with a storage tank and a filling bay. Most beginners start as retailers, learn the trade, and grow into a bigger operation later. This guide focuses mainly on retail, with notes on what it takes to grow.

## Step 1: Learn the Business and the Safety Rules

Before you spend money, learn how cooking gas works. Visit two or three existing gas shops in your area and observe how they operate. Ask the owner about their suppliers, their prices, and the challenges they face. Many owners will share useful advice if you approach them respectfully and show that you want to learn, not to copy them blindly.

Learn the safety basics. LPG is stored as a liquid under pressure and turns to gas when released. It is heavier than air, so a leak can settle low in a room and spread. Never smoke, light a naked flame, or use a phone near a filling point. Always check cylinders for leaks, rust, and damaged valves before you fill them. Never fill a cylinder beyond its safe limit. Never keep gas cylinders in direct sun or near heat.

Take a safety training course if one is available in your state. The Nigerian Midstream and Downstream Petroleum Regulatory Authority, known as NMDPRA, and other agencies run guidance for LPG operators. Your state fire service can also advise on fire safety for your shop. Keep a fire extinguisher on site, learn how to use it, and display safety signs clearly.

Safety is not just a rule. It is your business. One fire incident can end your business and harm your neighbours. Customers also notice safety. A clean, organised shop with clear safety signs builds more trust than a crowded, careless one.

## Step 2: Choose Your Business Model

There are three main ways to sell cooking gas in Nigeria. The first is retail refill, where customers bring their empty cylinders to your shop and you refill them from your stock. This is the most common model for beginners. You need a shop, a scale, cylinders, and a reliable supply of gas.

The second is cylinder exchange, where you keep filled cylinders ready and swap a customer's empty cylinder for a filled one of the same size. This is faster for the customer, but you need more filled cylinders in stock and a good system to track which cylinders belong to you and which belong to customers.

The third is home delivery. You take orders on WhatsApp or by phone, load filled cylinders on a bike or small van, and deliver to homes and offices. Delivery adds income and helps you reach customers who cannot carry a heavy cylinder, but it needs extra staff, transport, and careful handling.

Many successful retailers combine refill and delivery. Start with refill in your shop, add delivery once you have steady customers and a reliable rider. Do not start delivery on day one if you cannot yet manage your shop well.

## Step 3: Get the Right Licences and Approvals

Cooking gas is a regulated business in Nigeria. Do not open a shop without the required approvals. The exact requirements can vary by state and local government, so confirm the current list at your state office before you commit money to rent.

In general, you will need a business name registration with the Corporate Affairs Commission, a licence or permit from NMDPRA for LPG retail, approval from your state fire service, and a local government permit for your location. Your shop location may also need approval from the town planning authority, especially if it is in a residential area. Some states have rules about how far a gas shop must be from houses, schools, and petrol stations. Check those rules before you sign a lease.

Keep copies of all your approvals in your shop and renew them on time. Officials do visit gas shops, and an unlicensed shop can be closed. A licensed shop also earns more trust from customers and suppliers. Budget for licence and approval fees in your startup plan. The total can vary widely by state, so ask for the current fees in writing from the relevant offices, and avoid paying anyone who cannot give you a receipt.

If you plan to build a bigger plant with a storage tank, the approvals are stricter. You will need a site plan, an environmental assessment, and a more detailed safety plan. For a small retail shop, the process is simpler, but still take it seriously.

## Step 4: Work Out Your Startup Costs

Startup costs depend on your location, your shop size, and how many cylinders you start with. Here is a realistic guide for a small retail shop.

Shop rent is often the first cost. In a busy residential area in Lagos, a small shop can cost ₦300,000 to ₦700,000 per year. In smaller towns, rent may be ₦120,000 to ₦300,000 per year. Many landlords ask for one year upfront. Look for a shop with good access for customers carrying cylinders, space to store cylinders safely, and room for a dispensing area.

Cylinders are a major cost. You will need your own stock of filled cylinders for exchange and for filling customer cylinders. A new 12.5kg cylinder can cost from ₦30,000 upwards, while a 6kg cylinder costs less. Prices change with the market, so get current prices from at least three suppliers before you buy. Start with a mix of sizes: 3kg, 6kg, and 12.5kg are common for homes, while 50kg is used by restaurants and businesses.

Equipment includes a certified weighing scale, a dispensing pump or filling hose, a manifold if you use a bulk supply, fire extinguishers, safety signs, and a small office table and chairs. A good scale is essential. Customers trust a seller whose scale is accurate and visible. Do not buy a cheap, faulty scale. It will cost you customers and may lead to disputes.

Your first gas stock is another cost. You will buy LPG in bulk from a depot or a licensed supplier, or in cylinders from a plant. Prices change often, so buy from a supplier you trust and keep records of each purchase. A realistic total for a small retail shop, including rent, cylinders, equipment, licences, and first stock, is between ₦1,500,000 and ₦3,500,000 in many Nigerian cities. If that is too much, start smaller with fewer cylinders and a smaller shop, and grow from profit. Do not borrow heavily to start big. A small safe shop that grows steadily is better than a big shop loaded with debt.

## Step 5: Choose a Good Location

Location matters a lot in gas retail. The best locations are busy residential areas where many families live and cook with gas. Look for a shop on a street with good foot traffic, easy access for cars and bikes, and space for customers to park briefly while they load a cylinder.

Avoid locations too close to a school, a hospital, or a crowded market with open flames, unless the authorities have approved it. Check the distance rules for gas shops in your state. A shop that is easy to reach, visible from the road, and safe will attract more customers than a hidden shop with cheaper rent.

Visit the area at different times. Count how many homes are nearby. Check how many gas shops already serve the area. If there are already three shops on one street, you will need a clear advantage, such as longer opening hours, home delivery, or better service. If there is no gas shop in a growing estate, that can be a good opportunity, but make sure the residents actually use gas and can afford regular refills.

Check power and water access. You need light for your shop, and water for cleaning and safety. Check security too. Gas cylinders are valuable, so your shop needs a strong door, good locks, and, if possible, a security guard or a good relationship with local security.

## Step 6: Find a Reliable Gas Supplier

Your supplier decides whether you have gas to sell. A shop without gas loses customers fast. Find a licensed LPG depot or plant near you and build a good relationship with them. In Lagos, there are depots in Apapa, Ijegun, and other areas. In other states, ask other retailers where they buy and compare prices and reliability.

Buy from a supplier who delivers on time, gives you a receipt, and sells gas at a fair market price. Do not rely on a single supplier if you can avoid it. Have a backup supplier for days when your main supplier has no stock or a truck delay. Keep a record of every purchase: date, quantity in kg, price per kg, total cost, and supplier name. That record helps you price correctly and spot when a supplier's price is too high.

Check the quality of the gas. Good LPG should burn with a blue flame and cook efficiently. If customers complain that their gas finishes too quickly, check your scale, your filling method, and your supplier. A faulty scale or a dishonest filling practice will destroy your reputation faster than a high price.

Order before you run out. Track your daily sales in kg and know how many days your stock will last. A good rule is to order when you have enough stock for three to four more days of sales. That gives you time to arrange delivery without closing your shop.

## Step 7: Price Fairly and Weigh Honestly

Gas prices change often, so check the current market price per kg before you set your price each week. Your price should cover the cost of gas, transport, rent, staff, and a fair profit. Do not sell below cost to attract customers. You will run out of money and close.

Weigh every cylinder in front of the customer. Put the empty cylinder on the scale, show the weight, fill it, and show the final weight. This transparency builds trust. Many customers have been cheated by sellers who underfill cylinders, so an honest scale is a strong advantage. Display your price per kg clearly in your shop and on WhatsApp, and update it when the market price changes.

Offer clear prices by cylinder size. Customers want to know the price for 3kg, 6kg, and 12.5kg without asking many questions. If you offer delivery, state the delivery fee separately. Do not hide charges. A customer who feels cheated will not return and will warn others.

## Step 8: Run Your Shop Safely Every Day

Safety is a daily habit, not a one-time licence. Start each day by checking your cylinders, hoses, valves, and scale. Look for leaks, rust, and damage. Keep the shop clean and free of clutter. Store cylinders upright, in a ventilated area, away from direct sun and heat. Do not store cylinders inside a closed room with no ventilation.

Train your staff in safety. Anyone who fills a cylinder must know how to check for leaks, how to handle a cylinder safely, and what to do if there is a smell of gas. Keep fire extinguishers in working order and make sure everyone knows where they are. Do not allow smoking or open flames near the shop. Display emergency numbers where staff can see them.

Inspect customer cylinders before you fill them. If a cylinder is badly rusted, has a damaged valve, or is past its safe use date, refuse to fill it and explain why. It is better to lose one sale than to risk an accident. Offer to help the customer get a safer cylinder. That honesty will earn respect.

## Step 9: Attract Customers and Keep Them

Tell your neighbourhood that you sell gas. Use a clear signboard with your shop name, your prices, and your phone number. Share your WhatsApp number so customers can order or ask for prices. Use WhatsApp Business to post your daily price, your opening hours, and your delivery service.

Offer home delivery for customers who cannot carry a cylinder. A bike rider with a proper carrier can deliver 6kg and 12.5kg cylinders safely if trained. Charge a fair delivery fee and deliver on time. Many families will choose a shop that delivers over a cheaper shop that does not.

Serve restaurants, hotels, and food vendors. They buy larger quantities and refill often. Visit them, introduce your shop, and offer a reliable supply and a clear price. A few regular business customers can give your shop steady income even on slow days for homes.

Keep a record of your customers. Note their name, phone number, cylinder size, and last refill date. A simple reminder message when they are likely to need a refill can bring them back. Thank loyal customers and treat every customer politely, even when the shop is busy. In gas retail, trust and convenience matter more than a small price difference.

## Step 10: Keep Records and Grow Carefully

Record every sale: date, cylinder size, kg sold, amount received, and payment method. Record every expense: gas purchases, transport, rent, salaries, and repairs. At the end of each week, compare your income and expenses. That simple check will show whether you are making a profit or losing money.

Pay yourself a fixed amount, like a salary, and leave the rest in the business for restocking. Do not spend your gas money on personal needs and then find you have no money to buy the next supply. That is one of the fastest ways a gas shop fails.

As you grow, you can add more cylinders, start a second shop in another estate, or move from retail to a small plant with a storage tank. Before you expand, make sure your first shop is profitable, safe, and well managed without you being there every minute. Train a trusted staff member to run the shop, and check the books regularly.

Avoid common mistakes. Do not fill cylinders beyond their safe limit. Do not buy gas from unlicensed sellers. Do not use a faulty scale. Do not ignore licence renewals. Do not store cylinders carelessly. Each of these mistakes can cost you money, customers, or safety.

## Final Checklist Before You Open

Before you open, check that you have your licences and fire service approval, a safe shop in a good residential area, a reliable gas supplier, enough cylinders in the common sizes, a certified scale and safety equipment, a clear price list, a WhatsApp Business account, and a record book for sales and purchases.

Start small, stay safe, weigh honestly, and serve your neighbours well. A cooking gas business rewards patience and trust. Your first months may be slow, but steady service, fair prices, and safe handling will build a customer base that returns week after week.
