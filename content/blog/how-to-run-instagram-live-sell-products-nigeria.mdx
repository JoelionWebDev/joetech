Instagram Live is one of the fastest ways to sell products in Nigeria today. A vendor in Lagos can show twenty dresses, bags, or wigs to hundreds of viewers in one hour, answer questions in real time, and close sales before the session ends — all without paying for a shop, a billboard, or an advert. Customers love it because they see the real product, hear the real price, and can ask questions before they pay.

This guide shows you how to run an Instagram Live that actually sells: how to plan the session, prepare your products and your space, present confidently on camera, handle orders and payments, and follow up afterwards. It is written for Nigerian sellers — fashion vendors, food sellers, skincare brands, and thrift dealers — and it works with the phones, data, and budgets you already have.

## Why Instagram Live Works So Well for Nigerian Sellers

Live selling works because it removes doubt. A photo can hide a stain, a bad zip, or a faded colour. A live video cannot. When a customer watches you hold a bag up to the camera, turn it around, open it, and show the inside, they trust what they see. That trust is the reason live viewers buy faster than people who only see a photo on your feed.

Live also creates urgency. When you say "I have only three pieces of this dress left" and viewers can see other people commenting "I want it," the fear of missing out pushes buyers to act now instead of thinking about it until tomorrow. A static post cannot create that feeling.

There is a third reason: Instagram rewards live video. When you go live, Instagram pushes a notification to your followers and puts your profile at the front of their Stories bar. People who have not seen your posts for weeks will see that you are live and join. A well-run live session can bring in new followers as well as new sales, because viewers share the session with friends who might want the same products.

The cost is almost nothing. You need a phone with a decent camera, a data bundle, good light, and products worth showing. Compared with renting a shop in a Lagos market or paying for adverts, a live session is the cheapest shop you will ever open.

## Step 1: Plan Your Live Session Like an Event

A successful live sale is planned before the camera turns on. Decide four things at least a day before: the date and time, the products, the prices, and the offer.

**Time matters.** The best times for live selling in Nigeria are weekday evenings between 7 p.m. and 9 p.m., when people are home from work and scrolling, and Saturday afternoons between 12 p.m. and 4 p.m. Avoid Sunday mornings, when many people are in church, and weekday mornings, when people are commuting. Test different times and watch when your own followers join — your audience may behave differently from the average.

**Choose 15 to 25 products.** Fewer than 10 and the session ends too quickly; more than 30 and viewers get tired before you reach the end. Pick your best sellers and your newest stock. Mix prices so there is something for every pocket: a few items at ₦5,000, several at ₦10,000 to ₦15,000, and one or two premium pieces at ₦25,000 or more.

**Fix every price before you go live.** Write the price on a small card next to each product, or keep a list beside you. Nothing kills a live sale faster than a seller who hesitates over the price or changes it mid-session. Customers notice, and they lose trust.

**Prepare one special offer.** A live-only discount gives people a reason to buy now instead of later. It can be simple: "10 percent off everything during this live," or "Free delivery in Lagos for orders placed before the live ends." One clear offer is enough; three offers confuse people.

Announce the session a day before. Post on your feed, your Stories, and your WhatsApp Status: the date, the time, and one or two of the best products you will show. A short video of you holding up the star product works better than a plain text announcement.

## Step 2: Set Up Your Space, Light, and Phone

You do not need a studio. You need a clean background, good light, and a steady phone.

**Light is the most important thing.** Natural daylight is free and flattering, so sit near a window during the day. In the evening, use a ring light — a small one costs ₦8,000 to ₦15,000 in Lagos computer village or on Jumia — or place two bright bulbs in front of you. Never sit with a bright window behind you, because the camera will darken your face and the products.

**Background matters.** A plain wall, a clean curtain, or a simple cloth backdrop is best. Remove clutter from the frame: the laundry, the food plates, and the children's toys distract viewers and make the business look careless. If you sell fashion, a clothing rack behind you looks professional and lets you reach products quickly.

**Steady the phone.** A tripod costs little and keeps the picture stable. If you do not have one, lean the phone against a stack of books at eye level. A shaky picture makes viewers leave within seconds. Use the back camera if your phone allows it, because it is usually sharper — but the front camera is fine if you need to see yourself while presenting.

**Check your data and battery.** A live session uses a lot of data — roughly 1 GB to 1.5 GB per hour on good quality. Make sure your MTN, Glo, or Airtel bundle is enough, and that you have a strong signal where you sit. Charge the phone fully and keep a charger plugged in if possible. Nothing ends a live sale faster than a dead battery or a network drop halfway through.

**Do a 30-second test.** Go live privately, or record a short video in the same spot, and watch it back. Check the light, the sound, and the background. Fix what looks wrong before the real session.

## Step 3: Open Strong and Keep Viewers Watching

The first 60 seconds decide whether people stay or leave. Start with energy, even if you feel nervous. Smile, greet your viewers by name when you can, and say clearly what they will see.

A strong opening sounds like this: "Good evening, my people. Welcome to Adaeze Fashion Hub live sales. Tonight I have 20 beautiful dresses, prices from ₦7,500, and free delivery in Lagos for orders placed during this live. Stay with me, share this live with your friends, and let's start with this first dress."

Then show the first product immediately. Do not spend five minutes greeting people and waiting for viewers to join. The people who are there want to see products. Latecomers will catch up, and Instagram will keep sending notifications as more followers come online.

Keep the energy up through the session. Talk while you show each product — the fabric, the colour, the sizes available, the price — and read the comments out loud so viewers feel involved. When someone comments "I like the blue one," answer them by name and hold the blue one up again. That personal attention is what makes live selling different from a website.

Pace yourself. Spend about two to three minutes per product. Less than that and viewers cannot decide; more than that and they get bored. If a product gets no reaction, move on quickly. If a product gets many comments, spend a little longer on it and show it from every angle.

## Step 4: Present Each Product So People Can Buy It

Every product you show needs the same five pieces of information: what it is, the colour and sizes, the price, how to order, and how delivery works. Give all five, every time, without being asked.

Hold the product close to the camera so viewers can see the details: the stitching on a dress, the clasp on a bag, the texture of a fabric. Turn it around. Show the inside. If it is a shoe, show the sole. If it is a wig, wear it or put it on a stand so viewers see how it falls. The more a customer can see, the less they need to ask, and the faster they buy.

State the price clearly and repeat it. "This dress is ₦12,000. Twelve thousand naira." Saying the price twice is not rude; it helps the viewers who joined late and the ones watching with the sound low. If you have a live-only discount, say so: "During this live only, this dress is ₦10,000 instead of ₦12,000."

Give each product a simple code — "Dress 1," "Dress 2," or "Blue Ankara" — and ask buyers to comment the code plus "sold" or send it to your WhatsApp or DM. Codes prevent confusion when two dresses look similar, and they make your order list easy to follow.

Be honest about flaws and limits. If a dress is available only in size 12 and 14, say so. If a bag has a small scratch, show it and reduce the price. Honesty on a live session builds a reputation that brings customers back for the next one. A buyer who discovers a hidden flaw on delivery will not buy from you again, and will tell their friends.

## Step 5: Take Orders and Payments Without Chaos

Orders during a live session can get messy fast: ten people comment "I want it" on the same dress, and you have three pieces. You need a system, and you need to explain it at the start.

Announce your rule clearly: "First person to send payment gets the item. Commenting 'I want it' reserves it for 10 minutes. If payment does not come in 10 minutes, it goes to the next person." This rule is fair, it creates urgency, and it protects you from buyers who claim items and never pay.

Direct buyers to WhatsApp or DM to confirm. Say it every few products: "Send 'Dress 3' to my WhatsApp number in my bio to confirm your order." WhatsApp is better than DM for most Nigerian sellers, because it is easier to manage, it handles payment screenshots, and it keeps the order in one place.

Accept payment by bank transfer and confirm it fast. During a live session, check your bank app between products and call out confirmations: "Mrs. Ade, I have received your ₦12,000. Dress 3 is yours." Public confirmation reassures other viewers that buying from you is safe, and it pressures the undecided to act.

Keep a notebook or a sheet beside you during the session. Write down each product code, the buyer's name, and whether they have paid. At the end of the live, this list is your order record. Without it, you will forget who bought what, and the follow-up messages will take hours to sort out.

Do not accept "pay on delivery" for live sales unless you know the customer well. Too many buyers change their minds or refuse the delivery, and you pay the dispatch rider for nothing. For new customers, payment before delivery is the standard, and most serious buyers accept it when your page looks trustworthy.

## Step 6: Handle Comments, Questions, and Difficult Viewers

A live session is a conversation, and the comments are part of the show. Read them often and respond warmly. A viewer who feels seen is more likely to buy, and more likely to stay until the end.

Answer the common questions fast and calmly: "Do you deliver to Abuja?" — "Yes, delivery to Abuja is ₦3,500 and takes 24 to 48 hours." "Is this available in red?" — "This one is only in blue, but I have a similar style in red that I will show later." Keep your answers short so you can get back to the products.

Ignore or remove rude comments. Every live session attracts a few people who are there to disturb, not to buy. Do not argue with them on camera; it wastes time and lowers the mood. Tap and hide the comment, or block the user, and continue. Your real customers will respect a seller who stays calm and professional.

If your network drops or the live freezes, do not panic. Apologise briefly when you return, repeat the last product, and continue. Viewers understand that Nigerian networks are unpredictable. What they do not forgive is a seller who disappears without explanation. If the problem continues, end the live gracefully, save it, and announce a new time.

Watch your language. Speak clearly and avoid rushing, even when you are excited. Many viewers watch with the sound low or in a noisy place, so clear speech matters. Mixing English and Pidgin is fine and often more relatable — speak the way your customers speak.

## Step 7: Close the Sale and Follow Up After the Live

The last ten minutes of your live are for closing. Remind viewers of the offer: "Free delivery in Lagos ends when this live ends." Show the two or three best products one more time, quickly. Thank everyone who joined, and tell them when the next live will be.

Save the live to your profile as a video. Instagram lets you share the replay, and it keeps selling after you go offline: viewers who missed the session can watch it and message you about the remaining products. Add a caption with the products still available and your WhatsApp number.

Follow up the same evening. Message every buyer who paid, confirm their order, and give them a delivery date. Message the buyers who said "I want it" but did not pay, with a polite reminder: "Hello, Dress 3 is still available for you until tomorrow morning. Would you like to complete your order?" Many of them will pay once reminded.

Record the results. Write down the date, the number of viewers at the peak, the number of products sold, and the total money made. Compare each live with the last one. If a Tuesday evening live made ₦150,000 and a Saturday afternoon live made ₦80,000, you know which time works better for your audience. If dresses sold out but bags did not move, your next live should show more dresses.

## A Simple Plan for Your First Live Sale

Two days before: choose your 15 to 25 products, fix every price, and prepare your offer. Announce the session on your feed, Stories, and WhatsApp Status.

One day before: set up your space, check your light, charge your phone, buy your data bundle, and do a 30-second test recording. Prepare your notebook and your list of product codes and prices.

During the live: open with energy, show each product with its price and code, read comments by name, confirm payments out loud, and keep the session to 60 to 90 minutes. Longer than that and viewers drift away.

After the live: save the replay, message every buyer, confirm deliveries, and record your results. Post the remaining products on your Status the next morning for the people who missed the session.

Your first live will not be perfect. The light may be wrong, you may forget a price, or the network may drop once. That is normal, and your regular customers will forgive it if the products are good and the prices are honest. What matters is that you start, learn from each session, and keep showing up. The seller who goes live every week builds an audience that a seller who posts photos once a month never will. In Nigerian online selling, the camera is your shop — open it, and let people see what you have.
