A customer is ready to buy. They have seen your product on WhatsApp, they like the price, and they ask, "How do I pay?" If your answer is slow, confusing, or limited to one option, many of them will quietly disappear. They will not tell you why. They will simply buy from the next seller who makes payment easy.

This happens every day to small businesses in Nigeria. A seller who only accepts bank transfer loses the customer whose bank app is down. A seller who only takes cash on delivery loses the customer in Abuja while the seller is in Lagos. A seller with no payment link loses the customer who wants to pay with a card at midnight.

Online payments fix this. With Paystack or Flutterwave, your customers can pay you with a card, a bank transfer, USSD, or a mobile wallet, at any time of day, from anywhere in Nigeria and beyond. The money lands in your account, you get a notification, and you can confirm the order in seconds. You do not need to be a tech expert. You do not need a website. You can set it up on your phone in an afternoon.

In this guide, you will learn how to choose between Paystack and Flutterwave, set up an account, create payment links, and avoid the mistakes that cost small businesses money.

## Why Your Business Needs Online Payments

Cash and bank transfer alone are no longer enough. More Nigerians now shop on their phones, and they expect to pay on their phones too. When a customer sees your product on Instagram at 11 at night, they want to pay immediately, before they change their mind. If they have to wait until morning for your account number, the excitement fades and the sale is lost.

Online payments also build trust. When a customer pays through a known payment page with your business name on it, they feel safer than when they send money to a personal account number from a stranger on WhatsApp. The payment page shows your business name, the amount, and a receipt. That professionalism matters, especially for new customers who have never bought from you before.

There is a practical benefit too. Payment links let you sell without being online. You can post a link on your WhatsApp status, your Instagram bio, or your Facebook page, and customers can pay while you are cooking, driving, or sleeping. You wake up to payment notifications instead of a pile of "I want to pay, send account number" messages.


## Paystack or Flutterwave: Which One Should You Choose?

Paystack and Flutterwave are the two most popular payment platforms for Nigerian small businesses. Both are trusted, both are regulated, and both work well. You do not need both to start. Pick one, set it up properly, and add the second later if you need it.

Paystack is known for being simple and reliable. Its dashboard is clean, its payment links are easy to create, and its customer support responds quickly. Paystack charges 1.5 percent plus 100 naira on local card payments, with the fee capped at 2,000 naira.

Flutterwave is known for its flexibility. It supports more payment methods, including international cards, mobile money, and payment in other African currencies. If you plan to sell to customers outside Nigeria, Flutterwave gives you more options. Flutterwave charges 1.4 percent on local card payments.

For most small businesses selling mainly to customers in Nigeria, either platform works well. Choose Paystack if you want the simplest setup, or Flutterwave if you expect international customers. What matters most is that you pick one and start using it this week.

## What You Need Before You Sign Up

Before you start the registration, gather the documents and details you will need. Having them ready makes the process fast and prevents your account from getting stuck halfway.

You need a business name. This can be your registered business name, or the trading name you use on WhatsApp and Instagram. If your business is registered with the Corporate Affairs Commission, use the exact registered name. If it is not registered yet, use the name you trade with, but plan to register it when you can, because a registered business builds more trust with customers and banks.

You need a bank account in the business name, or a personal account you are willing to use for now. A business account is better, because it keeps your business money separate and looks more professional to customers. If you only have a personal account, you can still start, but open a business account as soon as you can. Most Nigerian banks will open a business account for a registered business with your CAC documents and a valid means of identification.

You need a valid means of identification, such as your National Identity Number, international passport, driver's licence, or voter's card. You will also need a Bank Verification Number, which is linked to your account. Both Paystack and Flutterwave will ask for these during verification, and the process is faster when the names on all your documents match.

You need an email address you check regularly and a phone number you can receive SMS codes on. Use a business email if you have one, because it looks more professional than a personal Gmail address.

Finally, you need your business address and a short description of what you sell. Write one or two sentences in advance, such as "We sell handmade leather bags and shoes to customers in Lagos and across Nigeria." This description appears on your payment pages and helps customers trust you.

## How to Set Up a Paystack Account Step by Step

Setting up Paystack takes about thirty minutes if you have your documents ready. Follow these steps in order.

First, go to paystack.com and click on the sign-up button. Enter your business email address and create a strong password. Use a password you do not use anywhere else, and write it down somewhere safe. Paystack will send a confirmation email. Open it and click the link to confirm your address.

Second, complete your business profile. Paystack will ask for your business name, the type of business, your phone number, and your address. Fill in everything honestly. For the business type, choose the option closest to what you do, such as retail, food, fashion, or services. If your business is registered, enter your RC number or business name registration number. If it is not registered, select the option for an unregistered business and continue.

Third, add your bank account. Enter the account number and select your bank from the list. Paystack will check that the account name matches your business name. This is how your money will be paid to you, so double-check the account number before you submit it. A wrong digit means your money goes to the wrong place or the setup fails.

Fourth, complete the verification. Paystack will ask for your BVN and a means of identification. Enter your BVN carefully. The name on your BVN must match the name on your account. Upload a clear photo of your ID. Make sure the photo is bright and readable. Blurry ID photos are the most common reason verification is delayed.

Fifth, wait for approval. Paystack usually approves accounts within one or two business days. While you wait, you can explore the dashboard and set up your payment links. Once your account is approved, you can start receiving live payments. Until then, you can test everything in test mode without real money moving.

Sixth, set your settlement details. Paystack usually pays your money into your bank account on the next business day. Check the settlement settings in your dashboard and turn on email and SMS notifications so you know immediately when a customer pays you.

## How to Set Up a Flutterwave Account Step by Step

Flutterwave follows a similar process. Go to flutterwave.com and click on the sign-up button. Enter your business email and create a strong password, then confirm your email address. Complete your business profile with your business name, type, phone number, and address. Add your bank account and double-check the account number. Then complete the verification by entering your BVN and uploading a clear photo of your ID. Flutterwave usually approves accounts within one or two business days.

Flutterwave asks for more detail about your business during setup, because it supports more payment methods and currencies. Answer each question simply and honestly, and give a realistic estimate if you are asked about your expected monthly sales. Once approved, explore the dashboard and turn on notifications so you never miss a payment.

## Create Payment Links for Your Products

A payment link is a simple web page with your product name, price, and a pay button. You can send it to a customer on WhatsApp, post it on your status, or put it in your Instagram bio. The customer clicks the link, chooses how to pay, and the money comes to you.

On Paystack, go to the payment pages section of your dashboard and create a page for each product. Enter the product name, the price in naira, and a short description. You can add a photo of the product, which helps customers confirm they are paying for the right item. Save the page and copy the link. On Flutterwave, the process is similar: go to the payment links section, enter the product details and price, and save the link.

Create one link per product, not one link for your whole business. A link that says "Handmade leather bag, 45,000 naira" is clearer than a link that says "Pay me." Customers trust a link that shows exactly what they are paying for.

Test every link yourself before you share it. Click the link, check that the product name and price are correct, and confirm that the payment page shows your business name. Keep a list of your links in a note on your phone, with the product name next to each one, so you can find the right link in seconds.

## Understand the Fees Before You Price Your Products

Both Paystack and Flutterwave charge a small fee on every payment. This is normal, and every payment platform in the world works this way. The fee pays for the security, the technology, and the transfer of money to your account. What matters is that you know the fee and plan for it.

Paystack charges 1.5 percent plus 100 naira on local card payments, capped at 2,000 naira per transaction. That means on a 50,000 naira sale, the fee is 850 naira. On a 10,000 naira sale, the fee is 250 naira. Flutterwave charges 1.4 percent on local card payments. Bank transfer and USSD payments have their own fees, which are shown on each platform's website and can change, so check the current rates on the platform before you set your prices.

Do not hide the fee by adding it to the price at the last minute, because that annoys customers. Instead, build it into your pricing from the start. If a product costs you 20,000 naira to make and you want to earn 10,000 naira on it, price it at 30,000 naira or slightly above, so the fee comes out of your margin rather than your profit.

Also check the settlement time. Both platforms usually pay your money into your bank account on the next business day. Plan your cash flow around this. If you need money to buy materials for the next order, remember that today's payments may not reach your account until tomorrow.

## Offer More Than One Way to Pay

Even with Paystack or Flutterwave set up, keep your other payment options open. Some customers prefer a direct bank transfer, especially for large orders. Others want to pay on delivery. A few will ask for your POS machine if they are buying in your shop. The more ways a customer can pay, the fewer sales you lose.

Keep your bank transfer details ready in a saved message you can send quickly. After a transfer, ask the customer to send a screenshot, and confirm it in your bank app before you send the product. Never send a product based on a screenshot alone, because fake transfer alerts are common.

If you sell from a physical shop, consider getting a POS machine from your bank. It costs little to set up, and it lets walk-in customers pay with their cards. Some banks also offer a simple QR code that customers can scan to pay you. Ask your bank what options they have for small businesses.

For deliveries outside Lagos, decide your policy in advance. Many small businesses ask for full payment before dispatch, or at least a deposit of 50 percent. State this clearly in your product description and in your payment link, so customers know before they pay. Clear rules prevent arguments later.

## Common Mistakes That Cost Small Businesses Money

The first mistake is using a personal account for business payments and never separating the money. When business and personal money mix, you cannot tell whether you made a profit or a loss. Open a business account and connect it to your payment platform.

The second mistake is sharing payment links without testing them. A link with the wrong price or the wrong product name creates confusion and can cost you the sale. Test every link before you share it.

The third mistake is ignoring the dashboard. Your Paystack or Flutterwave dashboard shows every payment, every failed attempt, and every refund. Check it daily. If a customer says they paid but you did not receive a notification, the dashboard will show you the truth. Do not rely on screenshots alone.

The fourth mistake is forgetting about failed payments and disputes. Sometimes a card payment fails because the customer's bank declined it. When this happens, stay calm, check your dashboard, and contact the platform's support team with the transaction reference. Keep records of every order, including the customer's name, the product, the amount, and the delivery address.

The fifth mistake is not telling customers that you accept online payments. Put it in your WhatsApp bio, your Instagram bio, and your product descriptions. Say "We accept card, transfer and USSD payments" so customers know before they ask. Many sellers set up the account and then never mention it, which defeats the purpose.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need a registered business to use Paystack or Flutterwave?

No. Both platforms accept unregistered businesses, though you will need to provide your BVN and a means of identification. A registered business builds more trust and makes it easier to open a business bank account, so register when you can.

### How much does it cost to open an account?

Opening an account is free on both platforms. You only pay a small fee on each payment you receive, usually around 1.4 to 1.5 percent plus a small fixed amount. There is no monthly subscription for the basic setup.

### How long does it take to receive my money?

Both platforms usually settle payments into your bank account on the next business day. Check your settlement settings in the dashboard to confirm the timing for your account.

### Can customers outside Nigeria pay me?

Yes, especially with Flutterwave, which supports international cards and other African currencies. Paystack also supports international card payments. Check the current options on each platform before you promise an international customer.

### What happens if a customer's payment fails?

The customer will see a message that the payment did not go through, and no money will be taken. Ask them to try another card, use USSD, or pay by bank transfer. Check your dashboard to confirm whether the payment was received before you send the product.

## What to Do Next

1. Choose one platform today and create your account. Gather your BVN, your ID, and your bank account details, then follow the setup steps in this guide. Submit your verification and wait for approval.

2. Create payment links for your five best-selling products this week. Test each link yourself, check that the name and price are correct, and save the links in a note on your phone so you can send them quickly.

3. Tell your customers. Update your WhatsApp bio and Instagram bio to say you accept card, transfer, and USSD payments, and start sending payment links instead of account numbers. Track your sales for one month and compare them with the month before to see the difference.
