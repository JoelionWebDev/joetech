When someone in Lagos searches Google for "plumber near me," "best salon in Lekki," or "cake vendor in Ikeja," Google does not show a random list of websites. It shows a map with a short list of businesses, each with a name, a star rating, opening hours, and a phone number. Those businesses get the calls. The ones that do not appear on that map are invisible to the person searching, no matter how good their work is. That map comes from Google Business Profile, a free tool from Google, and any Nigerian small business can set one up.

A Google Business Profile is a free listing that puts your business on Google Search and Google Maps. When customers search for what you sell in your area, your profile can appear with your photos, your services, your prices, your opening hours, and reviews from your customers. It costs nothing to create, it takes less than an hour to set up, and it works around the clock. While you sleep, someone searching at midnight can find your number and call you in the morning. For a small business, that kind of visibility is hard to get anywhere else for free.

Many Nigerian business owners have heard of Google Business Profile but have never set one up, often because it sounds technical or only for big companies. It is not. It is designed for small local businesses: the tailor, the pharmacy, the restaurant, the laundry service, the electrician, the event hall, the school. This guide takes you through the whole process step by step, from creating the profile to getting verified, filling it in, adding photos, and collecting the reviews that push you higher in the results.

## What a Google Business Profile Is and Why It Matters

A Google Business Profile is your business's official listing on Google. It is what appears in the box on the right side of a Google search result when someone searches for your business by name, and it is what appears in the map results when someone searches for a type of business in an area. The profile shows your business name, category, address or service area, phone number, website, opening hours, photos, customer reviews, and short posts you publish. It is the first thing many customers see before they ever visit your website or your WhatsApp.

It matters because of how Nigerians search today. Before choosing a restaurant, a salon, a hotel, or a repair service, most people search on Google or Google Maps. They compare the businesses that appear, read a few reviews, check the photos, and then call or get directions.

It also builds trust. A business with a verified Google profile, real photos, and genuine reviews looks established and reliable. A business with no online presence at all raises questions. Customers wonder whether the business is real, whether it is still operating, and whether anyone has used it before. A complete profile answers those questions before the customer even calls you. And because Google shows your star rating next to your name, good reviews become a powerful reason for a stranger to choose you over a competitor with fewer or no reviews.

## Create Your Profile Step by Step

Start by going to the Google Business Profile website and signing in with a Google account. Use the Google account you want tied to your business — ideally one you control and will keep, such as your business Gmail address. If you do not have a Gmail account, create one first. It is free and takes a few minutes. Once signed in, click the option to add your business or manage your business, and Google will guide you through the setup.

First, enter your business name exactly as customers know it. Use your real trading name, without adding extra keywords or your location to the name. For example, use "Ada's Kitchen" rather than "Ada's Kitchen Best Food in Lekki." Google can penalise listings that stuff keywords into the business name. Next, choose your business category. Pick the category that best describes what you do, such as "Restaurant," "Hair Salon," "Pharmacy," or "Event Venue." You can add more categories later, but the first one should be the closest match to your main service, because it strongly affects which searches you appear for.

Next, Google will ask about your location. If customers visit your shop, office, or restaurant, add your full address, including the street, area, and city — for example, "14 Admiralty Way, Lekki Phase 1, Lagos." If you serve customers at their location instead, such as a cleaning service or a dispatch rider, choose the option to hide your address and set your service area instead, listing the areas you cover, such as Lekki, Ajah, and Victoria Island. Then add your phone number and website if you have one. Use a phone number you actually answer, because customers will call it. If you do not have a website yet, you can leave that field empty for now and add it later.

Finally, Google will ask you to verify your business. Verification proves that you are the real owner and that the business exists at the location you gave. The most common method in Nigeria is verification by video. Google will ask you to record a short video showing your business: the outside of the building with the street name or sign, the inside of your shop or office, and proof that you operate there, such as your equipment, stock, or branded materials. Follow the instructions carefully, record the video in good daylight, and upload it. Verification usually takes a few days, though sometimes it is approved within a day. Until your profile is verified, it will not appear fully in search results, so complete this step as soon as you can.

## Fill In Every Detail Properly

Once your profile is created, fill in every section. An empty profile looks abandoned and ranks poorly. A complete profile looks professional and shows up more often. Start with your opening hours. Add the hours you are actually open, including different hours for different days if needed. If you are open on Saturdays but closed on Sundays, say so. Customers who arrive at a closed shop because Google said you were open will be annoyed, and some will leave a bad review about it. Keep your hours updated during public holidays and festive periods too.

Write a clear business description. Google gives you space to describe your business in your own words. Say what you do, who you serve, and what makes you different, in simple language. For example: "We are a family restaurant in Surulere serving Nigerian dishes, grills, and fresh juice, with dine-in, takeaway, and delivery across Lagos mainland." Avoid filling the description with a long list of keywords. Write it for a human customer, not for a search engine. A clear description helps customers decide quickly whether you are what they are looking for.

Add your products and services. Google lets you list the services you offer, with a short description and a price for each. A salon can list hair styling, braiding, manicure, and pedicure with their prices. A pharmacy can list the categories of medicine it stocks. A catering company can list its packages. This section helps your profile appear when people search for those specific services, and it helps customers know what to expect before they call. Add as many of your main services as you can, with honest prices.

## Add Photos and Posts That Sell

Photos are one of the most important parts of your profile. Customers decide with their eyes, and businesses with good photos get far more calls and direction requests than those with none. Add a clear logo as your profile photo, and a good photo of the outside of your business as your cover photo, so people can recognise your shop when they arrive. Then add photos of the inside of your premises, your products, your staff at work, and your finished results. A restaurant should show its food and its dining area. A tailor should show finished outfits. A hotel should show its rooms and its event hall.

Take your photos in good daylight, keep the background tidy, and make sure the subject is clear. You do not need a professional camera — a phone camera is fine. Add new photos regularly, at least a few each month. Fresh photos show Google and customers that your business is active. Photos of new stock, a recently completed job, a decorated event space, or a special dish all help. Avoid blurry photos, photos with heavy filters, and stock photos downloaded from the internet. Customers trust real photos of your real business.

Use Google Posts to share short updates. Posts appear on your profile and can announce a new product, a promotion, an event, or a seasonal offer. A post is short — a photo, a few lines of text, and a button such as "Call now" or "Learn more." For example, a bakery can post about a Christmas cake promo, a gym can post about a new year discount, and a pharmacy can post about a health talk. Posts expire after a while, so publish something new every week or two. Regular posts keep your profile looking alive and give customers a reason to choose you this week rather than later.

## Get Reviews and Respond to Every One

Reviews are the engine of a Google Business Profile. They affect where you rank in the map results, and they are often the first thing a customer reads before deciding to call. A business with twenty genuine five-star reviews will beat a business with no reviews almost every time, even if the second business is better. Your job is to collect reviews steadily and honestly, starting from your very first customers.

Ask for reviews at the right moment: right after a customer has had a good experience. When a customer compliments your food, your service, or your work, say thank you and ask if they would be willing to leave a short review on Google. Make it easy by sharing your review link. Google gives you a short link you can send on WhatsApp or print on a card or receipt. The link opens Google directly at the review form, so the customer only needs to tap the stars and write a sentence. Most happy customers will do it if you ask at the right time and make it simple.

Never buy fake reviews and never write reviews for your own business. Google detects suspicious review patterns and can remove the reviews or suspend your profile. Fake reviews also damage trust: customers can often tell when reviews are not genuine, and one discovered fake can cost you more credibility than ten real reviews would earn you. Build your reviews slowly and honestly, one real customer at a time.

Respond to every review, good and bad. Thank customers who leave positive reviews, mentioning something specific if you can: "Thank you, Mrs. Adeyemi, we are glad you enjoyed the jollof rice." For negative reviews, respond calmly and professionally. Apologise for the poor experience, explain briefly what happened if appropriate, and invite the customer to contact you so you can fix it. Never argue with a reviewer in public. Other customers read your replies, and a polite, helpful response to a complaint tells them more about your character than the complaint itself does. A business that handles criticism well earns more trust, not less.

## Keep Your Profile Active and Accurate

A Google Business Profile is not a one-time task. It needs regular attention to keep working for you. Check it at least once a week. Make sure your hours are still correct, your phone number still works, and your photos are current. If you move location, change your number, or change your opening hours, update the profile the same day. Outdated information leads to missed calls, lost customers, and bad reviews from people who followed wrong directions.

Watch the insights Google gives you. Your profile dashboard shows how many people found you on Search and on Maps, what words they searched for, how many called you, how many asked for directions, and how many visited your website. These numbers tell you what is working. If most people find you by searching for a particular service, consider adding more photos and services around it. If many people ask for directions, your area targeting is working. If nobody is calling, your photos or reviews may need attention. Use the data to improve, not just to admire.

Stay consistent with your business information everywhere. Your business name, address, and phone number should be the same on your Google profile, your website, your Facebook page, and your Instagram bio. When Google sees the same details repeated consistently across the internet, it trusts your listing more. If your profile says one phone number and your website says another, customers get confused and Google gets less certain about which details to show.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is a Google Business Profile really free?

Yes. Creating and maintaining a Google Business Profile costs nothing. Google does offer paid advertising separately, but the profile itself, the map listing, the reviews, the photos, and the posts are all free. Be careful of anyone who charges you a large fee just to create a profile for you. The setup is simple enough to do yourself in under an hour, following the steps in this guide.

### What if my business has no physical shop or office?

You can still create a profile. If you serve customers at their location, such as a home service, a caterer, or a dispatch business, choose the service-area option and hide your address. Your profile will show the areas you cover instead of a street address. Home-based businesses can do the same. You only need a physical address for verification purposes, and Google allows you to keep it hidden from the public.

### How long does verification take in Nigeria?

It depends on the method. Video verification is the most common method in Nigeria, and it usually takes between one and seven days after you submit the video. Make sure your video clearly shows your business name or signage, the street outside, and the inside of your premises with your stock or equipment. A clear video gets approved faster. If your verification is delayed, check your email for a message from Google asking for more information.

### How many reviews do I need to rank well?

There is no fixed number, but more genuine reviews help, especially when they mention your service and your area. A business with 20 to 30 honest reviews and a good average rating will usually outrank a similar business with only two or three. Focus on collecting reviews steadily from real customers rather than trying to get a large number quickly. A few new reviews every month is a healthy pace.

### Can I manage my profile from my phone?

Yes. Google offers a mobile app and a mobile-friendly website for managing your profile. You can reply to reviews, add photos, publish posts, update your hours, and check your insights from your phone. This makes it easy to keep your profile active even when you are away from your shop. Set aside a few minutes each week to check it.

## What to Do Next

First, create your Google Business Profile today. Sign in with your business Google account, enter your business name, category, location, and phone number, and submit your verification video showing your premises clearly in daylight. Do not delay this step — verification takes days, so the sooner you start, the sooner you appear in search results.

Second, complete every section of your profile this week. Add your opening hours, a clear business description, your main products and services with prices, your attributes, and at least ten good photos of your premises, your products, and your work. A complete profile ranks better and convinces more customers to call.

Third, ask your next five happy customers to leave a review on Google, and send each of them your review link on WhatsApp right after they buy. Then reply to every review you receive, good or bad, within a day. Reviews and replies are what turn a simple listing into a steady source of new customers.
