Think about how you find a new salon, pharmacy, or mechanic in Lagos. You open Google Maps, type what you need plus the area — "salon near Lekki Phase 1" — and tap the first result with good reviews. Your customers do the same.

If your business is not on Google Maps, you are invisible to those customers. They will find your competitor instead. If your details are wrong or you have no reviews, they will skip you too.

This guide shows you how to use Google Maps to get found in Nigeria: create and verify a Google Business Profile, choose the right category, add photos and hours that build trust, and get reviews that bring walk-in customers. You do not need a website or money to start — just a phone, a Google account, and an hour.

## Why Google Maps Matters for Nigerian Businesses

Most buying in Nigeria starts with a search. A person in Chevron needs a tailor. A family in Abuja needs a cake for Saturday. They search on their phone, often while on the road. Maps shows businesses nearby, with distance, hours, phone numbers, and reviews in one place.

That is powerful for small businesses. A shop in a side street in Yaba can appear next to a big brand, if its profile is complete and reviews are strong. A home-based baker in Magodo can get orders from across Ikeja who would never have found her on Instagram alone.

Maps also builds trust. When a customer sees your real location, photos, and reviews, they feel safer to call or visit. In a market where fake pages are common, a verified Maps listing is proof you are real.

Best of all, it is free. Google Business Profile, the tool behind Maps listings, costs nothing. You can update it from your phone in minutes. It works even if you do not have a website yet, and it helps your website rank better when you do have one.

## Set Up Your Google Business Profile

Your Google Business Profile is the listing customers see on Maps and in Google Search. Setting it up takes about thirty minutes if you have your details ready.

Step one: go to google.com/business on your phone or computer and sign in with a Google account. Use a business email if you have one, such as info@yourbusiness.ng, not a personal email you might lose. If you do not have a business email yet, use your main Gmail and keep the login safe.

Step two: enter your exact business name as customers know it. Use your real trading name, for example "Lily Gold Fashion Home" or "De Phantom Continental Hotel." Do not add extra keywords like "Best Salon in Lagos Cheap Price" to your name. Google may reject it, and customers will trust you less.

Step three: choose your business type. If customers visit you, choose a shop or office with a physical address. If you deliver or visit customers, such as a home cleaning service or a dispatch rider, choose a service-area business and list the areas you cover, such as Lekki, Ajah, and Victoria Island. If you run a home-based business and do not want your home address public, you can hide the exact address and show only your service area. That is allowed and common in Nigeria.

Step four: choose your primary category carefully. It decides which searches you appear for. A fashion designer should pick "Fashion Designer," not just "Shop." A website designer should pick "Website Designer." Pick the closest match, then add two or three secondary categories if they truly fit. Do not add ten categories hoping to appear everywhere.

Step five: add your phone number, WhatsApp number, and website if you have one. Use the number you actually answer during business hours. Many customers will call or WhatsApp before they visit. If the number does not answer, they move to the next listing.

Step six: write a short description in plain language. Say what you sell, who you serve, and where you are. For example: "We make ready-to-wear and custom dresses for women in Lekki. Visit our shop at Tulip Heaven Estate Gate 1, Chevron Alternative Route, or order on WhatsApp for delivery across Lagos." Keep it honest and specific. Do not copy another business description.

Step seven: set your opening hours correctly, including weekends and holidays. Use special hours for Christmas, Eid, and New Year. Nothing frustrates a customer more than driving to a shop Google said was open, only to find it locked.

## Verify Your Business So Customers Trust You

Verification proves to Google that your business is real and that you control the listing. Until you verify, your listing may not show fully, and you cannot reply to reviews or update some details.

Google usually offers verification by postcard, phone, email, or video. In Nigeria, video verification is now common. You may be asked to record a short video showing your shop sign, your street, your products, and your business documents. Follow the instructions exactly. Show your signboard clearly, walk from the street into the shop, and show your CAC certificate or utility bill if asked.

Prepare before verification. Make sure your signboard matches your business name. Have your business registration or a utility bill nearby. Charge your phone and use good daylight for the video.

If verification fails, do not create a second listing. That creates duplicates that confuse customers. Read the reason Google gives, fix it, and try again. Common reasons are a name that does not match the signboard or a video that is too dark.

Once verified, you will see a tick or a "Verified" label in your dashboard. Keep your login safe. Do not share your Google account password with staff. If staff need to help, add them as a manager from your dashboard instead.

## Add Photos That Make People Visit

Photos are the first thing customers look at on Maps. A listing with clear, recent photos gets far more calls and visits than one with no photos or old, blurry ones.

Add at least ten photos. Start with the outside of your shop, so customers can recognise it. Add the inside, your products, and your team at work. A tailor should show finished dresses and fabric. A restaurant should show the dining area and plated food.

Use your phone camera in daylight. Do not use stock photos from the internet. Customers can tell, and they will feel cheated when the real shop looks different. Update photos every few months when you change stock or decor.

Add a short video if you can. A thirty-second walk from the gate to your shop helps customers find you where addresses are confusing. Mention landmarks: "We are opposite the Chevron gate." Landmarks help more than street numbers in Lagos and Abuja.


## Get Reviews That Bring More Customers

Reviews are the main reason customers choose one business over another on Maps. A business with twenty honest reviews and a 4.5-star rating will beat a business with no reviews, even if the second business is closer.

Ask for reviews at the right moment: just after a customer is happy. When a customer says, "I love this dress," or "The food was great," reply, "Thank you. Please help us by leaving a short review on Google Maps. It takes one minute." Show them the link or a QR code on your counter. Many customers will do it immediately if you make it easy. You can create a QR code that opens your review page with a free tool — for example, Joetech's free QR code generator at /tools/qr-code-generator — and print it on your receipt or shop wall.

Make the review link easy to share. In your dashboard, go to "Ask for reviews" and copy the short link. Add it to your WhatsApp status after a delivery and to your thank-you message. Send it personally after a good experience, not as a spam broadcast.

Never buy fake reviews, and never ask family to post reviews for a business they have never used. Google can detect fake reviews and may remove them or suspend your listing. Worse, customers can tell when reviews sound fake. Ten real reviews with specific details — "The jollof rice was fresh and delivery to Ikeja took forty minutes" — are worth more than fifty generic "Great service" reviews.

Reply to every review. Thank good reviewers by name. For a bad review, stay calm: apologise, explain what you will fix, and invite the customer to contact you directly. Other customers will judge you by how you handle problems.

If you get a fake review from someone who never bought from you, do not argue in public. Reply politely asking for their order details, then report the review to Google as fake. Keep your order records so you can show Google the person was never a customer.

## Use Posts, Offers and Questions on Your Profile

Your Google Business Profile is not just a listing. It is a free place to post updates, offers, and news, just like social media. Many Nigerian businesses ignore this, which is your chance to stand out.

Post at least once a week. Share a new product, a price offer, a holiday opening time, or a photo of a recent job. Keep posts short: one photo, two or three sentences, and a clear next step such as "Call or WhatsApp to order." Posts expire after a while, so keep them fresh. A profile with recent posts looks active and trustworthy. A profile with nothing since last year looks closed, even if you are open.

Add products and services with prices in naira where possible. Customers want to know the price before they call. A salon can list "Braids from ₦15,000." Clear prices reduce wasted calls and attract serious buyers.

Answer the Questions section. Customers often ask, "Do you deliver to Abuja?" or "Are you open on Sundays?" or "Do you have parking?" Answer these questions yourself on your profile before customers ask. That way the answer is there for everyone, and you save time replying to the same question daily.

Use the messaging feature if you can reply quickly. Turn on WhatsApp or Google messaging only if you or your staff will reply within an hour during business hours. A message left unanswered for a day is worse than no messaging at all. Set an automatic reply that says when you will respond.

## Help Customers Find You in Real Life

Google Maps is only useful if customers can actually reach you. In Nigeria, street addresses alone are often not enough. Estates, markets, and new areas may not have clear house numbers. Help customers with landmarks and directions.

In your description and in your posts, add a landmark sentence: "We are inside Tulip Heaven Estate, Gate 1, Chevron Alternative Route, opposite the filling station." Or: "Find us on Allen Avenue, two shops after the Zenith Bank." That one sentence can save a customer twenty minutes of calling you while lost.

Check your pin yourself. Search for your business on Maps. Is the pin on your building or the next street? If it is wrong, drag it to the correct spot. A wrong pin sends customers to the wrong gate.

Add your plus code if your address is hard to find. A plus code is a short code Google gives to any location, even without a street address. You can find it by tapping your location on Maps. Share it with dispatch riders and customers who struggle with your address.

If you are inside a market, plaza, or estate, add that to your address line: "Shop 12, Block B, Computer Village, Ikeja." If you move location, update Maps the same day. Do not leave an old address online for months while customers go to the wrong place.

## Track What Is Working and Fix What Is Not

Google gives you free insights about how customers find you. In your Business Profile dashboard, check how many people found you by searching your business name and how many found you by searching a service, such as "salon near me." Check how many called you, asked for directions, or visited your website. Check which photos get the most views.

Use that data to improve. If most people find you by searching "cake in Abuja," make sure your description mentions cakes and Abuja clearly. If many ask for directions but few call, your phone number or hours may be unclear.

Check your ranking weekly. Search for your service plus your area, for example "fashion designer Lekki." Are you in the top three on Maps? If not, add more photos, get more reviews, and post more often.

Do not pay someone who promises to put you first on Google Maps in one day for a fee. No one can guarantee the top spot. Ranking depends on relevance, distance, and reputation over time. Anyone who asks for money to "activate" your Maps listing is likely running a scam. Your listing is free, and you can manage it yourself.

## Common Mistakes Nigerian Businesses Make on Maps

The first mistake is creating two or three listings for the same business. Search for your business name on Maps, claim duplicates, and ask Google to merge or remove the extras. Keep one listing with the correct details.

The second mistake is using a personal name instead of a business name, or adding a phone number to the business name. Your listing should be your real business name only. Put the phone number in the phone field, not in the name.

The third mistake is ignoring reviews. A business that never replies to reviews looks like it does not care. Even a short "Thank you, Ada. We appreciate you" builds loyalty.

The fourth mistake is leaving outdated information online. If you change your phone number, move shop, or close on Sundays, update Maps immediately. Customers who call an old number will think you have closed down.

The fifth mistake is using only Instagram and ignoring Google. Instagram is great for showing your products, but Google Maps is where people search when they are ready to buy or visit now. Use both. Post your products on Instagram, and make sure your Maps listing has the same phone number and address so customers can find you.

## A Simple Weekly Routine to Stay Visible

You do not need to spend hours on Google Maps every day. A short weekly routine keeps your listing fresh and visible.

Monday: check your reviews and reply to any new ones. Check your messages and missed calls from Maps.
Wednesday: post one photo or offer. It can be a new product, a discount, or a customer photo with permission.
Friday: check your opening hours for the weekend and any holiday. Update if needed. Check your insights to see how many people called or asked for directions.
Monthly: add five new photos, check your pin location, search for your business name to spot fake listings, and ask five happy customers for reviews.

That routine takes less than an hour a week, but it keeps you ahead of competitors who set up a listing once and forget it. Google rewards active profiles with better visibility, and customers reward them with more visits.

## Conclusion: Be Easy to Find, Easy to Trust, Easy to Contact

Customers in Nigeria are searching on Google Maps every day for the services you offer. Your job is to make it easy for them to find you, trust you, and contact you. Create a complete Google Business Profile with the right category, correct phone number, clear hours, and real photos. Verify it. Ask happy customers for reviews and reply to them all. Post regularly, help customers with landmarks, and keep your details updated.

You do not need a big budget to appear on Maps. You need accuracy, consistency, and good service that earns reviews. Start today: search for your business on Google Maps. If you are not there, create your profile now. If you are there but the details are wrong, fix them now. Your next customer may already be searching for you.
