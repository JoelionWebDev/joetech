A customer messages a food vendor on WhatsApp and asks, "What do you have today, and how much is it?" The vendor replies with a long list typed from memory, then scrolls through the gallery to send photos. By the time the price is confirmed, the customer has moved on.

Now imagine the same customer opens the vendor's WhatsApp profile, taps the catalogue, and sees every dish with a photo and a price. She taps the one she wants and sends the order in two minutes, and the vendor never typed a list at all.

That is what a WhatsApp catalogue does. It turns your WhatsApp Business profile into a small shop window, and for many Nigerian small businesses it is the simplest online store they will ever need.

## What a WhatsApp catalogue is

A WhatsApp catalogue is a list of your products or services inside WhatsApp Business, each with a photo, a name, a price, and a short description. Customers can open your business profile, browse the catalogue, and tap any item to ask about it or order it. The item arrives in the chat automatically, so there is no confusion about which product the customer means.

A catalogue can hold hundreds of items, but most small businesses do well with their best twenty or thirty. It works for physical products like food, fashion, skincare, and electronics, and it works for services too: a photographer can list packages, a cleaner can list cleaning plans, and a tutor can list lesson packages, each with a price and a description.

The catalogue is free with the WhatsApp Business app. You do not need a website or any technical skill to set it up, just clear photos, honest prices, and a little time to organise it properly.

## Step one: set up WhatsApp Business properly

A catalogue only works well when the rest of your WhatsApp Business profile is set up. Before adding products, check these basics:

- **Use the WhatsApp Business app**, not the regular WhatsApp app. The catalogue feature lives in the Business version.
- **Fill in your business profile completely.** Add your business name, a clear description of what you sell, your location, your working hours, and your email or website if you have one.
- **Use a clear profile photo.** A logo or a clean photo of your product works far better than a random personal photo.
- **Set a greeting message.** A short automatic greeting tells a new customer they have reached the right business and what to do next.

## Step two: add products the right way

Adding a product takes about a minute each. Open WhatsApp Business, go to your catalogue, and add an item with these four things done properly:

1. **A clear photo.** Use a real photo of the actual product, taken in good daylight, with a plain background. One good photo is better than four dark or blurry ones. If you sell food, show the food as the customer will receive it. If you sell clothes, show the item being worn or laid flat.
2. **A clear name.** Name the product the way a customer would search for it: "Ankara midi dress, size 12" is better than "Dress 1". Include the size, colour, or flavour in the name when it matters.
3. **An honest price.** Put the real price on every item. Customers who have to ask the price for every single product get tired and leave. If a price changes, update the catalogue the same day.
4. **A short description.** Two or three sentences are enough: what it is, who it is for, and any important detail such as delivery time, minimum order, or what is included.

## Step three: organise and promote the catalogue

A catalogue nobody sees is wasted effort. Once your products are listed, tell people about it in every place your customers already find you:

- **Share the catalogue link.** WhatsApp Business gives you a link to your catalogue. Put it in your Instagram bio, your Facebook page, your status, and your email signature.
- **Send it to new contacts.** When a new customer messages you, send them the catalogue link early in the conversation instead of typing a list.
- **Post about it in your status.** Once or twice a week, share one product from the catalogue in your WhatsApp status with a short note about it. Do not post ten products at once. One clear product at a time works better.
- **Use catalogue collections.** If the app offers collections, group your products: "New arrivals", "Best sellers", "Under a budget", or by category. Collections make a large catalogue easy to browse.

## How to turn catalogue browsers into buyers

A catalogue gets customers browsing. Your replies turn browsing into buying. A few habits make the difference:

- **Reply fast.** When a customer taps a product and messages you, they are ready to buy. A reply within minutes closes far more sales than a reply hours later.
- **Confirm the details once.** Repeat the product, the price, the delivery area, and the delivery time in one short message, then ask how they want to pay. This removes doubt and prevents mistakes.
- **Offer a clear way to pay.** Tell the customer exactly how to pay: transfer to a named account, or payment on delivery if you offer it. Send the account details in the same message as the order confirmation.
- **Follow up once, politely.** If a customer asks about a product and then goes quiet, one friendly follow-up the next day is good business. "Hello, just checking if you still want the dress in size 12. I have two left." More than one follow-up starts to feel like pressure.
- **Ask for a photo or a kind word after delivery.** When a happy customer shares a photo of your product, ask permission to share it in your status. Real customer photos are the best advertising a catalogue can have.

## Common catalogue mistakes to avoid

- **No prices on products.** This is the biggest mistake. Customers who must ask the price of every item will ask one and leave.
- **Poor photos.** Dark, blurry, or misleading photos make customers doubt the product itself. If you cannot photograph a product well, ask someone to help you, and retake the photos in daylight.
- **Outdated items.** Products you no longer sell, prices from last year, and items marked available that are out of stock all damage trust.
- **Too many items.** A catalogue should be a shop window, not a warehouse. Start with your best products and add more only when you can keep them all accurate.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is a WhatsApp catalogue free?**

Yes. The WhatsApp Business app and the catalogue feature are free. You only pay your normal data costs. There are no listing fees or commissions on sales made through the catalogue.

**How many products can I add to a catalogue?**

The catalogue supports hundreds of items, but most small businesses do best with their strongest twenty to fifty. A tidy catalogue outperforms a huge one that is hard to browse.

**Can customers pay through the catalogue?**

Customers cannot pay inside the catalogue itself. They tap a product, message you about it, and you agree payment the usual way: bank transfer, payment link, or payment on delivery. The catalogue makes choosing easy. Payment still happens in the chat.

**Do I still need a website if I have a WhatsApp catalogue?**

A catalogue is a great start for selling to customers who already know you. A website becomes useful when you want customers to find you on Google and trust you before they message you. Many businesses use both.

## Make your WhatsApp work like a shop

A WhatsApp catalogue costs nothing, takes an afternoon to set up, and saves you hours of typing the same lists every week. More importantly, it lets customers help themselves: they browse, they choose, and they message you ready to buy.

Set it up properly, keep it accurate, and share the link everywhere. A tidy catalogue with honest prices and quick answers is often all a small business needs to turn WhatsApp from a chat app into a real shop.
