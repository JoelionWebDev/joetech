You delivered the work. The client was happy. The invoice went out. And then — silence. Days pass. You send a polite "just checking in" message. It is read, but not answered. Two weeks later, you are still waiting for ₦250,000 that you already spent in your head on rent, stock and salaries.

If this sounds familiar, you are not alone. Late payment is one of the most common problems facing small businesses in Nigeria. It is rarely about dishonesty. Customers forget. Finance departments are slow. A company that promised to pay in 14 days pays in 45 because nobody chased them. A customer who meant to transfer the money got distracted and never came back to it. The uncomfortable truth is that many late payments happen because nobody asked firmly enough, at the right time, in the right way.

A good payment reminder system fixes this. It is not about being aggressive or embarrassing your customers. It is about being organised, consistent and polite — and making it easy for people to pay you. This guide shows you how to set up that system, what to say at each stage, and how to handle the difficult customers without losing them or your money.

## Why Customers Pay Late in Nigeria

Understanding the reason helps you choose the right response. Late payment usually falls into one of five categories.

**They forgot.** This is the most common reason, especially with individual customers and small businesses. Your invoice arrived on a busy day, they meant to pay, and life got in the way. A friendly reminder solves this instantly.

**Their process is slow.** Companies, schools, churches and government-adjacent organisations often need multiple approvals before money moves. The person you dealt with may have approved the payment weeks ago, but the finance officer or the MD has not signed. These customers need reminders that help them push the payment through their own system.

**They are unhappy with something.** A customer who has a complaint — a late delivery, a small defect, a service that fell short — may withhold payment without telling you. If your reminder uncovers a complaint, that is useful information. Fix the issue and the payment usually follows.

Most of your late payers are in the first three categories. That is good news, because those three respond well to a polite, well-timed reminder system.

## Step 1: Prevent Late Payment Before the Invoice Exists

The best payment reminder is the one you never have to send. Most late payment problems are created before the work starts, when terms are vague and expectations are unclear.

Agree the payment terms in writing before you start work. A simple message is enough: "The total is ₦350,000. Payment terms: 50 percent upfront (₦175,000) before we start, and the balance of ₦175,000 on delivery." When the terms are written down and the customer has agreed to them, chasing payment later is a matter of referring to an agreement, not starting an argument.

Always collect a deposit. For services like web design, branding or event planning, 50 percent upfront is standard and reasonable. For goods, full payment before delivery is normal for new customers. A deposit does two things: it covers your costs if the customer disappears, and it proves the customer is serious. Customers who have already paid half are far more likely to pay the balance on time, because they have already invested in the outcome.

Put the payment details on every invoice and every quote. Your bank name, account number and account name should be impossible to miss. If you use Paystack or Flutterwave, include a payment link so the customer can pay in two taps. Every extra step between "I want to pay" and "I have paid" is a chance for the customer to get distracted.

Send the invoice immediately. The day the work is delivered or the goods are handed over, send the invoice the same day. Every day you delay the invoice is a day added to the time you wait for payment. Include the invoice number, the date, the amount, the due date and a short description of what was delivered.

## Step 2: Build a Reminder Schedule and Stick to It

A reminder system is a timetable, not a mood. Decide in advance when each reminder goes out, and follow the timetable for every customer, every time. Here is a schedule that works well for Nigerian small businesses.

**Before the due date — 3 days before.** Send a friendly heads-up. "Good morning, Mrs Adeyemi. This is a gentle reminder that invoice INV-104 for ₦180,000 is due on Friday, 28 November. Here are the payment details again. Thank you." This message does two things: it reminds the customer, and it gives them time to arrange the money before the deadline. Many customers pay at this stage, simply because the reminder arrived while the payment was still easy to make.

**On the due date.** If the payment has not arrived, send a short message. "Good afternoon, Mr Okafor. Invoice INV-087 for ₦250,000 is due today. Kindly confirm once payment has been made. Thank you." Keep it warm and professional. The customer is not yet late; the payment is simply due.

**3 days after the due date.** Now the payment is overdue, and your tone should shift slightly. "Good morning, Mrs Adeyemi. Invoice INV-104 for ₦180,000 was due on 28 November and is now 3 days overdue. Please let us know when we can expect payment, or if there is any issue we can help with." This message asks for a specific answer — a date — rather than a vague promise.

**7 days after the due date.** This is the firm reminder. "Good afternoon, Mr Okafor. Invoice INV-087 for ₦250,000 is now 7 days overdue. We would appreciate payment by Friday, 5 December. If there is a challenge with the payment, please let us know so we can discuss a plan." You are now giving a new, specific deadline, and you are offering a conversation about the problem.

**14 days after the due date.** This is the serious reminder. "Dear Mrs Adeyemi, invoice INV-104 for ₦180,000 is now 14 days overdue. We value our relationship, but we need to resolve this. Please make payment by Wednesday, 10 December, or contact us to agree a payment plan." At this point, you should also consider pausing further work or deliveries until the account is settled.

**30 days after the due date.** This is the final notice before you escalate. "Dear Mr Okafor, invoice INV-087 for ₦250,000 has been outstanding for 30 days. If payment is not received by 17 December, we will have to hand this matter over for further action." What "further action" means depends on your business — it may be a formal demand letter, a report to a trade association, or legal advice. Do not threaten something you are not prepared to do.

The exact number of days can flex for your business, but the principle does not: reminders go out on schedule, every time, to every customer. The moment you chase one customer firmly and let another slide for a month, you teach both of them how your system works — and the one you let slide will slide further next time.

## Step 3: Write Reminders That Get Replies

Most payment reminders fail because they are vague. "Please pay up" or "We are still waiting for payment" gives the customer nothing to act on. A good reminder has five elements: the invoice number, the exact amount, the due date, how many days overdue it is, and how to pay. Put all five in every reminder.

Keep the tone polite but direct. Nigerian business culture values respect, and a rude reminder can damage a relationship that is worth far more than one invoice. But polite does not mean vague. Compare these two messages. "Good morning sir, please we are still waiting for the payment, kindly assist us" is polite and useless. "Good morning sir, invoice INV-087 for ₦250,000 was due on 21 November and is now 7 days overdue. Kindly transfer to Zenith Bank, 1012345678, Joetech, or use this payment link. Please confirm once paid" is polite, specific and actionable. The customer knows exactly what to do.

Use the customer's name and the right title. A message that starts "Good morning, Mrs Adeyemi" feels personal. A message that starts "Dear customer" feels like a mass broadcast and is easy to ignore. If you know the person prefers to be called "Engr. Musa" or "Pastor Johnson", use that title. Respect costs nothing and opens doors.

Choose the right channel. For most Nigerian small businesses, WhatsApp is the best channel for payment reminders. Messages are read quickly, and you can see when they have been delivered and read. Email works well for corporate clients, where the invoice needs to go to a finance department. A phone call works best when a message has been ignored twice — it is harder to ignore a voice than a text.

## Step 4: Use Templates So You Never Stare at a Blank Screen

The hardest part of chasing payment is deciding what to say. Templates remove that friction. Write five templates once, save them in your phone's notes or in a document, and adapt the names, amounts and dates each time you use them. Here are templates you can copy and adjust.

**Template 1 — Before the due date.** "Good morning [Name]. A friendly reminder that invoice [INV-000] for ₦[amount] is due on [date]. Payment can be made to [bank, account number, account name] or via this link: [link]. Thank you for your business."

**Template 2 — On the due date.** "Good afternoon [Name]. Invoice [INV-000] for ₦[amount] is due today, [date]. Kindly confirm once payment has been made. Thank you."

**Template 3 — 3 days overdue.** "Good morning [Name]. Invoice [INV-000] for ₦[amount] was due on [date] and is now 3 days overdue. Please let us know when we can expect payment, or if there is any issue we can help with. Thank you."

**Template 4 — 7 days overdue.** "Good afternoon [Name]. Invoice [INV-000] for ₦[amount] is now 7 days overdue. We would appreciate payment by [new date]. If there is a challenge, please let us know so we can discuss a plan."

**Template 5 — 14 days overdue.** "Dear [Name], invoice [INV-000] for ₦[amount] is now 14 days overdue. We value our relationship and would like to resolve this. Please make payment by [new date] or contact us to agree a payment plan. Further work will be paused until this is settled."

Keep your templates short. A reminder that fits on a phone screen without scrolling is more likely to be read in full. And always attach or resend the invoice with the reminder. Customers often delay because they cannot find the original invoice or the account details. Removing that excuse costs you ten seconds.

## Step 5: Handle Difficult Customers Without Losing Them

Most late payers will pay after the second or third reminder. A few will not, and they need a different approach.

**The customer who keeps promising "tomorrow".** Ask for a specific date and a specific amount, then confirm it in writing. "Thank you, so we agree you will pay ₦150,000 by Friday, 5 December. I will check in on Friday morning." If Friday passes without payment, follow up on Friday. A promise with a date is a commitment. A promise without a date is a delay tactic.

**The customer who disputes the work.** Listen properly before defending yourself. Ask what exactly is wrong. If the complaint is valid, fix it quickly and then remind them that payment is due. If the complaint is a stalling tactic — the work was approved weeks ago and the complaint only appeared when payment was chased — say so calmly.

**The customer who asks for a discount after the work is done.** This is common in Nigeria, and it is almost always a negotiation tactic, not a genuine complaint. Do not agree to a discount under pressure. If you want to offer anything, offer a small goodwill gesture tied to immediate payment: "If you pay the full balance today, we will include the extra social media graphics at no cost." Never reduce the price simply because the customer is late. That rewards late payment.

**The customer who cannot pay.** Sometimes the money genuinely is not there. In that case, a payment plan is better than no payment. Agree in writing: the total owed, the amount of each instalment, the date of each instalment, and what happens if an instalment is missed. For example, ₦300,000 paid as ₦100,000 on 5 December, ₦100,000 on 19 December and ₦100,000 on 2 January. A customer who pays in instalments is a customer who is trying.

## Step 6: Protect Yourself With Records and Policies

A reminder system only works if you can prove what happened. Keep a simple record for every invoice: the invoice number, the customer name, the amount, the date sent, the due date, every reminder sent with its date, every reply received, and every payment made. A spreadsheet is enough. When a customer says "I never received the invoice" or "nobody told me it was due", your records answer the question in seconds.

Also decide your policies in advance and apply them consistently. What happens when an invoice is 30 days overdue — do you pause work? Do you charge a late fee? Many Nigerian small businesses are nervous about late fees, but a modest one is reasonable and effective: for example, 2 percent of the outstanding amount per month after 14 days overdue, stated clearly on the invoice and in your terms. Even if you never enforce it, stating it signals that late payment has a cost.

For new customers, consider stricter terms until trust is built. Full payment upfront for the first order, or 70 percent upfront and 30 percent on delivery, is reasonable when you have no history with a customer. For returning customers with a good payment record, you can offer more flexible terms. Trust is earned through payment history, not given on the first transaction.

Finally, know when to stop working with a customer. If a customer has paid late three times, disputed invoices without cause, or left a balance unpaid for more than 60 days, the relationship is costing you more than it earns. Politely decline future work unless payment is made in full upfront. This is not punishment; it is business sense.

## What to Do When Nothing Works

Sometimes, despite your best system, a customer simply will not pay. Before you write the money off, try three final steps.

First, send a formal demand letter on your business letterhead, by email and by WhatsApp, stating the amount, the invoice number, the dates, and a final deadline of seven days. A letterhead changes the tone. It tells the customer this is now a formal matter, and many customers pay at this stage.

Second, visit in person if the amount justifies it and the customer is in Lagos or Abuja. A polite, in-person visit to an office is hard to ignore, and it often produces a cheque or a transfer on the spot. Take a colleague with you, stay calm, and keep the visit short.

Third, get advice. If the amount is significant — say, above ₦500,000 — a lawyer's letter often costs less than the amount owed and frequently produces payment. Your trade association or a small claims process may also be an option. Do not threaten legal action unless you are prepared to follow through, because an empty threat teaches the customer that your deadlines mean nothing.

## Build the Habit This Week

You do not need new software to start. You need one hour, a spreadsheet, and five templates. This week, list every unpaid invoice you have, with amounts and due dates. Send the right reminder for each one today. Then set a recurring time — Friday mornings work well — to review unpaid invoices and send the next round of reminders. Make it a weekly habit, the same way you check your stock or your bank balance.

Good payment reminders are not about conflict. They are about clarity, consistency and respect — for your work, for your time, and for the customer. When you remind politely, on schedule, with all the details in place, most customers will pay, and many will respect you more for it.

Your cash flow is the lifeblood of your business. Protect it with a system, and you will spend less time chasing money and more time earning it.
