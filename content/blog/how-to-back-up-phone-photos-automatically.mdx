Your phone holds your children's birthdays, your family events, your holidays, and — if you run a business — your product photos, your customer orders, and your receipts. Now imagine your phone is stolen tomorrow, or it falls into water, or it simply stops working. What happens to all those photos?

If you have automatic backup switched on, the answer is: nothing. Your photos are already safe online, and you can get them all back on a new phone in minutes. If you do not, they are gone forever.

Setting up automatic photo backup takes five minutes, costs nothing to start, and then runs by itself. This guide shows you how, whether you use an Android phone or an iPhone.

## What Automatic Backup Does

Automatic backup means that every photo and video you take is copied to the internet — to your Google account or your Apple account — without you doing anything. You take a photo, and in the background, your phone uploads a copy to the cloud.

Two things make this powerful. First, it happens by itself. You do not have to remember to back up, and you do not have to plug your phone into a computer. Second, it protects you from the worst day: the day your phone is lost, stolen, or damaged. A new phone, the same account, and your photos are back.

It also frees up space. Once your photos are safely backed up, you can remove the copies from your phone and keep only the recent ones there. Your phone runs better, and you stop seeing the "storage almost full" warning.

## Google Photos: For Android and iPhone

Google Photos is the best option for most Nigerians. It works on Android and iPhone, it is free to install, and it comes with 15 GB of free storage, shared across your Google account.

Here is how to set it up:

1. Install **Google Photos** from the Play Store or App Store, and open it.
2. Sign in with your Google account — the same one you use for Gmail.
3. Tap your profile picture, then **Photos settings**, then **Backup**.
4. Switch **Backup** on.
5. Choose your upload settings. Set it to upload on **Wi-Fi only**, so it does not eat your mobile data. If you have a good data plan, you can allow mobile data too, but Wi-Fi only is the safe choice.

That is it. From now on, every photo and video you take is uploaded automatically when you are on Wi-Fi. You can check that it is working by opening Google Photos and looking for the small cloud icon on your photos — a tick means the photo is backed up.

### The "Free up space" trick

Once your backup is complete, Google Photos can remove the copies from your phone for you. Tap your profile picture, then **Free up space**. The app deletes the phone copies of photos that are already backed up. Your photos stay safe in your Google account, and you can still see them in the app any time you have internet. This can free up several gigabytes in one tap.

### What to do when the 15 GB runs out

Google gives you 15 GB free, shared across Gmail, Google Drive, and Google Photos. That holds thousands of photos, but videos fill it faster. When you run low, you have three options: delete large videos and old files you no longer need, buy more space with a Google One plan, or back up your oldest photos to a computer or external drive and delete them from Google Photos. Check your storage in the Google Photos app under your profile picture, then **Account storage**.

## iCloud Photos: For iPhone

If you use an iPhone, Apple gives you iCloud Photos, which does the same job inside Apple's own system. It is already on your phone — you only need to switch it on.

1. Open **Settings** and tap your name at the top.
2. Tap **iCloud**, then **Photos**.
3. Switch on **Sync this iPhone**.

Your photos will now upload to iCloud automatically when you are on Wi-Fi and your phone is charging. You get 5 GB of free iCloud storage, which fills up fast — 5 GB holds a few thousand photos, but not much video. If you run out, Apple will ask you to buy more storage or free up space.

### The "Optimise iPhone Storage" trick

This is the iPhone version of freeing up space. In the same Photos settings screen, choose **Optimise iPhone Storage**. Your phone keeps small versions of your photos on the device and the full versions in iCloud. You can still see all your photos, and when you open one, the full version downloads. This can free up a lot of space without deleting anything.

## Which One Should You Use?

**If you use an Android phone**, use Google Photos. It is built for Android, it has the most free space, and it works well.

**If you use an iPhone**, you can use either. iCloud Photos is built in and works smoothly with your phone, but it only gives you 5 GB free. Google Photos gives you 15 GB free and works on both Android and iPhone, so if you ever switch phones, your photos move with you. Many iPhone users run Google Photos as well as iCloud, because the extra free space is worth it.

**If you run a business**, use Google Photos. Your product photos are business assets, and losing them means losing work. Back them up to Google Photos, and also keep a second copy somewhere else — a computer, an external drive, or a second cloud service. Two copies in two places is the rule for anything you cannot afford to lose.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Assuming backup is on when it is not.** Backup is not always switched on by default. Open your photo app today and check. On Google Photos, look for the backup status under your profile picture. On iPhone, check Settings, then your name, then iCloud, then Photos. If it is off, switch it on now.

**Backing up on mobile data.** Uploading thousands of photos on mobile data will finish your data bundle in hours. Set backup to Wi-Fi only, and let it run overnight when you are at home. The first backup takes the longest — after that, only new photos upload, which is quick.

**Never checking that it worked.** Backup is only useful if it actually happened. Once a month, open Google Photos or iCloud on another device — a laptop, a tablet, or a friend's phone with your account signed in — and check that your recent photos are there. If they are, your backup is working. If they are not, find out why before you need it.

**Relying on one copy.** A cloud backup is excellent, but it is still one place. If someone gets into your Google or Apple account, or you forget the password and cannot recover it, your photos are at risk. For your most precious photos — family, children, important business records — keep a second copy on a computer or an external hard drive as well.

**Deleting photos from your phone before the backup finishes.** The "free up space" feature only removes photos that are already backed up. But if you delete photos by hand before the upload finishes, they are gone. Wait until the app says the backup is complete before you delete anything.

## What Happens When You Get a New Phone?

This is where automatic backup shows its real value. When you get a new phone, sign in with the same Google or Apple account, install Google Photos or switch on iCloud Photos, and your photos start appearing. You do not need to transfer anything by hand.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is automatic photo backup really free?

The apps are free, and the storage is free up to a limit: 15 GB on Google Photos, 5 GB on iCloud. That is enough for thousands of photos. Videos use far more space, so if you record a lot of video, you may need to buy more space or move old videos to a computer.

### Will backup use up my mobile data?

Only if you let it. Set backup to Wi-Fi only in the app settings, and it will wait until you are on Wi-Fi. The first backup is the biggest — after that, only new photos upload, which uses very little data.

### Can I get my photos back if my phone is stolen?

Yes. Sign in to your Google or Apple account on a new phone or a computer, open Google Photos or iCloud Photos, and your backed-up photos are there. Download the ones you need, or keep them in the cloud and view them from the app.

### What happens to my photos if I stop paying for extra storage?

Your existing photos stay safe. You just cannot upload new ones until you free up space or buy more storage. Nothing is deleted because you stopped paying.

### Should I use Google Photos and iCloud at the same time?

You can, and many people do. It gives you two copies in two places, which is safer. But it also uses more data and more battery, because every photo uploads twice. For most people, one service set up properly is enough, plus a second copy of the most important photos on a computer or external drive.
