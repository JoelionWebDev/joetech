If you run a business in Nigeria, you already know the questions by heart. "Do you deliver to Abuja?" "How much is delivery to Lekki?" "Can I pay on delivery?" "What is your account number?" "Is this still available?" "When will it arrive?" "Do you sell wholesale?" You answer them on WhatsApp, on Instagram DM, in phone calls, and in person — ten, twenty, thirty times a day. The questions are fair. Customers need the answers before they spend their money. But answering the same questions over and over steals hours you could spend packing orders, cooking, sewing, or finding new customers.

An FAQ page — Frequently Asked Questions — solves that problem. It is a single page on your website where the most common questions are listed, with short, honest answers. A customer who is wondering about delivery fees can find the answer in ten seconds, at midnight, without waiting for you to wake up and reply. You save time, the customer gets instant answers, and your business looks more professional and trustworthy.

This guide shows you how to create an FAQ page that actually works for a Nigerian business: how to find the real questions your customers ask, how to write answers that are clear and honest, how to organise the page, and how to keep it updated as your business grows.

## Why Every Nigerian Business Website Needs an FAQ Page

An FAQ page does four jobs at once, and each one matters in the Nigerian market.

First, it saves you time. Every question answered on the page is a question you do not have to answer in a chat. If 20 customers a day ask about delivery, and your FAQ answers it, you have just saved yourself an hour of typing. Over a month, that is a full working week.

Second, it builds trust. Nigerian customers are rightly careful about buying online. Stories of scams, fake vendors and undelivered goods make people cautious. A business that answers questions openly — about payment, delivery, returns and refunds — looks honest. A business that hides that information looks suspicious. Your FAQ page is a trust signal.

Third, it helps customers buy. Many people abandon a purchase not because they do not want the product, but because one small question is unanswered. "Can I pay in two parts?" "Do you deliver on Sunday?" "What size is this in Nigerian sizing?" If the answer is on your FAQ page, they buy. If it is not, they leave and may never come back.

Fourth, it helps Google find your website. When people search "how much is delivery in Lagos" or "can I pay on delivery in Nigeria", Google can show your FAQ page as an answer. That brings you free visitors who are already interested in buying. This is one of the easiest ways for a small Nigerian business to get search traffic without paying for adverts.

## Step 1: Collect the Real Questions Your Customers Ask

Do not guess what your customers want to know. Do not copy an FAQ page from a big foreign website. Your customers ask Nigerian questions, and your page must answer Nigerian questions. The best source is your own phone.

Open WhatsApp Business, Instagram, Facebook Messenger and your email. Scroll back through the last month of messages. Write down every question that appears more than twice. You will quickly see a pattern. The same eight or ten questions will appear again and again, with different wording. Those are your FAQ questions.

If you are just starting and you do not have many messages yet, use these sources instead. Ask your friends and family to look at your website or your catalogue and write down every question they have. Check the comments on your competitors' Instagram posts — customers ask them the same things they will ask you. Check Google: type your product plus "Nigeria" into Google and look at the "People also ask" box. And think about the questions you would ask before buying from a stranger online.

Group the questions into themes. For most Nigerian businesses, the themes are the same: ordering, payment, delivery, returns and refunds, product details, and contacting you. Aim for 12 to 20 questions in total. Fewer than 10 and the page feels thin. More than 25 and customers will not read it.

## Step 2: Write Answers That Are Short, Honest and Specific

A good FAQ answer is short, specific and honest. A bad one is vague, long and full of corporate language. Compare these two answers to the same question.

Bad: "Delivery times may vary depending on several factors including location, availability and prevailing conditions. We endeavour to process all orders in a timely manner."

Good: "We deliver within Lagos in 24 hours, and to Abuja and Port Harcourt in 2 to 3 working days. Delivery within Lekki costs ₦2,000, within Ikeja costs ₦2,500, and to Abuja costs ₦4,500. You will receive a tracking message from our rider once your order leaves our shop."

The good answer gives numbers, places and times. The bad answer gives nothing. Nigerian customers need numbers. They need to know the fee before they pay, not after.

Write each answer in two to four sentences. Use plain English. Avoid jargon. Write the way you would speak to a customer standing in front of you. If a question has a complicated answer, give the short answer first, then add one sentence of detail. For example: "Yes, we accept payment on delivery within Lagos only. For orders outside Lagos, payment is by bank transfer or Paystack link before dispatch. This is because waybill and interstate delivery cannot be returned easily if a customer changes their mind."

Be honest about your limits. If you do not deliver to Maiduguri, say so. If you do not offer refunds on custom-made clothes, say so clearly. If your shop is closed on Sundays, say so. Customers respect honesty. They do not respect discovering a limit after they have paid.

## Step 3: Organise the Page So Customers Find Answers Fast

Nobody reads an FAQ page from top to bottom like a novel. They arrive with one question and they want that one answer, fast. Organise the page so they can find it in seconds.

Start with your most important questions at the top. For most Nigerian businesses, those are delivery and payment. A customer who cannot find the delivery fee will leave. Put "How much is delivery?" and "How do I pay?" near the top, not buried at the bottom.

Group the questions under clear headings. For example: Ordering, Payment, Delivery, Returns and Refunds, Products, and Contact Us. Under each heading, list three to five questions. This structure lets a customer scan the headings, jump to the right group, and find their answer without reading the whole page.

Use an accordion layout if your website supports it. An accordion shows the questions as a list, and the answer expands when the customer clicks or taps the question. This keeps the page short and tidy on a phone, which is how most Nigerian customers will read it. If your website is built on WordPress, most themes and page builders include an accordion block. If you built your site with a developer, ask them to add one — it is a small job.

Add a search box at the top of the page if your site supports it, so customers can type "refund" or "delivery" and jump straight to the answer. And at the bottom of the page, add a line for the questions you did not cover: "Still have a question? Chat with us on WhatsApp" with a link to your WhatsApp number. That way, the FAQ page reduces your chats without removing the human option for customers who need it.

## Step 4: Answer the Questions Nigerian Customers Actually Ask

Every business is different, but these are the questions that appear on almost every Nigerian FAQ page. Use them as a starting point and adapt the answers to your own business.

**How do I place an order?** Explain the exact steps: browse the catalogue, send a message on WhatsApp, confirm the price and delivery fee, pay, and receive confirmation. If you have a website shop, explain how to add to cart and check out.

**What payment methods do you accept?** List them all: bank transfer, Opay, Moniepoint, Paystack, Flutterwave, POS, and cash on delivery if you offer it. Give your account name and number, or explain that account details are sent after the order is confirmed. Never publish your full account number on a public page if you can avoid it — send it privately in a chat instead.

**Do you deliver, and how much does it cost?** Give real prices for real places: Lekki, Ikeja, Surulere, Abuja, Port Harcourt, Ibadan, Enugu, Kano. If you use waybill for interstate orders, say so, and give the approximate cost and time. If delivery is free above a certain order amount, say so clearly.

**How long will my order take?** Give honest times, and add a buffer. "Within Lagos: 24 hours. Abuja and Port Harcourt: 2 to 3 working days. Other states: 3 to 5 working days by waybill." If there is a delay, explain how you will communicate it.

**Can I pay on delivery?** Answer plainly. If yes, say where: "Yes, within Lagos only." If no, explain why in one sentence, and offer the alternative. Many Nigerian businesses offer pay on delivery only in their own city, because returning an interstate parcel is expensive.

**What is your return and refund policy?** This is the question customers care about most and businesses fear most. Be clear. "If your item arrives damaged or wrong, send us a photo within 24 hours and we will replace it or refund you." State the time limit, the condition, and what the customer should do. A clear policy builds more trust than a generous-sounding but vague one.

**Do you sell wholesale?** If you do, give the minimum quantity and the discount. "Yes, from 10 pieces, at 15 percent off the retail price. Chat with us for a wholesale price list." If you do not, say so.

**Where are you located?** Give your area and city, even if you do not have a walk-in shop. "We are based in Chevron, Lekki, Lagos. We operate online, with pickup available at our Lekki office." Customers want to know you are a real business in a real place.

## Step 5: Build the Page on Your Website

You do not need a developer to build a basic FAQ page, but you do need to know where it goes on your site.

If your website is on WordPress, add a new page called "FAQ" or "Frequently Asked Questions". Use your page builder or the block editor to add a heading, a short introduction, and then your grouped questions. Most WordPress themes include an accordion or toggle block — search for "accordion" in the block inserter. Add the page to your main menu, in the footer, and link to it from your contact page and your checkout page.

If your website was built by a developer, send them the questions and answers in a document and ask them to build the page with an accordion layout. Give them a deadline of a few days. This is a small job, and it should not cost much. If you are using a website builder such as Wix, Shopify or Squarespace, each one has a built-in FAQ section you can add in minutes.

Wherever you build it, test the page on your phone before you publish it. Most of your customers will read it on a phone, on mobile data, in a place with imperfect network. The page should load fast, the text should be readable without zooming, and the accordion should open and close smoothly. Ask a friend to find the answer to "How much is delivery?" on your page, and time them. If it takes more than 20 seconds, reorganise the page.

Add the FAQ link everywhere customers ask questions: in your WhatsApp Business profile, in your Instagram bio link, in your email signature, and at the bottom of every product page. The more places it appears, the fewer repetitive chats you will receive.

## Step 6: Keep the Page Updated

An FAQ page is not a one-time job. Prices change. Delivery areas change. Your policies change. An FAQ page with old prices is worse than no FAQ page, because it gives customers wrong information and creates arguments.

Review the page once a month. Check every price, every delivery fee, and every time estimate. If the cost of dispatch to Abuja has risen from ₦4,000 to ₦5,000, update the page the same day you raise the price. If you start delivering to a new city, add it. If you stop offering pay on delivery, remove it.

Also watch for new questions. Every month, look at your WhatsApp chats again. Is a new question appearing three or four times? Add it to the page. Is a question on the page that nobody ever asks? Remove it or rewrite it. Your FAQ page should reflect the questions your customers are actually asking this month, not the ones they asked last year.

Keep a simple log. When you add or change an answer, write the date in a notebook or a phone note. That way, if a customer says "Your website says delivery is ₦2,000", you can check when the page was last updated and whether you missed a change.

## Common FAQ Mistakes to Avoid

- **Copying another business's FAQ.** Their delivery areas, prices and policies are not yours. Customers will catch the mismatch.
- **Writing long, vague answers.** If an answer is longer than four sentences, shorten it. If it has no numbers, add them.
- **Hiding bad news.** If you do not offer refunds, say so. Customers who discover it after paying will be far angrier than customers who read it before buying.
- **Forgetting mobile users.** Most Nigerian customers browse on phones. Test every change on your phone, not only on a laptop.
- **No contact option.** The FAQ should reduce chats, not remove them. Always give a WhatsApp link for unanswered questions.
- **Never updating it.** An out-of-date FAQ creates more arguments than it prevents. Review it monthly, without fail.

## A Real Example: A Fashion Seller in Ikeja

Consider Funke, who sells dresses and shoes from her shop in Ikeja and on Instagram. Before her FAQ page, she answered the same five questions about 40 times a day: delivery fees, payment methods, sizes, returns, and whether she delivered to Abuja. She was spending two hours a day on those chats alone.

She built an FAQ page with 16 questions, grouped under Ordering, Payment, Delivery, Sizes, and Returns. She linked it in her Instagram bio and in her WhatsApp Business profile, and she added a quick reply that sent the link whenever somebody asked a question the page already answered.

Within a month, her repetitive chats dropped by about half. Customers arrived on WhatsApp already knowing the delivery fee and the payment method, and their first message was often "I want to order the red dress in size 42" instead of "How much is delivery?" She used the saved time to photograph new stock and to follow up with customers who had abandoned their carts. Her sales did not fall because she was answering fewer questions. They rose, because she was spending her time selling instead of repeating herself.

## Your Two-Week FAQ Plan

Week 1: Collect your real questions from WhatsApp, Instagram and email. Write down every question that appears more than twice. Group them under headings, and write short, honest answers with real prices and real times. Aim for 12 to 20 questions.

Week 2: Build the page on your website with an accordion layout. Test it on your phone. Add the link to your menu, footer, WhatsApp profile and Instagram bio. Then tell your customers: post on your status that the answers are now on your website, and send the link to anyone who asks a covered question.
