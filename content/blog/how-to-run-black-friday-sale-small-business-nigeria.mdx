Black Friday 2026 falls on Friday 27 November, which means your sale is happening this week. If you run a small business in Nigeria — a fashion store in Lekki, a skincare brand in Abuja, a phone accessories shop in Ikeja, or an online store selling to customers across the country — this is one of the biggest buying weekends of the year. Customers are already expecting discounts, they are already saving their money, and they are already comparing prices on Instagram, WhatsApp and Google.

The problem is that many small businesses lose money on Black Friday. They slash prices without doing the maths, they run out of stock on the first day, they cannot answer the flood of WhatsApp messages fast enough, and they spend more on adverts than they make back in sales. A good sale is not about the biggest discount. It is about a clear plan, honest prices, enough stock, fast replies and a smooth way for customers to pay and receive their orders.

This guide walks you through the whole process, step by step, in a way that works for Nigerian realities: delivery by dispatch riders and logistics companies, payment by bank transfer, Paystack and Flutterwave, customers who prefer WhatsApp to a website checkout, and the cost of data, transport and packaging that eats into every order. Follow it this week and you can run a sale that brings in real orders and real profit, not just likes and comments.

## Step 1: Decide What You Want the Sale to Achieve

Before you design a flyer or post a single price, write down one clear goal. A sale without a goal usually ends with a lot of activity and very little money in the bank.

Common goals for a Nigerian small business include:

1. **Clear old stock.** You have last season's clothes, slow-moving products or items that have been sitting in your shop for months. A discount helps you turn them into cash.
2. **Bring in new customers.** You want people who have never bought from you to try your product at a lower price, then come back at full price later.
3. **Increase total sales.** You want more orders in one week than you normally get in a month, even if the profit on each order is smaller.
4. **Grow your WhatsApp list or Instagram following.** You want more people on your broadcast list or following your page so you can sell to them again in December.

Pick one main goal and one secondary goal. For example, your main goal might be to clear old stock, and your secondary goal might be to add 200 new customers to your WhatsApp broadcast list. Every decision after this — which products to discount, how deep the discount should be, and where to advertise — should serve that goal.

Set a simple target you can measure. Write it down like this: "Sell 80 units, make ₦800,000 in sales, and add 150 new WhatsApp contacts between Friday 27 November and Sunday 29 November." A target like that keeps you focused. At the end of the weekend you will know whether the sale worked, and you will know what to change next year.

## Step 2: Choose the Right Products to Discount

Do not discount everything. When everything is on sale, nothing feels special, and your profit disappears across the whole shop. Choose a small, focused list instead.

Start by looking at your stock. Sort your products into three groups:

- **Fast sellers:** products that already sell well. You do not need a deep discount here. A small discount, such as 10 percent off, is enough to push people who were already thinking about buying.
- **Slow movers:** products that have been in stock for a long time. These are good candidates for a bigger discount, such as 20 to 30 percent off, because any sale is better than stock sitting unsold.
- **New or premium products:** products you just launched or high-value items. Keep these at full price, or offer a small gift instead of a discount. Discounting a new product too early teaches customers to wait for a sale before they buy.

A practical rule for a small business is to discount 10 to 20 products, not your entire catalogue. Pick products you have enough stock of. There is nothing worse than advertising a dress at 30 percent off and running out after five orders while 50 customers are still asking for it. Check your actual stock count before you publish prices. If you have only three pieces of a shoe in one size, say so in the post, or leave that product out of the sale.

Think about bundles too. Instead of discounting one item heavily, you can offer two items together at a better price. For example, a skincare brand can offer a cleanser and a moisturiser together for ₦25,000 instead of ₦30,000 when bought separately. A fashion store can offer a dress and a matching bag at a combined price. Bundles raise the average order value, which means each customer spends more, and they help you move two products at once.

Make a simple sale list in a notebook or spreadsheet. For each product, write the normal price, the sale price, the discount percentage, and how many units you have. This list becomes your price list for the whole weekend, and it stops you from quoting different prices to different customers in the rush.

## Step 3: Set Prices That Still Leave You Profit

This is the step where many small businesses make a costly mistake. A discount that looks attractive can still lose you money if you forget delivery, packaging, advert costs and payment charges.

Before you fix a sale price, work out your true cost for one unit. Add up:

- The cost of buying or producing the product
- Packaging: nylon, box, branded bag, thank-you card
- Delivery: what you pay the rider or logistics company, or what you absorb when you offer free delivery
- Payment charges: Paystack and Flutterwave charge a percentage on card payments, and that comes out of your money
- Advert cost: if you spend ₦20,000 on Instagram adverts to get 40 orders, that is ₦500 per order

Only after you know the true cost should you set the sale price. The sale price must be higher than the true cost. If it is not, you are paying customers to take your products.

Use round, simple prices. ₦15,000 is easier to pay and remember than ₦14,850. Customers paying by transfer will thank you for it, and you will make fewer mistakes when you are tired at the end of a long day.

Decide your delivery terms before the sale starts. Will you offer free delivery within Lagos for orders above ₦50,000? Will customers outside Lagos pay their own delivery? Will you deliver yourself, use a rider, or use a logistics company such as GIG Logistics or DHL? Write the delivery fees down and include them in your captions. Surprise delivery fees at the point of payment are one of the main reasons customers abandon their orders.

## Step 4: Prepare Your Stock, Packaging and Delivery

A Black Friday sale tests your operations. If your operations are not ready, the orders will overwhelm you. Spend a day before Friday getting ready.

Count your stock physically. Do not rely on memory. Count each product, each size and each colour. Write the numbers down. If a product is out of stock, remove it from your sale list before you advertise it.

Prepare packaging in advance. Pack the most popular products before Friday if you can, or at least have boxes, nylons, tape, branded bags and thank-you cards ready and within reach. On a busy sale day, every minute you spend looking for packaging is a minute a customer waits for a reply.

Sort out delivery now. If you use dispatch riders in Lagos, confirm their availability for Friday to Sunday and agree on rates. Riders are very busy on Black Friday weekend, and prices can rise. If you deliver to Abuja, Port Harcourt or other states, confirm the logistics company's cut-off times and delivery times so you can give customers honest dates. A customer in Abuja who pays on Friday should know whether to expect delivery on Monday or Wednesday.

Prepare a simple order form. You can use a Google Form, a WhatsApp message template, or a notebook. For every order, record the customer's name, phone number, product, size or colour, delivery address, delivery fee, total amount, and payment status. This prevents mix-ups when you have 30 orders in one day and several customers with the same first name.

Charge your phone, get a power bank ready, and make sure you have enough data. Your phone is your shop during Black Friday. If your battery dies or your data runs out in the middle of the rush, orders will go to your competitors.

## Step 5: Create Simple, Honest Sale Content

You do not need expensive graphics to sell on Black Friday. You need clear, honest content that answers the customer's questions before they ask them.

Every sale post should include:

- The product name and a clear photo or short video
- The normal price, crossed out, and the sale price in bold
- The discount, such as "20% off"
- The sale dates: "Friday 27 to Sunday 29 November"
- How to order: "Send a DM" or "Chat us on WhatsApp"
- Delivery information: "Delivery within Lagos ₦3,000, outside Lagos from ₦5,000"

Take good photos. Use natural light near a window, a clean background, and show the product from more than one angle. A short video of you holding the product, showing the size, the fabric or the packaging, often sells better than a still photo because customers trust what they can see moving. You do not need a studio. A tidy table, good light and a steady hand are enough.

Write captions in plain language. Avoid long paragraphs. Use short lines and line breaks so the caption is easy to read on a phone. Here is a simple structure you can copy:

"BLACK FRIDAY IS HERE. Our best-selling Ankara dress is now ₦18,000 instead of ₦25,000. Only 15 pieces available. Sale ends Sunday 29 November. Send us a DM or chat us on WhatsApp to order. Delivery within Lagos ₦3,000."

Use WhatsApp well. WhatsApp is where most Nigerian small business sales actually happen. Update your WhatsApp status with your sale products. Send a broadcast message to your existing customers on Thursday evening — not too early, and not more than once or twice, or people will mute you. Make sure your WhatsApp Business catalogue is updated with the sale prices so customers can browse and tap to order.

## Step 6: Make Paying Easy and Safe

In Nigeria, a customer who wants to buy can still fail to complete the order if payment is hard. Make payment as easy as possible.

Offer more than one payment option. Many customers prefer bank transfer to your GTBank, Access Bank or Opay account. Others prefer to pay with a card through a Paystack or Flutterwave link. Some prefer to pay on delivery, though that carries the risk that the customer will not be available when the rider arrives. Offer transfer and card payment at least, and state clearly which options you accept.

If you use a payment link, test it before Friday. Click it yourself, try a small payment if you can, and make sure it works on a phone. A broken link on Black Friday morning will cost you orders you will never get back.

Send payment details quickly. When a customer says "I want it," reply within minutes with the total amount, including delivery, and your account details or payment link. A slow reply gives the customer time to change their mind or buy from someone else. Prepare a saved reply with your account details so you can send it in seconds. For example: "Thank you! Your total is ₦21,000, including delivery within Lagos. Please pay to GTBank, account name [Your Business Name], account number [Your Number], and send your receipt here. Thank you!"

Confirm payment before you dispatch. Ask for a screenshot of the transfer receipt, check your bank alert, and only then mark the order as paid. This protects you from fake receipts, which are a real problem during busy sale periods. If a receipt looks suspicious, check your account balance directly before you send the product.

## Step 7: Reply Fast and Treat Every Customer Well

Speed is one of the biggest advantages a small business has on Black Friday. Big stores can be slow and impersonal. You can be fast and personal, and customers remember that.

Set up quick replies on WhatsApp Business for the questions you will hear again and again: "What is the price?", "Do you deliver to Abuja?", "Is this still available?", "How do I pay?" Write the answers once, save them, and send them with one tap. This saves you from typing the same message 50 times a day.

Be honest about stock. If a product is sold out, say so immediately and offer a similar product. Do not take payment for a product you do not have and hope you can find it later. That leads to refunds, complaints and bad reviews.

Be polite even when you are tired. A customer who asks five questions before buying is not wasting your time; they are deciding whether to trust you with their money. Answer patiently, use their name when you know it, and thank them after they pay. A small thank-you message and a neat package can turn a one-time Black Friday buyer into a regular customer.

After the sale, follow up. On Monday or Tuesday, send a short message to customers who bought during the sale: "Thank you for shopping with us during Black Friday. We hope you love your order. Let us know when it arrives." That message costs nothing, but it builds loyalty and it reminds them that you exist when they need something else in December.

## Step 8: Review the Sale and Plan for December

When the sale ends on Sunday night, do not just rest. Spend one hour reviewing what happened while the numbers are still fresh.

Write down:

- How many orders you received, and how many were paid for
- Your total sales in naira
- Your total costs, including adverts, delivery, packaging and payment charges
- Your profit
- Which products sold out first, and which did not sell at all
- Which advert or post brought the most orders
- How many new WhatsApp contacts or Instagram followers you gained
- What went wrong: late replies, stock mix-ups, delivery delays

This review is gold. It tells you what to repeat and what to fix before the December rush, when many Nigerians buy gifts, clothes and food for Christmas and the New Year. If a product sold out in hours, order more of it for December. If a product did not sell even at 30 percent off, do not order it again. If Instagram adverts brought orders but WhatsApp broadcast brought more, spend your December advert money where it worked.

## Black Friday Checklist for This Week

Use this checklist to get ready before Friday 27 November:

- [ ] Write down your sale goal and your sales target
- [ ] Choose 10 to 20 products to discount and count your stock
- [ ] Work out the true cost of each product and set a sale price that leaves profit
- [ ] Decide your sale dates: Friday 27 to Sunday 29 November
- [ ] Confirm delivery rates with your rider or logistics company
- [ ] Prepare packaging, order forms and saved replies
- [ ] Take clear photos and short videos of every sale product
- [ ] Post a countdown, then your full price list on Thursday evening
- [ ] Test your payment link and prepare your account details
- [ ] Reply fast, confirm payment before dispatch, and thank every customer
- [ ] Review your sales, costs and profit on Monday, and keep your new customers for December

Black Friday is a chance to make real money and win new customers, but only if you plan it like a business, not like a panic sale. Start with your goal, pick the right products, price them honestly, prepare your stock and delivery, and treat every customer well. Do that, and this week can be one of the best sales weeks of your year.
