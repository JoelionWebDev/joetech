Raw footage is not a finished video. It is the raw material that becomes a finished product through editing. Video editing is where pacing, emotion, and clarity are created.

Good editing is invisible. The viewer should not notice the cuts. They should be focused on the message, not the technique. Bad editing is distracting and undermines your content.

Here is a beginner's guide to video editing basics for Nigerian businesses.

## Choosing Editing Software

Free options are excellent for starting. DaVinci Resolve is the most powerful free editor. It offers professional color grading tools, multi-track editing, and audio post-production. The learning curve is steeper but the capability is unmatched at the price.

CapCut is popular for mobile editing and short-form content. It is intuitive and includes templates, effects, and music libraries. It works on both mobile and desktop.

iMovie for Mac users and Clipchamp for Windows users are simple, free editors for basic cutting and arranging.

Paid options worth considering include Adobe Premiere Pro for professional projects and collaboration, Final Cut Pro for Mac users who want optimized performance, and Filmora for beginners who want an accessible interface with built-in effects.

## The Editing Workflow

Import your footage into your editing software. Organize clips into folders or bins by scene or type. Label clips descriptively so you can find what you need.

Review all your footage and mark the best takes. This is called logging. You do not need to watch every frame during editing — mark the sections you want to use during review.

Create a rough cut by arranging your selected clips in sequence on the timeline. Do not worry about precise timing or transitions yet. Focus on telling the story in the right order.

Refine the rough cut into a fine cut. Trim clips to precise lengths. Adjust the timing of cuts. Remove anything that does not serve the story.

Add audio including your voiceover or main audio track, background music, and sound effects. Adjust volume levels so dialogue is clear and music is supportive.

Add titles, graphics, and transitions where they enhance the message. Apply color correction to ensure consistent, appealing visuals.

## Cutting Techniques

Cut on action, not between actions. Cutting while the subject is moving creates a natural flow that the brain barely registers. Cutting between static shots is more noticeable.

The rule of six suggests rotating between close-up, medium, and wide shots. This creates visual variety and maintains viewer interest.

Remove pauses and filler words from speaking footage. Tighten the pace without making the speaker sound unnatural. This is one of the most impactful editing skills.

Use J-cuts and L-cuts where audio from the next clip starts before the video transition or continues after. This creates smooth, professional transitions.

## Audio Editing

Background music sets the emotional tone. Choose music that matches your video's mood. Keep volume low enough that dialogue remains clear. Use music with a consistent energy level throughout.

Sound effects add production value. A subtle whoosh on a transition, a click on a text appear, or ambient room tone creates a polished feel.

Remove background noise using your editor's noise reduction tools. Consistent audio quality throughout a video is a hallmark of professional production.

## Color Correction

Color correction makes your footage look natural and consistent. Adjust exposure so the image is neither too dark nor too bright. Adjust white balance so whites appear white, not blue or orange. Adjust contrast for visual depth.

Color grading is a creative choice that gives your video a consistent look. Warm tones feel inviting. Cool tones feel professional or serious. High saturation feels vibrant. Desaturated feels dramatic.

## Export Settings

Export in H.264 format for web distribution. This format balances quality and file size. Use 1080p resolution for standard HD or 4K for higher quality.

Choose a bitrate between 10 and 20 Mbps for 1080p video. Higher bitrates produce better quality but larger files. Lower bitrates are sufficient for social media.
