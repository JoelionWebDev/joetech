A customer in Abuja orders a set of ceramic plates from your Lagos shop. You wrap them in newspaper, drop them in a carton, and send them by waybill. They arrive in pieces. You refund ₦45,000, lose the customer, and get a bad review. The plates were good. The packing was the problem.

Fragile goods — glassware, ceramics, mirrors, electronics, bottled drinks, cakes, perfumes, and framed art — are some of the most profitable things a Nigerian small business can sell. They are also the easiest to lose money on, because one bad delivery wipes out the profit from five good ones. The good news is that breakage is almost always preventable. It is not luck. It is packing, labelling, and choosing the right way to move the goods.

This guide shows you exactly how to pack fragile items for Nigerian roads, what materials to use and where to buy them cheaply, how to label and insure a parcel, and what to do when something still breaks.

## Why Fragile Goods Break on Nigerian Roads

Before you fix the packing, understand what your parcel is up against. A carton travelling from Lagos to Onitsha will be carried by hand at least four times, stacked under heavier cartons, squeezed into a bus boot or a truck, and driven over roads with potholes, speed bumps, and sudden stops. During the rainy season it may sit in a puddle at a park. At a busy park like Ojota or Upper Iweka, loaders work fast and do not read labels unless the label is impossible to miss.

The three main causes of breakage are impact, crushing, and vibration. Impact is a drop or a knock. Crushing is weight from above collapsing the box. Vibration is the constant shaking that loosens and cracks items over a long journey. Good packing protects against all three, and it does so with a simple principle: the item should not move inside the box, and the box should not collapse under weight.

## The Materials You Need — and What They Cost

You do not need imported packing materials. Everything below is available in Lagos markets like Balogun, Oyingbo, and Idumota, in Onitsha Main Market, and in most big markets across Nigeria. Buying in bulk from a packaging supplier is far cheaper than buying one roll at a time from a roadside shop.

- **Bubble wrap.** The single most important material for fragile goods. A roll costs roughly ₦8,000 to ₦15,000 depending on size and quality. Wrap each item individually, bubbles facing inward, with at least two layers.
- **Corrugated cartons, double-wall.** Single-wall cartons collapse under weight. Double-wall cartons cost about ₦1,000 to ₦2,500 each depending on size, and they are worth every naira for fragile goods.
- **Packing tape.** Proper wide packing tape, not cellotape. A roll costs around ₦1,500 to ₦3,000. Buy the strong brown or clear type.
- **Foam sheets and foam peanuts.** Foam sheets protect flat items like mirrors and framed pictures. Foam peanuts or crumpled paper fill empty space so items cannot shift.
- **Old newspaper and tissue paper.** The cheapest void fill in Nigeria. Crumple newspaper tightly and pack it firmly around items. It works well, but do not rely on it alone for very fragile goods — combine it with bubble wrap.
- **Stretch film (cling film).** Useful for wrapping a whole box to keep out rain, and for holding a cake box steady. A roll costs about ₦5,000 to ₦8,000.
- **"Fragile" stickers and red tape.** A roll of fragile stickers costs under ₦2,000. Red tape across the box gets more attention than a small sticker.
- **Dividers.** For glassware and bottles, cardboard dividers that create separate compartments stop items knocking into each other. You can make them from old cartons.

Keep a packing kit in your shop: a roll of bubble wrap, a stack of double-wall cartons in two sizes, tape, a marker, and stickers. When an order comes in, you pack immediately instead of running around looking for materials.

## The Golden Rules of Packing Fragile Goods

Whatever you are sending, these rules apply:

1. **Wrap each item separately.** Two glasses wrapped together will chip each other. Wrap each one, then wrap the pair together if you want extra protection.
2. **No empty space.** A box that is half empty will collapse, and items inside will slide and smash. Fill every gap firmly with crumpled paper, foam, or bubble wrap until nothing moves when you shake the box gently.
3. **Two inches of cushioning on all sides.** There should be at least 5 centimetres of padding between the item and every wall of the box — top, bottom, and all four sides. If the item touches the wall of the box, the box is too small.
4. **Heaviest items at the bottom.** Pack heavy items first, lighter items on top. Never put a heavy ceramic vase on top of wine glasses.
5. **Double-box very fragile items.** Put the wrapped item in a small box with padding, then put that box inside a bigger box with more padding. This is the professional method for mirrors, electronics, and artwork, and it is how couriers ship high-value goods worldwide.
6. **Seal like it will be dropped.** Because it will be. Tape every seam, then tape around the box in both directions. A box that bursts open on the road is a box of broken goods.
7. **Test it.** Close the box, seal it, and shake it gently. If you hear or feel movement, open it and add more padding. This thirty-second test catches most packing mistakes.

## How to Pack Specific Fragile Items

### Glassware, Plates, and Ceramics

Wrap each piece in two layers of bubble wrap and secure with tape. Stand plates vertically in the box, like records on a shelf — plates stacked flat are far more likely to crack under pressure. Put a layer of crumpled paper between each plate even when they are vertical. Fill the box so tightly that nothing shifts. Mark the box "FRAGILE — GLASS" on all four sides.

### Bottles: Perfume, Drinks, Creams, and Oils

Seal each bottle cap with tape so it cannot unscrew in transit. Wrap each bottle in bubble wrap. Stand bottles upright, never on their sides, and use dividers so they cannot knock together. If you are sending palm oil, honey, or any liquid, put each bottle inside a sealed polythene bag first — if one leaks, it will not soak the whole carton. For very valuable bottles, use a double box.

### Electronics: Phones, Laptops, TVs, and Gadgets

Use the original box if you still have it — it was designed for the product. If not, wrap the device in bubble wrap, then in foam, then place it in a double-wall carton with padding on all sides. Remove batteries where possible and pack them separately. For a TV or monitor, protect the screen with a foam sheet or a thick cardboard panel taped across the face, then bubble wrap the whole unit. Never pack an electronic device loose with heavy items. Take a photo of the device working before you pack it, and send the photo to the customer, so there is no argument later about whether it was working when you sent it.

### Cakes

Cakes are the most stressful fragile delivery in Nigeria, and the most common source of arguments between bakers and customers. Use a strong cake box that fits the cake snugly — a box that is too big lets the cake slide. Put a non-slip mat or a damp paper towel under the cake board so it grips. For tiered cakes, transport the tiers separately and assemble at the venue if possible. Keep the cake flat in the car boot or on a flat surface in the vehicle, never on a seat where it will slide. In Lagos heat, cream and buttercream cakes soften fast; deliver early in the morning or in the evening, and use a cooler box with ice packs for long journeys. Tell the customer the cake must be carried flat and kept out of direct sun.

### Mirrors, Frames, and Artwork

Tape a large X across the glass with masking tape — if the glass cracks, the tape holds the pieces together and reduces damage. Cover the face with a foam sheet or cardboard panel, then bubble wrap the whole piece. Pack it in a flat box or build a cardboard sandwich around it, and mark it clearly. Transport mirrors upright, never flat under other goods.

## Labelling: Make It Impossible to Ignore

A small "fragile" sticker on one side of a box will be missed by a loader in a hurry. Label like you mean it:

- Write FRAGILE in big capital letters with a thick red or black marker on at least two sides, preferably all four, plus the top.
- Add an arrow showing which way is up, with "THIS WAY UP" written clearly.
- Write the contents in general terms: "GLASSWARE — HANDLE WITH CARE" is better than just "FRAGILE".
- Put your phone number and the receiver's phone number on the box.
- Use red tape in a cross over the top of the box. Loaders notice red tape.

Do not write the value of the goods on the outside of the box. That invites theft. Keep the declared value on the waybill receipt and in your own records, not on the carton.

## Choosing How to Send Fragile Goods

The cheapest transport is rarely the best choice for fragile goods. Match the method to the value:

- **For low-value fragile goods under ₦20,000,** a reputable bus park with careful packing and clear labelling is usually fine.
- **For mid-value goods from ₦20,000 to ₦150,000,** use a logistics company that offers tracking and handles parcels rather than throwing them into a bus boot. GIG Logistics, Kwik, and similar firms are set up for this.
- **For high-value goods above ₦150,000,** use a tracked courier, double-box the item, declare the full value, and consider insurance. If the customer is in the same city, a dedicated dispatch rider is often safer than any inter-state option, because the parcel is carried by one person from your door to theirs.
- **For cakes and food,** same-day delivery by a rider or driver you trust is almost always better than waybill. A cake on an overnight bus is a gamble.

Wherever possible, hand the parcel directly to the driver or the logistics officer and explain briefly what is inside: "This is glass, please keep it on top." A thirty-second human conversation does more than any sticker.

## Insurance and Declaring Value

Many Nigerian transporters and couriers offer goods-in-transit cover, or limit their liability to a declared value. Always declare the true value of fragile goods on the waybill. If you declare ₦10,000 for a ₦120,000 mirror to save a small fee, the transporter will compensate you based on ₦10,000 if it breaks. That is the rule almost everywhere.

Ask these questions before you pay:

- What is the maximum compensation if this parcel is damaged?
- Is insurance available, and what does it cost?
- What proof do I need to make a claim — receipt, photos, or a written report?

Photograph the packed box from all sides before handing it over, and photograph the receipt. Send both to the customer. If a claim is needed, these photos are your evidence that the goods left your hands in good condition.

## When Goods Arrive Broken: What to Do

1. **Ask the customer to photograph everything before touching it.** The broken items, the box, the packing, and the label. Tell them not to throw anything away.
2. **Report to the transporter the same day.** Take your receipt and your photos to the park or the courier office. File a written complaint and get a reference number and the name of the person you spoke to.
3. **Be honest with the customer.** Tell them what happened and what you are doing. Offer a clear next step: a replacement, a repair, or a refund, with a date.
4. **Replace or refund promptly.** For fragile goods, a fast replacement or refund protects your reputation far more than a long argument about whose fault it was. One viral post about broken goods and a seller who refused to help can cost you many future orders.
5. **Learn from it.** Was the box too big? Was there empty space? Was the transporter wrong for the item? Fix the process before the next order.

## The Cost of Packing Well vs the Cost of Breakage

Good packing costs money, and some sellers resist it because the materials eat into their margin. Do the maths honestly. A roll of bubble wrap at ₦10,000 might protect twenty orders. That is ₦500 per order. A double-wall carton at ₦1,500 protects an order worth ₦40,000. Compare that with the cost of one broken order: the refund, the replacement, the transport fee you already paid, and the customer you lost. In almost every case, packing well is cheaper than breaking goods.

You can also recover the cost. Many sellers add a small packaging fee of ₦1,000 to ₦3,000 for fragile items, stated clearly before the customer pays. Customers accept this when they understand it protects their order. Some sellers include it in the delivery fee. Either way, be transparent about it.

## Packing for the Rainy Season and Harmattan

Nigeria's weather is part of the packing problem. From April to October, rain can soak a carton left on a wet park floor or on the roof of a bus. From November to February, harmattan dust gets into everything. Protect against both by wrapping the sealed carton in stretch film or a large polythene bag, taped shut. It costs very little and keeps rain and dust out. For goods that must stay dry — electronics, books, fabrics, and anything in a paper box — this step is not optional. If you are sending goods during the heaviest rains, ask the transporter to keep the parcel inside the vehicle rather than on the roof, and confirm this before you pay. A soaked carton collapses, and a collapsed carton is a broken-goods claim waiting to happen.

## A Simple Packing Checklist

- [ ] Each item wrapped separately in bubble wrap
- [ ] At least 5cm of padding on all sides
- [ ] No empty space — box is full and firm
- [ ] Heavy items at the bottom
- [ ] Box sealed with strong tape in both directions
- [ ] Shake test passed — nothing moves inside
- [ ] FRAGILE and THIS WAY UP marked on multiple sides
- [ ] Receiver's name and phone number on the box
- [ ] Photos and video of packed goods sent to the customer
- [ ] True value declared on the waybill receipt
- [ ] Waybill number sent to the customer immediately

Fragile delivery in Nigeria is a skill, and like any skill it improves with practice. Pack as if the road will be rough, label as if nobody will read the small print, and choose your transporter as if the goods were your own money — because they are. Do that consistently, and fragile goods stop being the part of your business you fear and become one of the most profitable parts of it.
