Scroll through Instagram or WhatsApp status in Nigeria and you will see two kinds of business graphics. The first kind stops you: clean layout, readable text, colours that match, a photo that looks sharp, a business name you can actually read. The second kind you scroll past without thinking: crowded text, clashing colours, a blurry photo, five different fonts fighting on one flyer.

The difference is rarely money. The businesses making the first kind are often using the same free tool as everyone else — Canva. They just know how to use it well. Canva is free, it works on a phone, it needs no design training, and it can produce flyers, price lists, social media posts, business cards and logos that look genuinely professional. This guide shows you how, step by step, with Nigerian examples throughout.

## Getting started: set up Canva properly

Canva works on the web at canva.com and as a free app on Android and iPhone. For business graphics, the phone app is convenient for quick posts, but a laptop or a tablet with a bigger screen makes detailed work much easier. If you only have a phone, you can still do everything in this guide — just expect to zoom in and out more often.

Create your account with your business email, not a random personal address you might lose. Use the same email you use for your business, so your designs stay tied to your business identity. Once you are in, do one thing before you design anything: set up your Brand Kit. On the free plan, the Brand Kit is limited, but you can still upload your logo and note your colours. On Canva Pro, which costs around ₦5,000 a month when billed in naira through the app stores, you get a full Brand Kit with your exact brand colours, your fonts and your logo saved in one place. Every design you make afterwards can pull from it in one tap.

If you are not ready to pay for Pro, the free plan is genuinely enough to start. It includes thousands of templates, free photos, free fonts and free design elements. You can design, download and post without spending a kobo. Upgrade later, when your business is earning steadily and the Pro features — background remover, brand kit, premium photos and the ability to resize a design in one click — will save you real time.

Set your default design size habit early. Most Nigerian businesses design mainly for WhatsApp status and Instagram, so get comfortable with the square post size (1080 by 1080 pixels) and the portrait story size (1080 by 1920 pixels). A flyer for printing needs a different size — A5 or A4 — so choose the right size at the start, because resizing a finished design is fiddly.

## Choose a template — then make it yours

Canva's greatest strength is its templates: thousands of ready-made designs for Instagram posts, flyers, menus, price lists, business cards, posters and more. Search for what you need — "Instagram post food," "price list," "sale flyer" — and you will find dozens of options.

But here is the mistake most people make: they pick a template, change the text to their own words, and post it. The result looks like a template, because it is one. Your customers have seen the same template from ten other businesses. To look professional, you must customise it.

Start by changing the colours to your brand colours. If your business uses blue and white, change the template's pink and yellow to blue and white. Change the fonts to one or two fonts you will use everywhere — your business should have a recognisable look, and using a different font on every post destroys that. Replace the template photo with your own product photo, or a photo from Canva's free library that actually looks like your product. A skincare seller in Abuja should not use a stock photo of a product she does not sell.

Then change the layout slightly: move the headline, resize an element, remove a decoration you do not need. Small changes make the design yours. After a few weeks of this, you will stop needing templates at all for simple posts — you will start from a blank design with your colours, your fonts and your logo, and build in minutes.

## The design rules that make graphics look professional

You do not need a design degree. You need six rules, applied every time.

One: use at most two fonts in a design. One bold font for headlines, one clean font for smaller text. Mixing five fonts makes a design look chaotic. Canva suggests font pairings when you pick a font — use those suggestions until you develop your own taste.

Two: use at most three colours. Your main brand colour, a second colour that goes with it, and white or black for text. A fashion brand might use deep purple, gold and white. A food business might use green, orange and white. Whatever you choose, use the same three colours on every design, and your customers will start recognising your posts before they read them.

Three: leave empty space. Beginners fill every corner of a design with text, stickers and shapes. Professionals leave breathing room. If a flyer feels crowded, remove something rather than shrinking everything to fit. White space is not wasted space — it is what makes the important parts stand out.

Four: make the text readable. Your headline should be big and bold enough to read on a small phone screen while scrolling. Your smaller text should never go below a size people can read without zooming. Never put light text on a light photo, or dark text on a dark photo, without a solid colour block or a shadow behind it. And keep your text short: a headline of five to eight words, a line of supporting text, and a call to action. A flyer is not a letter.

Five: use good photos. Blurry, dark or cluttered photos ruin even the best layout. Take product photos in daylight, near a window or outside, against a plain background. Wipe your camera lens first — a smudged lens is the most common reason phone photos look hazy. In Canva, you can brighten a photo, crop it, and use the background remover on Pro to cut a product out cleanly.

Six: keep your logo and contact details in the same place every time. Put your logo at the top or bottom corner, and your WhatsApp number or Instagram handle at the bottom, in the same spot on every design. Consistency builds recognition, and recognition builds trust.

## Designing the graphics your business actually needs

Most Nigerian small businesses need the same handful of designs, and Canva handles all of them well.

A price list is one of the most valuable designs you will ever make. Customers ask for prices on WhatsApp all day; a clean price list image saves you hours of typing. Use a simple table layout: product name on the left, price on the right, your logo at the top, your WhatsApp number at the bottom. Keep it to one page if you can. Update it when prices change, and pin it in your WhatsApp Business catalogue or your Instagram highlights so customers can find it without asking.

A sale or promo flyer needs a strong headline, the offer, the price, and a deadline. "Weekend Sale — 20% off all Ankara ready-to-wear — Friday to Sunday — Lekki and nationwide delivery." That is the whole message. Add one good photo of the product and your contact details. Resist the urge to list every product you sell on one flyer; one offer, one flyer, one clear action.

A social media post for Instagram or Facebook should be designed for the scroll: a bold headline, a striking image, and very little text. Save the details for the caption underneath. A post that says "New stock has landed" with a beautiful photo will outperform a post crammed with twenty lines of text every time.

A WhatsApp status graphic is portrait-shaped and short-lived, so it should be even simpler: one message, one image, one call to action. "Fresh bread today — order before 10am." People view statuses quickly, often with the sound off and the brightness low, so make the text large and the contrast high.

A business card in Canva takes ten minutes: your name, your business name, your services in a few words, your phone number, your WhatsApp number, your Instagram handle, and your logo. Print a batch at a local printer in Ikeja or online, and hand them out at every market, event and delivery. A digital version of the same card can be your WhatsApp profile image or a status post.

A logo in Canva is a good starting point, though not a substitute for a professional brand identity if your business grows. Use a simple icon and your business name in a clean font. Avoid clip-art style logos with many colours and tiny details that disappear when the logo is small. Keep it simple enough to read on a phone screen.

## Working with photos and product images

Good design starts with good photos, and your phone camera is enough if you use it well.

Photograph products in natural daylight, ideally in the morning or late afternoon when the light is soft. Midday sun creates harsh shadows. Place the product on a plain background — a white bedsheet, a clean table, a plain wall — so the product is the only thing the eye sees. Take several shots from different angles and pick the sharpest one.

In Canva, upload your photos and use the editing tools: crop to remove clutter, adjust brightness and contrast, and apply the same filter to all your photos so your feed has a consistent look. On Canva Pro, the background remover is worth the subscription alone for product sellers — one click removes a messy background and leaves a clean product shot you can place on any coloured background.

If you do not have a good photo of your own, Canva's free photo library has millions of images. Search precisely: "Nigerian food," "African fashion," " Lagos street," and you will find relevant images. But be honest in your choice. Do not use a photo of a luxury hotel room to advertise a small guesthouse that looks nothing like it. Customers who arrive and find something different will not come back, and they will warn their friends.

## Saving, sharing and printing your designs

When a design is finished, download it correctly. For social media and WhatsApp, download as JPG or PNG at good quality. For printing, download as PDF print quality, which keeps the text sharp. Check the file before you post: open it on your phone, zoom in, and read every word. Typos on a posted flyer are embarrassing and hard to undo once people have screenshotted it.

Share directly from Canva to Instagram or Facebook if you like, but downloading and posting manually gives you more control over the caption and the timing. For WhatsApp status, download the image and post it from your gallery so you can add a caption and a link sticker if needed.

For printing, take the PDF to a printer and ask for a test print of one copy before you print a hundred. Colours on screen and colours on paper are never identical, and a test print saves you from a batch of flyers with the wrong shade of blue. Most printers in Lagos can print from a PDF on a flash drive or sent by WhatsApp, and a batch of one hundred A5 flyers typically costs a few thousand naira.

Plan your designs in batches to save data and time. Data is a real cost in Nigeria, so avoid opening Canva ten times a day on mobile data. Instead, sit down once or twice a week — ideally on Wi-Fi at home, in your shop, or at a coworking space — and design everything you need for the coming days in one session. Canva Pro lets you schedule posts directly to Instagram and Facebook, or you can simply save the finished images in a folder on your phone and post them manually each morning. Either way, batching means you design when you have time and a good connection, instead of rushing a flyer on expensive mobile data while a customer waits. It also keeps your posting consistent, which matters more than any single design: customers trust a business that shows up every day far more than one that posts brilliantly once a month and then disappears.

## Building a consistent look your customers recognise

The businesses whose graphics stand out are not the ones with the fanciest designs. They are the ones with the most consistent designs. Same colours, same fonts, same logo placement, same style of photo, every time. After a month of consistent posts, your customers will recognise your content in their feed before they read the name on it. That recognition is branding, and it is free.

Create a simple habit: save your best designs as templates in Canva. When you make a design you love, duplicate it and reuse the layout for the next post, changing only the text and photo. This keeps your look consistent and cuts your design time from an hour to ten minutes.

Make a small weekly routine: on Sunday evening, design the week's posts in one sitting — three or four graphics for the week ahead. Save them in a folder on your phone. Then, each morning, post the one you planned. Batching the work means you are never scrambling to design a flyer at the last minute with a customer waiting.

## When to hire a designer instead

Canva can carry a small business a long way, but there is a point where a professional designer earns their fee. If you are launching a serious brand, designing packaging that will sit on a shelf next to competitors, or creating a logo that must work everywhere from a billboard to a favicon, hire a designer. A good designer brings strategy, originality and technical skill that a template cannot.

The smart approach is a mix: use Canva yourself for daily posts, price lists, status graphics and quick flyers — the high-volume, low-stakes work — and hire a designer for the few high-stakes pieces that define your brand: your logo, your packaging, your shop signage, your launch campaign. That way you save money where templates are enough and spend it where originality matters.

If you do hire a designer, give them a clear brief: your business name, your colours, your audience, examples of designs you like, and the exact sizes you need. A clear brief saves you both time and revisions.

## Your first week with Canva

Today, create your account and upload your logo. Tomorrow, pick your two fonts and your three colours and write them down. On the third day, design your price list — the single most useful graphic your business owns. On the fourth day, design one social media post from a template you have customised to your colours. On the fifth day, design a WhatsApp status graphic for the weekend. By Sunday, you will have three finished designs, a consistent look, and the confidence to make the next one faster.

Good design is not a talent you are born with. It is a set of simple rules, practised weekly, with a free tool that does the hard parts for you. Your customers judge your business by what they see before they ever taste your food, wear your clothes or use your service. Make what they see worth their attention, and Canva will help you do it — one clean, professional graphic at a time.
