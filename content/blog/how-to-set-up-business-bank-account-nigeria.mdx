Opening a business bank account is one of the first grown-up steps a Nigerian business takes. It is the moment your business stops living inside your personal account and starts to look, feel, and behave like a real company. Customers take you more seriously when they pay into an account that carries your business name. Suppliers trust you more. And when it is time to apply for a loan, a grant, or a partnership, the first thing any bank or organisation will ask for is your business bank statement.

Yet many Nigerian small business owners delay this step for months or even years. Some think it is expensive. Some think the paperwork is too much. Others have tried once, were asked for a document they did not have, and never went back. This guide removes that confusion. It walks you through exactly what you need, how to choose the right bank, what it costs, and how to avoid the common delays that frustrate first-time applicants.

Whether you sell fashion in Lekki, run a food business in Surulere, offer digital services from Abuja, or trade goods in Onitsha, the process is the same at its core. The details differ slightly from bank to bank, but the principles in this guide will help you walk into any branch prepared.

## Why a Business Bank Account Matters

The most obvious benefit is separation. When business money and personal money share one account, it becomes very hard to know whether your business is actually making a profit. You might feel busy and successful, yet have nothing to show for it at the end of the month because personal spending quietly ate the business income.

A separate business account gives you clarity. Every payment in is business income. Every payment out is a business expense. At the end of the month you can look at one statement and know exactly where you stand. That clarity helps you price properly, plan for rent and salaries, and set money aside for tax.

There is also a trust benefit. Imagine a customer in Abuja who wants to pay ₦250,000 for your service. If you send them a personal account number with a different name, they may hesitate. If you send them an account in your registered business name, the payment feels safer and more professional. The same applies to corporate clients. Many companies in Lagos will not pay a vendor into a personal account at all. Their finance department requires a business account, an invoice, and proper documentation before they release payment.

A business account also builds a financial history for your business. Banks look at your account activity when you apply for an overdraft, a loan, or a point-of-sale terminal. A steady record of money coming in and going out, over six to twelve months, is one of the strongest signals you can give a bank. Without it, you are starting from zero every time you ask for help.

## Business Name, Limited Company, or Sole Trader: Know Your Structure

Before you approach a bank, you need to know what type of business you have, because the documents required depend on it.

A business name registration is the most common starting point for Nigerian small businesses. You register a name like "Adaeze Styles" or "Lagos Fresh Foods" with the Corporate Affairs Commission. It is quick and affordable, and it is enough to open a business account at most banks. The business is tied to you as the owner, which means you and the business are closely linked in law, but for everyday banking it works well.

A limited liability company, often written as Ltd or Limited, is a separate legal entity. Examples include "Joetech Ventures Limited" or "Greenleaf Foods Limited." Opening an account for a limited company requires more documents, including a board resolution and the particulars of directors, but it gives you stronger legal separation and is often required when you want to work with larger corporate clients or raise investment.

A sole trader who has not registered a business name can sometimes open a basic business account with some banks, but this is becoming less common. Most banks now ask for at least a CAC business name certificate before they open an account in a trading name. If you have not registered yet, that is your first step, and it is worth doing before you visit the bank.

If you are unsure which structure fits you, start with a business name registration if you are a one-person business or a small partnership. Move to a limited company when your turnover grows, when you take on partners or investors, or when your clients start asking for it. Do not let the choice of structure delay you for months. A registered business name and a working business account are better than a perfect plan that never starts.

## Documents You Will Need

Banks in Nigeria follow Know Your Customer rules set by the Central Bank of Nigeria. This means they must verify who you are and that your business is real before they open an account. The exact list varies slightly, but you should prepare the following before you go.

First, your CAC certificate. For a business name, this is your Business Name Registration Certificate. For a limited company, you will need the Certificate of Incorporation, the Memorandum and Articles of Association, and the CAC forms showing directors and shareholding, often called CAC Form 1.1 and the status report. Bring the originals and photocopies.

Second, a valid means of identification for each signatory and director. A National Identity Card, an international passport, a driver's licence, or a voter's card is usually accepted. Make sure the ID has not expired. An expired passport is one of the most common reasons applications are delayed.

Third, proof of address. This can be a recent utility bill, a tenancy agreement, or a bank statement showing your home or business address. The address on the document should match the address you give the bank. If you recently moved, update your address first or bring a tenancy agreement for your current address.

Fourth, passport photographs of the signatories and directors. Most banks ask for two recent passport photographs each. Get them taken properly, with a plain background, before you go to the branch.

Fifth, your Bank Verification Number. Every signatory will need a BVN, and the bank will verify it. If you do not have a BVN yet, visit any bank with your ID to enrol first. It is free, and it takes only a few minutes.

Sixth, a reference. Some banks ask for two references from existing account holders, while others have dropped this requirement for basic business accounts. Ask your chosen bank in advance so you are not caught off guard. If references are needed, choose people who have held a current account with a Nigerian bank for at least six months and who can confirm they know you.

For a limited company, you will also need a board resolution authorising the opening of the account, naming the signatories, and stating the mandate, which is who can sign alone and who must sign together. You will also need the company seal in some cases, and the Tax Identification Number of the company. If you have not yet obtained a TIN, you can get one from the Federal Inland Revenue Service or the Joint Tax Board, depending on your business type. Many banks will open the account and ask you to provide the TIN within a set period, but it is better to have it ready.

For partnerships, bring the partnership agreement and the CAC registration showing all partners. Each partner will need to provide identification and a BVN.


## Step-by-Step: Opening Your Business Account

Here is the process, step by step, as it works in most Nigerian banks.

Step one, register your business with the CAC if you have not done so already. You can do this online through the CAC post-incorporation portal or with the help of an accredited agent. A business name registration is the fastest route for most small businesses. Keep your certificate safe and make copies.

Step two, gather your documents using the checklist above. Do not skip this step. The most common reason for delay is an incomplete file. Put everything in one folder, originals and photocopies together, and check it the night before your branch visit.

Step three, choose your bank and account type. Most banks offer a basic business current account and a premium or corporate current account. The basic account is usually enough when you are starting. It has lower minimum balance requirements and lower fees. You can upgrade later as your business grows.

Step four, visit the branch with your documents. Some banks now allow you to start the application online, but you will still need to visit a branch to submit originals and complete biometric verification in most cases. Go early in the day, when the branch is less crowded, and take a book or something to do while you wait. Account opening can take one to three hours in the branch, depending on how busy it is.

Step five, fill in the account opening form carefully. Write your business name exactly as it appears on your CAC certificate. A single spelling difference can cause a delay. Write your address clearly. Choose your signatories and mandate carefully. If you are the only signatory, that is simplest. If you have a partner, decide in advance whether either of you can sign alone or whether both must sign together. This decision affects how quickly you can make payments, so think about your daily operations.

Step six, deposit the minimum opening balance if required. Some banks require an initial deposit of ₦10,000, ₦20,000, or ₦50,000 to activate the account. Others have no minimum balance for basic SME accounts. Ask in advance and bring the money with you or be ready to transfer it.

Step seven, wait for verification and activation. The bank will verify your BVN, your CAC details, and your address. This can take a few hours or a few working days. Some banks issue your account number on the same day and activate the account within 24 hours. Others take up to five working days, especially for limited companies where more checks are needed. Ask the officer for a realistic timeline and a contact number so you can follow up without visiting the branch again.

Step eight, collect your debit card, cheque book, and token if applicable. Test your mobile app and internet banking login before you leave the branch area. Make a small test transfer to confirm everything works. Set up transaction alerts so you know immediately when money enters or leaves the account.

Step nine, link your payment channels. If you sell online, link your business account to your Paystack or Flutterwave account so customer payments settle into the right place. If you use WhatsApp Business, update your payment details so customers see your business account number, not your personal one. Update your invoice template, your website, and any printed materials that carry your old personal account number.

Step ten, set up your record keeping from day one. Open a simple spreadsheet or use an accounting app to record every transaction. At the end of each week, reconcile your records with your bank statement. This habit takes ten minutes a week and saves you hours of stress at tax time or when you apply for a loan.

## What It Costs: Fees and Charges to Expect

Business banking in Nigeria is not free, and you should budget for it honestly. The charges are not huge for most small businesses, but they add up if you do not watch them.

Account maintenance fees are charged monthly on current accounts. The amount depends on the bank and the account type, and it is often linked to your turnover. Ask for the exact figure before you open the account.

SMS alert charges apply each time the bank sends you a transaction alert. This is usually a small amount per message, but if you do many transactions a day, it adds up. Some banks allow you to switch to email alerts, which are often free. Ask about this option and decide what works for you.

Transfer charges apply when you send money to another bank. Transfers within the same bank are often free or very cheap, while interbank transfers attract a fee that depends on the amount. The Central Bank has set standard transfer fees, but banks may add their own charges, so check the schedule.

Card and cheque book charges are usually one-off or annual. A debit card may attract an issuance fee and an annual maintenance fee. A cheque book is charged per leaflet. If you rarely use cheques, you may not need a cheque book at all.

Stamp duty is a government charge of ₦50 on electronic transfers of ₦10,000 and above into your account. This is deducted automatically and applies to most business accounts. It is not a bank profit; it is a statutory charge.

Value Added Tax applies to some bank charges. You will see it as a small addition on your statement. It is normal, but it is another reason to review your statement regularly and understand what you are paying for.

## Common Mistakes That Delay Approval

The first mistake is a name mismatch. Your business name on the CAC certificate, your ID, and your application form must agree. If your CAC certificate says "Adaeze Styles Enterprise" and your form says "Adaeze Style Enterprise," the bank will send the file back. Copy the name exactly, including every word.

The second mistake is an expired ID. Check the expiry date on your passport, driver's licence, or National Identity Card before you go. If it expires within a few weeks, renew it first or bring a second valid ID.

The third mistake is an incomplete address. A proof of address that is more than three months old may be rejected. Bring a recent utility bill or a current tenancy agreement. If you run your business from home, use your home address and bring proof for that address.

The fourth mistake is forgetting the board resolution for a limited company. This is the most common delay for companies. The resolution must be on company letterhead, signed by the directors, and must clearly state that the account should be opened, who the signatories are, and what the mandate is. Prepare it before your branch visit, and ask your bank for a sample format if you are unsure.

The fifth mistake is choosing the wrong signatory mandate. If you set a mandate that requires two signatures for every payment, but your partner travels often, you will struggle to pay suppliers on time. Think about your real working pattern and choose a mandate that is secure but practical.

The sixth mistake is not having a Tax Identification Number ready. Some banks will still open the account, but the process is faster and smoother when you already have a TIN. Apply for one early, even before you visit the bank.

The seventh mistake is opening the account and then never using it properly. Some business owners open the account, receive one or two payments, and then go back to using their personal account because it feels easier. This defeats the purpose. Commit to using the business account for all business transactions from the first day, even if it feels less convenient at first.


## Your Next Steps

Opening a business bank account in Nigeria is not as hard as it first looks. The process rewards preparation. Register your business, gather your documents, compare banks on fees and service, and walk into the branch with everything in one folder. That preparation is what separates a one-visit success from a three-visit frustration.

Start today by checking your CAC certificate, your ID expiry dates, and your proof of address. Make a shortlist of two or three banks and call them to confirm their exact requirements and fees. Then pick a day this week to visit your chosen branch. By the end of that visit, your business will have its own financial home, and you will have taken a clear step towards growth.

Your business deserves its own account. Your customers deserve the confidence of paying a real business. And you deserve the clarity that comes from knowing exactly how your business is doing. Open the account, use it properly, and let it work for you.
