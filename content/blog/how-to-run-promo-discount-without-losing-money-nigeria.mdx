Every Nigerian seller knows the feeling. You announce a promo, your WhatsApp starts buzzing, orders pour in, and for a few days you feel like business is booming. Then the promo ends, you sit down to count your money, and you realise you worked harder than ever — and made less than a normal week.

Promos are not the problem. Badly planned promos are. A discount that is too deep, too long, or offered on the wrong product can wipe out your profit, attract buyers who will never pay full price again, and leave you with no money to restock. A well-planned promo does the opposite: it brings in new customers, clears slow stock, and turns first-time buyers into repeat customers.

This guide shows you how to run a promo or discount in Nigeria without losing money. You will learn how to work out your true costs, how to choose a discount you can afford, which types of promo work best for different businesses, and how to plan the timing, delivery, and follow-up so the promo actually grows your business.

## Why Most Promos Lose Money

The most common reason a promo loses money is simple: the seller never worked out the margin. If you buy a product for ₦10,000, sell it for ₦15,000, and give a 30 percent discount, your selling price drops to ₦10,500. After packaging, delivery, and any platform fee, you are selling at a loss. Many sellers only discover this after the promo, when the money for restocking is not there.

The second reason is the wrong product. Discounting your best seller at a deep rate rarely makes sense, because those customers would have bought it anyway. You are simply giving away profit. Discounting slow stock, an old design, or a product you want customers to try for the first time makes far more sense.

The third reason is a promo with no end date. "Promo ongoing" tells customers there is no reason to buy today. They will wait, ask for a further discount, or forget about you. A promo without a deadline also trains your customers to never pay full price. If your cakes are always on promo, customers will think ₦20,000 is the real price, not ₦25,000.

The fourth reason is hidden costs. Delivery is the biggest one in Nigeria. A seller in Lekki who offers free delivery to Abuja on a ₦8,000 order can lose ₦4,000 or more on delivery alone. Packaging, dispatch rider fees, fuel for your generator, and the time you spend packing orders all count. If you do not include them in your maths, your promo will look profitable on paper and lose money in real life.

## Know Your Real Numbers Before You Discount Anything

Before you announce any promo, sit down with a pen and paper and work out the numbers for each product you plan to discount. This takes twenty minutes, and it is the difference between a profitable promo and a painful one.

Start with your true cost. That is not just what you paid your supplier. Add packaging, delivery to you, and any fee you pay to sell. For example, suppose Funke sells handmade soaps in Surulere. Each soap costs ₦1,200 to make, packaging costs ₦200, and she pays ₦100 in platform or transfer charges per sale. Her true cost is ₦1,500. She sells each soap for ₦2,500, so her profit is ₦1,000 per soap.

Now work out the discount. A 10 percent discount brings the price to ₦2,250, leaving a profit of ₦750. A 20 percent discount brings it to ₦2,000, leaving ₦500. A 40 percent discount brings it to ₦1,500, which is exactly her cost — zero profit. Any discount above 40 percent means she pays the customer to take the soap. That simple maths shows why a 50 percent discount, which sounds exciting, is often a bad idea for a small seller.

Do the same check for a service. If Emeka, a barber in Ikeja, charges ₦5,000 for a haircut, his costs are not just the clippers. He pays for rent, fuel for the generator, blades, and his time. If his real cost per haircut is ₦2,000, a "buy one, get one free" offer means he earns ₦5,000 for two haircuts that cost him ₦4,000 to deliver. That leaves only ₦1,000 for two customers' worth of time. A smaller offer, like 20 percent off on weekdays, would protect his profit far better.

Set a floor price for every product: the lowest price you can sell at and still make a profit you are happy with. Write it down and do not go below it, no matter how much a customer begs in your DM. If a customer asks for a price below your floor, offer a smaller pack, a different product, or a later date — not a loss-making sale.

Finally, set a budget for the promo. Decide the maximum you are willing to spend on discounts, free delivery, and adverts. For example, "I will spend at most ₦50,000 on this promo, and I will stop when I have sold 40 pieces." A budget protects you when a promo goes better than expected and orders keep coming in at a price that does not work.

## Choose a Promo Type You Can Afford

Not all promos are discounts. Some offers bring in customers without cutting your price deeply, and those are often the safest for a small business.

A small percentage discount is the simplest option. Ten percent off is easy to understand, easy to calculate, and usually safe if your margin is healthy. Twenty percent off works for a short, special event like a birthday sale or a festive season offer. Anything above 30 percent needs very careful maths, and should only apply to slow stock or products with a high margin.

Buy one, get one at half price is often safer than buy one, get one free. The customer still feels they got a deal, but you still earn money on the second item. A food seller in Lekki, for example, can offer "Buy two packs of small chops, get the second at half price" and still cover the cost of both packs.

A free gift with purchase works well when you have small, low-cost items to give away. A skincare seller can add a free sample sachet to orders above ₦20,000. The gift costs little, the customer feels rewarded, and they get to try a product they may buy next time. Make sure the gift is useful, not a leftover item nobody wants.

Free delivery above a set amount is one of the best promos in Nigeria, because delivery cost stops many buyers from ordering. Instead of free delivery on everything, offer it on orders above ₦30,000 within Lagos, or above ₦50,000 nationwide. That encourages customers to add one more item to reach the threshold, which raises the value of each order.

A bundle deal groups products together at a slightly lower price than buying them separately. A hair seller can bundle a wig, a wig cap, and a small bottle of serum for ₦45,000 instead of ₦50,000 if bought separately. The customer saves ₦5,000, and you sell three items at once instead of one.

A loyalty reward brings customers back without a public discount. Offer a stamp or a simple record: buy five times, get a small discount on the sixth order. This rewards your best customers instead of strangers who only buy when there is a sale, and it does not train everyone to wait for a discount.

## Plan Your Promo Step by Step

A good promo is planned, not announced on a whim. Give yourself at least a week to prepare, and follow these steps.

First, pick one clear goal. Are you trying to clear old stock, bring in new customers, reward loyal customers, or test a new product? One goal is enough. A promo that tries to do everything usually does none of them well. If your goal is to clear old stock, discount the old designs only. If your goal is new customers, offer a small first-order discount and collect their numbers for follow-up.

Second, choose the products and the offer, and do the maths for each one. Write down the normal price, the promo price, the true cost, and the profit left. If the profit is too small or negative, change the offer: a smaller discount, a bundle, or a free gift instead. Do not skip this step because you are in a hurry.

Third, set the dates and the limits. A promo of three to seven days works well for most small businesses. It is long enough for people to see it and order, and short enough to create urgency. State the end date clearly: "Ends Sunday, 22 November" is far better than "Limited time only". Also set a quantity limit if you need one: "Only 30 pieces at this price" protects you from running out of stock and from losing money on unlimited orders.

Fourth, prepare your stock, packaging, and delivery. Count what you have before you announce the promo. If you have 20 dresses and the promo could bring 50 orders, you will disappoint 30 customers. Prepare extra packaging, confirm your dispatch rider is available, and check that your supplier can restock quickly if the promo goes well.

Fifth, announce the promo clearly. Your announcement should state the product, the normal price, the promo price, the dates, and how to order. Use one clear message on WhatsApp, Instagram, and TikTok, so customers do not get confused by different prices on different platforms. Add a photo or video of the real product, and state the delivery fee plainly.

Sixth, track orders every day. Write down how many orders you received, how much you earned, and how much you spent. If orders are coming too fast and your profit per order is thin, you can end the promo early — that is better than selling at a loss for three more days. If a product is not selling, you can change the offer or swap in a different product.

Seventh, follow up after the promo ends. Send a thank-you message to every buyer, ask how they liked the product, and tell them about your next restock or a small loyalty offer. This is where the real profit from a promo comes from: the second order, not the first.

## Timing, Stock and Delivery Matter as Much as Price

The best promo at the wrong time will fail. In Nigeria, paydays, festive seasons, and school resumption periods affect when people spend. A promo just before Christmas, Sallah, or Valentine's Day can work well for gifts, food, and fashion. A promo in the middle of January, when many people are short of money after December spending, needs a smaller discount and a lower-priced product to succeed.

Watch your stock closely during a promo. When a product sells out, update your announcement immediately and remove it from your shop or status. Taking orders for products you no longer have leads to refunds, complaints, and a damaged reputation. It is better to sell out early and announce "Sold out, restock coming Friday" than to take money you cannot fulfil.

Delivery can make or break a promo. During a promo, you will have more orders than usual, so plan for it. Confirm your dispatch rider's availability and rates before the promo starts, not after orders pile up. Tell customers the honest delivery time: "Orders within Lagos will arrive in one to two days during the promo." Customers accept a short delay if you tell them in advance. They do not accept silence and a parcel that arrives a week late.

## Common Promo Mistakes to Avoid

The first mistake is copying a big brand's discount. A large supermarket can afford to sell at a loss for a week to bring in customers. A small seller with a thin margin cannot. Your promo must fit your numbers, not someone else's.

The second mistake is discounting everything. A shop-wide 40 percent discount sounds generous, but it cuts the profit on every single product, including the ones that were selling well at full price. Discount a few chosen products instead, and keep the rest at normal price.

The third mistake is forgetting delivery costs. If you offer free delivery, set a minimum order amount and a delivery area. Free delivery within Lekki on orders above ₦30,000 is manageable. Free delivery to any state in Nigeria on a ₦5,000 order is not.

The fourth mistake is a promo with no clear end. Always state the end date and stick to it. Extending a promo again and again tells customers your deadline was not real, and they will not believe the next one either.

The fifth mistake is no follow-up. A promo that brings in new buyers is only valuable if you keep their contact details and stay in touch. Save their numbers, with their permission, and send them useful updates — new stock, a helpful tip, or a small loyalty offer. Do not spam them every day, or they will block you.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a safe discount for a small business in Nigeria?

For most small businesses, 10 to 20 percent is a safe range, if your margin is healthy. Work out your true cost first, then check the profit left after the discount. If a 20 percent discount leaves you with a profit you are happy with, it is safe. If it leaves almost nothing, use a smaller discount, a bundle, or a free gift instead.

### Should I offer free delivery during a promo?

Free delivery works well when you set a minimum order amount, such as free delivery within Lagos on orders above ₦30,000. That way, the extra sales cover the delivery cost. Free delivery on small orders usually loses money, so avoid it unless your product has a very high margin.

### How long should a promo last?

Three to seven days is a good length for most small businesses. It gives people time to see the offer and order, while keeping a clear deadline that creates urgency. A promo that lasts a whole month stops feeling special, and customers will keep postponing their order.

### How do I stop customers from only buying during promos?

Space your promos out, keep them short, and reward repeat customers in other ways: good service, early access to new stock, and a small loyalty offer. Also, make sure your normal prices are fair and clearly explained, so customers feel they get value even without a discount.

### Can a promo help me clear old stock?

Yes, that is one of the best uses of a promo. Discount the old designs, sizes, or colours that are not selling, and use the money to buy new stock. Be honest that it is a clearance offer, and do not mix old stock with new stock at the same price.

### What if my promo brings many orders but little profit?

Stop and check your numbers. If the profit per order is too small, end the promo early, raise the price for the remaining stock, or change the offer to a bundle. It is better to end a promo early with a small profit than to finish it with many orders and no money left to restock.

6. Announce the promo clearly on WhatsApp, Instagram, and TikTok, with the product, prices, dates, and delivery fee.
