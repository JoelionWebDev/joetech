Ask any successful small business owner in Lagos, Abuja or Port Harcourt how they got their best customers, and you will hear the same answer again and again: somebody told somebody. A satisfied customer mentioned a salon to her sister. A happy client recommended a caterer to his office. A neighbour told another neighbour about a reliable plumber. In Nigeria, where trust is everything and people buy from people they know, word of mouth is still the most powerful form of advertising you can get, and it costs far less than running adverts every week.

A referral programme simply takes this natural habit and gives it structure. Instead of hoping your customers will mention you, you give them a clear reason to do it, a simple way to do it, and a reward when it works. The customer who refers a friend gets something valuable. The new customer gets a warm welcome, often with a small discount or bonus. You get a new customer who already trusts you before they have even walked through your door, because somebody they trust vouched for you.

This guide walks you through building a referral programme that fits a Nigerian small business. You do not need expensive software, a marketing team or a big budget. A fashion designer in Surulere, a cleaning company in Abuja, a restaurant in Port Harcourt, a tutor in Ibadan or a beauty salon in Lekki can all run one with a notebook, a phone and a clear set of rules. By the end of this guide, you will know how to design your rewards, how to track referrals without confusion, how to promote the programme, and how to keep it running month after month.

## Why Referrals Work So Well in Nigeria

Nigerians are careful with their money, and for good reason. Many people have been disappointed by a business that promised one thing and delivered another. That is why a recommendation from a friend, a colleague or a family member carries so much weight. When your sister says a tailor in Yaba sews well and delivers on time, you believe her far more than you would believe a sponsored post on Instagram. Research across many markets consistently shows that people referred by someone they know are more likely to buy, more likely to stay loyal, and more likely to spend more over time.

## How a Referral Programme Works

At its simplest, a referral programme has four parts. First, an existing customer recommends your business to someone else. Second, that new person contacts you and mentions the customer who sent them. Third, you record the referral so you know who sent whom. Fourth, once the new customer buys, you give the reward you promised to the person who referred them, and often a welcome benefit to the new customer too.

## Step 1: Decide What You Want the Programme to Achieve

Before you choose rewards or write any message, decide on one clear goal. A programme designed to bring in many new customers will look different from one designed to bring back old customers or to sell a particular service. Write your goal down in one sentence. For example: "I want 20 new customers a month from referrals." Or: "I want my existing customers to bring their friends to try our new lunch menu." Or: "I want to fill empty slots in my salon on weekdays."

Your goal shapes every other decision. If you want volume, you need a reward that is easy to earn and easy to explain. If you want high-value customers, you might offer a bigger reward but only pay it when the new customer spends above a certain amount. If you want to promote a slow service, you can offer a double reward for referrals that lead to that service. Be specific about the numbers too. Decide how many referrals you want each month and what a new customer is worth to your business. If your average customer spends ₦15,000 and comes back three times a year, that customer is worth far more than a one-off ₦5,000 sale, and your reward should reflect that.

Also decide who can refer. Most small businesses open their programme to all existing customers, and that is usually the right choice. Some businesses also allow staff, suppliers or partners to refer people. Think about whether that fits your business. A staff member who refers a friend to your restaurant is still bringing you a real customer, and rewarding them can motivate your team. Whatever you decide, write it down so there is no argument later.

## Step 2: Choose a Reward People Actually Want

The reward is the heart of your programme. If it is too small, nobody will bother. If it is too generous, you will lose money on every referral. The right reward is one that feels valuable to the customer but still leaves you with a healthy profit. As a rough guide, your reward should cost you no more than 10 to 20 percent of what the new customer spends on their first purchase. If a new customer typically spends ₦20,000, a reward worth ₦2,000 to ₦4,000 is reasonable.

Cash is the simplest reward, and in Nigeria it is often the most attractive. A transfer of ₦2,000 or ₦5,000 to the referrer's bank account or mobile money wallet is easy to understand and easy to deliver. But cash is not the only option, and sometimes a non-cash reward works better. A discount on the referrer's next purchase costs you less than cash and brings the customer back to your shop. A free service, such as a free wash and blow-dry after three successful referrals, can feel more generous than its actual cost to you. A free product, a gift hamper, or airtime and data can also work well, especially for customers who use your business regularly.

You should also decide whether to reward the new customer. Many successful programmes reward both sides: the referrer gets something, and the new customer gets a welcome discount, such as 10 percent off their first order or a free add-on. This makes the referral easier to make, because the referrer is not just asking a favour; they are offering their friend a genuine benefit. It also gives the new customer a reason to buy now rather than later. A 10 percent discount on a first order of ₦10,000 costs you ₦1,000, which is a small price for a new customer who may stay with you for years.

## Step 3: Set Simple, Fair Rules

A referral programme without clear rules will create arguments. Someone will claim they referred a customer who actually found you on Instagram. Two people will claim the same referral. A customer will expect a reward for a referral who never bought anything. Clear rules prevent all of this, and they protect your relationship with your customers.

Start with the most important rule: the reward is paid only when the new customer actually buys. A referral that leads to a chat but no purchase does not earn a reward. This is fair, because you only pay when you have actually gained something. Make this rule very clear from the beginning, in every message about the programme.

Next, decide how a referral is claimed. The simplest method is for the new customer to mention the referrer's name when they contact you. You then write both names in your referral record. Another method is to give each customer a unique referral code, such as their name plus a number, which the new customer quotes when they order. Codes are more accurate, but they add a small step. For most small businesses in Nigeria, asking the new customer to mention the referrer's name works well enough, especially if you confirm it with a quick message to the referrer.

Finally, decide who is not eligible. A common rule is that a person cannot refer themselves, and a new customer cannot claim to have been referred by someone after they have already bought. Write these rules in simple language, keep a copy where you can find it, and share the rules with anyone who asks. Transparency builds trust, and trust is the whole point of referrals.

## Step 4: Make It Easy to Share

A referral programme fails when sharing is hard. Your customers are busy, and if recommending you takes effort, most of them will not do it, no matter how much they like your business. Your job is to remove every obstacle between a happy customer and a successful referral.

Start with a short, ready-made message your customers can forward on WhatsApp. Most referrals in Nigeria happen on WhatsApp, so prepare a message that does the selling for them. It should say what you do, where you are, why you are good, and what the new customer gets. For example: "Hi! I use a great tailor in Yaba who makes beautiful Ankara styles and always delivers on time. Mention my name when you order and you will get 10 percent off your first outfit. Here is her number." Keep it under five lines. Send this message to your regular customers and ask them to forward it to anyone who might need your service.

You should also make it easy for people to find you. A new customer who hears about you should be able to reach you in one tap. Make sure your WhatsApp number is easy to share, your Instagram page is clear about what you do, and your location is easy to describe. If you have a website, your contact page should be simple and your phone number should be visible without scrolling. Every extra step loses people.

## Step 5: Track Every Referral Carefully

Tracking is where many small referral programmes fall apart. A customer refers a friend, the friend buys, and then nobody remembers who referred whom. The referrer feels cheated, the business looks disorganised, and the programme quietly dies. A simple tracking system prevents all of this, and it does not need to be complicated.

A notebook or a spreadsheet is enough when you are starting. Create a simple table with five columns: the date, the referrer's name, the new customer's name, what the new customer bought, and whether the reward has been paid. Every time a new customer mentions a referrer, write it down immediately, before you forget. If you use a spreadsheet on your phone, you can update it anywhere, even while you are on the move. Google Sheets is free and works well for this, and you can share it with a staff member so that more than one person can update it.

Check your referral record at least once a week. Look for referrals where the new customer has bought but the reward has not been paid, and pay those rewards promptly. Speed matters here. A customer who waits three weeks for a promised reward will not refer anyone again. A customer who receives their reward within a day or two will feel appreciated and will tell even more people. Set yourself a rule: pay every referral reward within 48 hours of the new customer's purchase, and stick to it.

## Step 6: Tell People About the Programme

A referral programme that nobody knows about will bring you nothing. You need to tell your customers clearly, repeatedly and in the places where they already pay attention. Do not assume that one announcement is enough. People are busy and they forget, so remind them regularly without being annoying.

Start with your happiest customers. After a customer tells you they love your work, that is the perfect moment to mention the programme. Say something simple like, "Thank you so much. If you know anyone who needs this, please send them my way. I will give you ₦2,000 for every friend who buys, and they will get 10 percent off their first order." A personal mention at the right moment is more powerful than any poster.

Use WhatsApp to spread the word. Send a short broadcast or status update explaining the programme in plain language. Keep it to a few lines, include the reward, and include your phone number. You can repeat this once or twice a month. Put the programme on your WhatsApp Business profile too, so that anyone who checks your business details can see it. If you have a catalogue, add a simple item or message about referrals there.

Use your physical space as well. A small poster or flyer at your counter, a note on your price list, or a line on your receipt can remind customers every time they visit. If you deliver products, include a small card in the delivery bag explaining the programme. If you send invoices, add one line at the bottom. These small reminders cost almost nothing and they keep the programme visible.

## Step 7: Thank People and Keep the Programme Alive

The final step is the one most businesses forget: thanking the referrer properly. A reward is not the same as thanks. A reward is a transaction. Thanks is a relationship. When someone refers a customer to you, send them a personal message. Thank them by name, tell them the referral worked, and let them know their reward is on its way. A message like, "Thank you, Mrs Adeyemi. Your friend Tolu came in today and ordered a dress. Your ₦2,000 reward has been sent. I really appreciate you," takes thirty seconds to write and makes the customer feel valued.

Review your programme every three months. Ask yourself whether it is bringing in enough new customers, whether the rewards still make sense, and whether anything needs to change. If referrals have slowed down, it may be time to remind people, to refresh the reward, or to run a short double-reward period to create excitement. For example, you could offer double rewards for two weeks in December, when people are buying gifts and planning events, or during a quiet month when you need more customers. A programme that is reviewed and refreshed stays alive. One that is launched and forgotten quietly fades away.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

The first mistake is making the reward too complicated. If customers need a calculator to work out what they will earn, they will not bother. Keep the reward to one simple number or one simple benefit. The second mistake is delaying payment. A reward that arrives late feels like a broken promise, and a broken promise damages trust. Pay quickly, every time.

The third mistake is only rewarding the referrer and forgetting the new customer. A welcome benefit for the new customer makes the referral easier to make and gives the new customer a reason to buy immediately. The fourth mistake is having no record. If you rely on memory, you will forget referrals, and forgotten referrals become unhappy customers. Write everything down from day one, even when the programme is small.

## How to Know If Your Programme Is Working

Measure three simple numbers every month. First, count how many new customers arrived through referrals. Second, work out how much those customers spent in total. Third, work out how much you paid in rewards. If you gained 15 new customers who spent ₦300,000 between them, and you paid ₦30,000 in rewards, the programme is working well. If you gained only two customers in three months, something needs to change: the reward may be too small, people may not know about the programme, or sharing may be too difficult.

## Start Small, Then Grow

You do not need to launch a perfect programme. Start with ten of your best customers this week. Tell them about the reward, give them the WhatsApp message to forward, and write down every referral in a notebook. After a month, look at what happened. If it worked, tell more customers. If it did not, ask your customers why and adjust the reward or the message. Small, steady steps will build a referral programme that brings you customers month after month, without the cost and uncertainty of constant advertising.

Your happiest customers are already your best marketers. A referral programme simply gives them a reason to do more of what they already do naturally: tell people about a business they trust. Set it up this week, keep the rules clear, pay rewards quickly, and thank every person who sends someone your way. In a market where trust is everything, there is no better way to grow.
