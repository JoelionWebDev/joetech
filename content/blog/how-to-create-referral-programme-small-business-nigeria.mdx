Ask a small business owner why customers do not refer them, and the answer is almost always the same: "My customers are happy, but they just don't tell anyone." That is rarely true. Happy customers do tell people — about the restaurant they loved, the tailor who delivered on time, the vendor whose product worked. What they do not do is tell people on your schedule, with your reward in mind, unless you ask them properly.

The problem is usually the asking. Most owners either never ask at all, or they ask in a way that makes the customer uncomfortable: a long speech about the programme, a form to fill, a link to click. A referral ask should take ten seconds and feel like a normal part of a good conversation. The words matter more than the reward. Get the words right, and customers will do the rest.

Below are the exact scripts that work for Nigerian small businesses. Copy them, change the names and numbers to fit your business, and use them as they are. Each one says when to use it and what never to say instead.

## Script 1: The WhatsApp message after delivery

Send this within 24 hours of a customer receiving her order, after you have confirmed she is happy with it. Never send it before the delivery arrives, and never send it to a customer who has complained.

> "Hi Ada, thank you for your order. I hope the dress fits perfectly. If you know anyone who needs the same, please share my number with them. When they buy, you get ₦2,000 off your next order and they get 10% off their first one. Just ask them to mention your name. Thank you!"

Why it works: it thanks first, it asks second, and it gives the reward in one sentence. The customer does not need to read rules or click anything. She can forward the message itself to a friend, and the message does the explaining for her.

What never to say instead: "Please refer your friends and family to our business for amazing discounts and offers!!!" That sentence says nothing. No reward, no amount, no next step, and three exclamation marks do not make it more convincing.

## Script 2: Face to face, at the moment of happiness

Use this when the customer is standing in front of you, happy with the result — the hair is done, the dress is fitted, the food has been tasted, the phone is fixed. Say it out loud, in your own voice, while she is still smiling.

> "I am so glad you love it. If you have a sister or a friend who needs the same thing, send her to me. Tell her to mention your name and I will take care of both of you."

Why it works: it is personal, it is short, and it names the relationship — sister, friend — which helps the customer picture exactly who she could tell. "Send her to me" is warmer than "refer a customer", and "I will take care of both of you" is a promise, not a sales pitch.

What never to say instead: "We have a referral programme running, if you are interested I can explain the terms and conditions." That sentence turns a friendly moment into paperwork. Nobody has ever been excited by terms and conditions.

## Script 3: The WhatsApp Status post

Post this once a week, no more. It is a reminder, not a broadcast blast, and it should look like good news, not an advert.

> "Thank you to Ada for sending her sister Chiamaka to us this week. Chiamaka got 10% off her first order, and Ada has ₦2,000 off her next one. If you know someone who needs what we sell, share our number with them. Your name is your code."

Why it works: it is a real story about real people, and it proves the programme is alive. A customer reading it thinks: Ada got rewarded, Chiamaka got a discount, and all it took was a name. No form, no app, no code to remember — your name is the code.

What never to say instead: a flyer image with ten lines of small text about tiers, expiry dates and exclusions. Nobody reads it, and the people who try to read it decide the programme is too complicated and walk away.

## Script 4: The line on a receipt or invoice

Print or write one line at the bottom of every receipt, invoice or delivery note. It costs nothing and it reaches every single customer, including the ones you never speak to.

> "Know someone who needs this? Tell them to mention your name — you both get 10% off."

Why it works: the customer reads it at the exact moment she has the product in her hand and the proof that it works. She does not need to remember a conversation from last week. The invitation is sitting in her bag.

If you use a stamp instead of a printed receipt, get a small rubber stamp made with the same line and stamp the back of the receipt. A printer in Lagos will make one for a few thousand naira, and it will stamp a thousand receipts.

## Script 5: The gentle re-ask to old customers

Some of your best referrers are customers who bought from you months ago and drifted away. Send this to them once a month at most, and only to people who were happy with you. Never send it to someone who complained or asked you to stop messaging them.

> "Hi Tunde, it has been a while. We miss you at Joetech. If you know anyone starting a business who needs a website, please share our number. If they come to us, you get ₦5,000 off your next job with us. How is business going on your side?"

Why it works: it is a real message to a real person, not a blast. It asks how business is going, which shows you remember him as a person, not a lead. And it gives him something useful to share — a friend starting a business is someone he knows right now.

What never to say instead: "Dear valued customer, we have amazing offers this month, refer and earn!!!" Nobody has ever felt valued by a message that starts "Dear valued customer."

## How to answer "I don't know anyone"

Sometimes a customer will say it honestly: "I don't know anyone who needs this right now." Do not push. Give her something she can use later, and let the conversation end well.

> "No problem at all. If someone asks you where you got it, just share my number. The offer will still be there whenever you need it."

Then send her your number as a contact card, or a photo of your product she can forward. The next time someone asks her where she got her dress or her hair done, she has something to send in two seconds. Most referrals do not happen because you asked at the right moment. They happen because, weeks later, someone asked her a question and she had your number ready.

## The thank-you script

When a referral actually buys, message the referrer the same day. Speed is the whole point. A thank-you that arrives two months later feels like an afterthought. A thank-you that arrives the same day feels like a business that means what it says.

> "Thank you, Ada. Your friend Chiamaka ordered from us today, so you have ₦2,000 off your next order. We appreciate you sending her to us."

That message does three things at once. It proves you keep your promises. It tells Ada her reward is real and ready. And it reminds her, gently, that sending the next person will earn the next reward. Do not add a sales pitch to this message. Let the thank-you be the whole message.

## Adapting the scripts to your business

The words above fit a shop or an online seller, but the structure fits anything. Keep four things the same and change the rest: thank first, name the reward in one sentence, name the next step in one sentence, and keep it short enough to read in ten seconds.

A food vendor changes the reward to a free pack of small chops. A salon changes it to a free wash. A tutor changes it to a free extra lesson. A photographer changes it to a free extra edited photo. The product changes. The shape of the ask does not.

Write your own version of each script tonight, with your own numbers and your own voice. Save them in your phone's notes so you can copy and paste them without thinking. Then use one of them tomorrow, with a real customer, at a real moment of happiness. Referrals do not come from a programme document. They come from ten seconds of the right words, said at the right time, to a customer who is already glad she found you.
