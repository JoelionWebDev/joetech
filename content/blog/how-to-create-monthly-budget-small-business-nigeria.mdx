Adaeze sells hair from a small shop on Admiralty Way in Lekki Phase 1. Bone straight bundles, wigs, closures, and the odd frontal for a bride who planned ahead. Her shop is one room with a glass display, a mirror, and a generator that hums louder than the radio. For two years she ran the business the way most traders do: money came in, money went out, and at the end of the month she looked at her account and guessed whether the month had been good.

March was the month she stopped guessing. She bought a hardcover notebook for ₦2,500, sat down on the evening of 28 February, and planned March before it started. Then she checked the plan mid-month, when it still mattered, and sat down again on 31 March to let the numbers tell her what April should look like. This is that month, with the real figures.

## 28 February: The Night Before the Month Starts

Adaeze closed the shop at 7pm, counted the till, and opened her notebook to a fresh page. She wrote "MARCH" at the top and drew two columns: money coming in, money going out.

For income she did not use her best month. She used the average of December, January and February. December had been big, ₦1,120,000, because of Detty December weddings and parties. January had been thin, ₦640,000, because everybody was broke after Christmas. February came in at ₦790,000. Added together and divided by three, her honest expectation for March was ₦850,000. Not the December figure she wished for. The average.

Then the spending side. She went through her bank alerts and her notebook from February and wrote every line:

- Stock (bundles, wigs, closures): ₦420,000
- Rent share: ₦75,000 — her yearly rent is ₦900,000, so she puts aside one-twelfth every month instead of panicking when the landlord's message arrives
- Fuel and generator: ₦60,000
- Transport and delivery: ₦35,000
- Data and Instagram adverts: ₦25,000
- Her assistant Blessing's salary: ₦70,000
- Her own pay: ₦100,000 — a fixed amount, taken once, not whenever she felt like it
- Savings: ₦30,000, moved to a separate account on the 1st, before spending starts
- Small odds and ends: ₦20,000

The total came to ₦835,000 against ₦850,000 expected income. A buffer of ₦15,000. Thin, but honest. She stared at it for a while. In the old days she would have looked at ₦850,000 coming in and felt rich. Written down, the month was nearly spent before it began. That was the first lesson: a budget does not make money appear. It stops you from spending money that was never yours to spend.

She made two decisions that night. First, the ₦30,000 savings would leave the account on 1 March, not "when there is something left", because there is never anything left. Second, she would not buy stock on credit from her supplier in Trade Fair unless a customer had already paid a deposit. Credit stock had quietly eaten her January profit.

## Week One: 1–7 March, the Good Week That Lies to You

March opened well. A bride booked three wigs on the 2nd, ₦145,000 in one sale, and two regulars came in for closures. By Saturday 7 March she had taken in ₦310,000 — already more than a third of the month's target. The till felt fat. Blessing suggested they buy the new ring light they had been eyeing, ₦45,000 at the plaza. Adaeze almost said yes, but her notebook said no: the plan allowed ₦25,000 for the whole month on data and adverts, and nothing at all for a ring light. The ₦310,000 was not profit; most of it was already promised to the supplier, the landlord's monthly share, and Blessing's salary. She wrote the week's figures in the notebook on Sunday night: income ₦310,000, stock bought ₦150,000, fuel ₦14,000, transport ₦9,000. She was on track, not ahead. That distinction is the whole point of writing the plan down. A good week feels like a good month, and a good week is how traders end up broke by the 20th.

## 15 March: The Mid-Month Check That Changes the Plan

On Sunday 15 March, Adaeze did what she had never done before: she stopped in the middle of the month and compared the plan with reality. The numbers were mixed, and mixed is exactly when a check matters most.

Sales were fine. She had taken in ₦480,000 by the 15th, against a halfway target of ₦425,000. The bride's friends had come in, and one of them bought a frontal for ₦98,000. But the spending side had a hole in it. Fuel had already swallowed ₦48,000 of the ₦60,000 monthly allowance. Power supply on her street had been poor for two weeks, the generator ran most afternoons, and a litre of fuel was not getting any cheaper. At that rate she would hit ₦90,000 on fuel alone by month end — ₦30,000 over plan.

She also noticed a second problem. The price of bone straight bundles had gone up at her supplier. The same bundle she bought for ₦42,000 in February was now ₦48,000. She had already bought six at the new price without adjusting her selling price, which meant her margin on those six was thinner than she thought.

So she changed the plan, mid-month, in writing. Three moves:

- Data and adverts dropped from ₦25,000 to ₦10,000. She paused the Instagram advert for two weeks and posted with her phone instead. That freed ₦15,000 for fuel.
- She moved ₦15,000 from the "odds and ends" line to fuel as well, leaving ₦5,000 for miscellaneous spending. No more small cash gifts to the plaza boys that week.
- She raised the price of bone straight bundles from ₦65,000 to ₦72,000, and told Blessing to explain it plainly to customers: the supplier raised the price, so the shop price moved too. Two customers grumbled. Nobody walked away.

She also made a note that changed April: the slow-moving coloured wigs at the back of the display had sat there since January. She would not restock them, no matter how fine they looked on the shelf. Dead stock is money sleeping.

## Week Three: The Credit Sale and the Generator

On Wednesday 18 March, a regular customer, Mrs. Okonkwo, took a wig worth ₦85,000 and promised to transfer "before Friday". Adaeze wrote it in the notebook as credit, not income. That matters. In February she had counted two such promises as sales, and then spent against money that never arrived. This time the ₦85,000 sat in a separate column until the alert came. It came on Saturday, ₦85,000, and only then did it move into the income column. Small discipline, big difference.

On Friday 20 March the generator coughed and died. The repair man at the junction charged ₦18,000: a coil and labour. In the old days that would have come straight out of the stock money, and she would have bought less stock and wondered why sales dipped. This time it came out of the savings line — which is what savings are for. She moved ₦18,000 from the ₦30,000 she had saved on 1 March, leaving ₦12,000 in the pot. It stung, but the stock money stayed untouched. A budget does not stop generators from breaking. It stops a broken generator from breaking the business.

The last week of March is salary week in Lekki, and it behaved like salary week always does. Offices paid, girls got their hair done for the weekend, and between the 24th and the 28th she took in ₦260,000. Blessing asked again about the ring light. Adaeze said April, maybe, if the month-end review allowed it. Waiting a week for a want is not suffering. It is the difference between a business and a shopping habit.

## 31 March: Month-End Review, With the Real Numbers

On the night of 31 March, Adaeze closed the shop, made tea, and filled in the last column of her notebook. Planned against actual, side by side:

- Income: planned ₦850,000, actual ₦912,000. Better than planned, thanks to the bride's friends and salary week.
- Stock: planned ₦420,000, actual ₦438,000. Over by ₦18,000, because of the bundle price rise.
- Rent share: planned ₦75,000, actual ₦75,000.
- Fuel: planned ₦60,000, actual ₦88,000. Over by ₦28,000, even after the mid-month moves. Power supply was the villain of the month.
- Transport: planned ₦35,000, actual ₦31,000. Slightly under.
- Data and adverts: planned ₦25,000, actual ₦11,000. Under, because she paused the advert.
- Blessing's salary: ₦70,000, exactly as planned.
- Her own pay: ₦100,000, exactly as planned — and for the first time, she could see it clearly instead of taking odd amounts all month.
- Savings: planned ₦30,000, actual ₦12,000 left after the generator repair.
- Odds and ends: planned ₦20,000, actual ₦8,000.

Total spent: ₦868,000. Total in: ₦912,000. Profit for March: ₦44,000. Not a fortune. But it was the first month in two years she could prove, to the naira, what the business had made — and why.

## What the Review Changed for April

A month-end review is useless if it changes nothing. Adaeze wrote four changes into April's plan before she went to bed:

First, the fuel budget went up to ₦75,000. Pretending fuel would cost ₦60,000 when it had cost ₦88,000 was not optimism; it was lying to herself. A bigger fuel line meant a smaller stock line in April, ₦405,000 instead of ₦420,000, and that was fine, because she still had stock on the shelf.

Second, the bone straight price stayed at ₦72,000, and she added ₦5,000 to the frontal price too. The supplier's prices had moved, and her prices had to move with them. She also decided to check her supplier's price list on the 1st of every month, before she set prices, instead of finding out at the till.

Third, a new rule for credit: no customer takes goods above ₦30,000 without a deposit of at least half. Mrs. Okonkwo paid, and Adaeze likes her, but ₦85,000 of trust is too much trust for one week. The rule goes on the wall behind the till, so Blessing can point at it when someone asks.

Fourth, the ring light. April's plan had ₦20,000 set aside for it, half the price, with the other half coming in May if April went well. A want, spread over two months, instead of a hole blown in one good week.

You do not need Adaeze's shop or her figures. You need her rhythm: plan on the last two days of the month, check on the 15th while there is still time to fix things, keep credit out of your income until the money lands, and let the month-end review write next month's plan. Three months of this and your budget stops being a guess. It becomes a record of how your business actually behaves — and that record is worth more than any single good month.
