## Why Trade Fairs Still Matter in Lagos

Every year, thousands of Lagosians walk through trade fair gates looking for something to buy. Some come with a list. Some come to browse. Many come with cash in hand and a willingness to buy on the spot if a product impresses them. For a small business, a good trade fair can do in three days what months of online posting sometimes cannot: put your product in front of real people, let them touch it, try it, and pay for it there and then.

Blessing sells handmade bags from her workshop in Surulere. Online, her bags sold slowly, one or two a week, because customers could not feel the leather or check the stitching from a photo. At her first trade fair, held at a hall in Ikeja, she sold 40 bags in two days. More importantly, she collected the WhatsApp numbers of 120 visitors who did not buy on the day but ordered in the weeks that followed. The fair did not just give her sales. It gave her a customer list.

Lagos hosts many kinds of fairs: general trade fairs, food and agriculture exhibitions, fashion and beauty expos, technology shows, real estate exhibitions, and seasonal markets before Christmas, Easter, and Sallah. Some are large and expensive, held at venues like the Tafawa Balewa Square or the National Stadium in Surulere. Others are smaller community markets, church bazaars, school fairs, and estate exhibitions. Both can work, and the smaller ones are often the best place for a first-time exhibitor to learn.

## Choosing the Right Fair for Your Product

Not every fair is right for every product. A food seller at a technology exhibition will waste a weekend. A fashion seller at an agriculture fair will do the same. The first decision is fit: does this fair attract the people who buy what you sell?

Start by asking the organiser simple questions. Who attended last year, and how many people came? What kind of products sold well? Where will my booth be located? What is included in the booth fee, such as a table, chairs, electricity, and security? A serious organiser will answer these questions happily. An organiser who cannot answer them, or who pressures you to pay before giving details, is a warning sign.

Visit a fair before you exhibit at it. Walk the floor as a visitor, watch which booths are busy, and talk to exhibitors during a quiet moment. Ask them whether the fair was worth the fee and whether they will return. Exhibitors are usually honest about a fair, especially when you ask after the rush has passed. One afternoon of observation can save you a booth fee that would have been wasted.

Match the fair to your price point. A premium skincare brand will do better at a beauty expo in Lekki or Victoria Island, where visitors expect higher prices, than at a general market fair where the crowd is hunting for the cheapest deal. A seller of affordable children's clothes will do better at a family fair or a school bazaar, where parents are buying in bulk. Your product, your price, and your customer must all point to the same fair.

Consider the timing. Fairs before Christmas, Easter, and Sallah attract buyers who are shopping for the season. A fair in mid-January, when many people are short of cash after the holidays, is harder. A fair that clashes with a major football match or a public holiday may draw fewer visitors. Check the calendar, and ask the organiser about the dates of the previous edition and how it performed.

## Budgeting Honestly Before You Book

A trade fair costs more than the booth fee, and sellers who forget the other costs are the ones who say the fair "did not pay." Build a full budget before you commit, and be honest about every line.

The booth fee is the biggest cost. In Lagos, a small booth at a community fair may cost ₦20,000 to ₦50,000 for a weekend. A standard booth at a larger exhibition can cost ₦100,000 to ₦300,000, and a prime location near the entrance can cost more. Ask what the fee includes. Some fees include only the space, and you must bring your own table, chairs, and canopy. Others include a table, two chairs, and a power point. Knowing this in advance prevents a surprise expense on the morning of the fair.

Transport is the next cost. Moving stock from your shop in Yaba to a venue in Lekki, or from Ikorodu to Surulere, can cost ₦15,000 to ₦40,000 depending on the load and the vehicle. Lagos traffic means you may need to move stock the day before and pay for overnight storage or a security guard. Budget for two trips, not one, because you will almost always need to bring something you forgot.

Add the cost of your booth setup: a banner, printed price lists, packaging bags, a tablecloth, and lighting if the hall is dim. A simple roll-up banner costs around ₦15,000 to ₦25,000 in Lagos and can be reused at every fair. Packaging matters more than sellers think: a customer who buys your product in a neat, branded bag is more likely to remember you than one who leaves with a black nylon bag.

## Preparing Your Stock and Your Prices

Stock is the heart of a trade fair. Bring enough to sell, but not so much that you are left with goods you cannot move. The right amount depends on the size of the fair, the length of the event, and your price. As a starting point, bring enough stock to cover your sales target plus a third extra, and keep the rest at your shop or at home, ready to be fetched if you sell out.

Choose your best sellers, not your full catalogue. A booth with 50 products looks busy but confuses visitors. A booth with your 10 best products, clearly displayed and clearly priced, sells more. Bring your newest items too, because a fair is a good place to test whether a new product has demand. If a new Ankara design sells out on day one, you have learned something valuable for the next month.

Price your goods before the fair, and print the prices. A customer who has to ask the price of every item will ask about two items and walk away. A customer who can see the price can decide quickly, and quick decisions are what make fairs profitable. Print price tags, a price list on the table, and a larger price board for your best sellers. Make sure the prices are the same as your online prices, or slightly lower if you are offering a fair discount. Customers who later find the same item cheaper on your Instagram page will feel cheated.

Plan a fair-only offer. A discount of 10 percent, a "buy two, get one free" deal on small items, or a free gift with purchases above ₦20,000 can pull visitors to your booth. Make the offer simple and time-bound: "Fair price only, today and tomorrow." A complicated offer slows down selling, and a fair is no place for slow selling.

## Setting Up a Booth That Draws People In

Your booth is your shop for the weekend, and first impressions decide whether a visitor stops or walks past. You do not need an expensive setup. You need a clean, bright, organised booth that shows your best products at eye level.

Start with height. Products laid flat on a table are hard to see from a distance. Use boxes, crates, or small stands under your tablecloth to raise your best items. Hang clothes on a rail instead of folding them in a pile. Stand bottles and jars on different levels. A visitor walking down an aisle should be able to see what you sell from five steps away.

Put your business name, phone number, and WhatsApp number where visitors can see them without asking. A banner, a small sign on the table, and a card on every bag will do. Many fair sales happen after the fair, when a visitor who took your card orders on WhatsApp. If they cannot find your number, that sale never happens.

## Selling on the Day: How to Talk, Demonstrate, and Close

Selling at a fair is different from selling online. The customer is in front of you, holding your product, and deciding in seconds. Your job is to make that decision easy.

Greet, do not chase. A warm greeting and a short sentence about your product is enough. "Good morning, these are handmade leather bags, made in Lagos" opens a conversation. Following a visitor around the booth, repeating "Buy something, buy something," closes it. Let people look. Answer questions when they ask. Offer help when they pick up an item.

Demonstrate your product. If you sell skincare, let visitors try a small sample on the back of their hand. If you sell food, offer a small taste. If you sell clothes, hold the item up so they can see the cut and the fabric. If you sell electronics, switch the item on and show it working. A product that a customer has touched, tasted, or tried is far more likely to be bought than one they only looked at.

Know your prices and your stock by heart. When a customer asks "How much is this one?" you should answer immediately, without checking a list. When they ask "Do you have it in blue?" you should know whether you do. Hesitation makes a customer doubt you. Confidence makes them trust you.

Handle bargaining with grace. Many Lagos fair visitors will bargain, and that is normal. Decide your lowest price before the fair, and do not go below it. If a customer offers ₦8,000 for an item priced at ₦12,000, you can say, "The fair price is ₦10,000, and that is the lowest I can do today." Offer a smaller item as a free gift instead of cutting the price further. A gift costs you less than a deep discount and leaves the customer happy.

Take payments carefully. Confirm every transfer before the customer leaves with the product. Check the alert, confirm the amount and the name, and write the sale on your record sheet. For cash, count the money in front of the customer and give the correct change. Keep your cash in a waist pouch or a locked box, not loose on the table. At the end of each day, count your money, record your sales, and keep the cash safe.

## Collecting Contacts and Following Up After the Fair

The fair ends, but the selling should not. The most valuable thing you take home from a trade fair is not the cash in your bag. It is the list of people who showed interest in your product. A seller who collects 100 contacts and follows up properly will earn more in the month after the fair than a seller who only counts the cash made on the day.

Make it easy for visitors to leave their details. Keep a simple notebook or a form on a tablet at your booth, and ask visitors who did not buy to leave their name and WhatsApp number. Offer a small reason: "Leave your number and I will send you our catalogue and the fair discount, valid for one week." A visitor who gives you their number has given you permission to talk to them again, and that permission is valuable.

You can also use a QR code that opens your WhatsApp chat, printed on a small stand on your table. Visitors can scan it in seconds, without writing anything down. Test the code before the fair, and make sure it opens the right number. A code that does not work is worse than no code at all.

Follow up quickly. Send a thank-you message to every contact within 48 hours of the fair ending, while the event is still fresh in their mind. Keep the message short and personal: "Thank you for visiting our booth at the fair. Here is our catalogue, and your fair discount is valid until Friday." Do not send a long sales message to someone you just met. Send one helpful message, then one reminder before the discount ends, then stop if they do not reply.

Sort your contacts. Some visitors are ready to buy now. Some are interested but need time. Some were only browsing. Focus your follow-up on the first two groups. Send the ready buyers a direct message with the product they looked at and the price. Send the interested group your catalogue and a photo of your best sellers. The browsers can receive a general update later, when you have a new product or a new offer.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does it cost to exhibit at a trade fair in Lagos?

A small community fair can cost ₦20,000 to ₦50,000 for a booth, while a larger exhibition can cost ₦100,000 to ₦300,000. Add transport, setup, packaging, feeding, and a float for change. Build a full budget before you book, and set a sales target that covers your total cost plus your stock cost plus a profit.

### How do I choose the right trade fair?

Choose a fair that attracts the people who buy your product. Ask the organiser who attended last year, what sold well, and what the booth fee includes. Visit the fair as a visitor first, watch which booths are busy, and ask exhibitors whether it was worth the fee. Match the fair to your product, your price, and your customer.

### How much stock should I bring?

Bring enough to cover your sales target plus a third extra, and keep the rest ready to fetch if you sell out. Choose your 10 best products rather than your full catalogue, display them clearly, and keep a stock sheet so you can count what sold each day. Bring your newest items too, to test demand.

### Should I offer discounts at a trade fair?

A simple fair-only offer works well, such as 10 percent off, a buy-two-get-one-free deal on small items, or a free gift with purchases above ₦20,000. Keep the offer simple and time-bound. Decide your lowest price before the fair, and do not go below it, even when a customer bargains hard.

### How do I collect customer contacts at a fair?

Keep a notebook or a form on the table, and ask visitors who did not buy to leave their name and WhatsApp number in exchange for your catalogue and a fair discount valid for one week. You can also display a QR code that opens your WhatsApp chat. Follow up within 48 hours, while the fair is still fresh in their mind.

### What if I sell out on the first day?

Selling out is a good problem, but it still costs you sales. Keep backup stock at your shop or at home, and arrange for someone to bring it to you. If you cannot restock, use the rest of the fair to collect contacts, show samples, and take orders for delivery after the fair. Never leave your booth empty.

1. List three fairs in Lagos that attract your customers, and ask each organiser about attendance, booth fees, and what is included.
2. Visit one fair as a visitor, watch the busy booths, and talk to two exhibitors about whether it was worth the fee.
3. Build a full budget for your chosen fair, including booth fee, transport, setup, packaging, feeding, and a float for change.
4. Set a sales target based on your total cost, and work out how many units you must sell to reach it.
5. Choose your 10 best products, print your prices, and plan one simple fair-only offer.
6. Prepare your booth kit: banner, tablecloth, price list, packaging bags, power bank, and a notebook or QR code for collecting contacts.
7. Brief your helper on roles, prices, and the lowest acceptable price for each item before the fair begins.
8. After the fair, message every contact within 48 hours, track your follow-up sales for a week, and record what sold and what to change next time.
