Registering your business with the Corporate Affairs Commission, the CAC, is one of the most important steps you can take as a Nigerian entrepreneur. It turns your hustle into a legal entity, opens the door to a corporate bank account, and makes it possible to bid for contracts, apply for grants and loans, and build trust with customers who want to know they are dealing with a real business.

The good news is that you no longer need to travel to Abuja or queue at a CAC office in Lagos. The whole process now happens online through the CAC post-incorporation portal, and a business name registration can be completed in days rather than weeks if your documents are ready. This guide walks you through every step, the fees you should budget for, the documents you need, and the mistakes that delay most applications.

## Why you should register your business

Many small business owners in Nigeria operate for years without registering. They sell on WhatsApp, take payments into a personal account, and tell themselves registration is something for big companies. That approach works until it does not. The day a corporate client asks for your CAC certificate before paying an invoice, or a bank asks for it before opening a business account, an unregistered business hits a wall.

Registration gives your business a legal identity separate from you. It protects your business name so another trader in Lagos or Abuja cannot legally use it. It lets you open a corporate bank account in the business name, which immediately looks more professional to customers paying by transfer. It also makes your business eligible for government grants, SME loans from banks, and supplier credit, because lenders want to see that the business exists on paper.

Registration also builds trust. A customer about to pay ₦350,000 for a website or ₦500,000 for goods feels far more confident when your invoice carries a registered business name and a CAC registration number. Registration is one of the cheapest trust signals a small business can buy.

## Business name or limited company: which one should you choose?

The CAC registers two main types of businesses that most small business owners will consider. The first is a business name, registered under Part B of the Companies and Allied Matters Act. The second is a limited liability company, registered under Part A.

A business name is the simplest and cheapest option. It is ideal for sole traders and small partnerships: a fashion designer in Surulere, a food vendor in Ikeja, a freelance graphic designer working from home, or a small provisions store in Ajah. You register a name like "Adaeze Fashion House" and you trade under it. The business is not separate from you in law, which means you are personally responsible for its debts, but the registration is fast and affordable.

A limited liability company, usually written as "Limited" or "Ltd", is a separate legal person. It can own property, sue and be sued, and continue even if the owner leaves. Your personal assets are generally protected from the company's debts. This structure suits businesses that plan to grow, take on partners or investors, bid for corporate contracts, or operate in regulated sectors. It costs more and takes more paperwork, but it is the right choice if you are building something you want to scale.

If you are a one-person business just starting out and your main goal is a business bank account and a professional presence, start with a business name. If you already have partners, or you plan to seek investment or sign big contracts, go straight for a limited company.

## What it costs to register with the CAC

Fees change from time to time, so always confirm the current fee on the CAC portal before you pay. The figures below are the standard fees that have applied in recent years and will help you budget, but treat the portal as the final authority.

Business name registration typically costs around ₦10,000 for the name reservation and registration process, excluding the cost of any professional help you hire. Name reservation on its own is a small fee, and the registration fee makes up the rest. If you use an accredited agent or a lawyer, expect to pay a service fee on top of this, often between ₦20,000 and ₦50,000 depending on the firm.

Incorporating a limited liability company costs more. The statutory fees depend on your share capital, and for a small company with a share capital of ₦1,000,000, the CAC filing fees are generally in the region of ₦20,000 to ₦40,000, plus stamp duty. Add the cost of a lawyer or accredited agent if you use one, and the total for a basic incorporation usually lands between ₦80,000 and ₦150,000.

There are also smaller costs people forget. You will need passport photographs, a valid means of identification such as a National Identity Number slip, an international passport or a driver's licence, and possibly a utility bill or tenancy agreement for your business address. Budget a little extra for printing, scanning and data, especially if you are doing everything from your phone on MTN or Airtel data.

## Documents and details to gather before you start

Preparation is what separates a registration that finishes in a week from one that drags on for a month. Gather everything before you log in to the portal.

For a business name, you will need the proposed business name and at least one alternative in case your first choice is taken. You will need the full name, date of birth, phone number, email address and residential address of the proprietor, plus the nature of the business, meaning a short description of what you do, such as "retail of ladies' fashion items" or "web design and digital marketing services". You will need a valid means of identification and a passport photograph. If the business has partners, you will need the same details for each partner.

For a limited company, the list is longer. You will need at least two directors and a company secretary, although for a small private company the secretary requirement can be handled simply. You will need the proposed company name and alternatives, the registered office address in Nigeria, the objects of the company, which is a description of the business activities, and the share capital and how it is divided among shareholders. Each director and shareholder must provide identification, a passport photograph, an email address, a phone number and their residential address. You will also need the particulars of the directors and the memorandum and articles of association, which the portal generates from the information you enter.

Have a working email address that you check regularly, because the CAC sends approval and query notifications by email.

## Step 1: Create an account on the CAC portal

Start by visiting the CAC post-incorporation portal at post-incorporation.cac.gov.ng. Click on the option to create an account, fill in your name, email address and phone number, and choose a password. Verify your email address by clicking the link the CAC sends you, then log in.

The portal can be slow during working hours, especially on Mondays. If a page refuses to load, try again early in the morning or late in the evening. Use a laptop or desktop computer rather than a phone for the longer forms, because the company incorporation forms have many fields and are easier to complete on a bigger screen.

## Step 2: Reserve your business name

Name reservation is the first real step, and it is the same whether you are registering a business name or a company. On the portal, select the name reservation option and type your proposed name. The system will check it against existing names.

Choose a name that is distinctive. Generic names like "Best Foods Enterprises" or "Divine Grace Ventures" are very likely to be taken already, or to be so close to an existing name that the CAC rejects them. Add a distinctive word: your surname, a place name, or a unique word that sets you apart. For example, "Adaeze Couture" is more likely to be available than "Adaeze Fashion House", and "Lekki Fresh Agro" is more distinctive than "Fresh Agro Ventures".

Submit two alternative names along with your first choice so that if your preferred name is rejected, the CAC can approve one of the alternatives without you starting over. Pay the reservation fee online with your debit card or by bank transfer through the payment options on the portal. Name reservation is usually approved within 24 to 48 hours, and the reservation lasts for 60 days, so you must complete the registration within that window or reserve the name again.

## Step 3: Complete the registration application

Once your name is reserved, log back into the portal and start the registration itself. For a business name, this is the BN1 form. Fill in the proprietor's details, the business address, the nature of the business and the commencement date. Upload your identification and passport photograph. Review everything carefully, because a misspelt name or a wrong date of birth will cost you time later.

For a limited company, you will complete the incorporation forms: the particulars of directors, the statement of share capital, the registered address and the memorandum and articles of association. This is the stage where many people hire an accredited agent or a lawyer, because the forms are detailed and mistakes can lead to queries. If you are confident and careful, you can do it yourself, but read each field twice before submitting.

## Step 4: Respond to queries quickly

After submission, a CAC officer reviews your application. If everything is in order, the application is approved and your certificate is issued. If something is wrong, the application is queried, and you will receive an email explaining the problem. Common queries include a blurry identification document, a mismatch between the name on your ID and the name on the application, an incomplete business address, or a business name that conflicts with an existing one.

Respond to queries the same day you receive them. Log in to the portal, read the query carefully, correct the specific issue, and resubmit. Applications that sit unanswered for weeks can be delayed or abandoned. If you are unsure what a query means, the CAC has a helpdesk and an official contact email, and accredited agents can interpret queries for a small fee.

## Step 5: Download your certificate

When your application is approved, the CAC issues an electronic certificate and a status report for companies, or a certificate of registration for a business name. Log in to the portal and download them. Save the files in at least two places, for example on your phone and in your email, and print a hard copy for your records.

Your certificate carries your registration number, which you will quote on invoices, letterheads, your website and your bank account application. For a business name, the certificate is proof that you own the right to trade under that name. For a company, you will also receive the certified true copies of your incorporation documents, which banks and corporate clients may ask to see.

## What to do after registration

Registration is the beginning, not the end. Your first task is to apply for a Tax Identification Number, a TIN, from the Federal Inland Revenue Service. You can do this online through the FIRS website or at the nearest FIRS tax office. The TIN is free, and you will need it to open a corporate bank account and to file taxes.

Next, open a corporate bank account in the business name. Visit a bank branch with your CAC certificate, your TIN, a valid ID, a passport photograph, two references where required, and proof of your business address. Banks such as GTBank, Zenith Bank, Access Bank and First Bank all offer SME accounts, and the process usually takes a few working days once your documents are complete.

Then set up your basic business records. Open a simple accounting book or a spreadsheet to record sales and expenses, and keep receipts for everything the business buys. Decide whether you will charge and remit VAT, which applies once your turnover crosses the threshold set by the tax law, and speak to a tax consultant if you are unsure. Registering for VAT is done through the FIRS and is a separate step from getting your TIN.

Finally, update your public presence. Add your registered name and RC or BN number to your invoices, your WhatsApp Business profile, your Instagram bio and your website. Customers notice, and it signals that your business is serious and here to stay.

## How long does CAC registration take?

A business name registration with no queries is commonly completed within three to seven working days from submission, including the name reservation stage. A limited company incorporation usually takes one to three weeks, depending on how quickly queries are answered and how busy the portal is. If you need the certificate urgently, start at least a month ahead and respond to every query the same day.

## Mistakes that delay most applications

The first mistake is choosing a name that is too generic or too close to an existing business. Check your proposed name on the CAC public search before you pay for reservation, and have two strong alternatives ready.

The second mistake is uploading poor-quality documents. A photograph of an ID taken in bad light, or a cropped NIN slip, will be queried. Scan documents properly or photograph them flat on a table in daylight, and check that every word is readable before uploading.

The third mistake is inconsistent names. If your ID says "Igono Joel" and your application says "Joel Igono", expect a query. Use the exact name and spelling on your official identification everywhere in the application.

The fourth mistake is using an email address you never check, or a phone number that is not active. The CAC communicates by email, and a missed query can stall your application for weeks.

The fifth mistake is paying through unofficial channels. Only pay through the CAC portal or an accredited agent you trust. Anyone who promises a CAC certificate in 24 hours for a suspiciously low fee, or asks you to pay into a personal account, should be treated with caution.

## Your registration checklist

- Decide between a business name and a limited company
- Choose a distinctive name and two alternatives
- Gather valid ID, passport photographs and your business address
- Create an account on the CAC portal and verify your email
- Reserve your name and pay the reservation fee
- Complete the registration forms and upload clear documents
- Pay the registration fee and keep your receipt
- Answer any queries the same day
- Download and save your certificate in two places
- Apply for your TIN and open a corporate bank account

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I register a business name with only my NIN slip?** Yes. A National Identity Number slip is an accepted means of identification for CAC registration, along with an international passport, a driver's licence or a voter's card.

**Can I register a business while living outside Nigeria?** Yes, you can complete the process online, but you will need a registered address in Nigeria and a means of identification. Many Nigerians abroad use an accredited agent to handle the filing.

**What happens if my name reservation expires?** Your reserved name becomes available to others after 60 days. You will need to reserve it again and pay the reservation fee a second time, so complete your registration promptly.

**Do I need a lawyer to register a business name?** No. A business name registration is straightforward enough to do yourself. A lawyer or accredited agent is more useful for a limited company, where the forms are more detailed.

**Can I change my business name later?** Yes, but it is a separate application with its own fee and process. That is another reason to choose your name carefully the first time.

## Final word

Registering with the CAC is one of the highest-return investments a Nigerian small business can make. The cost is modest, the process is online, and the benefits, a bank account, credibility, contracts and access to finance, compound for years. Gather your documents, choose a good name, follow the steps in this guide, and answer any queries quickly. Within a few weeks, your business can go from an idea you run on WhatsApp to a registered enterprise with a certificate to prove it.
