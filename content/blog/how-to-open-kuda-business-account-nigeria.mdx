# How to Open a Kuda Business Account in Nigeria

Many Nigerian small business owners still receive payments into a personal account. It feels easy at first, but it quickly becomes a problem. Business money mixes with money for food, rent and school fees. At the end of the month, you cannot tell whether your business made a profit or a loss. Customers also take you less seriously when they pay a personal name instead of a business name. A business account solves these problems, and Kuda Bank has made opening one much simpler than the old days of long queues and heavy paperwork.

Kuda is a digital bank in Nigeria, licensed by the Central Bank of Nigeria. It has no physical branches, so everything happens on your phone. That suits traders in Lagos, fashion sellers in Abuja, food vendors in Port Harcourt, and online sellers who deliver nationwide. This guide explains what a Kuda Business account is, who can open one, the documents you need, and each step of the application. It also covers fees, transfer limits, and how to use the account well once it is open.

## What Is a Kuda Business Account?

A Kuda Business account is a current account for registered businesses, sole traders, and freelancers in Nigeria. It is separate from a personal Kuda account. It lets you receive payments in your business name, pay suppliers, pay staff, and keep clear records of what comes in and goes out. Your customers see your business name on transfers, which builds trust, especially for online sales where buyers are careful about scams.

The account lives inside the Kuda app. You can check your balance, send money, download statements, and manage your business from your phone at any time. There is no need to visit a branch, fill paper forms, or wait for a cheque book. For a small business owner who is always on the move, that convenience matters. You can confirm a payment while you are at a market in Onitsha, at a client meeting in Ikeja, or at home in Enugu.

## Who Can Open a Kuda Business Account?

Kuda Business is open to several types of businesses in Nigeria. Registered businesses with a CAC certificate can apply, including limited liability companies and business names. Sole traders who run a business under their own name can also apply, and freelancers who earn money from clients can open an account for their work. If you sell on Instagram or WhatsApp, run a small shop, offer a service, or run an online store, you are likely eligible.

You must be at least 18 years old and live in Nigeria. You also need a valid means of identification and a Bank Verification Number, known as a BVN. Your BVN links your identity across Nigerian banks, and Kuda will check it during the application. If you do not have a BVN yet, visit any bank branch with your ID to enrol first. Without a BVN, you cannot open a Kuda Business account, or any other bank account in Nigeria.

Your business must be legal and based in Nigeria. Kuda may ask what your business does and how much money you expect to receive, because banks must follow rules that prevent fraud. Answer honestly and clearly.

## Documents You Will Need

Gather your documents before you start, so the application goes smoothly. First, you need a valid ID. Kuda accepts a National Identity Number slip or card, a Nigerian international passport, a driver's licence, or a Permanent Voter's Card. Make sure the ID is clear, not expired, and that the name on it matches the name on your BVN. A mismatch is one of the most common reasons applications are delayed.

Second, you need your BVN. You do not need to upload a BVN card, but you will enter the number during the application, and Kuda will verify it. Third, you need proof of your business address. This can be a utility bill, a tenancy agreement, or another official document that shows where your business operates. If you run your business from home, use your home address and a bill in your name where possible.

Fourth, registered businesses need a CAC certificate. This is the document from the Corporate Affairs Commission that proves your business is registered. If you registered a business name, you will have a certificate of registration. If you registered a limited company, you will have a certificate of incorporation and other CAC documents. Keep clear photos or scans of these documents on your phone before you start. Fifth, take a clear selfie when asked. Kuda will ask you to take a photo of your face in the app to confirm you are the person on the ID. Do this in good light, without sunglasses or a cap, and look straight at the camera.

## Step 1: Download the Kuda App and Create Your Profile

Start by downloading the Kuda app from the Google Play Store or the Apple App Store. Search for "Kuda" and check that the developer is Kuda Technologies Ltd, so you download the real app and not a fake one. Install it, open it, and tap the option to sign up. You will be asked for your email address and phone number. Use an email you check regularly and a phone number you can receive SMS codes on, because Kuda will send verification codes to both.

Next, create a secure password and set a transaction PIN. Choose a password you do not use on other apps, and choose a PIN you can remember but others cannot guess. Do not use your date of birth or "1234" as your PIN. Write your password and PIN down in a safe place if you need to, but never share them with anyone, including someone who claims to be from Kuda. Real Kuda staff will never ask for your PIN or password.

Kuda will then ask for your personal details, including your full name, date of birth, and BVN. Enter these exactly as they appear on your ID and BVN records. If your name is "Igono Joel" on your ID, do not write "Joel Igono" or a nickname. Small differences can cause a failed verification. After you submit your details, Kuda will verify your BVN. This usually takes a few minutes. If it fails, check that you typed the BVN correctly and that your name matches.

## Step 2: Choose a Business Account

Once your personal profile is set up, look for the business account option in the app. In the Kuda app, you can switch between personal and business accounts, or add a business account to your existing profile. Tap on the option to open a business account. Kuda will ask what type of business you run. Choose the option that fits you best, such as sole trader, registered business name, or limited company.

You will then be asked for your business details. Enter your business name exactly as it appears on your CAC certificate, if you have one. Enter your business address, your business phone number, and a short description of what your business does. For example, write "We sell ready-to-wear Ankara dresses and deliver nationwide" or "We provide cleaning services to homes and offices in Lagos." Keep it simple and honest. Kuda uses this information to understand your business and set suitable limits.

You will also be asked about your expected monthly transactions. This is an estimate, not a promise. Think about how much money comes into your business in a normal month. If you receive about ₦500,000 a month, say so. If you receive about ₦2,000,000, say so. Do not exaggerate, and do not understate by a large amount, because Kuda may set your limits based on this figure. You can ask for higher limits later as your business grows, with supporting documents.

## Step 3: Upload Your Documents and Verify Your Identity

The next step is to upload your documents. Take clear photos of your ID and your CAC certificate. Make sure the whole document is visible, the text is readable, and there is no glare or shadow over important details. If a photo is blurry, retake it. A clear photo saves you days of back-and-forth. Upload each document in the section the app asks for, and double-check that you uploaded the right file to the right section.

Kuda will then ask you to verify your identity with a selfie or a short video. Follow the instructions on the screen. Hold your phone at eye level, look at the camera, and turn your head slowly if asked. Do this in a well-lit room, with no one else in the frame. This step confirms that the person applying is the same person on the ID. It is a standard security check used by digital banks in Nigeria and around the world.

After you submit everything, Kuda will review your application. This can take a few hours or a few days, depending on how busy they are and whether your documents are clear. You will receive a message in the app and by email when the review is complete. If Kuda needs more information, they will tell you what is missing. Respond quickly and upload the requested document. Do not create a second account while you wait, because that can confuse the review and delay your application further.

## Step 4: Fund Your Account and Set It Up

Once your Kuda Business account is approved, you need to set it up for daily use. First, note your account number. This is the number you will give customers so they can pay you. It is a standard Nigerian account number, and customers can transfer to it from any bank, including GTBank, Zenith, Access, First Bank, Opay, Moniepoint, and other fintech apps. Test it by asking a trusted person to send you a small amount, such as ₦100, to confirm it works.

Next, set up your business profile inside the app. Add your business logo if you have one, and make sure your business name is displayed correctly. This is what customers see when they receive a payment request or an invoice from you. A clear business name and logo make you look professional. If you do not have a logo yet, your business name in clear text is enough to start.

Then, explore the settings. Set your transaction limits to suit your business, turn on notifications so you know immediately when a payment arrives, and download your first statement to check that everything is correct. If you have staff, look at the team access options. You can give a staff member permission to view transactions without giving them permission to send money. This helps you stay in control while letting your team do their work.

## Fees, Charges and Limits to Expect

Kuda is known for low fees, but no business account is completely free. Transfers within Kuda are usually free. Transfers to other banks may attract a small fee, depending on the amount and your account plan. Kuda often gives a number of free transfers each month, and charges a small fee after that. The exact fee can change, so check the current fees inside the app under "Fees" or "Pricing" before you rely on a figure. Do not assume a fee from last year is still the same today.

A physical debit card may attract an issuing and delivery fee, so if you do most of your business by transfer, a virtual card for online payments may be enough to start.

Transfer limits depend on your account level and the information you provided. New accounts may have lower daily limits, which increase as you use the account and provide more documents. If you need to send a large amount, such as ₦1,000,000 to a supplier, and your limit is lower, contact Kuda support in the app to ask about raising your limit. They may ask for a reason and for supporting documents, such as an invoice from the supplier. Plan large payments in advance, so a limit does not delay your business.

## How to Use Your Kuda Business Account Every Day

Use your business account for all business money, and keep your personal money separate. When a customer pays you, let the money enter your business account. When you buy stock, pay rent for your shop, or pay a delivery rider, pay from the business account. This separation makes it easy to see your profit at the end of the month. It also makes tax and record-keeping much simpler if you ever need to show your business income.

Use payment links and invoices to look professional and get paid faster. In the Kuda app, you can create a payment link for a specific product or amount and share it with a customer on WhatsApp. The customer taps the link and pays with their card or transfer. You can also send an invoice that lists the items, the amount, and your business details. Customers in Lagos and Abuja are used to receiving invoices from professional businesses, and it reduces arguments about price.

Check your transactions regularly. Open the app each morning and review what came in and what went out the day before. If you see a transaction you do not recognise, contact Kuda support immediately. Keep your statements by downloading them monthly and saving them in a folder on your phone or Google Drive. A monthly statement is useful for tracking growth, for applying for a loan, and for showing a potential partner that your business is real and active.

## Common Problems and How to Fix Them

The most common problem is a BVN mismatch. If the name on your BVN does not match the name on your ID, Kuda cannot verify you. To fix this, visit the bank where you enrolled for your BVN and ask them to correct your details. Bring your ID and explain the difference. This can take a few days, so do it before you apply, not after.

Another common problem is a blurry or incomplete document upload. If Kuda rejects your CAC certificate, check that the whole certificate is visible and the text is clear. Retake the photo in daylight, on a flat surface, and make sure no part is cut off. If your CAC document is old or faded, request a fresh copy from the CAC portal if possible.

Some applicants get stuck at the selfie step. If the app cannot verify your face, try again in brighter light, remove glasses or a cap, and hold the phone steady. Make sure your face fills the frame. If it still fails, try a different phone or contact Kuda support. Do not keep trying many times in a row without changing anything, because repeated failures can lock the step temporarily.

If your application is delayed for more than a few days, check your email and the app for messages from Kuda. They may have asked for an extra document that you missed. If there is no message, contact support through the in-app chat. Explain your situation clearly, give your business name and the date you applied, and ask what is still needed. Keep a record of the conversation. If you do not get a reply, try again during working hours, when support staff are more likely to respond quickly.

## Your Kuda Business Account Checklist

Use this checklist to open your Kuda Business account without stress. First, confirm you have a BVN and that the name on it matches your ID. Second, gather your ID, your CAC certificate if you have one, and proof of your business address. Third, download the Kuda app from the official store and create your profile with your correct details. Fourth, choose a business account, enter your business name and details, and upload clear photos of your documents. Fifth, complete the selfie verification in good light. Sixth, wait for approval, and respond quickly if Kuda asks for more information. Seventh, test your account with a small transfer, set up notifications, and download your first statement. Eighth, start using the account for all business money, keep personal spending separate, and pay yourself a fixed monthly amount.

Opening a Kuda Business account is a simple step that makes your business look more serious and run more smoothly. It takes a little time to gather documents and complete the application, but once it is done, you have a professional account that works from your phone, anywhere in Nigeria. Download the app today, prepare your documents, and take this step for your business. Your future self, checking a clear monthly statement and seeing real profit, will thank you for it.
