Every weekend in Nigeria, somebody is getting married, burying a loved one, celebrating a birthday, holding a naming ceremony or organising a company party. Almost none of those hosts own 200 plastic chairs, 20 tables, three canopies and enough plates for their guests. They rent them. That simple fact is why catering equipment rental remains one of the most reliable small businesses in the country: demand repeats every weekend, the same equipment can be rented out again and again, and you can start smaller than most people think.

This is not a get-rich-quick business. It is a physical, weekend-heavy business that rewards organisation, honest pricing and careful handling of stock. A chair that costs ₦4,000 to ₦6,000 can earn its cost back after 15 to 20 rentals. A canopy that costs ₦120,000 to ₦250,000 can pay for itself within a busy season if it goes out regularly. The real work is logistics — delivery, setup, counting, cleaning and collection — and building a reputation that event planners, caterers and families trust enough to call again.

This guide walks you through the whole journey: understanding the market, choosing a niche, working out realistic startup costs, buying the right equipment, setting prices that make money, finding customers, and running the day-to-day operation without losing stock or sleep.

## 1. Understand How the Business Really Works

A catering equipment rental business buys durable items used at events — chairs, tables, canopies, plates, cutlery, glasses, coolers, serving dishes and tablecloths — and rents them out for a day or a weekend. The customer pays a rental fee, usually plus a refundable caution fee and a delivery fee. You deliver, set up, and later return to collect, count and clean everything.

Your income comes from repeat rentals of the same items. Your costs are the purchase price, storage rent, transport, staff wages, cleaning, repairs and replacements. Your profit is the difference, spread across many rentals. That is why the business suits patient owners: the first few months recover your investment, and real profit starts once your most popular items have paid for themselves.

There are three common models. The first is the small home-based rental: chairs, tables and one or two canopies stored in a compound or small shop, serving your street, church and neighbourhood. The second is a mid-sized rental shop with a storefront in a busy area, a branded van and a catalogue event planners can browse. The third is a large event-rental company supplying full setups — chairs with covers, decorated tables, chafing dishes and sound systems — for weddings of 500 to 1,000 guests.

Most beginners should start with the first model and grow into the second. You learn the market, build a customer base and discover which items rent fastest before tying up millions of naira in stock that rarely moves. A common beginner mistake is buying everything at once, including items like gold cutlery sets that rent only a few times a year, then struggling to pay rent on the warehouse holding them.

Demand is seasonal and weekly. Saturdays are the peak day, followed by Sundays for church programmes and thanksgivings. December is usually the busiest month, with weddings, end-of-year parties and homecomings. The rainy season can slow outdoor events but increases demand for canopies. Plan cash flow around weekends and December, and use quiet weekdays for cleaning, repairs and marketing.

## 2. Choose Your Niche and Target Customers

"Everything for everybody" sounds good but makes buying and marketing harder. Pick a clear starting niche and expand later.

**Chairs, tables and canopies only.** The simplest entry point. Every event needs seating and shade, the items are easy to count and clean, and a reliable operator with clean chairs stands out quickly. Competition is high, but so is demand.

**Tableware and serving equipment.** Plates, glasses, cutlery, trays, chafing dishes, coolers and water dispensers. This serves caterers and hosts who already have chairs but need quality serving items. Margins can be good, but breakage and loss are higher, so your counting and caution-fee system must be strict.

**Full event setup.** Chairs, tables, canopies, tablecloths, chair covers and centrepieces. This earns more per event — a full setup for 200 guests can bring in ₦250,000 to ₦600,000 depending on quality and location — but it needs more capital, more staff and a good eye for presentation. Many operators grow into this after a year or two.

**Cooking and catering support equipment.** Large pots, gas burners, food warmers, freezers and generators for caterers. This serves professional caterers rather than families, and works well only if you already have contacts in the catering industry.

Your target customers shape everything else. Families want affordable packages and flexible payment. Event planners want reliability, clean stock and on-time delivery, and will bring repeat business if you never disappoint them. Churches and mosques book regularly and value a clear written quotation. Corporate clients want invoices and receipts, pay more, and expect more. Decide who you serve first, then buy stock and set prices for them.

Before spending money, spend two or three weekends observing your area. Visit event centres in Lekki, Ikeja, Surulere, Abuja, Port Harcourt, Ibadan or Enugu. Notice which items appear at every event and which look old or dirty. Talk to two or three event planners and ask what they struggle to find. That free research will save you from buying the wrong stock.

## 3. Write a Simple Business Plan and Budget

You do not need a 40-page document. You need a clear one- or two-page plan answering five questions: what will you rent, who will rent it, how much will you charge, how much will it cost to start, and how will customers find you?

Start with a realistic starter budget. Prices change with the market and whether you buy new or fairly used, so confirm current prices in your local market — Onitsha, Oyingbo, Idumota, Alaba International or your nearest major market — before you buy.

A modest starter package might look like this. Plastic chairs: 200 chairs at ₦4,000 to ₦6,500 each, or ₦800,000 to ₦1,300,000 in total. Folding tables: 20 tables at ₦25,000 to ₦45,000 each, or ₦500,000 to ₦900,000. Canopies: two or three at ₦120,000 to ₦250,000 each, or ₦240,000 to ₦750,000. Tablecloths and chair covers for 200 guests: ₦150,000 to ₦300,000. Plates, glasses and cutlery for 200 guests: ₦200,000 to ₦400,000. Coolers and serving dishes: ₦150,000 to ₦300,000. Add delivery transport — hiring a truck per trip at ₦20,000 to ₦50,000 — plus storage rent, branding and a small float for repairs.

Altogether, a lean start with chairs, tables and canopies can be done for roughly ₦2 million to ₦4 million if you buy carefully and start from home. A mid-sized setup with tableware, covers and a shop front will more realistically need ₦5 million to ₦10 million. A full event-rental company can need ₦15 million or more, before the cost of a vehicle — a used truck or bus can cost ₦2.5 million to ₦8 million depending on condition.

If your budget is smaller, start smaller. Fifty chairs, five tables and one canopy can serve small birthdays, naming ceremonies and church meetings while you save profits to buy more. Renting out a small set reliably is better than owning a large set that sits dirty and unbooked.

Plan money in three buckets: stock, setup costs (registration, storage rent, branding, a phone line, marketing), and working capital — at least three months of transport, staff and cleaning costs, because bookings are slow in the first weeks. Do not spend working capital on extra chairs; you will need fuel and wages on the first busy Saturday. Also budget 5 to 10 percent of rental income for repairs and replacements, and build it into your prices from the start.

## 4. Register the Business and Set Up the Basics

Registering builds trust and opens doors. A registered business can open a business bank account, issue proper invoices, and be taken seriously by corporate clients who will not pay a personal account for a large booking. In Nigeria, register a business name with the Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC) if you are starting as a sole owner, or a limited company if you plan to grow big or bid for corporate work. The business-name route is cheaper and faster for most beginners.

Choose a simple, memorable name that says what you do — "Lekki Event Rentals" is easier to remember and search for than a clever name nobody understands. Check that the name is available with the CAC and that matching social media handles are free before you commit.

Open a separate bank account for the business, even if it is just a second savings account at first. Mixing business and personal money is the fastest way to lose track of whether the business is actually making a profit. Every rental payment goes in; every expense — fuel, repairs, staff pay — comes out. At month end, record what came in and what went out. A notebook or spreadsheet is enough at this stage.

Get the practical basics right. You need a dedicated business phone number and a WhatsApp Business account, because most bookings in Nigeria start on WhatsApp. Prepare a clear price list as a PDF you can send in seconds. You need a receipt book and a simple rental agreement stating the items, rental fee, refundable caution fee, delivery address, delivery and collection times, and what happens if items are damaged or lost. Both you and the customer should sign it. This one document prevents most arguments in the business. If you have a shop, check with the local government about permits or levies, and ask neighbouring shop owners what they pay so you are not surprised.

## 5. Buy the Right Equipment, in the Right Order

Equipment is your business. Buy durable items, buy popular items first, and buy from suppliers you can return to for spares.

**Chairs.** Plastic chairs are the workhorse of Nigerian events. Look for sturdy, stackable chairs from a known manufacturer, with thick legs and a comfortable seat. Avoid the cheapest chairs — they crack, fade in the sun and cost you customers. Metal folding chairs cost more but last longer and look better at corporate events. A good beginner mix is mostly plastic chairs plus a smaller number of banquet chairs for premium bookings. Count every chair, mark it with your business name or colour code, and keep a stock list.

**Tables.** Rectangular folding tables seating six to eight people are the most versatile. Round tables look better at weddings but take more storage and transport space. Buy tables with strong folding legs and a surface that is easy to wipe clean, and measure your storage space before you buy.

**Canopies.** Buy canopies with strong metal frames, waterproof covers and proper pegs and ropes. Inspect stitching and frame joints before paying, and buy spare covers if you can — a torn cover can be replaced while the frame keeps working.

**Tableware and linen.** Buy plates, glasses and cutlery in sets, with a few extra of each so one breakage does not leave you short. Stainless steel chafing dishes, coolers, trays and serving spoons are all useful, and they should stack well and survive rough handling. For linen, white, gold, cream, royal blue and wine are safe colours that match most Nigerian events. Arrange laundry in advance — either a washing machine at your base or a reliable laundry service — because dirty linen returned on Sunday must be clean by Friday.

Buy new where durability matters and consider fairly used items where you can inspect them properly. Always inspect before paying, count in front of the seller, and get a receipt. If you buy in bulk, negotiate — a 5 to 10 percent discount on a large order is realistic. Store equipment properly from day one: stack chairs neatly, dry canopies before folding so they do not develop mould, and keep plates in crates. A clean, organised store means you can load a truck in 30 minutes and spot damage before it reaches a customer.

## 6. Set Prices That Actually Make a Profit

Pricing is where many rental businesses quietly fail. They copy a competitor's price without knowing their own costs, then wonder why they are always busy and always broke. Your price must cover the item's cost over its life, plus delivery, cleaning, staff, breakage and a profit.

Work out how many times an item is likely to rent before it needs replacing. A plastic chair costing ₦5,000 and lasting about 20 rentals needs at least ₦250 per rental just to recover its purchase price. Add cleaning, transport, breakage and profit, and a realistic rental price is ₦300 to ₦600 per plastic chair per event in many Nigerian cities, higher in Lekki, Victoria Island, Ikoyi and central Abuja. A banquet chair might rent for ₦1,000 to ₦2,500, a table for ₦2,500 to ₦5,000, a canopy for ₦25,000 to ₦60,000 depending on size and location, plates for ₦100 to ₦250 each, and a chafing dish for ₦5,000 to ₦10,000. Confirm the going rate in your area by asking three competitors as a customer.

Always charge a refundable caution fee, typically 20 to 50 percent of the rental value, refunded after you count and inspect everything on collection. Explain it upfront, write it on the receipt, and refund it promptly when everything is in order. Charge separately for delivery: ₦5,000 to ₦15,000 within your neighbourhood, ₦20,000 to ₦50,000 or more across Lagos or Abuja, depending on distance and load size.

Create packages. A package for 100 guests — chairs, tables, one canopy and basic tableware — is easier to understand than a long per-item list, and helps you sell more in one booking. Offer basic, standard and premium packages. Take a booking fee to confirm a date, commonly 50 percent upfront, with the balance paid before or on delivery. Never hold a Saturday on a promise alone — the customer who paid first is the customer you serve. Keep a booking calendar so you never promise the same 200 chairs to two weddings on the same day, and review prices every three to six months as fuel and stock costs change.

## 7. Find Customers, Deliver Well and Grow

Reputation travels faster than advertising. One event planner who trusts you can send five bookings a month; one wedding where chairs arrived late or dirty can cost ten future bookings. So the first marketing rule is operational: deliver clean equipment, on time, counted correctly, every time.

Start with people who already know you — your church, mosque, estate association and family WhatsApp groups — and share your price list. Build relationships with event planners, caterers, decorators and DJs, who recommend suppliers they trust. Visit two or three planners with a printed price list and photos of your stock, and offer a clear commission for repeat bookings. Use WhatsApp Business as your shop front, with a catalogue and quick replies, and Instagram as your portfolio, posting photos of real setups on Thursdays and Fridays when people finalise weekend plans. A Google Business Profile also helps when people search for chair rental near them. Keep a list of every customer and send a short thank-you message after each event.

On delivery day, confirm the items, address, times and fees in writing first. Count everything as you load and again as you offload, take a photo of the setup, and make sure canopies are properly pegged — a canopy that collapses in wind is dangerous. On collection, count in front of the customer, note any damage calmly, and refund the caution balance within 24 to 48 hours. Clean everything before it goes back into storage: wipe chairs, wash plates, launder linen. Keep a stock register showing what you own, what is out, and what is damaged, and update it after every job.

Grow only when demand forces it: buy more chairs when you turn bookings away, add a new item when several customers ask for it, and add services your customers already want, such as chair covers, decoration or a generator. Set aside about 10 percent of every rental for replacements, and never underprice to win every job — you will be busy, broke and exhausted. Start small, stay organised, and let your reputation do the heavy lifting. In a country where celebrations never stop, a catering equipment rental business run with care and honesty can provide steady income for years.
