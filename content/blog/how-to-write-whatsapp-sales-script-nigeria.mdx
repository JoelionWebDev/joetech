If you sell anything in Nigeria today, your shop is WhatsApp. The hair vendor in Lekki, the food seller in Surulere, the phone accessories dealer in Ikeja Computer Village — they all close their sales in the chat. Yet most of them are losing customers every single day, not because their products are bad, but because their replies are bad.

A customer sends "How much?" and gets "30k" in reply. No greeting. No question. No next step. The customer reads it, opens another vendor's chat, and buys there instead.

A WhatsApp sales script is simply a planned conversation. It tells you what to say when a customer first messages you, how to present your price, how to answer "it's too expensive", and how to ask for payment without sounding desperate.

This guide walks you through building a WhatsApp sales script for your own business, step by step, with templates you can copy and adapt.

## Why WhatsApp Is Where Nigerian Sales Happen

Before writing a single line of your script, understand the ground you are selling on. WhatsApp is the most used messaging app in Nigeria. Customers prefer it because it is fast, familiar and free of the formality of email. A buyer in Abuja can message a seller in Lagos at 11pm, see the product photos, negotiate, pay with a bank transfer or Paystack link, and arrange delivery — all without leaving the app.

That convenience cuts both ways. The same customer who messages you has probably messaged three other sellers selling the same thing. Your reply speed, your tone and your structure decide whether you get the sale or become the vendor they used to compare prices.

## The Five Parts of Every Good WhatsApp Sales Script

Every sales conversation on WhatsApp, no matter what you sell, moves through five stages. Your script should have a prepared message for each one.

**1. The greeting.** Warm, quick and human. Use the customer's name if you know it. Confirm what they are interested in.

**2. The qualifying questions.** Two or three short questions that tell you what the customer actually needs, so you recommend the right product instead of dumping your whole catalogue on them.

**3. The presentation.** Show the product, state the price clearly, and give one or two reasons it is worth it. Photos and short videos work far better than long paragraphs.

**4. The objection handling.** The customer says it is too expensive, or they want to think about it, or they will come back later. Your script should have a calm, ready answer for each.

**5. The close.** A clear, polite ask for the order: payment details, delivery address, and a deadline that creates gentle urgency.

Most Nigerian sellers only prepare stage three — the price. Then they wonder why conversations end. Your script covers all five.

## Step 1: Write Your Greeting Message

The first message sets the tone for everything. It should arrive fast — ideally within five minutes — and it should do three things: greet the customer, confirm their interest, and ask one question that keeps the conversation moving.

Here is a template you can adapt:

> "Hello, good morning! Welcome to [Your Business Name]. Thank you for reaching out. Are you looking for something specific today, or would you like me to show you our latest collection?"

Notice what this message does not do. It does not ask "How may I help you?" and stop. It gives the customer two easy options to respond to. People reply faster when they have something concrete to answer.

If the customer came from a specific source — your Instagram ad, your status, a referral — mention it:

> "Hello! Thanks for messaging us from Instagram. I saw you were interested in the Ankara gown. Would you like it in the same colour, or should I show you other colours available?"


## Step 2: Ask Qualifying Questions

Qualifying means finding out what the customer needs before you start selling. It saves you time, and it makes the customer feel listened to. Two or three questions are enough. More than that feels like an interrogation.

If you sell clothes, your questions might be:

- "What size do you wear?"
- "Is this for yourself or as a gift?"
- "Do you need it for a particular occasion or date?"

If you sell food in Lagos, your questions might be:

- "How many people are you ordering for?"
- "When do you need it delivered?"
- "Which area should we deliver to?"

If you offer a service, such as cleaning or makeup, ask about the date, the location and the scope. The answers let you recommend the right package and quote the right price the first time, instead of going back and forth for an hour.

Write your qualifying questions down and keep them in your script. When a new customer messages, you copy, paste and adjust.

## Step 3: Present Your Product and Price

This is where most sellers lose the sale, and it is usually for one of two reasons. Either they send the price alone with no context, or they send a long paragraph nobody reads.

Present your product in this order: a photo or short video, the price, and one benefit. That is it.

> "This is the 22-inch bone straight wig, available in black and brown. Price is ₦85,000. It comes with a free wig cap and free delivery within Lagos. Would you like the black or the brown?"

The photo does the selling. The price is stated plainly — no "DM for price" games, because customers in Nigeria have learned that hidden prices usually mean inflated prices. The benefit (free cap, free delivery) gives the customer a reason to choose you over the next vendor. And the question at the end keeps the conversation moving toward a decision.

A few presentation rules that matter in Nigeria:

**State the price in naira, clearly.** "85k" is fine in chat, but write ₦85,000 in your catalogue and payment messages so there is no confusion.

**Mention delivery cost upfront.** Nothing kills a sale faster than a customer agreeing to buy, then discovering delivery to Abuja costs ₦7,500 they did not budget for. State the delivery fee before you ask for payment.

**Use real photos of your actual stock.** Customers have been burned by vendors who send internet photos and deliver something different. If you can, send a short video of the exact item. Trust is your best sales tool.

**Keep messages short.** Break information into two or three short messages rather than one long block. Long paragraphs get skimmed or ignored entirely.

## Step 4: Handle Objections Without Arguing

Objections are not rejections. When a Nigerian customer says "It's too expensive," they are usually saying one of three things: they genuinely cannot afford it, they want a discount, or they are comparing your price with another vendor. Your job is to find out which one, and respond calmly.

Here are the four objections you will hear most, and scripted replies for each.

### "It's too expensive"

Never argue, and never immediately slash your price — that teaches customers that your first price was a lie. Instead, restate the value and offer a cheaper alternative:

> "I understand, and I want you to get good value. This one is ₦85,000 because it's 100% human hair that lasts over two years with proper care. If your budget is lower, we also have the synthetic blend at ₦35,000 — same style, just a different material. Would you like to see it?"

You have done three things: acknowledged the concern, justified the price, and offered an option. The customer stays in the conversation instead of leaving.

### "Let me think about it"

This usually means the customer is not convinced yet, or they want to check other sellers. Give them a reason to decide now, without pressure:

> "No problem at all, take your time. Just so you know, we have only 3 pieces of this design left in your size, and we restock every two weeks. I can hold one for you until 6pm today if you'd like. Shall I hold it?"

Scarcity works only when it is true. Never invent fake stock levels — customers talk, and a lie about stock will cost you more than one sale.

### "I will get back to you"

This is the polite Nigerian goodbye. Most customers who say this never return. Your script should give them a soft reason to stay connected:

> "Sure, no pressure at all. I'll send you our catalogue so you can browse whenever you're ready. And if you order before Friday, delivery within Lagos is free. Have a lovely day!"

Then actually send the catalogue, and follow up once in two days. Which brings us to the follow-up.

### "Can you do last price?"

Negotiation is part of buying and selling in Nigeria, and pretending otherwise will cost you sales. Decide your real lowest price in advance — before the customer asks — and build it into your script. For example, if your price is ₦50,000 and your floor is ₦45,000, you can say:

> "For you, I can do ₦45,000 — that's the best I can offer, and I'll include free delivery within Lagos. Shall I send you the payment details?"

Notice the structure: a small concession, tied to an immediate close. Never drop your price without asking for the order in the same message, or you will negotiate yourself down to nothing.

## Step 5: Close the Sale Clearly

Many sellers do everything right and then fail at the last step: they never actually ask for the order. They wait for the customer to volunteer payment, and the customer drifts away.

A close is a simple, direct question:

> "Great! To confirm your order, I'll need your delivery address and your preferred delivery day. Payment is ₦85,000 plus ₦2,000 delivery. You can pay via transfer to [account details] or with your card through our Paystack link. Which would you prefer?"

Give two payment options, not five. Transfer and card cover almost everyone. If you use Paystack or Flutterwave payment links, send the link in the same message as the account details, so the customer chooses whichever is easier.

After payment, confirm immediately:

> "Payment received, thank you so much! Your order is confirmed and will be delivered on Thursday between 10am and 2pm. I'll send you the rider's number once it's on the way."

That confirmation message is part of your script too. It prevents the "Did you receive my money?" anxiety that makes customers nervous, and it sets a clear delivery expectation.

## The Follow-Up Script: Where Most Sales Are Won

Here is a fact most Nigerian sellers miss: a large share of your sales will come from customers who did not buy on the first chat. They were busy, they were waiting for salary, or they were comparing options. A polite follow-up a day or two later brings many of them back.

Write one follow-up template and use it once per customer — never more than twice, or you become the vendor people block.

> "Hello [Name], good afternoon! This is [Your Name] from [Business Name]. Just checking in — the [product] you asked about is still available. Would you still like it, or would you like me to show you something else?"

Send follow-ups in the morning or early afternoon, not late at night. And keep a simple record of who you followed up with, so you do not message the same person three times by accident. A notebook, a spreadsheet, or the labels feature in the WhatsApp Business app all work.

The WhatsApp Business app also lets you set up quick replies — saved messages you can send with a shortcut. Put your greeting, your price list and your payment details in as quick replies, and your response time will drop from minutes to seconds.

## Ready-Made Script Templates You Can Copy

Here are three short scripts for common Nigerian businesses. Adapt the names, prices and products to your own.

### Template 1: Fashion and clothing vendor

> "Hello, welcome to [Business Name]! What are you shopping for today — a dress, a two-piece, or something for an occasion?"
>
> "This design is available in sizes 8 to 16. Price is ₦45,000. Delivery within Lagos is ₦2,000, and we deliver nationwide in 2 to 3 days."
>
> "Would you like me to reserve your size? We have only a few pieces left in size 12."

### Template 2: Food vendor

> "Hello, good afternoon! Welcome to [Business Name]. Today's menu is jollof rice, fried rice and ofada, from ₦3,500 per plate. What would you like?"
>
> "For 10 plates, your total is ₦35,000 plus ₦3,000 delivery to Lekki. Delivery takes about 90 minutes. Shall I confirm your order?"
>
> "Please send your delivery address and your phone number, and you can pay by transfer or with your card."

Notice that every template ends with a question. A script that ends with a statement ends the conversation. A script that ends with a question keeps it alive.

## Common Mistakes That Kill WhatsApp Sales

Even with a good script, a few habits will quietly destroy your conversion rate. Check yourself against this list.

**Replying late.** If you cannot reply within minutes, set up a WhatsApp Business greeting message that tells the customer when you will respond: "Hello! Thanks for messaging us. We reply within 30 minutes during business hours, 9am to 8pm." Then keep that promise.

**Sending your full price list unasked.** A customer asks about one dress and receives a 40-item catalogue. They will not read it. Send the item they asked about first. Offer the catalogue only if they ask for more options.

**Using voice notes for everything.** Some customers love voice notes, but many read messages at work or in public transport and cannot listen. Use text by default. Match the customer's format — if they send a voice note, you may reply with one.

**Forgetting the customer's name and order.** When someone returns after two weeks, greet them by name and remember what they bought. "Hello Mrs Ade, how is the wig holding up?" That one sentence turns a one-time buyer into a repeat customer.

**No business profile.** The WhatsApp Business app gives you a proper profile with your address, opening hours, website and catalogue. If you are still selling from a personal WhatsApp account, switch. It costs nothing and it instantly makes you look more trustworthy.

## How to Build Your Script This Week

You do not need a week of planning. You need one focused hour.

First, write down the five stages and draft one message for each, using your real products and real prices. Second, write replies for the four objections above in your own words. Third, save everything as quick replies in WhatsApp Business, or in a note on your phone where you can copy and paste quickly.

Then test it for one week. Track three numbers: how many people message you, how many place an order, and where conversations die. If most die after the price, your price presentation needs work. If they die after "let me think about it", your objection handling needs work. Fix one stage at a time.

Review your script every month. Prices change, products change, and the questions customers ask change too. A script is a living document, not a tablet of stone.

## A Simple Checklist Before You Send Your Next Reply

- Did I greet the customer by name?
- Did I ask at least one question about what they need?
- Did I show a real photo or video of the actual product?
- Did I state the price and the delivery fee clearly?
- Did I end my message with a question that moves toward an order?
- If they objected, did I answer calmly instead of arguing or dropping my price immediately?
- Did I ask for the order directly, with payment options included?

If you can tick all seven boxes, you have a sales script — and a real advantage over most sellers in your market.

Start with the greeting. Write it today, save it as a quick reply, and use it on your next ten customers. Then build the rest of the script one stage at a time. Within a few weeks, you will notice the difference: fewer dead conversations, more confirmed orders, and customers who come back because buying from you felt easy.
