You send dozens of emails every week. Quotations, follow-ups, introductions, replies to customers. And at the bottom of every one of them is either a proper signature that tells people who you are and how to reach you, or nothing at all — or worse, a messy block of text, a giant logo, and a quote about success.

An email signature is a small thing, but it works hard. It is the last thing the reader sees, and it is often the thing that decides whether they reply, save your contact, or visit your website. A good signature makes you look organised and easy to reach. A bad one makes you look careless. This guide shows you how to build a signature that helps you get replies.

## What a Good Email Signature Does

A signature has three jobs. First, it identifies you: your name and what you do. Second, it makes you reachable: your phone number and website, one tap away. Third, it gives the reader a next step: a link to your website, your WhatsApp, or a booking page.

That is all it needs to do. A signature is not a CV, not a flyer, and not a place for your life philosophy. The best signatures are short enough to read in three seconds.

## What to Include

Keep it to four to six lines. Here is what belongs:

**Your full name.** Use the name people should call you. If your business name is what matters, put your name first and the business name second.

**Your role and business name.** For example: "Founder, Joetech" or "Manager, Adaeze Cakes". One line is enough. The reader should understand what you do without reading a paragraph.

**Your phone number.** One number, the one you actually answer. If you prefer WhatsApp, say so: "WhatsApp: +234 800 000 0000". Most Nigerian customers will reach for WhatsApp before they call.

**Your website.** One link, and make it clickable. This is the most valuable line in your signature, because it is the easiest next step for the reader.

**One call to action, at most.** A single link to the most useful thing: your services page, your catalogue, your booking page, or a free tool. Pick one. Two or three links compete with each other and the reader clicks none of them.

**Your logo, small and optional.** A small logo adds a professional touch, but it is optional. If your logo is large, blurry, or makes the email heavy, leave it out. Your name in clean text is better than a bad image.

**Social links, if they matter.** Pick one or two, at most — the ones where you are actually active. A LinkedIn link makes sense for business. An Instagram link makes sense if you sell through Instagram. Links to accounts you never update do more harm than good.

## What to Leave Out

Just as important as what you include is what you do not include:

- **Inspirational quotes.** "Success is not final, failure is not fatal" tells the reader nothing about you, and it takes up space that your phone number could use.
- **Long legal disclaimers.** Unless your company requires one, a five-line confidentiality notice at the bottom of every email is noise. Leave it out.
- **Five phone numbers.** One number. If a customer has to choose which number to call, they may not call at all.
- **Your photo.** It rarely adds anything, and in many email apps it shows up as a broken image or a large attachment. Skip it.
- **Animated images and GIFs.** They look unprofessional in business email and often get blocked.
- **"Sent from my iPhone" or "Sent from my Samsung".** Turn that off. It tells the reader about your phone, not your business.
- **Too many colours and fonts.** One font, one or two colours, and a size close to your normal email text. A signature that looks like a rainbow is hard to read.

## A Simple Template You Can Copy

Here is a clean structure that works for almost any small business. Replace the details with your own:

**Adaeze Okafor**
Founder, Adaeze Cakes
+234 803 000 0000 (WhatsApp)
www.adaezecakes.com
Order here: www.adaezecakes.com/order

That is five lines. It says who she is, what she does, how to reach her, and what to do next. A customer reading that signature knows everything they need in three seconds.

If you have a physical location that matters, add one more line with the area, for example "Lekki Phase 1, Lagos". A full street address is only needed if customers visit you.

## How to Set It Up in Gmail

Gmail is the most common email for Nigerian small businesses, so start there:

1. Open Gmail on your computer and click the gear icon, then **See all settings**.
2. Scroll down to the **Signature** section and click **Create new**. Give it a name, for example "Work".
3. Type your signature in the box. Use the formatting buttons to bold your name and add links.
4. Under **Signature defaults**, choose your new signature for **new emails** and for **replies and forwards**.
5. Scroll to the bottom and click **Save Changes**.

To add a link, highlight the text, click the link icon, and paste the web address. To add your logo, click the image icon — but keep the image small, ideally under 200 pixels wide.

## How to Set It Up in Outlook

If you use Outlook, the steps are similar:

1. Click **Settings** (the gear icon), then **Mail**, then **Compose and reply**.
2. Under **Email signature**, type your signature.
3. Tick the boxes to include it automatically on new messages and on replies and forwards.
4. Click **Save**.

On the Outlook mobile app, the signature is set separately, under Settings and then Signature. Keep the mobile version even shorter — two or three lines is enough on a phone.

## Mistakes That Cost You Replies

**No signature at all.** The most common mistake. An email that ends with just your first name leaves the reader guessing who you are and how to reach you. Every email should carry your name, business, and one way to contact you.

**A different signature on every email.** If your Gmail signature says one thing and your phone says another, you look disorganised. Set the same signature everywhere you send business email.

**Links that do not work.** Click every link in your signature after you set it up. A link to a website that no longer exists, or a WhatsApp link with a wrong number, is worse than no link at all.

**Outdated information.** Check your signature every few months. A signature that still shows an old phone number or an old website sends customers to a dead end. Update it the day your details change, not months later.

**Too much information.** If your signature is longer than your email, something is wrong. Cut it until only the useful lines remain.

## How a Good Signature Gets Replies

A signature gets replies because it removes friction. The reader does not have to search for your number, guess your website, or wonder what you do. Everything is right there, one tap away.

It also builds trust. A consistent signature on every email tells the reader you are organised and you take your business seriously. Over weeks and months, that consistency adds up. Customers remember the business whose emails always look professional, and they forget the ones whose emails look thrown together.

One more thing: your signature is a quiet advertisement that costs nothing. Every email you send carries it. If you send fifty emails a week, that is fifty small chances for someone to visit your website or save your number. Make each one count.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Should I include my photo in my email signature?

Usually not. Photos in signatures often show up as attachments or broken images, and they add weight to every email. Your name in bold, clean text does the job better. If you really want a face to the name, your photo belongs on your website and LinkedIn, not in your signature.

### How long should an email signature be?

Four to six lines is the sweet spot. Anything longer than that starts to look like a document rather than a sign-off. If you need more space, put the extra detail on your website and link to it.

### Do images in signatures always work?

No. Many email apps block images by default, so the reader sees a blank box with a small icon instead of your logo. If you do include a logo, keep it small and make sure the signature still reads well when images are blocked. Text should carry the important information, not images.

### Should my personal email and business email have different signatures?

Yes. Your business email should carry your business signature. Your personal email can be simpler — just your name and phone number. Mixing them, for example by sending business emails from a personal address with no signature, confuses customers.

### How often should I update my signature?

Check it every three months, and update it immediately whenever your phone number, website, role, or business name changes. An outdated signature is a small thing that can quietly cost you customers.

Your signature takes ten minutes to set up and then works for you on every email you send. Then send a test email to yourself and read it on your phone. If it looks clean and everything is one tap away, it is done.
