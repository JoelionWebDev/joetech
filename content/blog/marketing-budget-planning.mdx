Your marketing budget determines what you can achieve. Too little and your efforts are invisible. Too much and you waste resources that could be used elsewhere.

A well-planned marketing budget ensures every naira is spent effectively. It aligns your spending with your business goals and provides a framework for measuring return on investment.

Here is how to create a marketing budget for your Nigerian business.

## How Much to Spend

Industry benchmarks suggest spending five to fifteen percent of revenue on marketing. New businesses and those in competitive industries spend at the higher end. Established businesses with strong brand recognition spend at the lower end.

Startup and growth-stage businesses typically spend more aggressively. If you are establishing your brand and acquiring initial customers, ten to twenty percent of revenue or more may be appropriate.

## Budget Categories

Digital advertising includes Google Ads, social media ads, and display advertising. This category is scalable and measurable. Allocate based on your customer acquisition costs and conversion rates.

Content marketing includes blog writing, video production, graphic design, and content distribution. Content has compounding returns over time. Invest consistently for long-term growth.

Website and technology includes hosting, maintenance, SEO tools, analytics platforms, and marketing software. These are essential infrastructure for modern marketing.

Events and sponsorships include trade shows, industry events, and community sponsorships. These provide direct face-to-face engagement with potential customers.

Public relations includes media outreach, press release distribution, and PR agency fees. PR builds credibility and visibility over time.

Traditional advertising includes print, radio, billboard, and television. These are less targeted but can build broad awareness.

Personnel costs include salaries for marketing staff or agency fees. People execute your marketing strategy. Invest in good talent.

## Allocation Strategy

Allocate based on what works. Track which channels drive the best results and allocate more budget to them. Channels that underperform should receive reduced funding or be eliminated.

Test new channels with small budgets before scaling. A new platform or tactic should prove itself before receiving significant investment.

Consider your customer journey. Different channels work at different stages. Awareness channels reach new audiences. Consideration channels nurture interest. Conversion channels drive action.

## Measuring ROI

Track cost per lead for each channel. How much does it cost to acquire a lead through each channel? Compare costs to allocate budget to the most efficient channels.

Track customer acquisition cost — the total marketing spend divided by the number of new customers acquired. This tells you whether your marketing is profitable.

Track return on ad spend for paid channels. For every naira spent, how many naira in revenue do you generate?

Track marketing percentage of revenue — total marketing spend as a percentage of total revenue. This helps you maintain appropriate spending levels as your business grows.

## Adjusting Your Budget

Review your marketing budget monthly. Compare actual spending against budget. Adjust based on performance and changing priorities.

Be flexible. If a channel is performing exceptionally well, increase its budget even if it means reducing other areas. If a channel is underperforming, reduce or pause it.

Plan for seasonality. Many Nigerian businesses have seasonal patterns. Increase marketing spend before peak seasons and reduce during slow periods.
