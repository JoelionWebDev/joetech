Every day in Nigeria, thousands of people sell products they did not manufacture. The woman in Surulere who sells hair from her WhatsApp status did not import the hair herself. The young man in Onitsha moving cartons of sneakers did not own the factory. The student in Ibadan selling skincare to her hostel mates did not formulate the cream. They are resellers, and they are the reason many small brands grow faster than their advertising budget should allow.

A reseller programme is a structured way to turn these everyday sellers into your own distribution network. Instead of you chasing every customer yourself, you give other people a reason to sell your product, a price that leaves them profit, and the tools to do it well. Done properly, it is one of the cheapest ways a Nigerian small business can grow. Done carelessly, it becomes a source of price wars, brand damage and unpaid debts.

## What a Reseller Programme Actually Is

A reseller programme is an arrangement where independent sellers buy your product at a discounted price and resell it at a higher price, keeping the difference as their profit. You are the supplier. They are your sales channel. You do not pay them a salary. You do not control their working hours. You simply make the product available at a wholesale price and support them with marketing materials, product knowledge and, sometimes, delivery.

This is different from hiring sales agents on commission. An agent sells on your behalf and earns a percentage of each sale, but the customer still buys from you. A reseller buys the stock first, owns it, and sells it as their own business. That difference matters, because it changes who carries the risk. With agents, unsold stock sits with you. With resellers, unsold stock sits with them, which is exactly why they will only join your programme if they believe the product will sell.

## Is Your Product Right for a Reseller Programme?

Not every product works. Before you design anything, check your product against four tests.

**First, the margin test.** A reseller will only sell your product if the profit is worth their time. If your product sells for ₦5,000 and your cost is ₦4,500, there is only ₦500 to share, and nobody will hustle for that. As a rule, the reseller should be able to make at least 15 to 30 percent on each sale. Products with healthy margins — skincare, hair, fashion accessories, perfumes, supplements, packaged food, phone accessories, cleaning products — work best. Low-margin commodity goods only work at very high volumes, which a new programme cannot promise.

**Second, the demand test.** Resellers sell what their customers already want or what they can easily explain. If your product needs a 20-minute lecture before anyone understands it, resellers will drop it after the first week. Your product should solve a problem people already recognise: clearer skin, longer-lasting perfume, a phone case that actually protects the phone, a food item people already buy weekly.

**Third, the repeat-purchase test.** The best reseller products are bought again and again. A customer who buys a cream every month is worth far more to a reseller than a customer who buys a blender once in five years. Consumables — cosmetics, food, supplements, cleaning products, baby items — build the repeat orders that keep resellers active. Durable goods can still work, but you will need to recruit more resellers to replace the ones who run out of new customers.

**Fourth, the logistics test.** Can you supply consistently? A reseller who sells out on Monday and cannot restock until the following month will move on to another brand. Before launching, make sure you can keep stock available, deliver to resellers in Lagos within 24 to 48 hours, and ship to Abuja, Port Harcourt, Ibadan and Enugu within a few days. If your own supply chain is unreliable, fix that first. A reseller programme multiplies your supply problems; it does not hide them.

## Step 1: Design Your Pricing and Margins

Pricing is the heart of your programme. Get it wrong and either nobody joins or you work for nothing.

Start with three prices. First, your cost per unit — the true cost, including packaging and delivery to you. Second, your recommended retail price (RRP), the price the end customer pays. Third, the reseller price, the price your resellers pay you. The gap between the reseller price and the RRP is the reseller's profit, and it is the most important number in your programme.

Here is a worked example. Suppose you sell a body cream. Your cost per jar is ₦3,000. You set the RRP at ₦7,500. You sell to resellers at ₦5,500. The reseller earns ₦2,000 per jar, about 27 percent of the retail price. You earn ₦2,500 per jar before your other expenses. Both sides have a reason to push the product.

Most Nigerian reseller programmes use one of three structures. The simplest is a flat discount: every reseller gets the same percentage off the retail price, usually 20 to 30 percent.

The third structure is a minimum order quantity with a fixed wholesale price. For example, a minimum of 10 pieces at ₦5,500 each. This protects you from people who want reseller prices for a single piece for personal use, which is one of the most common abuses in Nigerian reseller programmes.

Whatever structure you choose, write it down in one page and share it with every reseller. Never negotiate the reseller price privately with individual sellers in the early days. The moment one reseller discovers another got a better price, your programme loses trust, and trust is the only thing holding a reseller network together.

Also decide your retail price policy. Will you insist resellers sell at the RRP, or can they discount? The safest approach for a new brand is to set a minimum advertised price. Resellers may sell at the RRP or slightly above it in areas where delivery costs are higher, but they may not advertise below the RRP. This stops the race to the bottom where resellers undercut each other until nobody makes money and customers start doubting the product is genuine.

## Step 2: Decide What Resellers Get Besides Stock

Margin alone is rarely enough. The programmes that keep resellers active are the ones that make selling easy. Think about what your resellers actually need to close a sale on WhatsApp or Instagram.

They need good pictures and videos. Most resellers are not photographers. Give them a shared folder — a Google Drive link or a WhatsApp group where you drop fresh product photos, short videos, before-and-after pictures and customer testimonials every week. Make sure the pictures are clean, well-lit and show the product clearly. A reseller armed with professional images will outsell one taking blurry photos on a bedsheet every single time.

They need words that sell. Write short, ready-to-copy captions in simple English, and if your market is local, in a mix of English and Pidgin where appropriate. Write answers to the questions customers always ask: How do I use it? How long does it last? Is it original? What is the delivery fee to Ikeja? When resellers do not have to think about what to say, they post more often.

They need product knowledge. A reseller who understands the product can answer objections. Run a short training when someone joins — a 30-minute WhatsApp call or a voice-note series explaining what the product does, who it is for, who should not use it, and how it compares to alternatives. Record it once and reuse it. The resellers who know the product best will always be your top sellers.

They need fast answers from you. When a reseller has a customer waiting, a reply that comes three hours later is a lost sale. Set a response-time standard for yourself: reply to reseller messages within one hour during business hours.

## Step 3: Set the Rules Before You Recruit Anyone

Every reseller programme that collapses does so because the rules were unclear. Write a simple one-page agreement before your first reseller joins. It does not need a lawyer at the start, but it needs to be clear and it needs to be shared with everyone.

The agreement should cover five things. First, the reseller price and minimum order. Second, the retail price policy — the RRP and the minimum price a reseller may advertise. Third, payment terms: do resellers pay before delivery, or do you allow pay-on-delivery for trusted resellers? For a new programme, payment before delivery is the only safe option. Fourth, returns and damaged goods: what happens if a product arrives damaged, and what happens if a customer returns a product? Fifth, territory: can two resellers sell in the same area, or do you give exclusive rights to one reseller per area? For most small businesses, open territory works better at the start, because you need coverage. Exclusivity only makes sense when a reseller commits to a large monthly volume.

Also state plainly what will get a reseller removed from the programme: selling fake versions of your product, advertising below the minimum price repeatedly, or refusing to pay for stock taken on credit. Having this in writing protects you when you need to act.

One rule deserves special attention: never give stock on credit to a new reseller. It is the most common mistake Nigerian small businesses make, and it is how many reseller programmes die. A new reseller who takes ₦100,000 worth of goods and disappears leaves a hole that takes months of profit to fill. Credit is something a reseller earns after three to six months of consistent, fully-paid orders. Even then, cap it at a small amount and keep a record of every naira owed.

## Step 4: Recruit Your First Ten Resellers

Your first ten resellers matter more than your next hundred. They will test your system, tell you what is broken, and become your proof that the programme works. Recruit carefully.

Start with people who already sell something. The woman selling hair on Instagram, the guy with a provision store in Ajah, the student who runs a small thrift business — these people already have customers and they already know how to close a sale. Your product becomes one more thing they offer. Announce the programme on your own WhatsApp status, Instagram page and Facebook page, and ask your existing customers if they would like to earn from selling the product. Your happiest customers are often your best first resellers because they already believe in the product.

When someone shows interest, do a short screening. Ask what they currently sell, how many customers they reach in a week, and whether they are willing to buy a starter pack. A starter pack — say, 10 pieces at the reseller price — filters out people who want free samples or reseller prices for personal use. It also gives the new reseller enough stock to make real sales in the first two weeks. If someone cannot afford the starter pack, they are not ready to be a reseller yet, and that is fine. You can point them to your affiliate or referral option instead, if you offer one.

Be careful about recruiting family and close friends first. It feels supportive, but if they treat the stock casually or expect special credit terms, it creates problems you cannot easily solve without damaging the relationship. Recruit on merit: people who can sell, not people you feel obliged to help.

## Step 5: Manage Orders, Payments and Delivery

A reseller programme lives or dies on operations. If ordering is confusing or delivery is slow, resellers will quietly stop. Make ordering simple. A dedicated WhatsApp number for reseller orders, a simple order form on your website, or even a Google Form — pick one channel and use it consistently. When a reseller sends an order, confirm it immediately with the total amount, the payment account details, and the expected delivery date.

For payments, use a proper business account and a payment link. Paystack and Flutterwave both let you generate payment links and virtual accounts, which means you can see exactly who paid and for what without scrolling through screenshots of transfers. Keep a simple spreadsheet — reseller name, phone number, location, date, items, amount paid, delivery status. This spreadsheet is your programme's memory. Review it weekly.

Delivery is where many Nigerian programmes fail. Inside Lagos, aim for same-day or next-day delivery using a dispatch rider or a logistics company. To Abuja, Port Harcourt or Ibadan, two to four days is reasonable. Build the delivery cost into your planning: either charge the reseller a flat delivery fee or include it in the reseller price and advertise "free delivery on orders above ₦50,000".

Track what each reseller orders. Your top 20 percent of resellers will usually generate 80 percent of your sales, and you should know who they are by name. Check in with your active resellers weekly and with silent ones monthly. A reseller who has not ordered in six weeks may have run into a problem. A short message often brings them back.

## Step 6: Keep Resellers Motivated Month After Month

Recruiting is easy. Retention is the real work. Most reseller programmes lose half their members in the first three months because the novelty wears off and orders slow down. Here is how to keep people selling.

Run a monthly incentive that is simple to understand. For example, the reseller with the highest sales in the month gets a bonus of free stock worth ₦20,000. Or every reseller who orders at least ₦100,000 in a month gets a 5 percent bonus in free products. Keep it to one incentive at a time so nobody is confused. Announce the winner publicly in the group. Public recognition is powerful in Nigeria's social selling culture, where status and reputation drive behaviour.

Hold a short monthly meeting, even if it is just a 20-minute WhatsApp voice session. Share what is selling, what new products are coming, and what is working for the top sellers. Let a top reseller share one tip. This creates community, and community keeps people from drifting to a competitor's programme.

Refresh your marketing materials regularly. The same three pictures get stale after a month. New photos, a short video of the product in use, a customer testimonial screenshot — fresh content gives resellers a reason to post again.

## Common Mistakes That Kill Reseller Programmes

**Giving stock on credit to strangers.** This is the number one killer. Payment before delivery, always, until trust is earned over months.

**Letting resellers set any price.** Without a minimum advertised price, resellers undercut each other, margins collapse, and customers begin to suspect the product is fake. Protect your price.

**Recruiting everyone who asks.** A programme full of inactive members is worse than a small programme of active sellers. Inactive members drain your time and dilute your attention. Screen, and be willing to remove people who never order.

**Failing to supply on time.** Nothing kills enthusiasm faster than a reseller who has customers waiting and no stock. Keep a buffer stock for resellers, separate from your retail stock, so a surge in your own sales never leaves your resellers empty-handed.

## A Simple 30-Day Launch Plan

Week one: confirm your margins, write your one-page rules, prepare your pictures, captions and price list, and set up your order and payment process. Test the whole flow yourself by placing a mock order.

Week two: announce the programme to your existing customers and on your social media pages. Screen applicants and onboard your first five resellers with a starter pack each.

Week three: follow up with each reseller, answer questions, share fresh content, and process their first reorders. Fix any problem in your ordering or delivery process immediately.

Week four: review the numbers. How many resellers are active? How much stock did they move? What did they struggle with? Announce your first monthly incentive and recruit five more resellers.

You do not need a large budget or a big team to start. You need a product people want, a margin that rewards the seller, clear rules, and the discipline to follow up. Many Nigerian businesses with a single product and ten committed resellers sell more in a month than businesses spending ₦200,000 on ads. The difference is that resellers bring their own trust, their own audience and their own hustle. Your job is to give them a product worth selling and a programme worth staying in.

Start small, stay consistent, and let your resellers grow your business one customer at a time.
