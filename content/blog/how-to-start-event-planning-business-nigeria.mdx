Nigerians love to celebrate. Weddings, traditional engagements, birthdays, baby showers, corporate launches, church programmes and graduations happen every weekend in every city. Behind the beautiful halls, the decorations, the food and the music, there is usually an event planner making it all work — and that planner is paid for it.

An event planning business is one of the most rewarding small businesses you can start in Nigeria if you are organised, creative and good with people. You do not need a big office or expensive equipment to begin. You need planning skills, a network of reliable vendors, and the ability to deliver a beautiful event on time and within budget.

This guide walks you through the whole process: the skills you need, your startup costs, finding your first clients, pricing your services, working with vendors, and growing into a brand that clients trust with their most important days.

## Understand the Events Market in Nigeria

The Nigerian events industry is large, busy and full of opportunity — but it is also competitive. Understanding the different types of events will help you choose where to focus.

### The main types of events

- **Weddings and traditional engagements** — the biggest and most profitable events. A wedding planner in Lagos can earn a substantial fee from a single wedding, and weddings often lead to referrals for years.
- **Birthdays and parties** — children's birthdays, milestone birthdays and surprise parties. Smaller budgets, but frequent and a good way to build experience.
- **Corporate events** — product launches, conferences, end-of-year parties, award nights and company dinners. Corporate clients pay well and often become repeat clients.
- **Religious and community events** — church programmes, mosque events, community celebrations and fundraisers.
- **Concerts and entertainment events** — bigger, more complex, and usually handled by experienced planners with large teams.

### Choose your niche

You do not have to plan every type of event. Many successful planners specialise: weddings only, corporate events only, or children's parties only. A niche helps you build expertise, a strong portfolio and a reputation. If you love weddings, focus on weddings. If you are good with corporate clients, focus on corporate events.

### Study your local market

Spend time researching event planners in your city. Look at their Instagram pages, their packages and their prices. Attend events as a guest and observe what works well and what goes wrong. Notice the halls, the decorators, the caterers and the MCs — these are the vendors you will work with. Understanding the local market before you launch will save you from expensive mistakes.

## The Skills You Need

Event planning is a skill business. Clients trust you with their money and their most important occasions, so competence matters more than anything.

### Organisation and attention to detail

An event has hundreds of moving parts: the hall, the food, the drinks, the decoration, the music, the photography, the seating, the programme and the timing. A good planner keeps track of all of them with checklists, timelines and budgets. If you are naturally organised, you have a head start. If you are not, you will need to build systems — spreadsheets, checklists and reminders — and follow them religiously.

### Communication and people skills

You will deal with clients, vendors, hall managers, family members and service providers, often under pressure. Clear, calm communication prevents most problems. Learn to listen to what a client really wants, explain options honestly, and say no politely when a request is impossible within their budget.

### Creativity and good taste

Clients hire a planner partly for ideas: colour themes, decoration styles, table settings and programme flow. You do not need to be an artist, but you need an eye for what looks good and the ability to bring a theme together. Follow Nigerian and international event trends on Instagram and Pinterest, visit event exhibitions, and study the work of planners you admire.

### Negotiation and budgeting

You will negotiate with vendors constantly — halls, caterers, decorators, DJs, photographers. A good negotiator gets better prices for clients and better margins for the business. You also need to manage budgets honestly: a client who gives you ₦3,000,000 for a wedding must see where every naira goes.

### Learn from experience and training

Take a short event planning course if you can — several training providers in Lagos and Abuja offer weekend or online courses. Even better, volunteer or work as an assistant with an established planner for a few events. Real experience at a live event teaches you things no course can: how to handle a late vendor, a sudden change in the programme, or a generator failure in the middle of a wedding.

## Work Out Your Startup Costs

Event planning has lower startup costs than many businesses, because your main asset is your skill and your network, not expensive equipment.

### A realistic starting budget

- **Business registration (CAC):** ₦30,000 to ₦60,000.
- **Branding:** ₦50,000 to ₦120,000 for a logo, business cards, flyers and a simple banner.
- **Phone and laptop:** you probably already have these, but budget ₦150,000 to ₦300,000 if you need a reliable laptop for proposals and budgets.
- **Website or online portfolio:** ₦50,000 to ₦150,000 for a simple website showing your services, photos and contact details. Your Instagram page can serve as your portfolio at the start.
- **Transport and meeting costs:** ₦50,000 to ₦100,000 for your first months of moving around to meet clients and inspect venues.
- **A small stock of event essentials:** ₦100,000 to ₦250,000 if you plan to own basic items like tablecloths, centrepieces or a backdrop stand. Many planners start by renting everything and buy items gradually.

Altogether, you can start an event planning business with ₦300,000 to ₦700,000, especially if you work from home and rent equipment per event. As you grow, you can invest in your own chairs, canopies, lighting and decoration items, which reduces your rental costs and increases your profit on each event.

### Decide: rent or own equipment?

In the beginning, rent. Renting decoration items, chairs, sound systems and canopies from suppliers lets you handle events without a large investment. As you grow and certain items are needed at almost every event, buying them makes sense. Keep a simple record of what you rent most often — those are the items worth buying first.

## Register Your Business and Set Up Properly

### Register with the CAC

Register your business name with the Corporate Affairs Commission. It protects your brand, lets you open a business bank account, and makes corporate clients take you seriously. Many corporate clients will not sign a contract with an unregistered planner.

### Open a business account

Keep your event money separate from your personal money. Clients pay deposits and full payments into your business account, and you pay vendors from it. Clear records protect you if a client ever questions a payment. Payment links from Paystack or Flutterwave let clients pay deposits online, and they make you look professional.

### Create your contracts and terms

Every event should have a written agreement: the services you will provide, the total cost, the payment schedule, the cancellation terms and what happens if the event is postponed. A simple written agreement, shared on WhatsApp or email and agreed by the client, prevents most disputes. Always collect a deposit — usually 50% to 70% — before you start spending on vendors.

## Build Your Vendor Network

An event planner is only as good as the vendors behind them. Your network is your real business asset.

### The vendors you need

Build relationships with reliable vendors in each category:

- **Event halls and venues** — know the halls in your area, their capacities, their prices and their rules.
- **Caterers** — food is the most talked-about part of any event. Find caterers whose food is good and whose service is reliable.
- **Decorators** — the people who create the look of the event.
- **Photographers and videographers** — clients judge the event partly by the photos.
- **DJs and MCs** — they control the energy of the event.
- **Makeup artists and hairstylists** — essential for weddings.
- **Cake makers, small chops vendors and drinks suppliers** — the details that make an event feel complete.
- **Sound, lighting and generator providers** — the technical backbone of the event.

### How to build the network

Attend events, visit vendors at their shops, and introduce yourself properly. Offer them your first jobs and pay them on time — vendors remember planners who pay promptly and bring repeat business. Keep a list of your vendors with their phone numbers, prices and strengths. Always have a backup vendor for the critical services: if your caterer falls ill on a wedding Saturday, you need another caterer you can call.

### Negotiate vendor rates

Because you bring vendors regular business, you can often negotiate better rates than a one-off client. Be honest with vendors about your budget, and be fair: a vendor who earns well from your jobs will prioritise your events.

## Design Your Services and Price Them

### Create clear service packages

Clients find it easier to book when your services are clear. A simple structure:

- **Full planning** — you handle everything from start to finish: venue, vendors, decoration, coordination and the day itself. This is your premium service and your highest fee.
- **Partial planning** — the client has booked some vendors already, and you handle the rest.
- **Day-of coordination** — the client has planned everything, and you manage the event on the day so nothing goes wrong.
- **Decoration only** — you handle the look of the event without full planning.
- **Corporate event packages** — tailored packages for launches, conferences and dinners.

### How to price your services

Event planners in Nigeria charge in different ways: a flat fee per event, a percentage of the total event budget (often 10% to 20%), or a combination. For a small birthday, a flat fee of ₦100,000 to ₦300,000 may be appropriate. For a wedding with a total budget of ₦5,000,000, a planning fee of 10% to 15% is common. For large corporate events, fees are higher and usually negotiated per event.

When you set your price, consider the size of the event, the number of guests, the complexity, the location and how many days of work it will take. Always state clearly what your fee covers and what it does not cover — for example, your fee may not include the cost of food, drinks or the hall. Clients must understand this from the start.

### Be transparent about budgets

Show the client a clear budget breakdown: hall, food, drinks, decoration, photography, music, your fee and a small contingency for unexpected costs. Transparency builds trust, and trust brings referrals. Never inflate vendor prices to increase your margin — if a client discovers it, your reputation is finished.

## Find Your First Clients

### Start with people you know

Your first events will come from your network: friends, family, church members, colleagues. Offer to plan a birthday or a small party for someone you know at a fair price, and do an excellent job. That one event, well photographed, becomes your portfolio.

### Build a portfolio from real events

Photograph every event you plan — the hall before guests arrive, the table settings, the decoration, the happy moments. With the client's permission, post these photos on Instagram and on your website. A strong portfolio is the single most important marketing tool for an event planner.

### Use Instagram and WhatsApp well

Instagram is where most Nigerian clients find event planners. Post your work consistently, use your location, and show behind-the-scenes content: venue visits, decoration setup, food tasting. WhatsApp Business lets clients message you easily, see your service list and your prices, and share your contact with friends.

### Partner with halls, churches and vendors

Event halls, churches and mosques meet people planning events every week. Build relationships with hall managers and offer a referral arrangement. Photographers, makeup artists and caterers also meet your future clients — refer work to each other and recommend each other.

## Plan and Deliver Events Like a Professional

### The planning process

A professional planning process usually follows these steps:

1. **First meeting** — understand the client's vision, guest count, budget and date.
2. **Proposal and budget** — send a written proposal with a budget breakdown and your fee.
3. **Agreement and deposit** — sign the agreement and collect the deposit.
4. **Venue and vendor booking** — secure the hall and key vendors early, especially for wedding season.
5. **Design and details** — agree the colour theme, decoration, food menu, programme and timeline.
6. **Final checks** — confirm every vendor a week before the event, and again the day before.
7. **The event day** — arrive early, supervise setup, coordinate the programme, and solve problems quietly.
8. **After the event** — follow up with the client, collect feedback and ask for a review and photos.



Things will go wrong: a vendor will be late, the rain will fall, the power will fail. Your job is to solve problems without panicking the client. Always have a backup plan: a second generator contact, a backup DJ, extra chairs, and a small emergency fund for last-minute fixes. Clients remember how you handled a crisis more than the crisis itself.

### Manage the money carefully

Track every payment from the client and every payment to vendors. Never spend a client's deposit on another event. Keep receipts and give the client a final account after the event. Honest money management is the foundation of a planning business that lasts.

## Grow Your Event Planning Business

### Hire and train a team

As you take on more events, you will need help: an assistant planner, a decoration team, or day-of coordinators. Train them to your standards and pay them fairly. A team that works well lets you handle bigger events and multiple events on the same weekend.

### Build your own inventory

Gradually buy the items you rent most: chairs, tables, tablecloths, centrepieces, backdrops, lighting and sound equipment. Owning inventory reduces your costs on each event and increases your profit. Store items properly and maintain them — clean, well-kept equipment looks better and lasts longer.

### Specialise and raise your prices

As your portfolio and reputation grow, you can specialise in the events you enjoy most and charge more for them. A planner known for beautiful weddings in Lagos can charge premium fees, because brides are paying for a proven track record.

### Add related services

Consider adding services your clients already ask for: decoration-only packages, event styling, corporate branding for events, or destination events outside your city. Each new service should serve your existing clients first.

### Collect reviews and referrals

After every successful event, ask the client for a written review and permission to use their photos. Offer a small discount on their next event if they refer a friend who books with you. Referrals are the cheapest and most effective marketing in the events business.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Taking on too many events at once** — one badly managed event damages your name more than the extra income is worth.
- **No written agreement** — verbal agreements lead to disputes about money and services.
- **Spending the deposit on other things** — a client's money is for their event, not for your expenses.
- **Relying on one vendor** — if that vendor fails, your event fails.
- **Underpricing to win jobs** — you cannot deliver a quality event on a budget that does not cover costs.
- **Poor communication** — clients who do not hear from you get anxious and lose trust.
- **Ignoring the small details** — seating, timing, lighting and sound are the things guests notice.
- **No backup plans** — power, weather and vendors can all fail; plan for it.

## Final Thoughts

An event planning business in Nigeria is built on trust, organisation and the ability to turn a client's dream into a real celebration. The demand is constant — Nigerians will always celebrate — and the planners who succeed are the ones who deliver beautiful events, manage money honestly and treat every client like their most important one.

Start with the skills you have, plan a few small events well, build your portfolio and your vendor network, and grow one event at a time. Your first wedding may feel overwhelming; your tenth will feel like routine. Keep learning, keep your standards high, and let every event you plan become an advert for the next one.
