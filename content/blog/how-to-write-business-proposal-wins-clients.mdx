You found a client who needs what you offer. They asked you to send a proposal. You open a blank document — and suddenly you are not sure where to start. What should it say? How long should it be? How do you price it without scaring them away or selling yourself short?

A business proposal is simply a written promise: here is your problem, here is how I will solve it, here is what it will cost, and here is why you can trust me. When it is written well, it does the selling for you. When it is written badly, even good work gets ignored.

## Before You Write a Single Word

The biggest mistake people make is starting with themselves — their company history, their services, their awards. The client does not care about any of that yet. They care about their own problem.

Before you write, find out:

- **What is the real problem?** Not what they said in one sentence, but what is actually going wrong. A client who says "I need a website" might really mean "customers cannot find us online and our competitors are taking the sales." Solve the real problem.
- **Who decides?** Are you writing for the owner, a manager, or a committee? Each one cares about different things. An owner cares about sales and cost. A manager cares about whether the work will disrupt their team.
- **What does success look like?** Ask the client what a good result would be. "More customers" is vague. "Twenty new enquiries a month" is something you can build a proposal around.
- **What is the budget range?** Knowing roughly what they expect to spend stops you from proposing something they cannot afford — or underpricing work they would happily pay more for.

If you cannot get these answers from a conversation, do your own research. Look at the client's website, their social media, their competitors, and their reviews.

## The Structure That Works

You do not need a 20-page document. For most small business work, a clear proposal of three to five pages is enough. Follow this structure:

### 1. A Short Cover

Your business name, the client's name, the date, and one line saying what the proposal is for. Keep it clean and professional. This is the first thing they see — make it look like you care about details.

### 2. Understanding the Problem

One or two short paragraphs showing you understand what the client is facing. Use their own words where you can. This section proves you listened. If you get the problem wrong here, the rest of the proposal does not matter.

### 3. Your Solution

Explain what you will do, in plain language, and how each part solves the problem. Do not list features — explain benefits. Instead of "we will build a five-page website," write "we will build a five-page website that shows your products clearly and lets customers reach you on WhatsApp in one tap, so enquiries stop getting lost." The client should be able to picture the result.

### 4. What They Will Receive

A clear list of deliverables: exactly what is included, and just as importantly, what is not. If the price covers design but not photography, say so. Clear boundaries prevent arguments later.

### 5. Timeline

When the work starts, the key milestones, and when it finishes. Be realistic. A timeline you cannot meet is worse than a longer one you can. If you need things from the client — photos, text, access — say so, and say when you need them by.

### 6. Investment

The price, broken down so the client can see what they are paying for. Show the total clearly. If there are options, present two or three packages (basic, standard, premium) so the client can choose, rather than a single take-it-or-leave-it number.

### 7. Why You

A short section about your experience — not a long history, but the relevant proof. Mention similar work you have done, results you achieved, and anything that makes you the safe choice. If you have testimonials from past clients, include one or two short ones here. Real words from real clients carry more weight than anything you say about yourself.

### 8. Next Steps

Tell the client exactly what happens next: "Reply to this email to approve, and we will send the invoice for the first payment and start on Monday." Make it easy to say yes.

### 9. Terms

Payment schedule, validity period of the proposal (for example, 14 days), and any conditions. Keep this short and fair.

## Writing Tips That Make a Difference

### Write about the client, not yourself

Count how many times your proposal says "we" versus "you." If "we" wins by a wide margin, rewrite it. The client is the hero of this document, not you. Every paragraph should answer the question: what does this mean for them?

### Be specific, not vague

"We will improve your online presence" means nothing. "We will rebuild your website so it loads in under three seconds, add a WhatsApp button to every page, and set up your Google Business Profile so customers in Lekki can find you" means everything. Specifics show you have thought about the work. Vague promises show you have not.

### Keep it short

Nobody reads a long proposal with joy. Cut every sentence that does not help the client decide. If a paragraph can be a bullet list, make it a bullet list. If a page can be a paragraph, make it a paragraph. Respect the client's time and they will respect your proposal.

## After You Send It

Sending the proposal is only half the job. Most proposals are won or lost in the follow-up.

- **Confirm they received it.** A short message the same day: "I have sent the proposal to your email. Please let me know if you have any questions."
- **Follow up in two to three days.** Ask if they have had a chance to read it and whether anything needs clarifying. Do not ask "have you decided?" — ask "is there anything I can explain?"
- **Know when to stop.** If a client has not replied after two polite follow-ups, send a final short message and move on. Chasing a client who will never buy wastes time you could spend finding one who will.

## Common Proposal Mistakes

- **Copying the same proposal for every client.** Clients can tell. Change the problem, the solution, and the examples every time.
- **Too much about you, too little about the client.** Flip the balance.
- **No deadline.** A proposal with no validity period and no next step drifts forever.
- **Spelling and grammar errors.** Read the proposal twice before sending it. Ask someone else to read it if you can. Errors make the client wonder what else you will get wrong.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long should a business proposal be?

Long enough to answer the client's questions, short enough to be read in one sitting. For most small business jobs, three to five pages is right. A very small job might need only one or two pages. A large project might need more, but even then, keep the main proposal short and put extra detail in an appendix.

### Should I send the proposal by email or WhatsApp?

Email is better for the formal document — send the PDF by email so it is easy to find, forward, and print. Then send a short WhatsApp message to let the client know it has arrived. That combination is professional and practical, and it matches how most Nigerian businesses actually communicate.

### What if the client asks me to reduce the price?

Do not reduce the price by cutting your own margin without changing anything else. Instead, offer a smaller version of the work for the lower price — fewer pages, fewer features, a shorter timeline. That way the client gets a lower price and you still earn fairly. If the client insists on the full work at a lower price, it is better to walk away politely than to take work that loses you money.

### How do I handle a client who asks for a free proposal with full details?

A proposal should show your thinking, not give away the whole solution for free. Describe what you will do and what the result will be, but keep the detailed how-to for after the client signs.

## Make Every Proposal Count

A proposal is not just paperwork. It is the moment a potential client decides whether to trust you with their money. Write it about them, be specific about what you will do, price it honestly, and follow up. Do that consistently, and more of your proposals will turn into work.
