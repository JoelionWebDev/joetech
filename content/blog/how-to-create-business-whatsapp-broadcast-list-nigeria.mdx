If you sell on WhatsApp in Nigeria, you know the problem. You have new stock, a price change, or a special offer, and you want to tell your customers quickly. Sending the same message to fifty people one by one takes hours. Posting on your status is faster, but many customers do not check statuses every day. Creating a WhatsApp group is another option, but groups can become noisy, customers can see each other, and people leave when the chat gets too busy.

A WhatsApp broadcast list solves this problem. A broadcast list lets you send one message to many people at once, and each person receives it as a private message from you. They cannot see the other people on the list, and they cannot reply to the whole list. It feels personal, like a one-to-one message, but it saves you hours of copying and pasting. For a small business in Lagos, Abuja, Port Harcourt, or any Nigerian town, that is a powerful tool.

This guide shows you how to create a WhatsApp broadcast list for your business step by step. You will learn the difference between a broadcast list and a group, who to add and how to get permission, how to create a list on Android, what to send and how often, how to track replies, and how to avoid being blocked or reported. By the end, you will be able to send your first broadcast with confidence.


## What Is a Broadcast List and How Is It Different From a Group?

A broadcast list is a list of contacts you choose on WhatsApp. When you send a message to the list, WhatsApp delivers it to each person individually, as if you had sent it to them alone. The recipient sees your message in their normal chat with you. They do not see a group name, they do not see other recipients, and they cannot reply to everyone at once. If they reply, only you see the reply.

A group is different. In a group, everyone sees everyone else, everyone sees all the messages, and anyone can reply to the whole group. Groups are good for communities, classes, or teams where people need to talk to each other. They are not ideal for business updates, because a customer may not want their number shared with strangers, and a single noisy member can disturb everyone else. A broadcast list protects your customers' privacy and keeps your business messages clean.

There are two important rules about broadcast lists. First, a recipient will only receive your broadcast if they have your number saved in their phone. If a customer has not saved your business number, your broadcast will not reach them, even if they are on your list. That is why you should always ask customers to save your number when they first buy from you. Second, WhatsApp limits how many people you can add to one broadcast list. The limit can change, but it is usually up to 256 contacts per list. If you have more customers than that, you will need more than one list, for example, one for Lagos customers and one for Abuja customers, or one for regular buyers and one for new customers.

## Who Should Be on Your Broadcast List?

Your broadcast list should contain people who want to hear from you. That sounds obvious, but many businesses make the mistake of adding every number they have ever collected, including people who bought once two years ago or who only asked for a price and never bought. Those people are likely to ignore your messages, block you, or report you as spam. A smaller list of interested customers is far more valuable than a large list of strangers.

Start with your existing customers. These are people who have bought from you, booked a service, or asked you to tell them when new stock arrives. They already know your business and trust you enough to buy. Next, add people who have given you permission. Permission can be as simple as a customer saying, "Yes, please send me new arrivals on WhatsApp," or ticking a box on a form, or replying "Yes" to your message asking if they want updates. Keep a note of who gave permission and when, so you can show it if someone complains.

Do not add people without asking. Do not buy phone numbers from a list seller and add them to a broadcast list. Those people did not ask for your messages, and WhatsApp may restrict your account if many of them report you. Do not add numbers from a group without permission. If you meet a potential customer in a Facebook group or at a market, ask them first: "Can I add you to my WhatsApp broadcast for new products?" If they say yes, save their number and add them. If they say no, respect it. A respectful business keeps customers longer.

Organise your contacts before you create the list. Save each customer with a clear name, such as "Ada - Dress Customer" or "Mr. Bello - Food Order," so you can recognise them later. If you use WhatsApp Business, use labels to group customers. You can label people as "New Customer," "Regular Customer," "Lagos," or "Paid." Labels help you create targeted broadcast lists later, instead of sending the same message to everyone. For example, you can send a Lagos delivery offer only to Lagos customers, and a new stock alert only to customers who buy that product.

## Create a Broadcast List on Android

Creating a broadcast list on Android is quick. Open WhatsApp or WhatsApp Business on your phone. Tap the three dots in the top right corner to open the menu. Tap "New broadcast." You will see a list of your contacts. Select the contacts you want to add by tapping their names. You can search for a name if your contact list is long. When you have selected everyone, tap the tick or "Create" button at the bottom.

WhatsApp will create the list and open a chat window for it. The list will have a name based on the contacts, but you can rename it. Tap the list name at the top, then tap the pencil icon or "Rename" to give it a clear name, such as "Regular Customers - November" or "Lagos Buyers." A clear name helps you remember which list is which when you have more than one. 

Before you send your first message, check the list. Tap the list name to see who is on it. Remove anyone you added by mistake. Make sure each person has your number saved, if you know they have. If you are not sure, send a short test message to a few people first, or ask them to save your number. A good first message is: "Hello, this is Ada from Ada Fashion in Ikeja. Thank you for buying from us. Please save this number so you can receive our new arrivals on WhatsApp. Reply YES if you would like to receive updates." That message asks for permission, explains who you are, and gives the customer a clear action.

To send a broadcast, type your message in the list chat window and send it as you would a normal message. You can send text, photos, videos, voice notes, documents, and links. Each recipient will receive it as a private message. You will see ticks to show delivery and reading, just like a normal chat. If a recipient replies, the reply will appear in your one-to-one chat with that person, not in the broadcast list. 

## What to Send and How Often

The success of a broadcast list depends on what you send and how often you send it. Send useful, interesting messages, and customers will look forward to them. Send too many sales messages, and customers will mute or block you. A good rule is to send one to three broadcasts per week, not every day, unless you have a genuine reason, such as a flash sale or a delivery update for people who ordered.

Send new arrivals first. When new stock arrives, send a photo, the price, and the sizes available. For example: "New Ankara dresses just arrived. Size 10 to 16, ₦18,000 each. Only 10 pieces. Reply to order." That message is short, clear, and creates urgency without pressure. Send special offers and discounts, but do not offer a discount every time. If every message is a discount, customers will wait for the next discount and never pay full price. Use discounts for a real reason, such as a holiday, a new product launch, or clearing old stock.

Send useful information, not just sales messages. A food seller can send a recipe or a tip on storing food. A fashion seller can send styling tips. A phone seller can send tips on caring for a battery. A beauty seller can send a short skincare tip. Those messages build trust and keep customers engaged between sales. You can also send reminders, such as "Your favourite hair oil is back in stock," or "We will be closed on Sunday for a public holiday." Those messages are helpful and show you care about the customer, not just the sale.

Keep each message short and clear. Start with the most important information. If you have a new product, start with the product name and price. If you have an offer, start with the offer and the end date. End with a clear action: "Reply to order," "Tap the link to pay," or "Visit our shop today." Avoid long messages with too many products. One product per broadcast is usually best. If you have five new products, send one broadcast with the best product, and tell customers to check your catalogue or status for the rest. That keeps the message focused and easy to act on.

## Manage Replies, Orders, and Follow-Ups

When you send a broadcast, replies will come into your one-to-one chats. That is where the real selling happens. Reply quickly, politely, and clearly. If a customer asks for the price, give it. If they ask for a size, confirm it is available. If they want to order, confirm the total price, delivery cost, and delivery time in writing, and send a payment link or account details. Do not leave a customer waiting for hours after they reply to a broadcast. They may lose interest or buy from someone else.

Use WhatsApp Business tools to manage replies faster. Save quick replies for common questions, such as prices, delivery areas, and opening hours. For example, save "/price" for your price list and "/delivery" for your delivery information. Use labels to mark customers who have ordered, paid, or need a follow-up. When a customer pays, label them as "Paid" and move them to a delivery list. When a customer asks a question but does not buy, label them as "Follow Up" and send them a polite message the next day: "Hello, did you still want the dress in size 12? It is still available." One follow-up is helpful. Three follow-ups in a day are pressure.

Track what works. After each broadcast, note how many people replied, how many ordered, and how many paid. You do not need a complex system. A simple note in your sales book is enough: "Broadcast 17 Nov - New dresses - 80 sent, 12 replies, 5 orders, ₦90,000 sales." Over time, you will see which messages work best. A broadcast with a clear photo and price may get many replies. A broadcast with no price may get few replies. A broadcast sent on Saturday morning may work better than one sent on Monday night. Use those lessons to improve your next broadcast.

Remove people who ask to be removed, immediately. If a customer replies "Stop," "Remove me," or "I don't want messages," remove them from the list and reply politely: "No problem, I have removed you. Thank you." Do not argue or send another sales message. Respecting that request protects your account and your reputation. Also remove people who have not replied or bought in a long time, after a polite check-in. A clean list of active customers is better than a large list of inactive numbers.

## Avoid Being Blocked or Reported

WhatsApp can restrict or ban an account that sends too many unwanted messages. That is a real risk for businesses, and it can stop your sales for days or longer. The best way to avoid it is to send only to people who want your messages, send at a reasonable frequency, and avoid spam-like behaviour. Do not send the same message to many lists in a short time. Do not send messages at midnight. Do not use all capital letters or too many emojis. Do not send links that look suspicious. If you send a payment link, use a trusted link, such as a Paystack link, and explain what it is for.

Watch for warning signs. If many people block you after a broadcast, your messages may be too frequent or too sales-focused. If WhatsApp shows a warning that your account may be restricted, slow down and review your list. Remove people who have not given permission. Send fewer messages for a while. If your account is temporarily restricted, do not create a new account to send the same messages. That can lead to a longer ban. Wait, follow WhatsApp's instructions, and rebuild your list with permission.

Use WhatsApp Business, not a personal account with a fake name, if you are selling regularly. WhatsApp Business has tools for catalogues, labels, quick replies, and business profiles that make you look professional and help customers trust you. It also makes it easier to separate business chats from personal chats. Keep your business profile complete, with your business name, address or area, opening hours, and a catalogue of your products. A complete profile reduces the chance that customers will think you are a scam account.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

The first mistake is adding people without permission. It leads to blocks, reports, and a damaged reputation. Always ask first, and always give people a way to leave the list. The second mistake is sending too many messages. A business that sends three broadcasts a day will be muted or blocked quickly. Send one to three per week, and make each one count.

The third mistake is sending messages with no clear price or action. A photo with no price forces the customer to ask, "How much?" That extra step loses sales. Always include the price and what the customer should do next. The fourth mistake is using the same message for everyone. A customer in Port Harcourt does not need a Lagos-only delivery offer. A customer who buys baby clothes does not need a message about men's shoes. Segment your lists and send relevant messages.

The fifth mistake is ignoring replies. A broadcast is not a one-way announcement. It is the start of a conversation. If you send a broadcast and then ignore the replies for a day, you waste the opportunity. Check your chats after a broadcast and reply promptly. The sixth mistake is not asking customers to save your number. If they do not save it, they will not receive your next broadcast. Remind new customers politely: "Please save this number so you can receive our updates." That small step makes your list work.

## Start With Your Best Customers Today

Start with ten of your best customers. Create a list, send a polite introduction, and ask them to save your number and reply YES if they want updates. Then send your first broadcast: a new product or offer, with a clear photo, price, and action.

After a week, check the results. How many people replied? How many ordered? What did they say? Use those answers to improve your next message. Add new customers to the list as they buy and give permission. Remove people who ask to leave. Keep the list clean, the messages useful, and the frequency reasonable. Over time, your broadcast list will become one of your most valuable business assets, because it lets you reach your best customers directly, without paying for advertising, and without hoping they see a post.
