## Equipment You Actually Need

The biggest misconception about video marketing is that you need expensive equipment. Here is what you actually need to start:

### Your Smartphone

Any mid-range or flagship smartphone released in the last 3-4 years shoots video good enough for social media. The iPhone 11 or newer, Samsung Galaxy A series or higher, Tecno Camon series, or Infinix Zero series all produce excellent results.

**Pro tip:** Clean your lens before every shoot. A smudged lens is the most common cause of blurry video.

### Natural Lighting

Good lighting matters more than your camera. The cheapest and best lighting source is the sun.

- Shoot near a window during daytime for indoor videos
- Shoot outdoors in the morning (7-10 AM) or late afternoon (4-6 PM) for golden hour quality
- Avoid harsh midday sun that creates strong shadows on faces
- Use a white cardboard or foam board as a reflector to bounce light onto your subject

## Types of Videos Your Business Can Create

You do not need to produce cinematic commercials. These video formats are proven to work for small Nigerian businesses:

### Product Demonstrations (1-3 minutes)

Show your product in action. A fashion brand shows how a dress fits and moves. A food business shows the cooking process. A service provider shows a before-and-after transformation.

**Example:** A Lagos skincare brand films a 60-second video applying their product, showing the texture, how it absorbs, and the immediate result. No voiceover needed â€” just text overlays explaining each step.

### Behind the Scenes (30-60 seconds)

Nigerian customers love seeing the human side of businesses. Show your workspace, your team, your production process, or your packaging process.

**Example:** A small bakery films the process of decorating a cake â€” from icing to final presentation. The video builds trust and makes customers feel connected to the brand.

### Customer Testimonials (30-90 seconds)

A video testimonial from a genuine customer is more powerful than any marketing copy you can write. Ask satisfied customers to record a short video explaining what they loved about your product or service.

**Pro tip:** Prepare 3-4 questions for them to answer, and edit the best parts together. Even if you do not have a professional editing setup, a simple string of short clips with text overlays works well.

### Educational Content (1-5 minutes)

Teach your customers something related to your industry. A makeup artist teaches "How to Choose the Right Foundation for Your Skin Tone." A web developer teaches "Three Signs Your Website Needs a Redesign." A real estate agent teaches "What to Look for When Viewing a Property in Lagos."

Educational content positions you as an expert and gets shared more than promotional content.

### Q&A and FAQ Videos (2-10 minutes)

Answer the questions you get most often from customers. Record yourself (or your team) answering each question. Compile them into a video or post them individually.

**Example:** "Our most asked question this week: Do you deliver to Ikeja? Here is our delivery policy and timeline explained."

## Creating a Video Content Strategy on a Zero Budget

Equipment is not the barrier â€” strategy is. Here is how to create a video content plan that works for your Nigerian business.

### Start with One Platform

Do not try to be on YouTube, Instagram, TikTok, Facebook, and LinkedIn simultaneously. Pick one platform where your target customers spend the most time and master it first. For most Nigerian consumer businesses, that is Instagram (Reels) or TikTok. For B2B businesses, LinkedIn or YouTube.

### Post Consistently, Not Perfectly

Consistency beats production value. Posting a simple, authentic video every day for 30 days will outperform posting one polished video per month. Each platform's algorithm rewards frequency. Do not overthink your first 10 videos â€” they will not be great, and that is fine. You improve by doing.

### Batch Your Content

Set aside one day every two weeks to record 10-15 videos. Set up your lighting, record multiple takes of different topics, and edit them over the next few days. This approach saves time and ensures you always have content ready to post.

Templates for batching:
- 3 product demos
- 2 behind-the-scenes clips
- 3 educational tips
- 2 customer testimonials or reviews
- 2 personal messages from the founder
- 3 trending audio challenges or trends (adapt to your brand)

### Repurpose Everything

One video can become multiple pieces of content:
- Post the full video on YouTube
- Post a 60-second cut on Instagram Reels
- Post a 60-second cut on TikTok
- Extract a 15-second clip for Instagram Stories
- Transcribe the audio and turn it into a blog post
- Pull quotes for image-based social posts
- Use screenshots for Pinterest

This multiplies your content output without multiplying your effort.

## Common Video Marketing Mistakes to Avoid

### Trying to Look Like a Big Brand

Do not try to mimic the polished, agency-produced videos of big Nigerian brands. Your advantage as a small business is authenticity. Lean into it. Show your real self, your real workspace, and your real customers. That is what builds trust.

### Ignoring Audio Quality

Viewers will forgive imperfect video before they forgive bad audio. If you are recording in a noisy Lagos environment, use a lapel microphone or record voiceover separately and sync it in editing.

### Making Every Video a Sales Pitch

The 80/20 rule applies: 80% of your videos should provide value (education, entertainment, inspiration), and 20% can be direct promotions. If every video is a sales pitch, people will stop watching.

### Posting and Forgetting

Posting the video is not the end. Engage with comments, reply to DMs, and share user-generated content. The algorithm rewards accounts that are actively engaged with their audience.

### Inconsistent Branding

Use consistent colours, fonts, and music across your videos. Your brand should be recognisable within the first second of a video. CapCut and Canva make this easy with templates.
