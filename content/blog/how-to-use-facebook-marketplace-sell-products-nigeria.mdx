Facebook Marketplace is one of the easiest places to start selling online in Nigeria. It is free to list, millions of Nigerians scroll through it every day, and buyers in your own neighbourhood can find your products without you spending a kobo on advertising. Phones, clothes, shoes, furniture, home appliances and handmade goods all move on Marketplace — and small businesses in Lagos, Abuja, Port Harcourt, Ibadan and Enugu use it daily to find real customers. This guide explains how to set up your account, create listings that get clicks, price your products, handle chats and payments safely, arrange delivery, and grow into a steady business.

## What Facebook Marketplace Is and Why It Works in Nigeria

Marketplace is Facebook's buy-and-sell section. Buyers browse by category, search for what they need, filter by location and price, and message sellers directly on Messenger. There is no listing fee for most items, no commission on the sale, and no need for a website. That is why it works so well for Nigerian small businesses: the barrier to entry is almost zero.

It works well because Nigerians already trust Facebook. A buyer in Surulere who sees a seller with a real profile, real photos and good reviews feels safer than buying from a random website. Location matters too: most buyers prefer sellers close to them, so a seller in Lekki gets enquiries from Lekki, Ajah and Ikoyi first. Marketplace suits products people can judge quickly from photos — fashion, electronics, furniture, phones, beauty products, baby items and fairly used goods. It is less suited to services or products that need long explanations.

## Step 1: Set Up Your Facebook Account for Selling

You need a personal Facebook account to use Marketplace — business Pages cannot list directly in the same way, so your personal profile is your shop front. If your profile looks empty or suspicious, buyers will scroll past you. Fix it before you list anything.

Use a clear photo of yourself as your profile picture, add your real name, fill in your city (for example, Lagos or Abuja), and make sure your profile shows a few genuine posts, photos and friends. A profile created yesterday with no photo and no friends is a red flag. Turn on Messenger notifications so you can reply quickly, and enable two-factor authentication — a hacked account can cost you your listings, chats and customers. Never share your password or any login code with anyone, even if they claim to be from Facebook.

Check that Marketplace is available on your account. Open the Facebook app, tap the menu, and look for the Marketplace icon (a small shop front). If you cannot see it, update the app, log out and back in, or try the Facebook website. New accounts sometimes need a few days of normal activity before Marketplace appears.

## Step 2: Choose What to Sell

Not everything sells well on Marketplace. The best products share three qualities: people search for them often, the price is clear, and the buyer can judge quality from photos. Start with products you can source reliably and price competitively.

Strong categories in Nigeria include fashion (dresses, sneakers, handbags, wristwatches), phones and gadgets (used and new), home appliances (fans, blenders, televisions, air conditioners), furniture (sofas, beds, dining sets), baby items (strollers, cribs, clothes), beauty products, and fairly used goods from relocation sales. A seller in Ikeja who lists fairly used laptops will get enquiries within hours. A seller in Abuja listing quality Ankara dresses will get messages the same day.

Before you commit to a product, search Marketplace for it. How many similar listings exist, and what prices are they asking? If fifty sellers list the same phone case at ₦3,000, you need a better photo, price or bundle to stand out. Start with five to ten products, not fifty. Learn which ones get messages and which ones actually sell, then buy more of what works and drop what does not.

## Step 3: Take Photos That Make People Stop Scrolling

On Marketplace, your photo is your advert. Buyers scroll fast, and a dark, blurry photo gets skipped in half a second. You do not need a professional camera — a clean phone camera and daylight are enough — but you do need to follow a few rules.

Photograph the product in bright natural light, near a window or outside in the shade. Use a plain background: a clean white wall, a bedsheet, or a table. Take at least four photos: the front, the back or side, a close-up of an important detail (the label, the stitching, the screen), and the product in use or next to something that shows its size. For clothes, photograph them on a hanger or on a person rather than crumpled on a bed. For phones, show the screen switched on and the back of the device.

Never use another seller's photos — buyers have learned to spot stolen images, and using them destroys trust. Edit lightly: brighten the image a little and crop out clutter, but do not change the product's colour. A dress that looks red in the photo and arrives pink will earn you a complaint. For expensive items, add a short video: a ten-second clip of a generator starting or a phone's screen working tells a buyer more than five photos.

## Step 4: Write a Listing That Answers Every Question

A good listing title is specific. "Sneakers for sale" tells a buyer nothing. "Nike Air Max 270 — Size 43 — Black — New" tells them everything in one line. Include the brand, the model, the size or capacity, the colour and the condition in the title whenever you can.

The description should answer the questions buyers always ask: What is it? What condition is it in? What size is it? What is included? Where are you located? How much is delivery? For example: "Original Samsung Galaxy A54, 128GB, 8GB RAM. Neatly used for six months, no cracks, battery health excellent. Comes with charger and pouch. Location: Ikeja, Lagos. Price: ₦185,000. Delivery within Lagos ₦2,000 to ₦3,000." Always state the condition honestly — new, neatly used, or fairly used. If there is a scratch or a missing accessory, say so and show it in a photo. Hiding a fault only leads to rejected deliveries and angry buyers.

Set the price carefully. Check what similar items sell for, then price yours within that range — slightly higher if yours is in better condition, slightly lower if you need a fast sale. Avoid writing "DM for price": most buyers will not message a seller who hides the price, and Facebook's search favours listings with a visible price. If you are open to negotiation, say "slightly negotiable" in the description.

## Step 5: Create Your First Listing, Step by Step

Creating a listing takes about five minutes. Open the Facebook app, tap Marketplace, tap "Sell" or the plus button, and choose "Item for sale". Select your photos, then fill in the title, price, category and condition. Choose the right category — a blender listed under "Electronics" gets found; the same blender under "Miscellaneous" gets buried.

Write your description, add your location, and decide whether to also share the listing to relevant buy-and-sell groups. Sharing to two or three active groups in your city can double your views. Pick groups with real members and real activity, not dead groups full of spam. In Lagos, groups for Lekki, Ikeja or Surulere buying and selling are good starting points.

Before you publish, preview the listing as a buyer would see it. Does the first photo look good? Is the price correct? Is your location right? Small errors cost sales. List one product at a time and do it well — ten strong listings will outsell fifty careless ones. If you have many units of the same product in different colours, create one listing that clearly states all available colours, rather than flooding Marketplace with near-identical posts. Facebook may flag duplicates as spam.

## Step 6: Handle Buyer Messages Like a Professional

Most of your selling happens in Messenger. Buyers will ask "Is this still available?", "What is your last price?", "Where are you located?" and "Can you deliver to Abuja?" Reply fast. A reply within five to ten minutes converts far better than a reply three hours later, because the buyer is often messaging three sellers at once and buys from the first one who responds well.

Set up saved replies for common questions. Keep your tone polite and direct. Confirm the product, the price, your location and the delivery cost in your first two messages, so the buyer does not have to drag the information out of you. For example: "Yes, it is available. Price is ₦25,000. I am in Lekki Phase 1. Delivery to Ikeja is ₦3,000, and you can also pick up from my location."

Know your lowest price before the chat starts. If your lowest price for a bag is ₦18,000 and a buyer offers ₦10,000, decline politely and move on. A buyer who offers ₦17,000 for a ₦20,000 item is worth a conversation — a small discount that closes a sale today beats a full-price listing that sits for three weeks. Watch out for time-wasters who ask twenty questions, promise to come tomorrow and never show up. Keep answers short, never send your home address to strangers, and meet buyers in a public place or arrange delivery through a rider.

## Step 7: Get Paid Safely

Payment is where most Marketplace scams happen, so protect yourself. The safest method for most sellers is bank transfer confirmed before you hand over the product. Ask the buyer to send the money, wait until you see the alert and confirm the amount in your bank app, then release the item. Do not rely on a screenshot alone — screenshots can be faked in seconds. Open your banking app and confirm the credit yourself.

Payment links through Paystack or Flutterwave are a good option if you sell regularly. They let buyers pay by card or transfer through a proper checkout page, and you get a clear record of every payment. This also helps if you later build a website or take orders on WhatsApp.

Payment on delivery can win nervous customers, but it carries risk: the buyer may reject the item, leaving you to pay the rider both ways. If you offer it, limit it to your own city, charge a ₦1,000 to ₦2,000 commitment fee upfront, and confirm the buyer's address and phone number before dispatching. Never send a product to another state on payment on delivery unless you can afford to lose the delivery cost.

Never accept payment by gift cards, cryptocurrency from strangers, or cheques from unknown buyers. Never click links a buyer sends you claiming to be a payment confirmation page. Real bank transfers and proper payment links do not need you to click anything. If a buyer pressures you to release goods before payment clears, walk away — a real buyer will wait the few minutes it takes for a transfer to reflect.

Keep a simple sales record — date, buyer, product, price, payment method and delivery status — to track profit and resolve disputes with evidence.

## Step 8: Arrange Delivery Without Headaches

Delivery makes or breaks Marketplace sales. Many buyers will not travel across Lagos traffic to collect an item, so offering delivery increases your sales — but it must be organised.

Within Lagos, use a dispatch rider for same-day delivery, typically ₦1,500 to ₦3,500 depending on distance. Agree the fee with the rider and the buyer before dispatch, and share the rider's name, phone number and estimated arrival time. Package well: bubble wrap for electronics and glass, sturdy bags for clothes, boxes for fragile goods. For Abuja, Port Harcourt, Ibadan or Enugu, use a trusted interstate courier: one to three days, ₦3,000 to ₦6,000 depending on size and weight. Share the tracking number with the buyer and follow up after delivery: "Please confirm you received it in good condition." That message prevents disputes and often earns a good review.

For meetups, choose a safe public place — a shopping mall, a busy junction or a bank premises — during the day, and bring a friend if the item is valuable. Never invite a stranger to your home, and never go to a buyer's house alone. If a delivery fails, ask the rider to return the product, confirm it is undamaged, and relist it.

## Step 9: Avoid Scams and Protect Your Account

Marketplace attracts scammers, and knowing their tricks saves you money. The most common scam is the fake payment alert: a buyer sends a forged bank alert or screenshot and pressures you to release the product quickly. Always confirm in your own bank app. Another common trick is the overpayment scam: a buyer claims to have sent more than the price by mistake and asks you to refund the difference. Check your balance first — the extra money is never there.

Some scammers pose as buyers who want you to click a link to "confirm" a payment or pay a "delivery insurance" fee before they pay you. Real buyers never ask sellers to pay to receive money — ignore those messages and block the sender. Others will ask for your bank login details, card number or an OTP code. No genuine buyer ever needs those. As a buyer, watch out for stolen photos, prices far below market value and pressure to pay immediately: a ₦400,000 phone listed at ₦90,000 is a scam. Verify high-value items in person and pay only after inspection.

Facebook also has rules. Do not list prohibited items such as weapons, drugs or counterfeit goods, and do not post the same listing dozens of times — Facebook may restrict your account. If a listing is removed, read the reason, fix the problem, and relist properly.

## Step 10: Grow From Casual Sales Into a Real Business

Once you are making regular sales, treat Marketplace as a business, not a hobby. Relist your best products every week so they stay fresh in search results — Facebook shows newer listings first, so an old listing buried on page five gets few views. Mark items as sold promptly and remove listings for products you no longer have, so buyers do not waste your time asking about unavailable stock.

Build a repeat-customer list. Every buyer who pays and receives their item happily should get a short follow-up message and an invitation to see your new arrivals. Keep their numbers in a broadcast list on WhatsApp and send new products there first. A customer who bought shoes from you last month is far more likely to buy a bag this month than a stranger who just found your listing.

Collect reviews and ratings. After a smooth sale, politely ask the buyer to leave a rating — good ratings make new buyers trust you instantly, and they are the cheapest marketing you will ever get. Expand to other channels when you are ready: use Marketplace sales to fund a WhatsApp catalogue, an Instagram page and eventually a simple website. Track your numbers weekly — listings published, messages received, sales closed, revenue and profit after delivery and sourcing costs. Improve one number each week — better photos, faster replies, or sharper prices — and sales will grow steadily.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

The biggest mistake is poor photos. Dark, blurry or stolen photos kill a listing before anyone reads the price. The second mistake is slow replies: Marketplace buyers move on fast. The third is dishonest descriptions. Hiding a fault, inflating a product's condition or using a misleading title brings rejected deliveries, angry messages and bad ratings that follow your profile.

Another mistake is pricing without research. A price far above the market rate gets ignored, while a price far below it makes buyers suspicious. Check the market, price fairly, and let your service justify your price. Finally, do not put all your products in one listing, do not spam the same item in twenty groups, and do not ignore Facebook's messages about your listings. Your account is your business asset — protect it.

## Conclusion

Facebook Marketplace is one of the simplest, cheapest ways to start selling online in Nigeria. You do not need a website, a shop or an advertising budget. You need a trustworthy profile, clear photos, honest descriptions, fair prices, fast replies, safe payments and reliable delivery. Start with five to ten products you can source well, list them properly, and learn from every message and every sale.

The sellers who do well on Marketplace are not the ones with the most listings. They are the ones buyers trust: real photos, real prices, quick replies, and products that arrive exactly as described. List your first product today, respond to every message like a professional, and let each happy customer bring you the next one.
