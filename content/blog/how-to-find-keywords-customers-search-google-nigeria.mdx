Every day, Nigerians type questions into Google. "Best tailor in Lekki." "Where to buy original human hair in Lagos." "Affordable web designer in Nigeria." "Plumber near me in Abuja." "How much is a 5-bedroom house in Ajah?" Behind each of those searches is a person who wants to buy something, hire someone, or solve a problem.

If your business shows up when they search, you get a customer without paying for an advert. If you do not show up, that customer goes to your competitor. The difference is often a single thing: keywords. Keywords are the words and phrases your customers type into Google. Find the right ones, use them on your website and your Google Business Profile, and Google will start showing your business to the right people.

Many Nigerian small businesses guess at keywords. They write what they think customers search for, not what customers actually search for. They use formal words when customers use simple words, or they use American terms when Nigerians use local terms. The result is a website that nobody finds.

This guide shows you how to find the real keywords your customers use in Nigeria, using free tools and simple methods you can do yourself, even if you have never done SEO before.

## What Keywords Are, in Plain Language

A keyword is simply a word or phrase someone types into Google. It can be short, such as "hair vendor Lagos", or long, such as "where can I buy original human hair in Lagos at affordable price". The long phrases are called long-tail keywords, and they are often the most valuable for a small business, because the person searching is very clear about what they want.

There are three main types of keywords you should know.

**Informational keywords** come from people who want to learn something. Examples: "how to start a POS business in Nigeria", "how to care for natural hair", "what is the best inverter for a 3-bedroom flat". These people may not buy today, but a helpful blog post can bring them to your website, and some of them will buy later.

**Commercial keywords** come from people who want to compare options before they buy. Examples: "best POS machine in Nigeria", "Paystack vs Flutterwave", "top 10 tailors in Lekki". These searchers are close to buying, and a good comparison page or service page can win them.

**Transactional keywords** come from people who are ready to buy now. Examples: "buy iPhone 13 in Lagos", "book hotel in Lekki", "hire web designer in Nigeria", "order food in Ikeja". These are the most valuable keywords for a business, because the searcher has their money ready.

A good keyword plan includes all three types, but it gives the most attention to transactional and commercial keywords, because those bring customers who are ready to spend.

## Step 1: Start With Your Customers, Not With a Tool

The best keyword research starts with a conversation, not a computer. Before you open any tool, write down what you sell, who you sell it to, and where you sell it.

Ask yourself these questions:

- What product or service do I sell?
- Who is my ideal customer? A young mother in Lekki? A business owner in Ikeja? A student in Nsukka?
- Where are they located? Lagos, Abuja, Port Harcourt, or the whole of Nigeria?
- What problem does my product solve for them?
- What words would they use to describe that problem to a friend?

Then, listen to your customers. Look at your WhatsApp messages. What words do customers use when they ask about your product? Do they say "human hair" or "bone straight"? Do they say "website" or "online shop"? Do they ask for "affordable" or "cheap"? Do they mention a place, such as "in Lekki" or "near Chevron"? The words in their messages are often the exact words they type into Google.

Ask a few customers directly. You can say: "If you were looking for this on Google, what would you type?" Most people will answer happily, and their answers are more useful than any tool, because they come from real buyers.

Write down 20 to 30 phrases from this exercise. Do not worry yet about whether they are perfect. This is your seed list, and you will improve it with tools in the next steps.

## Step 2: Use Google Itself — the Free Tool You Already Have

Google is the best free keyword tool, because it shows you what people actually search for. You do not need to pay for anything to start.

**Google autocomplete.** Open Google and start typing a phrase from your seed list, such as "web designer in". Do not press Enter. Google will suggest completions, such as "web designer in Lagos", "web designer in Abuja", "web designer in Nigeria for small business". Those suggestions come from real searches by real people. Write them down. Try the same with other phrases: "best tailor in", "where to buy", "affordable hotel in", "how to start".

**People also ask.** Search for a phrase and press Enter. Look for the box titled "People also ask". It shows questions people ask about that topic, such as "How much does a website cost in Nigeria?" or "How do I find a good tailor in Lagos?" Each question is a keyword idea, and each one can become a blog post, a FAQ, or a section on your service page.

**Related searches.** Scroll to the bottom of the Google results page. You will see "Related searches" with eight more phrases. Those are also real searches. Add the useful ones to your list.

**Google Trends.** Go to trends.google.com and type a keyword, such as "POS business" or "human hair". Set the location to Nigeria. You will see whether interest is rising or falling, and which states search for it most. This helps you spot seasonal trends — for example, searches for "Christmas hamper" rise in November and December, while searches for "school uniform" rise in August and September.

Do this exercise for each of your main products. In one hour, you can build a list of 50 to 100 real keyword ideas, all from Google itself, without spending a naira.

## Step 3: Use Free Keyword Tools to Check Volume and Difficulty

Autocomplete tells you what people search for, but it does not tell you how many people search for it, or how hard it is to rank for it. Free tools can help with that.

**Google Keyword Planner.** This is Google's own free tool, inside Google Ads. You need a Google account to use it, but you do not need to run an advert. Open Google Ads, go to Tools, then Keyword Planner, then "Discover new keywords". Type a phrase such as "fashion designer Lagos", set the location to Nigeria, and Google will show you related keywords and a rough range of monthly searches, such as 100 to 1,000 searches per month. It will also show competition: low, medium or high.

**Ubersuggest and AnswerThePublic.** Ubersuggest has a free version that shows keyword ideas, search volume and difficulty for a few searches per day. AnswerThePublic shows questions people ask about a topic, in a visual map. Both are useful for finding long-tail keywords — the longer, more specific phrases that are easier to rank for.

**Google Search Console.** If your website is already live and connected to Google Search Console, open it and look at the Performance report. It shows the exact search terms people already used to find your website, how many times your site appeared, and how many clicks you got. This is gold. It tells you which keywords already work, and which ones you are close to ranking for but need a better page to win.

When you check a keyword, look at three things: search volume, intent and difficulty. Search volume tells you how many people search for it. Intent tells you whether they want to buy, learn or compare. Difficulty tells you how hard it will be to rank on the first page. As a small business, your best keywords are usually the ones with modest volume, clear buying intent, and low to medium difficulty. Avoid very broad keywords such as "shoes" or "hair" — they are too competitive, and the people searching for them may not want what you sell.

## Step 4: Add Location to Your Keywords

Location is one of the most powerful parts of keyword research for a Nigerian business. Most customers search with a place in mind, because they want a business near them.

A tailor in Lekki should target "tailor in Lekki", "fashion designer Lekki Phase 1", and "best tailor Chevron Drive", not just "tailor in Nigeria". A hotel in Ikate should target "hotel in Ikate Lekki" and "hotel near Chevron Lagos". A restaurant in Abuja should target "restaurant in Wuse 2" and "best restaurant Abuja".

Location keywords work because they match how people search, and because they have less competition. It is very hard for a small business to rank for "best hotel in Nigeria". It is much easier to rank for "affordable hotel in Lekki Phase 1", and the people who search for that are much more likely to book with you.

Make a list of location keywords for your business. Include:

- Your area: Lekki, Ikeja, Yaba, Surulere, Wuse, Garki, GRA Port Harcourt
- Your city: Lagos, Abuja, Port Harcourt, Ibadan, Enugu
- Landmarks near you: Chevron Drive, Admiralty Way, Allen Avenue, Wuse 2, Bodija
- "Near me" searches: Google handles these automatically if your Google Business Profile is set up, but you should still mention your area clearly on your website

Use these location keywords on your homepage, your contact page, and your Google Business Profile. Make sure your business name, address and phone number are the same everywhere online. That consistency helps Google trust that you are really in that location.

## Step 5: Study Your Competitors' Keywords

Your competitors have already done some of this work. You can learn from them without copying them.

Search on Google for your main keyword, such as "web designer Lagos" or "skincare brand Nigeria". Look at the first five websites that appear. Open them and read their pages. What words do they use in their titles? What words appear in their headings? What topics do they write about on their blog? Those are clues to the keywords they target.

## Step 6: Choose Your Best Keywords and Organise Them

By now you may have a list of 100 keywords or more. That is too many to use at once. You need to choose the best ones and organise them.

Sort your list into groups by topic. For example, a fashion business might have groups for "Ankara dresses", "wedding outfits", "tailoring services in Lekki", and "fabric sales". A hotel might have groups for "rooms", "event hall", "restaurant", and "location". Each group will become a page or a section on your website.

For each group, pick one main keyword and two or three supporting keywords. The main keyword is the one you want that page to rank for. For example, a page about your event hall might have the main keyword "event hall in Lekki", with supporting keywords "event hall Chevron", "party hall Lekki Phase 1", and "event centre Lagos".

Choose keywords you can realistically rank for. If you are a new website, start with longer, more specific keywords. "Affordable event hall in Chevron Lekki for 200 guests" is easier to rank for than "event hall Lagos", and the person searching for it knows exactly what they want.

Write your chosen keywords in a simple spreadsheet. Include the keyword, the page it belongs to, the search intent, and the monthly search volume if you know it. This sheet becomes your SEO plan, and you can add to it over time.

## Step 7: Use Keywords on Your Website the Right Way

Finding keywords is only half the job. You also need to use them correctly on your website. Using them well helps Google understand what your page is about. Stuffing them everywhere hurts you.

Use your main keyword in these places:

- The page title, which shows in Google search results. For example: "Event Hall in Lekki | Tonia Hall — De Phantom Continental Hotel". Keep titles under 60 characters where you can.
- The first heading on the page, known as the H1. It should be clear and natural, not a list of keywords.
- The first paragraph of the page. Mention the keyword naturally in the first 100 words.
- The web address, or slug, if you can. A page at /event-hall-lekki is clearer than /page-123.
- The meta description, which is the short text under your title in Google results. Write it like a short advert that makes people want to click.
- Image file names and alt text. Instead of "IMG_1234.jpg", name your photo "event-hall-lekki-tonia-hall.jpg" and write a short alt text describing it.

Use supporting keywords naturally in subheadings and in the body of the page. Do not repeat the same keyword 20 times on one page. Write for a human first, and let the keyword appear where it makes sense. If a sentence sounds strange because you forced a keyword into it, rewrite the sentence.

Create a separate page for each important service. Do not put all your services on one page and hope Google will rank it for everything. A tailor should have one page for "Ankara dresses", another for "wedding outfits", and another for "tailoring in Lekki". Each page can then rank for its own keywords.

Write helpful content that answers the searcher's question. If someone searches for "how much is a website in Nigeria", they want a clear answer with prices, not a sales pitch. A page that answers the question honestly is more likely to rank, and more likely to turn the visitor into a customer, than a page that hides the price.

Update your Google Business Profile with your keywords too. Use the right business category, write a clear description that mentions what you do and where you are, and post updates regularly. When customers leave reviews, reply to them and mention your service naturally. Google reads all of that.

## Common Keyword Mistakes Nigerian Businesses Make

Avoid these mistakes and you will be ahead of many of your competitors.

Do not target keywords that are too broad. "Shoes", "hair" and "food" are searched by millions of people, but most of them are not looking for your business. Target specific phrases instead.

Do not ignore Pidgin and local language. Some Nigerians search in Pidgin or in simple English, such as "where I fit buy cheap phone for Lagos" or "who sabi sew better Ankara for Lekki". You do not need to write your whole website in Pidgin, but you should be aware of how your customers actually speak and search.

Do not use American spelling and terms when Nigerians use different ones. Nigerians search for "mobile phone", "flat" and "petrol", not always the American equivalents. Check what your customers actually type, and use their words.

Do not set your keywords once and forget them. Search behaviour changes. New products appear, new areas develop, and new phrases become popular. Review your keywords every three months, check Google Search Console for new search terms, and update your pages.

## Keyword Checklist

- [ ] Write down what you sell, who you sell it to, and where you are located
- [ ] Collect 20 to 30 phrases from your customers' WhatsApp messages and questions
- [ ] Use Google autocomplete, People also ask and Related searches to expand your list
- [ ] Check search volume and difficulty with Google Keyword Planner and Google Search Console
- [ ] Add location keywords for your area, city and nearby landmarks
- [ ] Look at the top five competitors for your main keywords and note the gaps
- [ ] Organise keywords into groups, with one main keyword per page
- [ ] Use the main keyword in the title, first heading, first paragraph and meta description
- [ ] Create a separate page for each important service, and answer the searcher's question clearly
- [ ] Review your keywords and Search Console data every three months

Your customers are already telling Google what they want. Your job is to listen, find those exact words, and make sure your business is the one Google shows them. Start with your customers, use Google's free tools, choose keywords you can realistically win, and use them naturally on your website. Do that consistently, and the right customers will start finding you on Google, without you having to pay for every click.
