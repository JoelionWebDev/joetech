Konga is one of Nigeria's oldest and most trusted online marketplaces. Millions of Nigerians shop there for phones, laptops, fashion, home appliances, groceries, and baby items. For a small business owner in Lagos, Onitsha, Aba, or Kano, Konga offers something hard to build alone: a ready-made audience of buyers who already trust the platform with their money.

Selling on Konga is not free money, and it is not automatic. You need the right products, good photos, honest prices, fast fulfilment, and good customer service. But if you get those right, a Konga store can bring in steady orders alongside your shop and your WhatsApp sales. This guide walks you through the whole process, from registration to your first sale and beyond.

## Why Sell on Konga

The biggest advantage of Konga is traffic. Building your own online store means you must find every customer yourself through adverts and social media. On Konga, customers are already searching. A person in Abuja who searches for "wireless earphones" on Konga can find your listing without ever having heard of your business. That discovery is valuable, especially for new sellers.

Konga also handles trust. Many Nigerian shoppers are nervous about paying an unknown Instagram vendor and never receiving their goods. On Konga, payment is protected: the customer pays Konga, and Konga pays you after delivery is confirmed. This removes the biggest objection new customers have, and it lets you sell to people in cities where you have no reputation yet.

Another advantage is logistics. Konga has delivery infrastructure across the country, including its own logistics arm, Kxpress, and a network of pickup centres. You do not need to negotiate with individual riders or worry about how a parcel gets from Lagos to Enugu. You prepare the order, and Konga's system moves it.

There are costs, of course. Konga charges a commission on each sale, and the rate depends on your product category. You also need to manage returns and keep your store performance high. But for many sellers, the commission is a fair price for access to millions of buyers.

## What You Need Before You Register

Before you start the registration, gather your documents. Konga will ask for your business details and your identity. For an individual seller, you will need a valid government ID, such as a National Identity Number, international passport, driver's licence, or voter's card. You will also need your bank account details for payouts, and the account name should match the name you register with.

If you have a registered business, have your CAC certificate and business name ready. Registering as a business rather than an individual can build more trust with customers, and some product categories may require it. If you have not registered your business yet, you can still start as an individual seller and upgrade later.

You will also need a phone number and an email address that you check every day. Konga will send order notifications, payment confirmations, and performance warnings to these. Use your main business email, not one you rarely open, because a missed order notification can lead to a cancelled order and a penalty on your store.

Prepare your first products before you register. You need clear product photos, accurate descriptions, and your prices. A store with no products looks abandoned. Aim to have at least ten products ready to list on your first day, so customers who visit your store see a real shop, not an empty page.

## Step 1: Create Your Seller Account

Go to the Konga website and find the "Sell on Konga" link, usually at the bottom of the homepage or in the main menu. This takes you to the seller registration page. You will fill in your personal or business details: your name, your store name, your phone number, your email address, and your location.

Choose your store name carefully. It should be short, easy to remember, and related to what you sell. "Adaeze Fashion Store" is better than a long string of words and numbers. Your store name appears on every product page and in search results, so make it professional. Avoid names that copy a famous brand — Konga will reject them, and they can cause legal trouble.

After submitting the form, you will receive a confirmation email. Follow the link to verify your email address and set your password. Then complete your seller profile by adding your bank account details, your pickup address, and your ID. Konga will review your application, which usually takes a few working days. Check your email regularly for any request for additional documents.

## Step 2: Set Up Your Store

Once approved, log in to your seller dashboard, often called Seller HQ. This is where you manage everything: products, orders, prices, promotions, and performance. Spend time learning it before you list products. Click through the menus, read the help guides, and understand where orders appear and how payouts work.

Set your store policies clearly. Your return policy, your handling time, and your delivery information should be accurate. If you need one working day to prepare an order, say so. If you only ship on weekdays, say so. Customers judge you on whether you keep the promises your store makes.

Add a store banner and a logo if you can. A clean logo and a simple banner make your store look established. You can design both on your phone with a free design app. Keep the design simple: your store name, what you sell, and maybe a line like "Fast delivery across Nigeria". Avoid cluttered text and low-quality images.

## Step 3: List Your First Products

Product listing is where many new sellers fail, so take it seriously. Each product needs a clear title, good photos, an honest description, a price, and the correct category. A sloppy listing does not sell, no matter how good the product is.

Write titles the way customers search. "Men's Leather Sandals — Brown, Size 43" is better than "Nice Sandals for Men". Include the key details: brand, colour, size, and material. Avoid keyword stuffing — repeating the same words many times to game search — because Konga's system penalises it and customers find it annoying.

Photos sell the product. Take photos in bright daylight against a plain background. Show the product from the front, the side, and the back. Show details like zips, stitching, labels, and packaging. If you sell clothes, show them on a hanger or a model, not crumpled on a bed. If you sell electronics, show the box, the accessories, and the ports. Use your phone camera — modern phones take good enough photos — but clean the lens, use natural light, and avoid filters that change the colour of the product. A customer who receives a red dress that looked orange in the photo will return it.

Write honest descriptions. State the material, the size, what is included in the box, and any limitations. If a phone is refurbished, say so. If a shoe runs small, say so. Honest descriptions reduce returns, and returns cost you money and hurt your store rating.

Price your products carefully. Check what similar products sell for on Konga before you set your price. Factor in Konga's commission, the cost of the product, packaging, and transport to the Konga warehouse or pickup point. A price that is too high will not sell. A price that is too low will leave you with no profit after commission. Aim for a price that is competitive and still leaves you a healthy margin, usually at least 20 to 30 percent above your total cost.

## Step 4: Understand Commission and Payouts

Konga charges a commission on each completed sale, and the percentage varies by category. Electronics and phones usually attract a lower percentage than fashion or beauty products, but the exact rates change from time to time. Check the current commission table in your seller dashboard before you price your products, and build the commission into your price from the start.

When a customer pays, the money goes to Konga first. After the order is delivered and the return window passes, Konga releases your share to your bank account, minus the commission and any applicable fees. Payouts are usually processed on a regular cycle, such as weekly. Make sure your bank details are correct in the dashboard, because a wrong account number delays your money.

Keep your own records of every sale. Write down the order number, the product, the price, the commission, and the amount you received. At the end of each month, compare your records with Konga's payout statement. If something does not match, raise it with seller support quickly, with your order numbers ready. Good records also make your tax filing easier.

## Step 5: Fulfil Orders Fast and Correctly

Speed matters on Konga. When an order comes in, you have a limited time to prepare it and hand it over for delivery. Late fulfilment leads to cancelled orders, bad reviews, and a lower store rating, which reduces how often Konga shows your products in search.

Check your seller dashboard at least twice a day — morning and evening. When a new order appears, confirm it, pack it carefully, and hand it over as soon as possible. Use sturdy packaging. For fragile items, add bubble wrap or extra padding. Include a thank-you note or a small branded card inside the package. It costs almost nothing and makes the customer remember your store.

Double-check every order before you seal it. The wrong colour, the wrong size, or a missing accessory is the most common reason for returns and negative reviews. A thirty-second check saves you the cost of a return and the damage to your rating.

If a product goes out of stock, update your listing immediately. Selling a product you no longer have leads to cancelled orders, and repeated cancellations can get your store penalised. Keep a simple stock sheet so you always know what you have.

## Step 6: Handle Returns and Customer Questions

Returns are part of selling online. A customer may receive a product that does not fit, does not match the photo, or arrives damaged. Konga has a return policy, and you must follow it. When a return request comes in, respond quickly and politely. Do not argue with the customer in the message thread. Accept the return if it falls within the policy, and learn from it.

Read the reason for each return. If three customers return the same shoe because it runs small, your size guide is wrong, and you should fix the listing. If a product arrives damaged repeatedly, your packaging needs improvement. Returns are feedback, not just losses.

Answer customer questions fast. A buyer who asks about a product's size or availability is close to buying. A reply within an hour can win the sale. A reply the next day often loses it. Use the Konga messaging system for all communication with buyers, because it keeps a record that protects both you and the customer if there is a dispute.

## Common Mistakes New Konga Sellers Make

The first mistake is copying another seller's photos and descriptions. It feels like a shortcut, but customers notice when the same photo appears on five stores, and Konga's system can flag duplicate content. Worse, the product you receive from your supplier may look different from the copied photo, which leads to returns and complaints. Take your own photos, even with a phone. Your own photos are honest, and honesty sells.

The second mistake is pricing without doing the maths. A new seller sees a product selling for ₦25,000 on Konga and lists theirs at ₦22,000 to undercut the competition, without accounting for commission, packaging, and transport. After Konga takes its share, the seller discovers the order lost money. Always calculate your full cost — product cost, commission, packaging, and delivery to the pickup point — before you set a price. A slightly higher price with a real profit is better than a low price that loses money on every order.

The third mistake is ignoring the seller dashboard. Orders, customer questions, and performance warnings all appear there. A seller who checks the dashboard once a week will miss orders, reply late to buyers, and only discover a problem when the store rating has already dropped. Check it twice a day. It takes five minutes, and it is the difference between a store that grows and a store that stalls.

The fourth mistake is listing products you do not have in stock. Some sellers list everything their supplier might have, hoping to buy it after an order comes in. When the supplier is out of stock, the order is cancelled, the customer is disappointed, and Konga records a cancellation against your store. List only what you physically hold or can reliably get within a day. Update your stock levels every time you sell or restock.

The fifth mistake is treating Konga like a notice board rather than a shop. A store with no reviews, no banner, slow replies, and late deliveries will not grow, no matter how good the products are. Konga rewards sellers who behave like real shopkeepers: present, responsive, and reliable. Put in the daily effort, and the platform will put your products in front of more buyers.

## Growing Your Konga Store

Once your store is running, focus on growth. List new products regularly — Konga's search system favours active stores. Aim to add a few new products every week, even if they are variations of what already sells, like a new colour or size.

Run promotions during Konga's big sale events, such as Black Friday, the Christmas sale, and the mid-year sale. These events bring a flood of buyers to the platform, and sellers who offer genuine discounts get a large share of the traffic. Plan your stock and your prices for these events in advance, and make sure you can handle a sudden increase in orders.

Watch your store performance metrics in the dashboard: your order defect rate, your cancellation rate, your late shipment rate, and your customer reviews. Konga rewards sellers with strong metrics by showing their products more often in search. A store with a 4.5-star rating and fast delivery will outsell a similar store with a 3.5-star rating, even at a slightly higher price.

Ask satisfied customers to leave a review on your Konga store. A polite message after delivery — "Thank you for your order. If you are happy with the product, a review on our store would mean a lot" — can steadily build your rating. Reviews on Konga work like reviews on Google: they build trust with the next buyer.

Finally, use Konga alongside your other channels, not instead of them. Your WhatsApp customers, your Instagram followers, and your walk-in shop customers are all part of the same business. You can even mention your Konga store to WhatsApp customers who prefer to pay through a marketplace. Each channel feeds the others, and together they make your business harder to ignore.

Selling on Konga takes work, but the steps are straightforward: register properly, list honestly, price carefully, deliver fast, and treat every customer well. Start with ten good products, learn the dashboard, and improve one thing each week. In a few months, you will have a store that brings in orders while you sleep.
