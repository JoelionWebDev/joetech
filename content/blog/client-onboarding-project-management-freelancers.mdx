Most freelancers don't lose clients to price. They lose them to chaos.

A client who pays you ₦150,000 for a project is not your biggest risk — the client who pays you ₦150,000 and then drains three weeks of your life with "small changes" is. The difference between these two outcomes is not talent. It's **onboarding**.

Good onboarding does four things at once: it protects your time, it raises perceived value, it kills ambiguity, and it turns one project into a retainer. Here's the complete playbook — including the free AI shortcuts that automate the admin.

> The project is won in the first two weeks. Onboarding is the difference between a one-time job and a five-year client.

---

## Step 1: The discovery call — charge with questions, not a pitch

Your first call is not a sales call. It's a **diagnosis**. The client, not you, should do 70% of the talking. Your script:

1. **Goals first** — "In a perfect world, what does this project help your business do 6 months from now?" (Register the outcome, not the features.)
2. **Constraints** — "What must stay the same and what's non-negotiable?" (Budget, deadline, old design, hosting, internal team, reference photos).
3. **The bill** — "What happens today if nothing changes?" (Pain is priced; features aren't.)
4. **The decision** — "Who else is part of this decision?" (If she has to "ask her partner", you're selling to the wrong person.)

**AI shortcut:** Record (with consent) and ask ChatGPT to summarise into "Goals, Constraints, Risks, Next" before you write the proposal. It will catch a risk you forgot.

> Rule: if the discovery call ends and you don't know their 3 biggest priorities, run a second call. Guessing is how scope creep begins.

---

## Step 2: The proposal — one page, done in 30 minutes with AI

A proposal is not a document; it's a **decision tool**. Keep it to one page with five blocks:

1. Your understanding (2 lines: their business + the goal).
2. What I'll deliver (numbered, concrete — "Home page, services page, WhatsApp sales chat integration").
3. What I won't (a "not included" box kills scope creep before it starts).
4. Timeline (start date, milestone dates, end date).
5. Price & terms (fixed price, 50% deposit, payment methods, revision allowance, late fee).

Draft it with AI:

> "Write a one-page proposal for a [project type] for [business type] in Nigeria. Use the structure: understanding, deliverables, not included, timeline, pricing (₦[x], 50% deposit), terms. Keep language warm but professional. Highlight the outcome: [goal]. Max 500 words."

Then check — because AI will occasionally put "video game assets" in a bakery proposal. Every line must be true to what the client actually said.

---

## Step 3: The contract — one email that saves your whole year

Before any work starts, get a signed agreement with three non-negotiables **in writing**:

- **Deposit** — 50% upfront before kickoff (bank transfer, payment link, or transfer to your account).
- **Revisions** — "2 rounds of revisions included; additional rounds billed at ₦[rate]/hour" — the number that kills endless "small changes."
- **Deliverable & deadline** — exactly what you will deliver and when, so "I expected more pages" can't happen later.

AI can draft the contract language, but you MUST read it. Put the three clauses in a simple Google Doc, share it as a link, and ask: "If the dates or amounts work, please reply 'agreed' and confirm with a transfer. We'll start the day the deposit drops."

> **The deposit is not the payment for the work. It is the payment for your time being yours.** Without it, the project has already started to cost you.

---

## Step 4: The kickoff — the week that makes you look senior

How you behave in the first 7 days sets the tone for the entire project. Do these three things:

1. **Welcome note + project summary** (day 0) — a short message: "We're starting. Here's what we'll build, what you'll receive each week, and how we'll work together." This alone makes you look twice your rate.
2. **Send a simple checklist** — what they need to provide (photos, logo, passwords, content) in one list with deadlines. Failure here is the #1 cause of delays.
3. **Set a regular check-in** — one short status update per week (WhatsApp, email, or shared doc). You drive it; the client never has to chase you.

**AI shortcut:** Have ChatGPT draft your kickoff line from the proposal, then paste it. You sound organised without the admin headache. The AI becomes "the copywriter in the office" you didn't pay a salary for.

## Step 5: Run the project on a dashboard everyone can see

Do not run a project between your brain and 14 WhatsApp messages. Put it somewhere visible — free options: Notion, Trello, or Google Sheets.

**Use this structure (columns):**
- **To do / In progress / In review / Done**

And 2 more essential rows:
- **Waiting on client** — any task blocked on them (uploads, login, feedback). Marking this column is your defense when deadlines slip: it's not laziness, it's a waiting room.
- **Scope guard** — "as of this week: original + [approved changes]." When the client asks for "one more section," sweep it here.

**Review every Monday:** move cards, flag blockers, send the week's status line. It costs you 25 minutes but saves you a client.

---

## Step 6: Deliver with a handover, not a "done"

When the project finishes, hand over like a professional:

1. **Demo call** — 20 minutes, walk them through what you built and why it helps their business. Answers land better when they're watching.
2. **Wrap email** — "Here's what was delivered, here's how to access/edit it, here's what we'd do next." Include the invoice and the next step (review period).
3. **Soft upsell** — "If you want to turn this into a monthly [care plan/maintenance], I have spots." Most first projects grow into retainers here.

4. **The testimonial ask** — right after the demo call, not a week later: "Could I have 3 lines I can show to businesses like yours?"

> **Your best salesperson is a delighted, well-managed client.** Onboarding done right converts a paid task into a referral engine.

## The 6 red flags to wallpaper during onboarding

1. **Client says "just a small change"** — define it now, in writing, or pay with your evenings.
2. **You don't have the deposit after 7 days** — the project's not real yet; don't build for "maybe."
3. **No decision-maker on the call** — the "wife that has to see it" is a delay promise.
4. **Vague goal** ("make it look nice") — pin down: what do they want more of — enquiries, bookings, sales?
5. **Unlimited revisions promised** — you're signing up for a year of surprises. Fix the cap.
6. **No check-in cadence** — two weeks of silence, then fire drills. Set the cadence first.

## The 3 signatures of a well-onboarded project

If you're doing this right, you should end a project with:
1. **A redesign-free deliverable** — the client got what they approved, not what "they always wanted."
2. **Appreciation in writing** — a testimonial, a referral, a future-looking retainer.
3. **A little margin in your week** — because scope and dates were agreed, not guessed.

Build the system once and it repays you with every single client after.

## FAQ

| Question | Short answer |
| -------- | ------------ |
| **What if my client doesn't want a contract?** | Start polite but firm: "It's a simple one-page summary so we both win." No contract + no deposit = you are the bank. |
| **When exactly should I cap revisions?** | In the proposal, before a page of the work is done. "2 revision rounds included; additional at ₦[rate]/hour." Never after. |
| **Do free tools really replace project management software?** | For one-to-few-client freelancers, yes — Notion/Trello + calendar + a shared doc is 80% of the value of the fanciest tool. |
| **My client keeps asking for little extras. What do I do?** | Cheerful handling: "Great idea — let's add it to the scope guard; we can plan it after the current scope lands." |
| **How do Nigerians normally pay deposits?** | By bank transfer, POS, or payment links like Paystack/Flutterwave. Send an invoice with your account details; work starts after the deposit lands. |
| **How does AI know it's not going to "sound like a machine"?** | Keep your brief: names, approvals, and your own spoken phrases go in; AI only drafts the bones, you fine-tune. |
