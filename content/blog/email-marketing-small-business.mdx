Every few years, someone declares email marketing dead. Social media killed it. AI killed it. Gen Z killed it.

And every year, email continues to deliver the highest return on investment of any marketing channel. For every $1 spent on email marketing, the average return is $36.

Email is not dead. Bad email marketing is dead. And there is a difference.

## Why Email Beats Social Media Every Time

When you post on social media, you are renting attention. The algorithm decides who sees your content. If the platform changes its rules, your reach disappears overnight.

Email is different. You own your list. When you send an email, it lands in the inbox of everyone who chose to hear from you. No algorithm. No bidding war. No shadowbanning.

That alone makes email the most reliable channel for a small business.

## How Small Businesses Are Using Email Right Now

### The Welcome Sequence

The moment someone subscribes, they get a series of automated emails over the first week. Not just one "thanks for signing up" email, but a sequence that builds trust and introduces what you offer.

A good welcome sequence might include:

- Email 1: Welcome and what to expect from your emails
- Email 2: Your best content or most popular offer
- Email 3: A case study or real customer result
- Email 4: A direct invitation to book a call or make a purchase

Businesses using a welcome sequence see up to 4x more engagement than those sending a single welcome email.

### The Value-First Newsletter

Instead of sending only promotional emails ("Buy this! Sale ends soon!"), smart businesses send a regular newsletter that actually helps the reader. Tips, insights, curated links, or industry news.

The promotional email works because the value-first newsletter has built trust. If every email asks for a sale, subscribers tune out. If 80% of emails deliver value and 20% ask for the sale, subscribers stay engaged.

### The Abandoned Cart or Missed Opportunity Follow-Up

If someone visited your service page, downloaded a guide, or started filling out a form but did not complete it, an automated follow-up email can recover that lead.

### The Re-Engagement Campaign

Subscribers go cold. It is normal. But instead of removing them immediately, a re-engagement email gives them one last chance: "We have not heard from you in a while. Still interested in hearing from us?"

This cleans your list, improves deliverability, and sometimes brings back a customer who had simply forgotten about you.

## What Small Businesses Get Wrong

### Buying Email Lists

This is the fastest way to get marked as spam, damage your sender reputation, and ensure your legitimate emails never reach an inbox. Never buy a list. Every person on your list should have explicitly chosen to hear from you.

### Sending Too Often (or Too Rarely)

Once a week is a good rhythm for most small businesses. More than that and you risk fatigue. Less than once a month and people forget they subscribed to you.

### Writing Only About Yourself

If every email starts with "We did X," "We launched Y," "We are excited to announce Z," subscribers lose interest. Talk about your customer's problems and interests, not your company's announcements.
