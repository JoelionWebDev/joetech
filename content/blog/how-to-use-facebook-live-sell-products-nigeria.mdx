Every evening, thousands of Nigerians open Facebook and join a live video where a seller holds up clothes, shoes, wigs, bags, or food items, calls out the price, and takes orders right there in the comments. Viewers type "Mine" or "I want the blue one", and within minutes the seller has sold ten, twenty, or thirty items without paying for a shop, a market stall, or a single advert. That is Facebook Live selling, and it is one of the cheapest ways to sell in Nigeria today.

All you need is a phone, a Facebook account, your products, and a steady voice. You do not need a website, a big following, or expensive equipment. What you need is a plan, a clear way of presenting your products, and a system for taking orders and money that does not fall apart when twenty people comment at the same time.

## Why Facebook Live Works for Nigerian Sellers

Facebook Live works because it combines three things that sell products: attention, trust, and urgency. When you go live, Facebook sends a notification to your followers and pushes your video to the top of their feeds. People who would never have seen a photo post will tap in just to see what is happening. That free reach is something adverts charge you for.

Live video also builds trust faster than any photo. A customer in Ibadan who has been cheated before by an online seller wants proof that your product is real and that you are a real person. When she sees you holding the dress, turning it around, showing the zip, and answering her question in real time, her fear drops. She can hear your voice, see your face, and watch you pack an order.

Urgency is the third advantage. When you say "I have only three pieces of this shoe left", people act. A photo post sits there all day and the buyer can decide tomorrow. A live sale ends in an hour, and the buyer knows the item might be gone by then. That pressure, used honestly, turns viewers into buyers.

## What You Need Before Your First Live Sale

Start with a phone that has a working camera and enough storage. Most Android phones and iPhones from the last few years are fine. Clean the camera lens before you start, because a smudged lens makes your products look dull and cheap. Keep a power bank or a charger plugged in, because streaming drains a battery fast, and nothing kills a sale like a phone that dies halfway through.

Data is the next thing to sort out. A live video eats data, so do not go live on a weak network or a nearly empty data bundle. If you have Wi-Fi at home or in your shop, use it. If you are using mobile data, buy a bundle that can cover at least an hour of streaming, and test your connection in the exact spot where you will sit. A live video that freezes every minute will empty your audience in seconds.

Lighting matters more than most new sellers think. Sit facing a window or a bright light so the light falls on you and your products, not behind you. A simple ring light, which costs a few thousand naira in Computer Village or on Jumia, makes a visible difference. Keep your background clean and simple: a plain wall, a tidy table, or a neat rail of clothes is enough. Customers judge your business by what they see behind you.

Prepare your products before you go live. Arrange them in the order you plan to show them, and put a small paper tag on each one with its code, price, and sizes. For example, write "A1 — Ankara Dress — ₦18,500 — Sizes 10, 12, 14" on a card and keep it next to the dress. That way you never pause to remember a price on camera, and you never quote the wrong price.

## How to Plan and Announce Your Live Sale

A live sale that nobody knows about is just you talking to an empty room. Plan your sale at least a day ahead and announce it properly. Choose a time when your customers are free. For most Nigerian sellers, evenings work best, roughly between 7 pm and 9 pm, when people are home from work and scrolling their phones. Weekends are also strong, especially Saturday afternoons.

Decide what you will sell and how many items you will show. Thirty to forty items is a good number for a one-hour live. Fewer than fifteen and the session feels empty; more than fifty and you will rush and make mistakes.

Write a short plan for the session. List the items in order, with the code, price, and one selling point for each. For example: "A3 — Leather slippers — ₦12,000 — soft sole, does not slip on tiles." That one line reminds you what to say when you hold the item up. Without a plan, sellers freeze on camera, repeat themselves, or forget to mention the price, and viewers drift away.

Announce the sale a day before and again a few hours before. Post on your Facebook Page, your personal profile, and your WhatsApp status. Say the date, the time, and a hint of what is coming: "Live sale tomorrow, 7 pm. New Ankara dresses from ₦15,000, plus shoes and bags." Add a photo of two or three of the best items to catch attention, and ask your regular customers to share the post.

## How to Set Up Your Phone and Space

Prop your phone up at eye level, about an arm's length away. A phone tripod costs little and keeps the video steady. If you do not have one, lean the phone against a stack of books or a sturdy box. Never hold the phone in your hand for a full hour. Your arm will shake, the angle will change, and viewers will get dizzy.

Frame yourself from the chest up, with enough space in front of you to hold up a product. Test the frame before you go live. Hold up your biggest item and check that it fits in the shot. If you sell small items like earrings or phone cases, hold them close to the lens so viewers can see the detail.

Sound matters as much as the picture. Go live in a quiet room. Turn off the television, close the windows if the street is noisy, and ask people in the house not to shout or play music during the session. Viewers will leave if they cannot hear your prices. Speak clearly and a little louder than normal, and repeat the price and the code for each item at least twice, because new viewers join throughout the session.

Check your internet connection in the exact spot where you will sit. Run a short test live video set to "Only me" so you can watch it back and check the picture and sound without an audience. If the video buffers or the sound breaks, move closer to the router or switch to a better network.

## How to Present Products So People Buy

The rule is simple: show the product, name the price, give one reason to buy, and tell people how to order. Do that for every item, in the same order, so viewers always know what is coming. Start by holding the item up clearly and naming it: "This is item B2, a chiffon blouse in wine colour." Then show the details: the fabric, the buttons, the lining, the zip, the length. Turn it around and hold it against yourself so viewers can judge the size.

Give the price early, not at the end. Many sellers talk about a product for two minutes before mentioning the price, and viewers who cannot afford it feel their time was wasted. Say the price in the first few seconds: "This dress is ₦18,500, sizes 10 to 16." Then give one or two selling points: the fabric does not fade, the shoe is true to size, the bag has a strong zip. Do not list ten features. One or two strong ones are enough.

Create honest urgency. If you have only two pieces left, say so. If the price is a special live-only price that goes up tomorrow, say so. But never lie about stock or prices. Viewers talk to each other in the comments, and a seller who claims "only one left" for the same item every week loses trust fast. Honest urgency sells; fake urgency destroys your reputation.

Answer questions as they come in. When someone asks "Do you have this in size 14?", check and answer on the spot. When someone asks "How much is delivery to Abuja?", give the figure. Answering questions live is the biggest advantage you have over a photo post, so use it. End each item with a clear instruction: "Type SOLD B2 and send me a message to confirm."

## How to Take Orders and Collect Payment

Order taking is where many live sales fall apart. The comments move fast, two people claim the same item, and by the end the seller cannot remember who bought what. Use a code for every item and insist that buyers use it. When you hold up a dress, say "Type SOLD A1". The first person to type the code gets the item. Announce the winner by name on camera: "SOLD A1 goes to Adaeze, thank you." That public confirmation stops arguments, because everyone saw who claimed it first.

Keep a notebook beside you and write down every sale as it happens: the buyer's name, the item code, and the price. If you have a helper, let the helper write while you present. A helper who watches the comments and reads out the names frees you to focus on selling, and it makes the session feel more professional. Many successful live sellers in Lagos work in pairs for exactly this reason.

At the end of the live, send a message to each buyer within an hour, confirming the order, the total price, the delivery fee, and your account details. A buyer who waits until the next day to hear from you may change her mind or spend the money on something else. Set a clear payment rule and say it often during the live: "Payment confirms your order. First to pay gets the item." Give buyers a deadline, such as payment within two hours, and be firm about it.

Offer payment by bank transfer to an account in your business name, and say the account name clearly during the live so buyers can confirm it. Many buyers also prefer a payment link from Paystack or Flutterwave, which lets them pay with a card or a transfer without typing your account number. Confirm every payment before you pack the order: ask for a screenshot, then check your bank app or payment dashboard to confirm the money actually arrived, because screenshots can be faked.

## How to Handle Delivery and Grow Your Audience

Be clear about delivery fees before the buyer pays. State the fee for Lagos, Abuja, Port Harcourt, and other states during the live, or at least the fee for Lagos and a note that other states cost more. A buyer who discovers a ₦5,000 delivery fee after paying ₦12,000 for a dress will feel cheated. If you offer free delivery within your area for orders above a certain amount, say so, because it encourages bigger orders.

For Lagos deliveries, a dispatch rider is usually the fastest option. Build a relationship with one or two reliable riders who know your area, and agree on their rates in advance. For deliveries outside Lagos, use a courier company with tracking, and share the tracking number with the buyer as soon as the parcel is sent. A buyer who can track a parcel from Lekki to Enugu is calm; a buyer with no information will message you every hour.

Pack orders neatly and label each parcel with the buyer's name and phone number. A small thank-you note inside the parcel costs almost nothing and makes customers feel valued. If something goes wrong with a delivery, tell the buyer before they ask. A short message saying "Your parcel is delayed by a day, it will arrive tomorrow" keeps trust. Silence makes the buyer assume the worst.

## Common Mistakes New Facebook Live Sellers Make

The first mistake is going live without a plan. A seller who holds up random items, forgets prices, and pauses for long silences will lose viewers in minutes. Prepare your items, your prices, and your order before you press the button. The second mistake is poor lighting and sound. Viewers cannot buy what they cannot see clearly, and they will not stay if they cannot hear you. Test your setup with a short private live before every session.

The third mistake is hiding the price or saying "DM for price" on a live sale. The whole point of live selling is speed. A viewer who has to message you for the price will often move on to a seller whose price is on the screen. Say the price out loud for every item, every time.

The fourth mistake is taking orders without a system. When two people claim the same item and you cannot remember who was first, you get arguments in the comments and a damaged reputation. Use item codes, announce the winner by name, and write every sale down as it happens. The fifth mistake is ignoring buyers after the live ends. A sale is not finished when the live ends. It is finished when the buyer has paid, the parcel has arrived, and the buyer is happy. Reply fast, pack fast, and deliver on time.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need a Facebook Page to sell on Facebook Live?

No, you can go live from your personal profile, but a Facebook Page is better for selling. A Page lets you build a business audience, run adverts later, and keep your personal life separate from your shop. Start with whichever account your customers already follow, and move to a Page as you grow.

### How long should a Facebook Live sale last?

Between 45 minutes and 90 minutes works well for most sellers. Shorter than 30 minutes and you will not show enough products. Longer than two hours and viewers get tired and leave. Start with one hour, watch when your viewers drop off in the replay, and adjust from there.

### What should I do if my network fails during a live sale?

Stay calm and go live again as soon as you can. Post a short message saying the network dropped and you are back. Most viewers will rejoin if you return within a few minutes. To reduce the risk, always test your connection before you start, use Wi-Fi where possible, and keep a second data source ready, such as a hotspot from another phone.

### How do I handle buyers who claim items but never pay?

Set a clear payment deadline, such as two hours, and say it during the live. If the buyer does not pay in time, release the item to the next person who wanted it. Keep a private note of repeat offenders and stop holding items for them. A firm payment rule protects your time and your stock.

### Can I sell on Facebook Live if I have a small following?

Yes. A small, loyal audience can buy more than a large, uninterested one. Ten viewers who trust you and buy every week are worth more than a thousand viewers who never order. Start with the audience you have, ask them to share, and grow steadily.

## What to Do Next

1. Choose your live sale days and time, and write them down where your customers can see them.
2. Pick thirty items for your first session, and write a code, price, and one selling point for each.
3. Set up your space with good light, a clean background, and your phone on a tripod at eye level.
4. Announce the sale a day before on Facebook and WhatsApp, with a photo of your best items.
5. Do a short private test live to check your picture, sound, and internet connection.
6. Go live, present each item with its price and code, and write down every sale as it happens.
7. Message each buyer within an hour to confirm the order, the total, the delivery fee, and your payment details.
8. Pack and dispatch orders the next day, share tracking details, and ask happy buyers to come back for your next live.
