Every day, millions of Nigerians open Instagram and tap through Stories before they ever scroll their feed. They check what their friends are doing, what a favourite vendor has in stock, and what is new in Lagos, Abuja or Port Harcourt. For a small business, that habit is an opportunity. Stories sit at the top of the app, they feel personal and immediate, and they disappear in 24 hours, which gives people a reason to look now rather than later. A well-used Story can do what a static post often cannot: start a conversation, answer a question, and move a customer from interest to payment in the same afternoon.

The mistake many Nigerian sellers make is treating Stories as an afterthought. They post a product photo once, with no price, no clear next step, and no reason to reply, and then wonder why nothing happens. Selling on Stories is not about posting more often for its own sake. It is about showing up every day with something useful, making it easy for a viewer to buy, and building the trust that turns a stranger into a customer. This guide explains how to do that, step by step, with ideas that work for fashion vendors, food sellers, skincare brands, phone accessories shops, bakers, hair vendors and service businesses across Nigeria.

## Start With a Profile That Turns Story Viewers Into Buyers

Before your Stories can sell, your profile has to do its part. When someone watches your Story and likes what they see, the first thing they do is tap your profile picture to check who you are. If your bio is unclear, your profile picture is blurry, or there is no way to contact you, they will leave. Make your bio simple and specific: say what you sell, who it is for, where you deliver to, and how to order. A fashion vendor in Lekki might write "Ready-to-wear dresses for women in Lagos. Nationwide delivery. Order on WhatsApp." That tells a visitor everything they need to know in one glance.

Your contact options matter just as much as your words. Add a WhatsApp link or button so a customer can message you in one tap, and make sure the number is a business WhatsApp number that you actually answer during the day. Put your location in your bio if you serve a particular area, such as Lekki, Ikeja, Surulere, Abuja or Enugu, because many Nigerian customers prefer to buy from a seller near them, or at least to know where the goods are coming from. Use a clear profile picture, ideally your logo or a clean photo of your best product, and keep your username simple so people can find you and recommend you by name. Your Story Highlights, which are covered later in this guide, should also be set up before you start selling seriously, because they are where new visitors go to check your prices, your reviews and your delivery information.

Think of your profile as your shop front and your Stories as the friendly shop attendant standing outside it. The attendant can call people in, show them what is new, and answer their questions, but if the shop itself looks closed or confusing, people will walk past. Spend one afternoon getting the profile right, and every Story you post after that will work harder. Check it from a stranger's point of view: if you found this page for the first time, would you know what is for sale, how much it costs, and how to order it within ten seconds? If the answer is no, fix that first.

## Post Every Day, and Mix Selling With Real Life

Stories work best when they become a daily habit, not a once-a-week announcement. The Instagram algorithm shows your Stories to the people who watch them most often, so consistency builds an audience that expects to hear from you. You do not need to post twenty Stories a day. Five to eight useful Stories a day is enough for most small businesses, and even three good ones every day will beat a big burst once a week. The goal is to stay visible at the top of your followers' screens without becoming noise they learn to skip.

The secret is variety. If every Story is a product photo with a price, people will stop watching, because it feels like an advert running on repeat. Mix your selling Stories with the human side of your business. Show the morning market run in Mile 12 or Balogun, the fabric you just bought, the cake coming out of the oven, the parcel being packed for a customer in Ibadan, or a quick behind-the-scenes look at your workspace. Show yourself, or your team, doing the work. Nigerian customers buy from people they trust, and trust grows when they can see that you are real, that your products exist, and that other people are buying from you.

Plan your day in simple blocks. Morning can be a greeting and a look at what is new or restocked. Midday can be a product showcase or a customer question answered on camera. Evening can be an order reminder or a thank-you to customers who bought that day. You do not need expensive equipment. A clean phone camera, good daylight near a window, and a tidy background are enough. Keep your Story text short and readable, and avoid covering the whole screen with words.

## Show the Product Properly, With the Price and the Next Step

The most common reason Stories do not sell in Nigeria is simple: the seller shows a beautiful product but leaves out the price and how to order. Customers do not want to send a direct message just to ask for a price, and many will not bother. They will move on to another vendor who states the price clearly. Put the price on the Story itself, in large, readable text, and add the sizes, colours or flavours that are available. If you sell dresses, say the sizes in stock. If you sell food, say the portion size and whether delivery is included. If you sell skincare, say the size of the bottle and how long it lasts.

Show the product in use, not just on a plain background. A dress looks better on a person than on a hanger. A plate of food looks better being served than sitting in a container. A wig looks better worn than lying flat. Take short videos as well as photos: a five-second clip of a bag opening and closing, a cream being rubbed into skin, or a shoe being tried on tells a customer far more than a still image. Use natural light where you can, keep the background uncluttered, and wipe your camera lens before filming. These small habits make a big difference on a small phone screen.

Every selling Story needs one clear next step. Tell the viewer exactly what to do: "Tap the link to order on WhatsApp", "Send a DM with the word DRESS", "Screenshot this and send it to us", or "Tap the poll if you want this in blue". Do not give three different instructions at once. One action per Story is enough. If you use the link sticker, check that it opens the right place, ideally your WhatsApp chat or your order page, and test it yourself before you post. A broken link is a lost sale. And when someone does message you, reply quickly. In Nigeria, the vendor who replies first often wins the sale, because the customer is usually talking to two or three sellers at the same time.

## Use Polls, Questions and Quizzes to Start Conversations

Instagram gives you stickers that turn a one-way advert into a conversation, and that conversation is where sales begin. Polls, questions, quizzes and emoji sliders are not toys. They are tools that tell Instagram your followers are interested in you, which means Instagram will show your Stories to more of them, and they give you direct information about what your customers want. A poll asking "Pink or nude for this weekend?" does two things at once: it entertains your audience, and it tells you which colour to stock more of.

Use the question sticker to invite the questions your customers already ask you in private. "Ask me anything about this hair" or "What do you want to know about delivery to Abuja?" will bring in the exact objections that stop people from buying, and your answers become content that sells for you. Answer those questions on camera or in text on the next Story, and tag the question so the person who asked feels seen. Use the quiz sticker for fun product knowledge, such as "How long does this cream last?" or "Which size fits a UK 12?" Customers who play along are already engaged with your product, and they are far more likely to buy than someone who simply scrolled past.

You can also use stickers to test new products before you spend money on stock. Post two styles and ask your audience to vote for their favourite. The answers help you avoid buying stock nobody wants, and the people who voted will feel involved when the winning product arrives. Always follow up on the result. If you asked a question, share the answer. If you ran a poll, announce the winner.

## Sell With Urgency, Offers and Social Proof — Honestly

Stories disappear in 24 hours, and that natural urgency is one of their biggest strengths. Use it honestly. When you have only three pieces left in a size, say so. When a price goes back up on Monday, say so on Sunday. When you are closing orders for the weekend, give a clear cut-off time. A countdown sticker for a restock, a sale, or a delivery day builds anticipation and gives people a reason to come back and check. Urgency works because it is real, and it stops working the moment customers realise it is not. Never claim a product is almost sold out when your shelf is full, and never invent a discount that was never real. Nigerian customers talk, and a vendor who cries wolf loses trust quickly.

Limited offers work well in Stories because they suit the format. A weekend discount, a free delivery day for Lagos orders, a buy-two-get-one offer, or a small gift for the first five orders are all easy to announce in a Story and easy to track. Keep the terms simple and put them on the Story itself: what the offer is, who qualifies, and when it ends. If you run a flash sale, say exactly when it starts and ends, and honour it. If you promise free delivery within Lekki, do not add a hidden charge at checkout. Small businesses in Nigeria win repeat customers through honesty more than through any single promotion.

Social proof is just as powerful as a discount. When a customer sends you a thank-you message, a photo of themselves wearing your product, or a screenshot of a successful delivery, ask their permission and share it on your Story. Real reviews from real customers, with names and locations where the customer agrees, do more to convince a nervous first-time buyer than any claim you make about your own products. Share delivery confirmations, parcels being handed to dispatch riders, and happy messages from customers in different cities. This shows new viewers that you deliver, that your products arrive as described, and that other Nigerians already trust you. Always blur or remove any private information, such as a customer's full address or phone number, before sharing a screenshot.

## Turn Watchers Into WhatsApp Conversations and Orders

Instagram is where many Nigerian customers discover a product, but WhatsApp is where many of them prefer to complete the purchase. Your job is to make that journey as short and smooth as possible. The link sticker is your best friend here: it can take a viewer straight to your WhatsApp chat with a pre-written message such as "Hello, I saw this dress on your Story and I want to order." That pre-written message saves the customer from having to think about what to say, and it tells you exactly which product they are asking about. Check the link regularly, because a link that opens the wrong chat or a dead page will quietly lose you sales for weeks.

Not every customer will tap a link. Some will reply directly to your Story, and you should treat every reply as a hot lead. Turn on notifications for Story replies, and answer them fast, politely and completely. If someone asks for a price, give the price, the delivery fee, and the delivery time in the same message, so they do not have to ask three more questions. If someone says "I will get back to you", thank them and ask if you can send a reminder later, then do so once, politely, the next day. Many Nigerian customers need a gentle follow-up before they buy, and a single friendly reminder is helpful, not pushy.

Keep your WhatsApp business profile ready for the traffic your Stories send. Use quick replies for common questions about prices, delivery and payment, and make your payment details easy to copy as plain text. When a customer pays, confirm it quickly and share the delivery plan. A customer who receives their order on time and in good condition is the customer who will watch your Stories tomorrow and recommend you to their friends.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How many Instagram Stories should a small business post each day in Nigeria?**

Five to eight useful Stories a day is a good target for most small businesses. Fewer than three a day makes you easy to forget, and more than fifteen a day usually means people start skipping. Quality matters more than quantity, so post regularly, mix selling with behind-the-scenes content, and watch which Stories get the most replies and taps.

**Do I need a large number of followers before Stories can sell?**

No. Stories are shown first to your most engaged followers, so a small audience that watches and replies every day is far more valuable than a large audience that ignores you. Focus on the people who already follow you, answer their questions, and ask them to share your Stories with friends who might need your product.

**Should I put prices on my Instagram Stories?**

Yes, in most cases. Stating the price on the Story saves you and the customer time, and it stops you losing buyers who will not send a message just to ask. If your price varies by size, location or custom work, give the starting price on the Story and explain what affects the final price.

**What is the best time to post Instagram Stories in Nigeria?**

Early morning before work, lunchtime, and evening after work are usually the busiest times, roughly 7 to 9 in the morning, 12 to 2 in the afternoon, and 7 to 10 at night. Test different times with your own audience, because a food seller and a fashion vendor may find different hours work best, and use your Instagram insights to see when your followers are actually online.

**Can I sell on Instagram Stories without a website?**

Yes. Many Nigerian small businesses sell entirely through Instagram and WhatsApp, using a link sticker to send customers to WhatsApp, where they confirm the order and pay by bank transfer or payment link. A website helps as you grow, but it is not required to start selling on Stories.
