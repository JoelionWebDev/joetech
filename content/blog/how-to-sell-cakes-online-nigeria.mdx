Every weekend in Lagos, Abuja, Port Harcourt and Ibadan, somebody is celebrating a birthday, a wedding, a baby naming, a graduation or a church anniversary. And almost every one of those celebrations needs a cake. That is why home baking has become one of the most reliable small businesses in Nigeria. You do not need a shop on a busy street. You need an oven, a phone, good recipes, and a way for customers to find you, order from you, and pay you — all online.

This guide walks you through the whole journey, from your first paid cake to a steady flow of daily and weekend orders, written for Nigerian realities: the cost of butter and flour, power cuts, dispatch riders, and customers who pay by transfer.

## Why Cakes Sell So Well Online in Nigeria

Cake is an emotional purchase. Nobody buys a birthday cake because they are hungry; they buy it because somebody they love is turning a year older, getting married, or celebrating something that matters. That emotion makes customers willing to pay well for a cake that looks beautiful and tastes even better.

The market is also constant. Birthdays happen every single day of the year. Add weddings, baby showers, office end-of-year parties, Valentine's Day, Christmas, Eid and countless owambes, and you have demand in every month, not just December.

The startup cost is lower than most people think. If you already have an oven, a mixer and basic baking pans, you can start with ingredients for a few cakes and a phone you already own. Your first customers will come from people who already know you: family, friends, colleagues, church members and neighbours.

## Step 1: Decide What Kind of Cakes You Will Sell

Do not try to bake everything for everybody. A baker who offers birthday cakes, wedding cakes, cupcakes, banana bread, doughnuts, parfait, small chops and cookies all at once will run out of time, run out of gas, and disappoint somebody. Start narrow and become known for one thing done brilliantly.

Here are cake lanes that work well online in Nigeria:

- **Birthday cakes:** The bread and butter of home baking. Single-tier and two-tier cakes in chocolate, vanilla, red velvet and strawberry, from 6 inches to 12 inches. Prices in Lagos typically run from ₦25,000 for a simple 6-inch cake to ₦80,000 or more for a decorated two-tier cake.
- **Wedding cakes:** Higher value, fewer orders, but one wedding cake can pay what ten birthday cakes pay. A three-tier wedding cake in Lagos can sell from ₦250,000 to ₦600,000 depending on design, size and flavours.
- **Cupcakes and cake slices:** Perfect for daily sales. A box of 6 cupcakes sells from ₦6,000 to ₦12,000, and cake slices in foil packs sell from ₦2,500 to ₦4,000. These bring in money on ordinary weekdays, not just weekends.
- **Banana bread and loaf cakes:** Very popular with offices and health-conscious buyers. A loaf sells from ₦5,000 to ₦12,000 depending on size and ingredients.
- **Kids' character cakes:** Cakes decorated with cartoon characters, football club colours or cartoon toppers. Parents pay a premium for these, and they photograph beautifully for your Instagram page.
- **Dessert tables and treats:** Cake pops, brownies, cookies and parfait cups for events. These are good add-ons to a main cake order.

Choose based on three things: what you bake best, what ingredients you can buy reliably near you, and what people around you already buy. If you live near offices in Ikeja or Victoria Island, cake slices and cupcakes for office treats will sell well. If you live in a residential estate in Lekki or Lugbe, birthday cakes and weekend celebration cakes will be your main income.

## Step 2: Price Your Cakes So You Actually Make Money

This is where many home bakers fail. They price by copying a neighbour, or by guessing what customers will accept, and then discover at the end of the month that they baked every weekend and made almost nothing. Price from your costs, not from feelings.

### Work out your cost per cake

Buy your ingredients for one cake and write down every naira. Include the flour, sugar, butter or margarine, eggs, milk, flavouring, fondant or buttercream, the cake board, the cake box, gas, electricity, and the dispatch rider if you deliver. Then add your time. Many bakers forget that their labour is a cost. If a cake takes you four hours to bake and decorate, your price must pay for those four hours.

Here is a simple example for a 8-inch birthday cake in Lagos. Flour, sugar, butter, eggs, milk, flavouring and cocoa might cost ₦12,000. Buttercream and decoration might cost ₦6,000. The cake board and box might cost ₦3,000. Gas and electricity might cost ₦2,000. That is ₦23,000 in direct costs. If you sell that cake for ₦35,000, you make ₦12,000 before delivery costs. If you sell it for ₦25,000, you make only ₦2,000 for four hours of work — and one spoilt batch wipes out a whole week.

Use this formula and write it on paper where you can see it:

**Selling price = ingredient cost + packaging cost + gas and power + your labour + profit margin**

Aim for at least 40 to 50 percent gross margin on cakes. If your total cost is ₦23,000, your price should be ₦35,000 to ₦45,000.

### Publish a clear price list

Publish a short price list and pin it on your WhatsApp Business catalogue and your Instagram highlights. For example:

- 6-inch birthday cake, single tier — from ₦25,000
- 8-inch birthday cake, single tier — from ₦35,000
- 10-inch birthday cake — from ₦50,000
- Two-tier celebration cake — from ₦80,000
- Box of 6 cupcakes — from ₦9,000
- Banana bread loaf — from ₦8,000

State clearly what is included: the number of layers, the flavour options, the basic decoration, and the box. Charge extra for fondant figures, fresh flowers, gold details, extra tiers, and rush orders. A cake ordered for tomorrow should cost more than a cake ordered a week ahead, because rush work disrupts your whole schedule.

Do not negotiate every order down. If a customer says "it is too expensive," you can offer a smaller cake, not a lower price for the same cake. Discounts should be planned, for example a free box of cupcakes with a wedding cake, not given away because somebody asked nicely in a chat.

## Step 3: Set Up Your Online Shopfront

Your customers will find you on WhatsApp and Instagram. Both are free, and both are already on your customers' phones. Set them up properly, like a real shop.

### WhatsApp Business

Download WhatsApp Business from the Play Store or App Store. It is free and it gives you a business profile, a catalogue, labels, quick replies, away messages and greeting messages. Use a dedicated number for the business if you can, even if it is a second SIM in the same phone. Mixing business chats with family chats is how orders get lost.

Fill in every field of your business profile. Business name, for example "Sweet Layers by Ada — Lekki." Category: Bakery. Address or area, for example "Chevron, Lekki, Lagos." Opening hours, for example "Monday to Saturday, 8 a.m. to 7 p.m." Add a clear profile photo: your best cake, your logo, or your smiling face in an apron. People order from people they can see.

Your catalogue is your menu. Add every cake as a product with a good photo, the exact name, the price, and a short description. "Chocolate fudge birthday cake, 8 inches, 3 layers, serves 20 — ₦35,000." Customers can browse the catalogue and tap to order without you typing the menu 40 times a day.

### Instagram

Create a business Instagram page with the same name and the same profile photo. Your feed is your portfolio. Post every cake you bake, even the simple ones. Use the bio to say what you sell, where you are based, and how to order: "Custom birthday and wedding cakes in Lagos. Order via WhatsApp. Link below." Put your WhatsApp link in your bio so a customer can tap once and start chatting.

Post Reels of your decorating process. A 30-second video of buttercream being smoothed, or a cake being unveiled, gets far more views than a still photo. Customers love to see how their cake is made.

## Step 4: Photograph Your Cakes Like a Professional

Cake is a visual product. Nobody can taste your cake from a photo, so the photo has to do all the selling. Good photos will bring you orders; bad photos will quietly kill your business.

Take your photos in daylight, near a window, against a clean background. A plain white or light-coloured wall, a clean table, and a simple cake stand are all you need. You do not need a professional camera. A clean phone photo in natural light will outsell a dark, blurry photo every time.

Photograph the cake from several angles: a straight-on shot, a top-down shot, and a close-up of the decoration. Cut one slice and photograph it too, so customers can see the layers and the moist crumb inside. Show the real cake, the real size, and the real decoration.

## Step 5: Take Orders Without Losing Any

Orders will come from everywhere: direct WhatsApp chats, Instagram DMs, group mentions, status replies, referrals, and voice notes. You need one simple order process that works every time, no matter where the customer came from.

Use an order format and ask every customer for the same details:

1. Name and phone number
2. Cake size and flavour
3. Number of tiers and layers
4. Design or theme, with a reference photo if they have one
5. Date and time needed
6. Delivery address with a landmark, or pickup
7. Message or name to write on the cake

Copy that list into a quick reply so you can send it in one tap. When the customer replies, confirm the order back to them in one message: "Confirmed: 8-inch chocolate birthday cake, 3 layers, 'Happy Birthday Tobi' written on top, needed Saturday 2 p.m., delivery to Ikate (₦2,000). Total ₦37,000. Please send payment to confirm." That single confirmation message prevents almost every misunderstanding.

### Payment before baking

For cakes, collect at least 60 percent upfront before you start baking, and the balance before delivery or pickup. This is standard for bakers in Nigeria. Ingredients are expensive and a custom cake cannot be resold if the customer disappears. A cake with a stranger's name written on it has no other buyer.

Accept transfers to your bank account, and consider a Moniepoint or Opay account for fast confirmation alerts. Always confirm when you have seen the money: "Payment received, thank you! Your cake will be ready on Saturday by 2 p.m."

## Step 6: Bake, Pack and Deliver Without Damage

A beautiful cake that arrives squashed, melted or late is a bad experience. Packaging and delivery are part of the product.

Buy proper cake boxes, strong enough to hold the cake, and cake boards that fit the box. Tape the box closed properly. Put the cake on a flat surface in the rider's box, never tilted. If the weather is hot, chill the cake well before it leaves your kitchen so the buttercream holds its shape.

Bake to a timetable. If you promise a cake by 2 p.m. on Saturday, the cake must be finished, boxed and ready by 1 p.m. at the latest. Lagos traffic is unpredictable, so build in at least 45 minutes of buffer for deliveries across town. It is better to be early than to arrive with a melted cake and an angry customer.

### Delivery options

- **Pickup:** Offer pickup for customers who drive. Give a clear pickup point and a time window. Many customers prefer to pick up their cake themselves because they can see it before they take it.
- **Dispatch riders:** For deliveries across Lekki, Ikeja, Surulere or Wuse, use a dispatch rider. Agree the fee with the customer before you bake, and add it to the total. A typical short delivery within Lekki might cost ₦2,000 to ₦3,500. Longer trips cost more. Use riders you trust, and give them the customer's phone number, a clear address with a landmark, and a warning that the package is a fragile cake.
- **Your own delivery for big orders:** For wedding cakes and large orders, deliver it yourself or send a trusted person. A three-tier wedding cake is too valuable to hand to a stranger on a bike.

Tell the customer the delivery fee upfront, before they pay. Nothing annoys a customer more than a surprise delivery charge after payment. If delivery to a far area is too expensive, say so honestly and suggest pickup instead.

## Step 7: Get Customers Every Day

You do not need paid adverts to start. You need consistency and visibility in the places your customers already are.

### WhatsApp Status, every day

Post on your WhatsApp Status at least once a day: a cake you baked, a cake in the oven, a customer testimonial, or a short video of decorating. The best time is the morning, around 7 a.m. to 8 a.m., and again in the evening when people are planning weekend parties. Add the price and the order link. Status is free advertising to everyone who has saved your number.

Ask happy customers to save your number so they see your status. Many bakers lose sales simply because customers never see their daily posts.

### Instagram Reels and stories

Post a Reel at least three times a week. Decorating videos, before-and-after transformations, and cake-cutting moments perform very well. Use stories for polls and questions: "Chocolate or red velvet for this weekend?" Customers love to vote, and every vote pushes your page to more people.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Baking without a deposit from strangers.** You will be left with a custom cake and no money.
- **Underpricing to win customers.** You will attract customers who leave the moment you raise prices, and you will burn out baking for almost no profit.
- **Poor photos.** Blurry, dark photos of a cake will kill your sales.
- **Saying yes to every design.** If a customer sends a photo of a five-tier fondant masterpiece and wants it tomorrow for ₦30,000, say no honestly.
- **No cut-off time.** Set an order cut-off, for example 48 hours before the date needed for a custom cake.
- **No records.** If you do not write down what you sold and what you spent, you will not know whether you are making money or losing it.
- **Ignoring hygiene.** Wash your hands, tie your hair back, keep your kitchen clean, and use clean water.

## How Much Can You Realistically Make?

It depends on your area, your menu and your consistency, but here is a realistic example. If you sell 6 birthday cakes a week at an average of ₦35,000, that is ₦210,000 in weekly sales. If your average cost per cake is ₦20,000, your gross profit is ₦15,000 per cake, or ₦90,000 a week. Add cupcakes and banana bread sold during the week, and a well-run home bakery can earn a solid monthly income.

## Your First Month Action Plan

Do not wait for everything to be perfect. Start small and start this month.

1. **Week 1:** Choose your cake lane. Cost it properly. Set your prices and publish your price list.
2. **Week 2:** Set up WhatsApp Business and Instagram. Photograph three of your best cakes in daylight.
3. **Week 3:** Post on your status daily. Send your menu to 30 contacts personally.
4. **Week 4:** Take your first paid orders. Collect deposits before baking. Deliver on time and ask for feedback and party photos.

If you sell only five cakes in your first month, that is a win. You have proof that people will pay for your cakes. Build from there, one happy customer at a time.
