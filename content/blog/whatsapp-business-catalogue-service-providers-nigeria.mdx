Somewhere in Lagos, a customer opens your WhatsApp Business profile, sees your catalogue, and makes a decision in under ten seconds. Do they scroll, smile, and place an order — or do they close the app and message the cleaner next door?

That ten seconds makes or breaks countless Nigerian service businesses, because WhatsApp is not just a chat app here — it is the de facto marketplace for everything from braids to business cards. The problem: most service providers treat their catalogue like a digital photo album instead of a sales engine.

This guide shows you how to turn your WhatsApp Business catalogue into a machine that converts — professionally, in naira, without wasting a single customer's time.

---

## Step 1: Understand why the catalogue beats a chat

Most Nigerian providers answer enquiries by typing details over and over: *"We're at 12 Adebayo Street, prices start at ₦5,000…"* The catalogue kills that repetition and — critically — it *shows, not tells*.

**Why it converts better:**

- Customers can browse without asking permission. No awkward "how much?" question.
- Images build trust instantly. A real photo of your cake is worth a thousand descriptions.
- Prices in naira stop the wrong customers from wasting your time and the right customers from being scared off.
- It works while you sleep. A midnight browser can order before your competitors wake up.

> Your catalogue is a salesperson who never sleeps, never repeats herself, and never forgets a price.

---

## Step 2: Set the foundation correctly

Before adding a single item, make the container professional:

- **Business name exactly as customers search for it.** If people know you as "Aunty B's Soups," don't call the profile "Amen Enterprises." Match your real-world name.
- **Description in plain words.** State what you do, your area, and your offer: *"Handmade aso-oke bags, delivery across Lagos, custom orders 7 days."*
- **Logo from your brand** — a real logo (or a clean photo) beats a blurry screenshot.
- **Contact essentials:** WhatsApp number, business hours, and address in the profile.
- **Catalogue link sharing** — send the catalogue link instead of typing everything. You can pin it and reuse it.

One more critical setting: **your reply-time expectation.** If you know you check WhatsApp at 7pm after work, say so. Fast, honest expectations prevent abandoned orders and angry customers.

---

## Step 3: Build products people actually buy

The biggest mistake Nigerian catalogues make is treating every product like a photo post. A converting catalogue lists *offerings*, not memories.

**For every item, include:**

1. **A real, clean photo.** One clear image of the actual product or service result (not a borrowed stock photo). Three photos per item beats a blurry collage.
2. **A customer-focusing name.** Not "Package 1" — "Anniversary Cake (2-tier, fondant)" or "Home Deep Clean + Oven".
3. **Nigerian pricing, clearly.** State the price or a range ("from ₦25,000"). Hidden prices kill conversions. If pricing varies wildly, say "Customer order — message for quote" with a from-price.
4. **A description that sells.** What's included, how long it takes, delivery options, what the buyer needs to know: *"Includes consultation, design, and one round of revisions. Delivery within 5 business days."*

**Order your catalogue like a menu,** not a museum: your bestsellers first, then categories (e.g., "Signature cakes," "Custom orders," "Add-ons"). Most browsing happens on the first screen — put your money-makers there.

---

## Step 4: Use dynamics that nudge buyers

WhatsApp Business has features most Nigerian providers never touch. Use them and you're already ahead of 80% of your competitors.

**The tools that convert:**

- **Catalogues and products** — your storefront (step 3).
- **Quick replies** — save your most common answers as shortcuts (price list, location, delivery policy). One tap instead of retyping.
- **Labels** — organise chats (New Order / Payment Pending / Delivered). Do not lose an order in the chat abyss.
- **Away and greeting messages** — set an instant greeting that sends your catalogue link + a friendly intro, and an away message for after-hours with order instructions.
- **Catalogue link automation** — auto-reply with your catalogue link to common questions so customers see your whole range instantly.

> Every automated message should do two jobs: answer the question and *push toward the catalogue or order*.

---

## Step 5: Follow up like the order depends on it (it does)

A catalogue gets the interest; follow-up gets the sale. Nigerian service providers lose commissions every day at exactly this step.

**The proven follow-up rhythm:**

1. **Immediately:** Reply within minutes if possible, confirm the exact item they viewed, and ask the one question that matters: *"For the anniversary cake, what date are we looking at?"*
2. **Same evening:** Send a gentle nudge to anyone who stopped responding: *"Hi, no pressure — I can hold the date for you till tomorrow if that helps."*
3. **After payment:** Confirm, thank them, and set the delivery expectation in one clear message.
4. **After service:** Ask for review or referral. A satisfied WhatsApp customer is your best free marketing.

Keep the messaging warm but clear. Nigerians respond to respect more than hype — greet by name, be direct about price and timing, and never make them chase you.

---

## Step 6: Nigerian-specific conversion boosters

A few local touches that move the needle:

- **Bank transfer + payment proof options.** Combine with Paystack/Flutterwave links for card-paying customers. Give choices and confirm promptly.
- **Deposit for custom work.** Many providers request a deposit to confirm. State it clearly in the product description to stop no-show "confirmations."
- **Delivery clarity.** Are you delivering? Is it pickup? Is there a delivery fee? Say it in the item description to avoid endless back-and-forth.
- **Reviews collection.** Screenshot and gift WhatsApp review snippets in your status: *"🖤 2-tier cake delivered in Magodo — thank you Mrs A!"* (no need for emojis, but the social proof works).
- **Status flashing product highlights.** A daily status of "what went out today" keeps your catalogue top of mind.

---

## Step 7: Watch your numbers and improve

A catalogue is a living thing. Check the simple signals monthly:

- Which items get the most views? Make those your featured first items.
- Which ones get ignored? Improve photos, price clarity, or descriptions.
- What questions do customers keep asking? Add those answers to your automatic replies and descriptions.

Treat the catalogue like a small shop you tidy every week — update prices, refresh photos, prune dead listings. A tidy catalogue signals a live business; a stale one signals a closed one.

---

## Your 7-day catalogue makeover

- **Day 1:** Fix profile basics — name, logo, description, hours.
- **Day 2:** Choose your 10 best "money products."
- **Day 3:** Photograph them cleanly (natural light, clear background).
- **Day 4:** Write selling descriptions with prices + delivery info.
- **Day 5:** Set greeting, away message, quick replies, and labels.
- **Day 6:** Share the catalogue link everywhere and on status.
- **Day 7:** Study the first week's questions and refine descriptions.

## Why the small details win

Customers don't buy from catalogues because they're "impressed." They buy because the catalog made it *easy to trust and easy to order*. Every photo, price, and reply is a small proof that you run a serious business — and in Nigeria's trust-shy market, that proof is what converts.
