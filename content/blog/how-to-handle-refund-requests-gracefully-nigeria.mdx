The message arrives on a Saturday evening: "This dress is not what I ordered. I want my money back." Your stomach tightens. You have already spent the money on new stock. The dress looks fine to you. And you know that if this goes badly, the customer will post it on her status, tell her friends, and maybe name your business in a group with two thousand members.

Every Nigerian seller who has been in business for more than a few months has received that message. Refund requests are not a sign that your business is failing. They are a normal part of selling, especially online, where the customer cannot touch the product before paying. What matters is not whether refunds happen — they will — but how you handle them.

Handled badly, a refund costs you the money, the customer, and her entire circle of friends. Handled gracefully, a refund can cost you the money and still keep the customer, earn you a public recommendation, and teach you something that prevents ten future refunds. This guide shows you how.

## Why Refunds Feel So Hard in Nigeria

Refunds are emotional for sellers here in a way they are not in countries with easy returns. Understanding why helps you handle them with a clear head.

**Margins are thin.** Many Nigerian small businesses work on small margins. A ₦25,000 sale may carry only ₦5,000 profit, so a full refund feels like losing money you never really had. That is real, and it is why a clear policy matters — it protects you from giving away money you cannot afford to give away.

**Cash is already spent.** Money from a sale often goes straight into new stock, rent, or a supplier payment. When a refund is requested a week later, the money is gone. This is a cash flow problem, not a customer problem, and the solution is a small reserve, which we cover below.

**Trust is fragile.** Scams are common in Nigerian online selling, so both sides are suspicious. The customer worries you will keep her money and disappear. You worry she is trying to cheat you — wearing the dress to a party and returning it, or swapping the product for a fake. Some of that worry is justified. Most of it is not, and acting on suspicion first is how a small problem becomes a public fight.

**There is no clear law most sellers know.** Nigeria's Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Act gives consumers real rights, including the right to goods that match their description. Most small sellers do not know the details, and most customers do not either. In practice, your written policy — if it is clear, fair, and applied consistently — is what governs the transaction. That makes writing a good policy one of the most important things you will do.

**Reputation travels fast.** A single angry post in a WhatsApp group or on Twitter can reach thousands of people in hours. The reverse is also true: a customer who was treated well after a problem tells people too. In a market built on trust and referrals, how you handle the one unhappy customer matters more than how you handle the hundred happy ones.

## The Golden Rule: Respond Fast, Stay Calm

The first hour after a refund request decides how the whole thing ends. A fast, calm reply de-escalates. A slow, defensive reply escalates. This is the rule that governs everything else in this guide.

**Reply within the hour, even if you cannot solve it yet.** "Good evening Mrs Eze, thank you for letting us know. I am sorry the dress did not meet your expectations. Please send a photo of what you received, and your order number, so I can check it immediately and sort this out for you." That message does three things: it acknowledges her, it apologises without admitting fault, and it asks for the facts you need. It buys you time without making her feel ignored.

**Never argue in the first message.** Do not say "but you chose the size yourself" or "the dress is exactly as pictured" in your first reply, even if it is true. The customer feels unheard, and the conversation becomes a fight about who is right instead of a conversation about how to fix it. Hear her first. Verify second. Decide third.

**Move the conversation to a calm channel.** If the complaint arrives as a public comment on Instagram or a post in a group, reply publicly once — "We are sorry about this and we are sorting it out with you now" — then move to WhatsApp or a call. Public arguments help nobody. A public resolution, on the other hand, is excellent marketing: "Thank you for your patience, your refund has been sent" posted publicly earns more trust than any advert.

**Stay polite even when the customer is not.** Some customers will shout, threaten, and insult you. It is hard not to respond in kind. Do not. Every message you send can be screenshotted. The seller who stays calm and professional in the screenshots wins the argument in the eyes of everyone who sees them, even if the customer was rude first.

## Step 1: Verify the Claim Before You Decide

Never agree to or refuse a refund before you know the facts. Verification protects honest customers and honest sellers alike.

**Ask for the order details.** The order number, the date, the product, the amount paid, and the payment reference. If you keep a simple order record — a notebook, a Google Sheet, or labelled WhatsApp chats — this takes one minute. If you do not keep records, this is the moment to start.

**Ask for photos or a video.** "Please send a clear photo of the item you received, including any damage or difference you noticed." Photos settle most disputes instantly. A torn seam is visible. A wrong colour is visible. A size that does not match the label is visible. A customer who cannot produce a photo of the problem is often a customer with no problem.

**Compare with what you sent.** Check the product description, the photos you posted, and the packing record if you have one. Did the listing say "navy blue" and the customer received black? Did the size chart say size 12 is 100cm chest, and the dress measures 90cm? Did the item leave your shop in good condition? Be honest with yourself here. If the error is yours, say so and fix it. If the product matches the description, you can explain that calmly with evidence.

**Consider the timeline.** When was the item delivered? When did the customer complain? A complaint the same day as delivery is very different from a complaint six weeks later, after the item has clearly been used. Your policy should set a window — three days, seven days — and the timeline matters for whether the request falls inside it.

**Check for patterns.** Is this the third complaint about the same product this month? Is this the second refund request from the same customer? Patterns tell you whether you have a product problem, a supplier problem, or a customer problem. Fix the pattern, not just the single case.

## Step 2: Know What You Will Offer — Refund, Exchange, Repair or Credit

Not every problem needs a full refund. Having four options, and knowing when to use each, saves you money while keeping the customer happy.

**Exchange.** Best when the size is wrong, the colour is wrong, or the customer simply changed her mind within your policy window and the item is unworn with tags. An exchange keeps the sale and keeps the customer. Offer it first when the product is fine but the choice was wrong: "We can exchange it for the correct size at no extra cost — you only cover the delivery."

**Repair or replacement.** Best when the product has a fault that can be fixed or replaced — a faulty zip, a missing button, a phone with a dead battery on arrival. "We will replace it with a new one and cover the delivery both ways" turns a faulty product into a story of excellent service.

**Store credit.** Best when the customer is unhappy but you cannot establish a fault, or when a full cash refund would hurt your cash flow badly. Offer credit slightly above the purchase price — "₦28,000 credit for a ₦25,000 item" — so the customer feels she gained something. Credit only works if you have products she wants, and if she trusts you enough to shop with you again.

**Full refund.** Best when the product is clearly wrong, damaged, or not as described — and the fault is yours. A full refund, sent promptly, is the fastest way to end a bad situation and protect your reputation. It costs money, but it buys something more valuable: the story that you refunded quickly and without a fight.

Offer the options in this order: exchange or replacement first, credit second, refund third. Many customers accept the first fair option. But if the customer insists on a refund and the fault is yours, give it. A forced exchange on a customer who wants her money back creates an enemy, not a repeat buyer.

## Step 3: Write a Clear Refund and Exchange Policy

A policy you can point to turns an emotional argument into a simple question: does this request fall inside the policy? Write yours down, publish it, and apply it the same way to everyone.

Your policy should answer six questions in plain language:

1. **How many days?** "You may request an exchange or refund within 3 days of delivery." Pick a number you can honour. Three days is common for fashion, seven for electronics. Longer is more generous; shorter protects you. Whatever you pick, state it clearly.
2. **What condition?** "Items must be unworn, unwashed, with tags attached, and in the original packaging." For food and custom-made items, state that they are non-returnable unless faulty — and explain why.
3. **Who pays for return delivery?** Decide in advance. If the fault is yours, you pay. If the customer changed her mind, she pays. State it, so it is never negotiated in anger.
4. **How is the refund paid?** "Refunds are sent by bank transfer within 24–48 hours of receiving the returned item." State the method and the time. A customer who knows when the money is coming stops worrying.
5. **What is not refundable?** Underwear, earrings, custom-made items, food, and items bought on clearance are commonly non-returnable. List them. A customer who knew before buying cannot argue after buying.
6. **What proof is needed?** "Please send your order number and a photo of the item." This keeps the process fast and fair.

Put the policy in your WhatsApp Business profile, in your Instagram highlights, on your website, and in the message you send when an order is confirmed. A policy nobody can find is a policy that does not exist. Review it every few months, and update it when you learn something new — for example, if a particular product keeps being returned, change the description or stop selling it.

## Step 4: Use the Right Words — Scripts That Work

When emotions are high, having the right words ready is a lifesaver. These scripts are starting points. Adapt them to your voice, but keep the structure: acknowledge, apologise, verify, offer, confirm.

**The first reply to any refund request:**

"Good afternoon, thank you for reaching out and for your patience. I am sorry the item did not meet your expectations — that is not the experience we want for you. Please send your order number and a photo of the item so I can look into this right away and find the best solution for you."

**When the fault is yours:**

"Thank you for the photo — you are right, this is not the colour you ordered, and I apologise for the mix-up. We will send the correct one today and cover the delivery. If you would prefer a full refund instead, I completely understand — please send your account details and I will transfer it within 24 hours."

**When the customer chose the wrong size:**

"I understand the size does not fit as expected, and I am sorry about that. The size chart on the listing shows size 12 as 100cm chest — would you like to exchange it for size 14 at no extra cost? You would only cover the delivery to return it. Or, if you prefer, I can offer store credit for the full amount."

**When the request is outside your policy:**

"Thank you for letting us know. I checked your order, and it was delivered three weeks ago, which is outside our 3-day exchange window. I understand this is disappointing. What I can offer is a discount on your next order, and I will personally check the size with you before we send it, so this does not happen again."

**When the customer is threatening to post publicly:**

"I completely understand your frustration, and I am sorry we got to this point. I would like to sort this out properly for you. Please give me until [time] today, and I will come back to you with a solution. Your satisfaction matters to us, and I do not want you to leave unhappy."

**When the refund is sent:**

"Thank you for your patience. Your refund of ₦25,000 has been sent to your account — please confirm when you receive it. I am sorry again for the inconvenience, and I hope you will give us another chance. Your next delivery is on us."

Notice what these scripts never do: they never blame the customer, never call her a liar, never hide behind "company policy" without explaining it, and never promise what you cannot deliver. They are firm, warm, and specific — and that combination ends most disputes in one or two messages.

## Step 5: Send the Refund Promptly and Confirm It

Once you have agreed to a refund, speed matters more than anything. A refund promised on Monday and sent on Friday feels like a lie. A refund promised and sent within 24 hours feels like excellent service.

**Agree the amount in writing.** State the exact amount, the account it goes to, and the time it will arrive — in the same chat, so there is a record. "Refund of ₦25,000 to your GTBank account ending 4821, within 24 hours."

**Send it on time, even if it hurts.** If you promised 24 hours, send it in 24 hours. If your cash flow makes that hard, that is a sign to build a small refund reserve — a separate amount, even ₦20,000 or ₦50,000, kept aside for refunds and exchanges. A business that cannot refund ₦25,000 without a crisis is a business one unhappy customer away from a public fight.

**Ask for confirmation.** "Please let me know when it arrives." When she confirms, thank her and close the loop warmly. Do not ask her to delete a post she already made — that feels like a cover-up. Instead, if she posted publicly, reply publicly that the refund has been sent and you are sorry. The public record then shows a business that fixes its mistakes.

**Record it.** Note the refund in your order record: order number, customer, amount, reason, and date. Over time, this record shows you which products cause refunds, which suppliers send faulty stock, and how much refunds cost you each month. That data is worth more than the refund itself, because it tells you what to fix.



## Final Word

A refund request is a test, and it is one of the few tests in business where the customer is watching closely to see who you really are. Anyone can be friendly when a sale goes well. The businesses customers trust are the ones that stay calm, verify the facts, offer a fair solution, and send the money on time when they owe it.

Write your policy today, build a small refund reserve this week, and save the scripts in this guide where you can reach them quickly. The next time the message arrives — "I want my money back" — you will not panic. You will reply within the hour, check the facts, offer a fair solution, and move on. And more often than you expect, the customer who asked for her money back will be the customer who buys from you again.
