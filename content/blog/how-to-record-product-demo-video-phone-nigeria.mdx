A customer in Lekki is scrolling through Instagram at night. She sees two sellers offering the same body cream. One posts a blurry photo with the caption "DM for price." The other posts a 45-second video: she opens the jar, shows the texture, rubs a small amount on her hand, explains what it does for dry skin, and shows the price and delivery areas at the end. Which seller gets the message? Almost always the second one.

That is the power of a product demo video. It answers the questions a photo cannot answer. How big is it really? How does it work? What does it look like in real life? Does it look like the picture? In Nigeria, where many buyers have been disappointed by products that looked different online, a honest demo video builds trust faster than any advert.

The good news is that you do not need a studio, a ring light worth ₦80,000, or a professional videographer. The phone in your hand right now is enough. This guide shows you how to plan, shoot, edit and post a product demo video that actually sells, using free tools, natural light, and the realities of Nigerian data, power and noise.

## Why Demo Videos Sell More Than Photos

Photos show what a product looks like. Videos show what it does. That difference matters for almost every product sold online in Nigeria: skincare, hair products, shoes, bags, kitchen gadgets, electronics, food, clothes, cleaning products, and even services like makeup or hair styling.

A demo video does five jobs at once. First, it proves the product is real and in your hand, not a picture copied from the internet. Second, it shows size and scale, which is the most common complaint in online shopping. Third, it shows the product working, so the buyer can imagine using it. Fourth, it lets the buyer hear your voice and see your face, which builds trust. Fifth, it can be shared, saved and forwarded on WhatsApp, Instagram and TikTok, reaching people you never met.

Short videos also get shown to more people. Instagram Reels, TikTok and YouTube Shorts all favour video over still photos. A 40-second demo can reach thousands of people for free, while a photo of the same product may reach only your followers. For a small business in Lagos, Abuja or Port Harcourt, that free reach is priceless.

## Step 1: Plan Your Video Before You Press Record

Most bad demo videos fail before recording starts. The seller turns on the camera, holds up the product, and starts talking without a plan. The result is a rambling three-minute video that nobody finishes. A two-minute plan fixes that.

Decide the one job of the video. Is it to show how the product works? To prove it is original? To show the result after use? To compare sizes? Pick one main job. A video that tries to do everything does nothing well.

Write a simple script on paper or in your phone notes. You do not need fancy language. Use this structure, and keep the whole video between 30 and 90 seconds:

1. **Hook (first 3 seconds):** Say the problem or the result. "Tired of cream that leaves white patches?" or "This blender crushes tiger nuts in 30 seconds."
2. **Show the product:** Hold it up, say the name, show the packaging and the size next to your hand or a common object.
3. **Demonstrate:** Use it on camera. Apply it, plug it in, wear it, open it, pour it, or switch it on.
4. **Explain the key benefits:** Pick two or three benefits, not ten. Price, sizes, colours, and what makes it different.
5. **Call to action:** Tell the viewer exactly what to do. "Send us a DM to order" or "Tap the WhatsApp link in our bio. Delivery available in Lagos and Abuja."

Read your script out loud once before filming. If it takes longer than 90 seconds to read, cut words. Short and clear beats long and clever every time.

Choose where you will film. A clean table, a plain wall, or a tidy corner of your shop works well. Remove clutter from the background: dirty plates, tangled cables, laundry, and random boxes distract the viewer and make the product look cheap. If you sell food, film in a clean kitchen. If you sell clothes, film against a plain wall or a neat rail. If you sell skincare, film at a clean dressing table.

## Step 2: Use Light You Already Have

Lighting is the difference between a video that looks professional and one that looks like a mistake. You do not need to buy lights to start. Daylight is free, bright and flattering, and Nigeria has plenty of it.

Film near a large window or outside in open shade during the day, between 8 a.m. and 10 a.m. or between 4 p.m. and 5:30 p.m. Avoid harsh midday sun, which creates hard shadows on your face and washes out colours. Never film with a bright window directly behind you, because your face and the product will go dark. Put the window in front of you or to your side, so the light falls on the product.

If you film at night, use the brightest room light you have and add a second light source, such as a torch or a phone torch held by a helper, from the other side. A small rechargeable LED light, which sells for around ₦5,000 to ₦15,000 in markets in Computer Village, Ikeja or online, is a worthwhile investment once you start posting videos regularly. Until then, daylight will carry you.

Check the colours on your phone screen before you record. Does the red dress look red, or orange? Does the cream look white, or yellow? Move closer to the window or change your angle until the colour looks true. Customers will complain if the colour in the video does not match the product they receive.

Film in the quietest place you can find, close the windows, turn off the television and music, and speak clearly a little slower than normal. Record a five-second test clip first and play it back: if a generator or traffic drowns your voice, move or wait for a quieter moment. Your phone earphones can work as a microphone in a pinch.

## Step 4: Set Up Your Phone Properly

Before you record, do five quick checks that save you from having to redo the whole video.

First, clean the camera lens with a soft, dry cloth. A fingerprint on the lens makes the whole video look foggy, and it is the most common reason phone videos look bad.

Second, check your storage. A one-minute video in high quality can use 150 to 300 MB. If your phone is almost full, the recording may stop halfway. Delete old videos, clear your WhatsApp media, or move files to Google Drive before you start.

Third, charge your phone or plug it in. Nothing is more frustrating than the phone dying in the middle of a perfect take. Aim for at least 50 percent battery.

Fourth, turn on Do Not Disturb or switch on aeroplane mode with Wi-Fi on if you need it, so a call does not interrupt your recording. A call in the middle of a demo ruins the take and can be embarrassing if the caller ID shows on screen.

Fifth, set the camera correctly. Record in 1080p, not 4K, because 4K files are huge, slow to edit, and expensive to upload on Nigerian data. Use the rear camera, not the selfie camera, when someone else can film you, because the rear camera is sharper. If you are filming yourself, the selfie camera is fine, but make sure your face and the product are both in the frame. Record vertically, in portrait mode, for Instagram Reels, TikTok, WhatsApp Status and YouTube Shorts, because that is how most Nigerians watch videos on their phones. Record horizontally, in landscape, only if the video is mainly for YouTube long-form or your website.

Keep the product in the centre of the frame, with your head and shoulders in the top half.

## Step 5: Shoot the Demo Like a Seller, Not an Actor

You do not need to act. You need to be clear, honest and helpful. Talk to the camera the way you would talk to a customer standing in front of you in your shop in Lekki, Wuse or GRA.

Start by showing the product in your hand, not on a table far away. Hold it close enough that the viewer can see the label, the texture and the size. Turn it around slowly so they can see all sides. If it is a shoe, show the sole. If it is a bag, open it and show the inside. If it is a phone accessory, show it connected and working. If it is food, show the portion, the packaging and the steam.

Show your hands using the product. Hands make a demo feel real. Rub the cream in and show the result after 10 seconds. Switch on the blender and let the viewer hear it working. Zip and unzip the bag. Try on the dress and turn around. Pour the juice and take a sip. Real use beats a long speech about quality.

Be honest about what the product cannot do. If a cream lightens dark spots gradually over four weeks, say so. Do not promise overnight miracles. If a power bank charges a phone twice, say twice, not five times. Nigerian customers remember sellers who were honest, and they return to them. They also warn their friends about sellers who exaggerated.

Film each part in short clips of 10 to 20 seconds, instead of one long take. It is easier to redo a short clip than a whole three-minute video. It also makes editing much simpler. Pause, breathe, and start the next clip. Film the hook twice, so you can pick the better one later.

Keep the camera steady. Rest your elbows on a table, lean the phone against a stack of books, use a small tripod if you have one, or ask someone to hold the phone with both hands. Shaky video makes viewers dizzy and makes the product look unprofessional. Move the camera slowly when you need to move it. Quick, jerky movements are hard to watch.

Film more than you need. Take a close-up of the label, a close-up of the texture, a wide shot of you holding the product, and a shot of the packaging. Extra clips give you choices when editing, and you can reuse them in future videos.

## Step 6: Edit on Your Phone With Free Apps

Editing is where a rough recording becomes a video people will watch to the end. You do not need a laptop or paid software. Free phone apps are enough, and they are designed for beginners.

CapCut is the most popular free editor for Nigerian sellers, and it works on Android and iPhone. InShot and VN Video Editor are also good, free options. Download one of them, open a new project, and import your clips in the order of your script: hook first, then the product, then the demo, then the benefits, then the call to action.

Cut out the mistakes first. Remove the long pauses at the start, the "erm" and "let me check," the part where you dropped the product, and any clip where the sound failed. Keep only the best take of each part. A tight 45-second video will outsell a loose two-minute video every time.

Add captions. Many Nigerians watch videos on mute, in a bus, in an office, or late at night. If your video has no text on screen, a muted viewer will not know what you are selling. Add the price and your WhatsApp number as text on the screen at the end, large enough to read on a small phone.

Add a short title at the start, in bold text, for three seconds: "Original Shea Butter — ₦8,000" or "This Blender Crushes Anything." That title tells a scrolling viewer to stop. Use a clean, readable font and a colour that stands out against the background, but keep it simple. Your product is the star, not the text effects.

Export the finished video in 1080p. Save a copy on your phone and a backup on Google Drive. Name the file clearly, for example "shea-butter-demo-nov-2026.mp4," so you can find it again when a customer asks for it on WhatsApp.

## Step 7: Post It Where Your Customers Already Are

A good video that nobody sees does not sell anything. Post it where your customers spend time, and post it properly.

On WhatsApp, send the video to your broadcast list and post it on your Status in the morning, when people are planning their day, and again in the evening. Add a short caption with the price, sizes and delivery areas. When a customer asks about the product in a chat, send them the video instead of typing a long explanation. It saves you time and answers their questions better.

On Instagram, post it as a Reel. Write a short caption, not a long essay. Include the price, how to order, and your location. Add three to five relevant hashtags, such as #LagosVendor #SkincareNigeria or #MadeInNigeria, depending on your product. Reply to every comment in the first hour, because early replies tell Instagram the video is interesting, and Instagram shows it to more people.

On TikTok, post the same video with a short, catchy caption and a trending sound if it fits. TikTok is powerful for discovery: a seller in Surulere can be discovered by a buyer in Enugu who has never heard of her shop. Post consistently, at least three times a week, and TikTok will start showing your videos to people who like your type of product.

On Facebook, post the video on your business page and in relevant buying and selling groups, where the group rules allow it. Add the price in the post itself, because Facebook users often will not click to ask. If you have a website or an online catalogue, add the video to the product page as well. A customer who watches a demo on your website is far more likely to buy than one who only sees a photo.

## Common Mistakes That Kill Demo Videos

- **Filming in a dark room.** If the viewer cannot see the product clearly, they will not buy it.
- **Talking for three minutes without showing the product in use.** Show, do not just tell.
- **Hiding the price.** "DM for price" frustrates buyers and kills impulse orders. State the price in the video or the caption.
- **Using a fake or exaggerated claim.** "This cream will make you fair in three days" will bring complaints, refunds and a damaged reputation.
- **Filming vertically for YouTube long-form and horizontally for TikTok.** Match the format to the platform.
- **Posting a video with no call to action.** Always tell the viewer what to do next, or they will scroll away and forget you.
- **Recording with a dirty lens or a nearly dead battery.** Check both before every shoot.
- **Copying another seller's video word for word.** Customers notice, and it does not build trust in your own brand.

## Your First Demo Video: A One-Day Plan

You can film your first demo today, without buying anything new.

1. **Morning:** Pick your bestselling product. Write a 60-second script using the hook, show, demonstrate, benefits, call to action structure.
2. **Late morning:** Clean a table near a window, lay out the product, and charge your phone.
3. **Midday:** Film in daylight. Record each part in short clips. Film the hook twice.
4. **Afternoon:** Edit in CapCut. Cut mistakes, add captions, add the price and your WhatsApp number as text.
5. **Evening:** Post the video on WhatsApp Status, Instagram Reels and TikTok. Reply to every comment and message the same evening.

That is it. Your first video will not be perfect, and it does not need to be. It needs to be clear, honest and helpful.

A good product deserves to be seen. Your phone, daylight, a clean background and a clear voice are enough. Plan the video, film it in short clips, edit out the mistakes, add the price, and post it where your customers are. Do that twice a week, and your phone will stop being just a phone. It will become your shop window, your salesperson, and your best marketing tool, working for you even while you sleep.
