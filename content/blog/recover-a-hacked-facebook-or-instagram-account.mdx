Few things feel as alarming as opening Facebook or Instagram and realising someone else has been in your account. Your password no longer works. Your profile photo has changed. Strange posts are going out to your friends, or your followers are receiving messages asking for money. For a business owner, it is worse: your business page, your customer chats and years of content can be at risk. The good news is that a hacked account can usually be recovered, especially if you act quickly. This guide walks you through what to do — first for Facebook, then for Instagram — and how to secure your account so it does not happen again.

## How to Tell If Your Account Has Been Hacked

Sometimes a hack is obvious: you cannot log in at all. Other times the signs are quieter. On Facebook or Instagram, watch out for posts, stories or reels you did not create; messages sent to your friends that you did not write — often asking for money, advertising a fake investment, or sharing a strange link; friend requests sent to people you do not know; changes to your name, profile photo, bio, email address or phone number; and login alerts about sessions from places you have never been.

You may also get emails saying your password or email address was changed, even though you did not request it. Do not ignore these emails. They usually arrive within minutes of the change, and the window to undo the damage is short. Facebook and Instagram both send a message when your email or password is changed, with a link to reverse the change if it was not you. If you get one of those emails, act on it immediately — do not wait until later in the day.



## First Steps: Act Fast, and Do Not Panic

Speed matters. The longer a hacker controls your account, the more damage they can do — messaging your contacts, changing your recovery details, and locking you out permanently. Start the recovery process as soon as you notice the problem, from a device you trust. Use your own phone or computer, not a borrowed device or a public computer, and avoid public Wi-Fi while you recover the account.

First, warn the people closest to you. Post from another account, or ask a friend to post, that your account may have been hacked and that any strange messages asking for money should be ignored. Hackers often message a victim's friends and family asking for urgent money transfers, and a quick warning can save someone from losing money.

Second, do not pay anyone who claims they can recover your account for a fee. You will see people in comments and on WhatsApp offering "hacking recovery services" for a price — many of them are scammers themselves. Facebook and Instagram recovery is free, and the official steps in this guide are the ones their support teams use.

Third, gather what you remember: the email address and phone number linked to the account, and your username. You will also need a government-issued ID if the recovery process asks you to prove who you are, so have one ready — a national ID card, driver's licence, international passport or voter's card.

## How to Recover a Hacked Facebook Account

Start at the Facebook login page. Click "Forgot password?" and enter the email address or phone number linked to your account. Facebook will show you the recovery options available — usually sending a code to your email or by SMS. Choose the one you can still access. If the hacker changed your email address, Facebook may still let you use your old email or phone number for recovery, so try both.

If you receive the code, enter it and set a new, strong password — one you have never used before, at least eight characters long, mixing letters, numbers and symbols. Once you are in, go straight to Settings and check your email address and phone number. If the hacker changed them, change them back. Then go to the security section and look at "Where you're logged in" — this shows every device and location currently signed into your account. Log out of any session you do not recognise. Also check your profile, your pages, and your ad account if you run ads, to see whether anything was changed.

If the "Forgot password?" route does not work because the hacker changed your recovery details, go to facebook.com/hacked. This is Facebook's dedicated hacked-account page. Select the problem that matches your situation — for example, "Someone else got into my account without my permission" — and follow the steps. Facebook will ask you to identify your account, usually by your email, phone number, username or your name plus a friend's name. You may be asked to verify your identity with a government ID or by identifying photos of your friends. Follow the instructions carefully and upload a clear photo of your ID if asked. Facebook reviews these requests, and it can take a day or two to hear back, so be patient and check your email regularly — including your spam folder.

If you run a Facebook Page or ads from the account, also check the Business settings once you are back in. Hackers sometimes add themselves as admins to Pages or create adverts. Remove any unfamiliar admins, and check your ad account for adverts or spending you did not approve. If money was spent, report it to Facebook and to your bank.

## How to Recover a Hacked Instagram Account

Instagram's recovery starts in the app. On the login screen, tap "Forgot password?" (on iPhone) or "Get help logging in" (on Android). Enter your username, email address or phone number. Instagram will offer to send a login link to your email or a code by SMS. If you can access your email, tap the link in the message from Instagram — make sure it is genuinely from Instagram and not a phishing email pretending to be one. The real messages come from Instagram's official addresses and never ask for your password.

If the hacker changed your email address, Instagram sends a message to your old email address when the change is made. That message contains a link to reverse the change — "Revert this change" — and this is your best chance of getting the account back. Search your inbox for emails from Instagram with the subject about your email being changed, and use that link as soon as possible. Instagram only keeps that reversal window open for a limited time, so do not delay.

If you cannot get in by email or SMS, tap "Need more help?" on the login screen, or go to Instagram's help pages and look for the hacked account form. Instagram may ask you to verify your identity by taking a video selfie — the app will ask you to turn your head in different directions so it can compare your face with photos on your account. This feels strange, but it is a genuine security step, and it is one of the main ways Instagram returns accounts to their real owners. You may also be asked to provide the email address or phone number you originally used to create the account, and the type of device you used.

For business and creator accounts, Instagram may ask for extra proof, such as business documents or evidence that you run the business. Keep anything that ties you to the account in case you need it.

## If the Hacker Changed Everything

In the worst cases, the hacker changes your password, your email and your phone number, so none of the normal recovery options work. This is harder, but not always hopeless. Both Facebook and Instagram have identity verification routes that rely on your government ID rather than your contact details. On Facebook, the hacked-account page can ask you to upload an ID. On Instagram, the video selfie check can work even when your email has been changed.

When you submit an ID, use a clear, well-lit photo of the whole document, with your name and photo visible. The name on the ID should match the name on the account. If your account uses a nickname or business name, this can slow things down. Reviews can take several days, so submit once and wait rather than sending the same request many times, which can slow the process further.

While you wait, set up a temporary way for customers to reach you — for example, your WhatsApp business number or a new page — and let people know your old account is compromised. If your business page is affected, you can also ask a trusted admin of the page to remove the hacked account from the page's admin list, if you have another admin. This is a good reason to always have more than one admin on a business page: if one admin account is hacked, the other can act.

## Secure Your Account After You Get It Back

Getting back in is only half the job. The first thing to do is change your password to something strong and unique — do not reuse a password from another site, and do not reuse the old one. A good password is long, mixes letters, numbers and symbols, and is not based on your name, birthday or phone number. If you struggle to remember passwords, use a password manager app, or write the password in a notebook you keep at home — never in a note on your phone or in a chat message.

Next, turn on two-factor authentication. This is the single most effective step you can take. With two-factor authentication, logging in needs your password plus a second code, usually from an authentication app or sent by SMS. Even if someone steals your password, they cannot get in without that second code. On Facebook, find it under Settings, then Security and Login, then Two-Factor Authentication. On Instagram, find it under Settings, then Accounts Centre, then Password and Security. Authentication apps are more secure than SMS codes, because SMS codes can be intercepted, but SMS is far better than nothing — turn on whichever one you can.

Then review everything the hacker may have touched. Check your email and phone number in settings, and remove any you do not recognise. Look at the list of logged-in devices and log out of all of them, then log back in only on your own devices. Check connected apps and websites and remove anything unfamiliar. On Facebook, also check your payment methods and ad accounts. On Instagram, check your bio, website link, and the accounts you follow.

Finally, check your email account too. Many hacks start with the email address linked to the social account. If your email password is weak or the same as your Facebook password, change it, and turn on two-factor authentication for your email as well. A secure email account protects every other account linked to it.

## How to Avoid Being Hacked Again

Most hacks do not come from clever technical attacks — they come from simple tricks. The most common is phishing: a message, email or post that looks like it comes from Facebook or Instagram, warning that your account will be closed or that you have won something, with a link to "verify" your account. The link takes you to a fake login page that steals your password. Always be suspicious of urgent messages asking you to log in through a link — instead, open the app directly and check for yourself.

Never share your password or your login codes with anyone — not even someone claiming to be from Facebook, Instagram or Meta support. Real support staff will never ask for your password or your two-factor code. Be careful with links sent by friends too: if a friend's account is hacked, the hacker may message you from it.

Keep your apps updated, because updates often fix security holes. Use a different password for every important account, especially your email, your bank and your social media. Avoid logging into your accounts on shared or public computers, and always log out when you do. And be careful with third-party apps that ask you to log in with Facebook or Instagram to see who viewed your profile or to gain followers — many of them are designed to steal your login details.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### My password does not work and I cannot get the reset code. What do I do?

First, check your spam or junk folder for the reset email, and make sure you are entering the email or phone number that was linked to the account. If the hacker changed your recovery details, use Facebook's hacked-account page at facebook.com/hacked, or Instagram's "Need more help?" option on the login screen, which can verify you with an ID or a video selfie.

### How long does it take to recover a hacked account?

If you can still access your email or phone number, recovery takes a few minutes. If the hacker changed your recovery details and you need identity verification, it can take a few days while the platform reviews your ID or video selfie. Submit your request once and wait — sending it many times can slow things down.

### Will Facebook or Instagram ask me to pay to recover my account?

No. Account recovery is free on both platforms. Anyone asking you to pay for recovery — whether by transfer, gift card or any other method — is almost certainly a scammer. Only use the official recovery pages inside the app or on the platform's own website.

### The hacker is messaging my friends asking for money. What can I do?

Warn your friends as quickly as you can, from another account or through someone else, and ask them to ignore and report the messages. Once you recover the account, post that you were hacked and that the messages were not from you. Friends can also report the messages to Facebook or Instagram, which helps the platform act faster.

### I got an email saying my email address was changed. Can I undo it?

Yes — both Facebook and Instagram send a message to your old email address when your email is changed, with a link to reverse the change. Open that email and use the reversal link as soon as possible, because it only works for a limited time.

### What is two-factor authentication and should I turn it on?

Two-factor authentication means logging in needs your password plus a second code, usually from an app on your phone or sent by SMS. You should absolutely turn it on — it is the most effective protection against your account being taken over, because a stolen password alone is no longer enough to get in.

### Can a hacked account be recovered if I do not have a government ID?

It is harder, but try the recovery options that use your email or phone number first — those do not need an ID. If those fail, Instagram's video selfie check may work without an ID, since it compares your face with photos on the account. Having an ID ready makes the process much smoother, so keep one available.

### How do I protect my business page if my personal account is hacked?

Always have at least two admins on your business page, so the second admin can remove a hacked account and keep the page safe. Check the page's admin list regularly, turn on two-factor authentication on every admin's account, and review ad account spending often so you notice unauthorised adverts quickly.

## What to Do Next

If your account is hacked right now, start the recovery immediately — do not wait. Try the password reset first, then the hacked-account page on Facebook or the "Need more help?" option on Instagram. Check your email for the message about your email being changed and use the reversal link if it is there. While you wait, warn your friends and customers so nobody falls for messages from the hacker.

Once you are back in, change your password, turn on two-factor authentication, log out of every device you do not recognise, and check your email, phone number and payment settings. Then make two-factor authentication a habit on every important account, especially your email.
