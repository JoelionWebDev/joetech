Tax is the topic most Nigerian small business owners avoid. They worry it will be expensive, complicated, or an invitation for officials to come knocking. So they stay informal, take payments into personal accounts, and hope nobody notices. The problem is that staying invisible has a cost of its own: no corporate bank account, no contracts with serious clients, no loans, and a constant fear that a tax issue will surface at the worst possible moment.

The reality is much less frightening than the rumours. Getting a Tax Identification Number, a TIN, is free. Small businesses with low turnover pay little or no company income tax. And the Federal Inland Revenue Service, the FIRS, now lets you register and file online.

## What is a TIN and why do you need one?

A Tax Identification Number is a unique number the FIRS issues to identify you or your business for tax purposes. Think of it as your business's tax identity card. It is a 10-digit number for companies and an 11-digit number for individuals in many cases, and once issued it stays with the business for life.

You need a TIN for almost every formal step your business will take. Banks ask for it before opening a corporate account. Corporate clients ask for it before paying you, because they need it for their own tax records. You need it to bid for government contracts, to apply for many grants and loans, and to register for VAT. Without a TIN, your business is effectively locked out of the formal economy.

A TIN is also how the FIRS knows you exist and can track your filings. That sounds intimidating, but it is actually protection: a business with a TIN and a record of filing on time is far less likely to face penalties or surprise assessments than one the tax authority discovers on its own.

## Who needs a TIN?

Every registered business in Nigeria needs a TIN. If you have registered a business name or a limited company with the Corporate Affairs Commission, your next step should be getting a TIN. Sole traders operating under a registered business name need one too, and many freelancers who earn regularly from clients are now asked for a TIN as well.

If you are an individual earning income, you also have a tax identity through your state tax authority, and your National Identity Number is increasingly linked to tax records. The principle is the same: if you earn money regularly, the tax system expects you to be identified and to file.

## Documents you need before applying

The FIRS application is straightforward, but having your documents ready makes it far quicker. Gather the following before you start.

For a registered business or company, you will need your CAC certificate of incorporation or business name registration, your CAC status report for a company, a valid means of identification for the directors or proprietor, such as a National Identity Number slip, an international passport or a driver's licence, and the business address, email address and phone number. You will also need your memorandum and articles of association for a company, and the particulars of directors where applicable.

For an individual applying as a sole trader, you will need your valid ID, a passport photograph, your business name registration if you have one, and your contact details.

Have your CAC registration number ready, because the FIRS will verify your business against CAC records. If your business is newly registered, give the system a day or two after your CAC certificate is issued before applying, so the records can sync.

## How to get your TIN online

The FIRS has an online self-service portal called TaxPro Max, and it is the main route for TIN registration and tax filing. Here is the process, step by step.

First, visit the TaxPro Max portal at taxpromax.firs.gov.ng. Click on the option to register and select the type of taxpayer you are: company, enterprise or individual. Fill in your basic details, including your CAC registration number, business name, date of registration, email address and phone number.

Next, upload the required documents: your CAC certificate, your identification and any other document the portal requests. Make sure the uploads are clear and readable, because a blurry certificate is the most common reason applications are delayed.

Then submit the application. The FIRS will review it, and if everything matches, your TIN will be issued. In many cases this takes a few working days. You can log back into the portal to check the status, and your TIN will appear on your TaxPro Max dashboard once it is approved.

If the online process fails or your details do not verify, visit the nearest FIRS tax office with your documents. Offices exist in Lagos, Abuja and every state capital, and the tax officers there can register you manually. Take the originals and photocopies of all your documents, and arrive early.

Your TIN is free. The FIRS charges no fee to issue a TIN, and you should never pay an agent a large sum simply to "get" a TIN for you. If someone offers to sell you a TIN quickly for tens of thousands of naira, be cautious and use the official portal or visit an FIRS office yourself.

## How to get a TIN through the Joint Tax Board

Some small business owners get their TIN through the Joint Tax Board route, which issues TINs at the state level. A TIN issued through the JTB is valid. If you are unsure whether you already have a TIN, check on the TaxPro Max portal or ask at an FIRS office before applying for a new one, because duplicate records take time to fix.

## Which taxes does a small business pay?

This is where most small business owners get confused, because Nigeria has several taxes and they apply to different people. Here are the main ones that affect a small business.

Company Income Tax, or CIT, is the tax on the profits of a limited company. The rate depends on turnover. Under the current law, companies with an annual turnover below ₦25 million pay zero per cent company income tax. Companies with turnover between ₦25 million and ₦100 million pay 20 per cent, and larger companies pay 30 per cent. This means that a genuinely small business with turnover below ₦25 million owes no company income tax, although it still needs to file a return.

Personal Income Tax applies to sole traders and partnerships, because the business income is treated as the owner's personal income. It is collected by the state tax authority, such as the Lagos State Internal Revenue Service, and the rate is graduated, meaning it rises with income.

Value Added Tax, VAT, is charged at 7.5 per cent on most goods and services. A business must register for VAT and charge it to customers when its turnover reaches the threshold set by law, which is ₦25 million in a calendar year. If your turnover is below that threshold, you are not required to register for VAT, although you may choose to register voluntarily if your customers are large companies that prefer dealing with VAT-registered suppliers.

Withholding Tax is tax deducted at source when certain payments are made, for example rent, consultancy fees or contract payments. If a corporate client pays you ₦500,000 for a service, they may deduct a percentage as withholding tax and remit it to the FIRS, giving you a credit note. That credit counts against your final tax liability, so keep every withholding tax credit note you receive.

## How to file and pay your taxes

Filing means telling the tax authority what you earned and what you owe, and paying means sending the money. Both now happen online through TaxPro Max.

For a company, you must file your annual company income tax return within six months after the end of your financial year. If your financial year ends on 31 December, your return is due by 30 June the following year. You will need audited financial statements prepared by a chartered accountant for a limited company.

For VAT-registered businesses, VAT returns are filed monthly, by the 21st day of the following month. You charge VAT on your sales, deduct the VAT you paid on your business purchases, and remit the difference to the FIRS through TaxPro Max. Keep every VAT invoice you issue and receive.

Payment is made through the TaxPro Max portal using a debit card, bank transfer or the payment channels the portal supports. After paying, download and save your receipt and your e-tax clearance certificate when it is issued. These documents are what banks and clients ask for when they want proof that your business is tax compliant.

If your turnover is below the company income tax threshold, you still file a return showing your income and expenses, and your tax payable will be zero. Filing a nil return on time keeps your record clean and avoids late filing penalties, which can be substantial. A business that never files is a business that looks non-compliant, even if it owes nothing.

## Keeping records your accountant will love

Good tax compliance starts with good records, and you do not need expensive software to keep them. A simple system you actually use beats a fancy system you ignore.

Record every sale, whether it came through a bank transfer, a Paystack or Flutterwave payment, or cash. Record every expense with a receipt: rent, data, transport, materials, salaries, and subscriptions. Keep your bank statements, because they are the easiest way to reconcile what came in and what went out.

Open a separate bank account for the business and keep personal spending out of it. Mixing personal and business money in one account is the single biggest cause of messy tax records for small businesses, and it makes your accountant's job harder and your tax bill less predictable.

Use a simple spreadsheet if you cannot afford accounting software yet. Create one sheet for income, one for expenses, and update them weekly rather than trying to reconstruct a whole year in June. Free tools such as Google Sheets work well for this, and they let your accountant view the same file from anywhere.

## Penalties for late filing and non-compliance

The FIRS charges penalties for filing late or not filing at all, and these penalties apply even when you owe no tax. Late filing of a company income tax return attracts a penalty that starts at ₦50,000 for the first month and increases for each additional month, and interest accrues on unpaid tax. VAT returns filed late attract their own penalties. These figures can change, so confirm the current penalty regime on the FIRS website, but the principle never changes: filing late is expensive, and filing on time is free.

The simplest way to avoid penalties is to put your filing dates in your calendar the moment your financial year ends. A business that files on time every year builds a compliance record that opens doors, because banks, clients and government agencies all ask for tax clearance before they deal with you.

If you have not filed for several years, do not panic and do not hide. Visit an FIRS office or speak to a tax consultant about regularising your position. A voluntary disclosure is almost always treated better than a discovery during an audit, and the longer you wait, the larger the penalties grow.

## Your tax clearance certificate

A tax clearance certificate is the document that proves you have filed and paid your taxes. It is issued by the FIRS for companies, or by your state tax authority for individuals and sole traders, and it is valid for one year. You will need it to open and maintain some corporate bank accounts, to bid for government contracts, to apply for certain licences, and to show clients who ask for proof of compliance.

To get one, you must have filed your returns and paid any tax due for the relevant years. Apply through TaxPro Max or at your tax office, and keep the certificate where you can find it. Renew it every year as part of your normal filing routine, so you are never caught without it when an opportunity appears.

## Should you hire a tax consultant?

Many small business owners ask whether they need an accountant or a tax consultant. The honest answer is that it depends on the size and complexity of your business. If you are a sole trader with a handful of clients and simple income and expenses, you can manage your own filing with careful records and the TaxPro Max portal.

If you run a limited company, have employees, are VAT registered, or deal with corporate clients who deduct withholding tax, a tax consultant will save you money and stress. A good consultant will prepare your financial statements, file your returns on time, claim every deduction you are entitled to, and keep you out of penalty trouble. Ask other business owners in your area for a recommendation, and choose a consultant who is registered with a recognised professional body.

Even if you hire a consultant, stay involved. Understand what is being filed in your name, ask questions, and keep copies of every return and receipt. Your tax record belongs to you, and you are responsible for it even when someone else files on your behalf.

## Common tax mistakes small businesses make

The first mistake is not registering for a TIN at all. Without one, you cannot open a corporate account or work with serious clients, and you remain invisible to the formal economy.

The second mistake is never filing because turnover is low. Small businesses often owe little or no tax, but the FIRS still expects a return. A nil return filed on time costs nothing and keeps you compliant.

The third mistake is ignoring withholding tax credit notes. Every time a client deducts withholding tax from your payment, ask for the credit note and keep it. Those credits reduce your final tax bill, and losing them is like throwing money away.

The fourth mistake is mixing personal and business money. When your business account is also your personal spending account, your records become impossible to reconcile, and your tax filings become guesswork.

The fifth mistake is waiting until a contract or loan application demands a tax clearance certificate before starting to comply. By then, you may need to file several years of back returns under pressure. Start now, while the stakes are low.

## Your tax compliance checklist

- Register your business with the CAC
- Apply for your TIN through TaxPro Max or an FIRS office
- Check whether you already have a TIN before applying twice
- Open a corporate bank account and keep business money separate
- Record every sale and expense, and keep all receipts
- Know your filing dates: annual company return within six months of year end, VAT monthly by the 21st
- File even when you owe nothing
- Keep every withholding tax credit note
- Renew your tax clearance certificate every year
- Consider a tax consultant once your business grows

## Frequently asked questions

**Is getting a TIN really free?** Yes. The FIRS does not charge a fee to issue a TIN. Anyone asking you to pay a large sum just to obtain a TIN is not using the official process.

**How long does it take to get a TIN?** Online applications through TaxPro Max are often processed within a few working days if your documents verify cleanly. Manual applications at an FIRS office can take a little longer.

**My turnover is below ₦25 million. Do I still need to file?** Yes. You still need to file a return, even if your tax payable is zero. Filing on time keeps your record clean and your tax clearance certificate available.

**Do I need to register for VAT as a small business?** Not until your turnover reaches the ₦25 million threshold in a calendar year. Below that, VAT registration is optional. Once you cross the threshold, register promptly.

**What if I have not paid tax for years?** Visit an FIRS office or speak to a tax consultant about regularising your filings. Voluntary disclosure is treated far better than being discovered.

## Final word

Tax compliance is not a punishment for success; it is part of running a real business. Getting a TIN is free, filing is mostly online, and a small business with modest turnover often owes little or no company income tax. Take the steps in this guide, keep simple records, file on time even when you owe nothing, and let your tax clearance certificate speak for your business. A compliant business is a bankable business.
