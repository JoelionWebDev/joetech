Ask a Nigerian buyer how she shops online and the answer is almost always WhatsApp. She sees a product on Instagram, taps through to WhatsApp, asks the price, asks the size, asks the delivery fee, and — if the seller answers quickly and clearly — she pays. WhatsApp is not a side channel for Nigerian small businesses. For many of them, it is the shop itself.

The problem is that most sellers run that shop badly. Products live in scattered chats. The same questions get asked and answered a hundred times a day. A customer asks for the price of a shoe you posted three weeks ago, and you scroll for ten minutes to find it. Meanwhile she has moved on to a seller who answered faster.

A WhatsApp Business catalog fixes this. It is a free, built-in shopfront inside WhatsApp: a list of your products with photos, prices, descriptions and links, that any customer can browse without asking you a single question first. Set it up once, and it works for you every day.

This guide walks you through setting up your catalog step by step, filling it with products that sell, and using it to take more orders with less chatting.

## What Is a WhatsApp Business Catalog?

A catalog is a collection of your products inside your WhatsApp Business profile. When a customer opens your profile or taps "View catalog" in a chat, she sees your products laid out like a small online shop: photo, name, price, and a short description for each item. She can tap a product, read about it, and send you a message about that exact item with one tap.

That last part is the key. When a customer messages you from a catalog product, her message arrives with the product attached — you both know exactly which item she means. No more "the red one" or "the shoe you posted last week". The confusion disappears, and the conversation starts at the buying stage instead of the explaining stage.

The catalog is free. It works on Android and iPhone. It works on MTN, Airtel, Glo or 9mobile data, and customers can browse it even when your own phone is off, because the catalog lives on WhatsApp's servers, not on your device. You can add up to 500 products, which is more than most Nigerian SMEs will ever need.

## WhatsApp Business vs. Regular WhatsApp

Before you build a catalog, make sure you are using the right app. A catalog only exists in WhatsApp Business, the free app made for businesses. Regular WhatsApp does not have it.

WhatsApp Business also gives you tools that regular WhatsApp does not:

- **A business profile** with your shop name, address, opening hours, email, website, and a description of what you sell.
- **Quick replies** — saved messages for the questions you answer fifty times a day, like your price list or delivery fees. Type a shortcut and the full message appears.
- **Labels** — coloured tags for chats, so you can mark customers as "New", "Paid", "Pending delivery", "Owes balance", or "VIP".
- **Automated messages** — a greeting message for new customers and an away message for when you are closed or busy.
- **Message statistics** — how many messages were sent, delivered, read and replied to.

If you currently sell through regular WhatsApp, switching is straightforward. Download WhatsApp Business from the Play Store or App Store, verify the same phone number, and your chats transfer over. Your customers will not notice a change, except that your profile now looks professional and your catalog appears.

One warning: use a dedicated business number if you can. Mixing your personal chats with business chats on the same number is how orders get lost among family messages. A second SIM or a dual-SIM phone solves it, and the cost is a small price for a business that never loses an order to a birthday message.

## Step 1: Set Up Your Business Profile Properly

Your catalog sits inside your business profile, so build the profile first. Open WhatsApp Business, tap the three dots, then Settings, then your business name to open the profile.

**Business name.** Use the name customers know you by, exactly as it appears on your Instagram and your shop sign. Consistency builds trust; a different name on every platform looks like a different business.

**Profile photo.** Use your logo, or a clear photo of your shop or your best product. Avoid a personal selfie as the business photo — customers are buying from a business, and a logo tells them so in one glance.

**Address.** Add your shop address, area and city. If you sell from home, add the area only — "Lekki Phase 1, Lagos" is enough. Customers need to know where you are, and whether pickup is possible, but your exact house number does not belong in a public profile.

**Category.** Choose the closest business category: Clothing Store, Beauty Salon, Food and Drink, Electronics, and so on. The category helps WhatsApp and Google understand what you sell.

**Description.** You have a short space to say what you sell and why a customer should buy from you. Write it like a shop sign: "Quality Ankara and ready-to-wear for women in Lagos. Nationwide delivery. Pay on delivery within Lagos." Include your main products, your location, and your strongest promise.

**Hours.** Set your opening hours honestly. If you reply to messages between 8am and 8pm, say so. An away message will handle customers who write at midnight, but honest hours set the expectation.

**Email and website.** Add your business email and your website if you have one. A website link in the profile is a trust signal — it tells the customer you are a real, established business.

Take your time with the profile. It is the first thing a new customer sees, and first impressions decide whether she browses your catalog or closes the chat.

## Step 2: Add Your First Products to the Catalog

Now the main work. In WhatsApp Business, tap the three dots, then Business tools, then Catalog, then Add new item. You will see fields for each product. Fill them carefully — this is your shopfront.

**Product image.** Add up to ten images per product. The first image is the most important: it is the one customers see in the catalog grid. Use a clear photo of the real product, in good light, against a plain background. Photograph clothes on a hanger or a person, shoes from the front and side, food in a clean container with the portion visible. Avoid blurry photos, dark rooms, and stock photos of a product you do not actually have.

**Product name.** Name the product the way a customer would search for it: "Ankara Midi Dress — Navy Blue" is better than "Dress 1". Include the key detail — colour, size range, or material — in the name itself, so the catalog grid is informative at a glance.

**Price.** Enter the real price in naira. This is not the place for "DM for price". A catalog without prices is a shop with no price tags, and customers walk out of shops with no price tags. If a product has different prices by size, state the starting price and explain the variation in the description.

**Description.** You have 5,000 characters, but use far fewer — three to five short lines. Cover the material, the sizes available, what is included, and the one benefit that matters most. Example: "Soft 100% cotton Ankara. Sizes 8–16. True to size, breathable in Lagos heat. Model wears size 12. Perfect for weddings, church and owambe parties."

**Link.** If the product is also on your website, Jumia, or an Instagram post, add the link. Customers who want more detail can follow it. If you have no website, leave it blank — the catalog works fine without it.

**Product code.** Use your own simple code, like "DRS-001" for dresses or "SHO-014" for shoes. It helps you and the customer refer to the exact product, and it helps you track stock later.

Add your best-selling products first — the ten or twenty items that earn most of your money. A catalog with twenty excellent listings beats a catalog with two hundred rushed ones. You can add the rest over the following days, a few at a time.

## Step 3: Organise Products Into Collections

Once you have more than twenty products, a single long list becomes hard to browse. Collections fix that. A collection is a group of related products with a name: "Ankara Dresses", "Men's Sandals", "Hair Bundles", "Skincare", "New Arrivals", "Sale Items".

To create a collection, open the Catalog, tap the three dots, then Create collection. Name it clearly, add a short description, and choose the products that belong in it. A customer can then browse by collection instead of scrolling through everything you sell.

Good collection ideas for Nigerian sellers:

- **By product type:** Dresses, Tops, Trousers, Shoes, Bags.
- **By occasion:** Wedding Guest, Church, Office, Owambe, Casual.
- **By price:** Under ₦10,000, ₦10,000–₦25,000, Premium.
- **By status:** New Arrivals, Best Sellers, Last Pieces, Clearance.
- **By audience:** Women, Men, Children, Unisex.

Keep the number of collections small — five to eight is enough. Too many collections are as confusing as no collections. And keep them updated: when a product sells out, remove it from the collection or mark it clearly, so a customer never tries to buy something that is gone.

## Step 4: Share Your Catalog Everywhere

A catalog nobody sees sells nothing. WhatsApp gives you several ways to share it — use all of them.

**Your catalog link.** Every catalog has a shareable link. Find it in Business tools, then Catalog, then the share icon. Copy it and put it everywhere your customers already find you: your Instagram bio, your Facebook page, your TikTok bio, your email signature, and your website. A customer who taps that link lands directly on your products.

**In chats.** When a customer asks "what do you have?", do not type a long list. Send the catalog link, or send a specific product from the catalog using the attachment button. One tap, and she is browsing instead of waiting for you to type.

**On your status.** WhatsApp Status is free advertising to everyone who has your number. Post a product photo from your catalog with the price and a note: "New in the catalog — tap to view and order." Status posts disappear in 24 hours, so post regularly, and post your best products when your customers are most active — early morning and evening.

**In groups — carefully.** If you are in a buy-and-sell WhatsApp group, share a single product or the catalog link with a short message, following the group's rules. Never spam a group with ten products at once. One good product, posted at the right time, with a clear price, earns orders. Ten products posted at 2am earns removal from the group.

**As a QR code.** WhatsApp Business can generate a QR code for your business. Print it on your shop sign, your packaging, your business cards, and your flyers. A customer at your market stall scans it and lands on your catalog. **In your broadcasts.** If you use broadcast lists for customers who have opted in, send a short message when new products arrive in the catalog: "New Ankara collection just added — 12 new designs from ₦18,000. Tap to view." Keep broadcasts rare and valuable — once a week at most — or customers will block you.

## Step 5: Set Up Quick Replies, Labels and Greeting Messages

Your catalog brings customers to your door. These tools make sure the conversation that follows is fast, organised, and professional.

**Quick replies.** Think of the ten messages you type most often: your price list, your delivery fees, your bank details, your location, your exchange policy, your opening hours. Save each one as a quick reply with a short command like /price, /delivery, /account, /location. When a customer asks, type the slash command and the full message appears. You answer in seconds instead of minutes, and your answers are always complete and consistent.

**Labels.** Create labels for the stages of a sale: "New enquiry", "To pay", "Paid", "Packing", "Delivered", "Owes balance", "VIP customer". Tap and hold a chat to assign a label. At a glance, your chat list becomes a sales pipeline: who has paid, who is waiting, who owes you money. Every morning, filter by "To pay" and follow up. Every evening, filter by "Packing" and make sure those orders go out.

**Greeting message.** Set an automatic greeting for customers who message you for the first time or after fourteen days of silence: "Hello, welcome to [your business name]. Browse our catalog to see our products and prices, or tell us what you are looking for and we will help you." It answers instantly, even at 2am, and points the customer straight to the catalog.

**Away message.** Set an away message for when you are closed: "Thank you for messaging [your business name]. We are currently closed and will reply by 8am. Meanwhile, you can browse our catalog here: [link]." The customer is not ignored, and she has something useful to do while she waits.

## Step 6: Take Orders Cleanly From the Catalog

When a customer taps a product in your catalog and messages you about it, the conversation starts well. Your job is to keep it clean and finish the sale.

**Confirm the details in one message.** Reply with the product name, size or variant, price, delivery fee, and total — all in one message. "Ankara Midi Dress, size 12, navy blue — ₦28,000. Delivery to Ikeja ₦2,000. Total ₦30,000. Please send your full name, phone number and delivery address to confirm." One message, everything the buyer needs, no back and forth.

**Take payment and confirm it.** When payment arrives, confirm it immediately with the order number and the delivery date: "Payment received, thank you. Order ORD-058 confirmed. Delivery Thursday between 10am and 2pm." Then label the chat "Paid" and move it to "Packing" when the order is packed.

**Share delivery updates.** When the order is dispatched, send a message with the courier or rider details and the expected time. When it is delivered, ask for confirmation and a quick review. These three messages — payment confirmed, order dispatched, delivery confirmed — are the difference between a customer who trusts you and a customer who spends the day worrying.

**Keep a simple order record.** The catalog does not track orders for you. Keep a small notebook, a Google Sheet, or a notes app list with the order number, customer name, product, amount, amount paid, balance, and delivery status. Five minutes a day of record-keeping prevents the lost orders and forgotten balances that cost Nigerian sellers real money.




## Final Word

Nigerian buyers already live on WhatsApp. The sellers who win are not the ones shouting loudest in the group chats; they are the ones whose shop is easiest to browse, whose prices are visible, and whose replies are fast and clear. A WhatsApp Business catalog gives you all three, for free, in an afternoon.

Set up your profile today, add your best ten products this week, and share the catalog everywhere your customers already are. The next time a customer asks "what do you have?", you will not type a long list. You will send one link — and she will do the browsing herself.
