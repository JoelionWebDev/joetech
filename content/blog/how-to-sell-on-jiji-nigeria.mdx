If you have a phone, a product, and a little patience, you can start selling on Jiji today without paying a kobo to list. Jiji is Nigeria's largest online classifieds platform, where millions of buyers search every day for phones, cars, furniture, fashion, electronics, and property. Unlike Jumia or Konga, you do not need a registered business, a warehouse, or inventory software to begin. You list an item, buyers contact you, and you agree on payment and delivery.

That simplicity is also the trap: buyers are cautious, competition is fierce, and scammers are active. The sellers who make real money are not the ones with the most products, but the ones with clear photos, honest descriptions, fair prices, and fast replies.

This guide walks you through the whole process, step by step: setting up your account, choosing what to sell, writing listings that show up in search, pricing for profit, taking photos that sell, handling buyers safely, delivering across Lagos and beyond, and growing from a few sales a week into a steady income.

## What Jiji Is — and What It Is Not

Jiji is a classified ads marketplace, not a store. When you list a phone for ₦150,000, Jiji does not buy it from you, hold your money, or deliver it. Jiji connects you with the buyer. You agree the price, the payment method, and the delivery. That is why Jiji is free to list on, and why trust matters so much.

The platform makes its money from optional paid promotions: boosting an ad, marking it as VIP or Top, and business packages for sellers who list many items. You can sell without paying for any of these, especially when you are starting, but promotions help your ad appear above free listings in search results.

Jiji is strongest in Lagos, Abuja, Port Harcourt, and Ibadan, where most buyers want to inspect an item in person. If you live in a smaller town, expect more delivery enquiries and be ready to ship with a trusted courier.

## Step 1: Create Your Jiji Account

Start on the Jiji website at jiji.ng or download the Jiji app from the Google Play Store or Apple App Store. The app is better for most sellers because you can take photos, reply to chats, and get notifications instantly.

Tap "Register" and sign up with your phone number or email address. Use a phone number you actually answer — buyers call and send WhatsApp messages, and a missed call is often a lost sale. Verify the number with the code Jiji sends you, then complete your profile: your name or business name, your location, and a clear profile photo or logo.

Choose your location carefully. Buyers filter by city and area, so a seller in Lekki should say Lekki, Lagos, not just Lagos. If you deliver across Lagos, say so in your ads, but keep your location honest — a buyer in Ikeja who discovers you are actually in Ajah may cancel the deal.

If you plan to sell regularly, use a dedicated WhatsApp Business number so customer enquiries stay separate from personal chats. Add a short profile description of what you sell, for example: "UK-used iPhones and Samsung phones, tested and guaranteed, Lekki, Lagos. Delivery available."

## Step 2: Decide What to Sell

You can sell almost anything legal on Jiji, but some categories move much faster than others. The most active categories are mobile phones and tablets, vehicles and vehicle parts, electronics, home furniture and appliances, fashion and beauty, and real estate. Phones are the fastest-moving category of all, because buyers search for them constantly and decide quickly.

Before you list anything, check what is already selling. Search Jiji for the item you want to sell and look at the prices, the photos, and how many views the top ads have. If twenty sellers are offering the same Infinix phone at ₦95,000, you need a reason for a buyer to choose you: a lower price, a better condition, a warranty, free delivery, or a free accessory.

Start with products you understand and can source reliably — your knowledge becomes your advantage in the chat, where most sales are won or lost.

Here are practical starting options for a beginner with little capital:

**Sell what you already own.** Old phones, a laptop you no longer use, furniture from a relocation, baby items your child has outgrown. This costs nothing, teaches you how Jiji works, and gives you your first reviews and confidence.

**Buy and resell UK-used phones or laptops.** This is the classic Jiji business. Buy from trusted suppliers at Computer Village in Ikeja, test each device thoroughly, and resell with a small markup. A phone bought for ₦120,000 and sold for ₦145,000 is a realistic margin if the condition is good.

**Sell new items from wholesalers.** Fashion, shoes, bags, hair, cosmetics, and phone accessories can be bought in bulk from markets like Balogun, Mandilas, or Onitsha Main Market, or ordered from suppliers on Instagram and WhatsApp. Start with ten to twenty pieces of a fast-moving item rather than a hundred pieces of something untested.

Whatever you choose, do not list items you cannot supply. An ad for a product you do not have wastes the buyer's time, earns you a bad reputation, and can get your account flagged.

## Step 3: Write a Listing That Shows Up in Search

Your ad title is the most important line you will write. Buyers search with specific words, and Jiji shows ads that match those words. A vague title like "Nice phone for sale" will never be found. A specific title like "iPhone 12 Pro Max 256GB — Factory Unlocked, Excellent Condition" will be.

Follow this formula for titles: **Brand + Model + Key Detail + Condition**. Examples: "Samsung Galaxy A54 5G 128GB — Neat, With Charger", "HP EliteBook 840 G8 Core i7 16GB RAM — UK Used", "3-Seater Leather Sofa — Barely Used, Lekki".

Your description should answer the questions a buyer would ask before messaging you. Include the brand and model, the condition, what is included in the box, the reason for selling, your location, delivery options, and whether the price is negotiable. Be honest about faults. A small scratch mentioned in the description builds trust; a scratch discovered at inspection kills the sale and your reputation.

Write in short paragraphs or bullet points, not one long block of text. Buyers scan on their phones. Start with the most important facts, and end with a clear call to action: "Call or WhatsApp 0803 123 4567. Available for inspection in Lekki. Delivery across Lagos."

Choose the right category and subcategory, and fill in every field honestly — a phone listed under electronics instead of mobile phones will be seen by fewer buyers.

## Step 4: Price It to Sell — and Still Make a Profit

Pricing is where most beginners fail. Price too high and nobody messages you. Price too low and you lose money or attract buyers who think the item is fake.

Research first. Search Jiji for the same item in the same condition and note the range. If similar iPhone 12s sell between ₦280,000 and ₦350,000, listing yours at ₦320,000 is reasonable if the condition is good. Listing at ₦450,000 means you will be ignored. Listing at ₦180,000 means buyers will suspect a scam.

Work out your true cost before you set a price. Your cost is not just what you paid for the item. Add transport, data and airtime for replies, packaging, delivery fees if you offer free delivery, and any paid promotion you plan to use. If you bought a phone for ₦200,000 and spent ₦5,000 on transport and ₦3,000 on a Jiji boost, your real cost is ₦208,000. Selling at ₦230,000 gives you ₦22,000 profit, not ₦30,000.

Decide whether your price is negotiable, and say so in the ad. "Price: ₦150,000 (slightly negotiable)" invites offers without inviting insults. "Price: ₦150,000 (fixed)" tells serious buyers not to waste time haggling. If you accept negotiation, decide your lowest acceptable price in advance, and never go below it in the heat of a chat.

## Step 5: Take Photos That Sell the Item

On Jiji, photos do most of the selling. Buyers decide in seconds whether to open your ad, and they decide from the first photo. Blurry, dark, or cluttered photos lose sales before your description is ever read.

You do not need a professional camera. A clean smartphone camera is enough if you follow a few rules. Clean the item first — wipe the screen, dust the furniture, iron the clothes. Photograph in daylight, near a window or outside, never in a dark room with a flash. Use a plain background: a white wall, a clean table, or a bedsheet. Remove clutter from the frame.

Take at least six photos from different angles: front, back, sides, close-ups of important details, and the item working — a phone screen switched on, a laptop showing its desktop, a chair from the side. For phones and laptops, photograph the screen, the ports, and any scratches honestly. For clothes and shoes, show the label, the size tag, and the item worn or laid flat.

Never use photos downloaded from Google or from another seller — buyers distrust stock photos, and your own real photos prove you actually have the item.

## Step 6: Post Your Ad

Posting is simple. Tap "Sell" or "Post a free ad", choose the category, upload your photos, write your title and description, set your price, and add your location and contact details. Review everything before you publish: a wrong price or a typo in your phone number can cost you sales.

Jiji reviews new ads before they go live, usually within a few minutes to a few hours. If your ad is rejected, read the reason — it is usually a prohibited item, a photo problem, or a category error — fix it, and repost.

Post in the evening, between 6pm and 10pm, or at weekends, when most buyers browse. If an ad gets no views after two or three days, improve the first photo, adjust the title, or lower the price slightly.

Do not post the same item several times: duplicate ads annoy buyers and can get your account restricted. One good ad per item is the rule.

## Step 7: Handle Buyer Enquiries Like a Professional

Most of your sales will be decided in the chat. A buyer messages five sellers at once; the one who replies first, answers clearly, and sounds trustworthy usually gets the sale.

Reply fast. Aim to answer within five minutes during the day. Jiji shows buyers when you are online and how quickly you usually reply, and buyers prefer sellers who respond promptly. If you cannot reply immediately, send a short message: "Hello, thank you for your message. I will reply in 10 minutes." Then keep that promise.

Answer the actual question. If the buyer asks "Is it still available?", say yes or no — do not reply with "Call me" or send a sticker. If they ask about the battery health, the RAM, or the size, give the exact answer. Short, direct answers build confidence.

Stay on Jiji chat for the first conversation, especially with a new buyer. It creates a record of what was agreed, and Jiji can help if there is a dispute. Moving to WhatsApp is normal for sharing a location or arranging delivery, but agree the price in the Jiji chat first.

Be polite and patient with negotiation. A buyer offering ₦100,000 for your ₦150,000 item is not insulting you; they are testing the price. Counter firmly and politely: "Thank you, but the lowest I can go is ₦140,000, and it comes with a charger and a pouch." If they walk away, thank them and move on. Another buyer will come.

Never send your bank details or your home address to a stranger before you have agreed a price and a meeting plan. Never pay any fee to a buyer. And never let a buyer rush you into a decision with "I will pay now if you send it immediately." Pressure is a classic scam tactic.

## Step 8: Payment and Delivery — Get Paid Safely

This is the step where money is lost. Follow these rules every time.

**For in-person sales, meet in a safe public place.** A busy mall, a bank branch, a police station entrance, or your shop if you have one. Bring a friend if the item is valuable. Let the buyer inspect the item properly, then collect payment before you hand it over — not after.

**Confirm payment before you release the item.** If the buyer pays by transfer, wait until the money shows in your account or you receive your bank's alert, not just a screenshot. Screenshots can be faked. For large amounts, ask the buyer to transfer while you are together, and confirm it on your own banking app before you leave.

**Avoid cash where possible.** Bank transfer leaves a record. If you must accept cash, count it carefully at the bank or in a safe place, and check for fake notes on large denominations.

**For delivery, use trusted dispatch riders or couriers.** In Lagos, services like GIG Logistics, Kwik Delivery, and local dispatch riders can deliver within hours. Agree who pays the delivery fee before you dispatch. Many sellers charge the buyer for delivery, or include it in the price and say "free delivery within Lekki."

## Step 9: Avoid Scams — Protect Yourself and Your Buyers

Scams are the biggest risk on Jiji, for sellers and buyers alike. Learn the common patterns.

**The fake payment alert.** A buyer sends you a fake bank alert or a doctored screenshot claiming they have paid. Always check your own bank app or balance. Never release goods based on a screenshot.

**The overpayment trick.** A buyer "mistakenly" sends more than the price and asks you to refund the difference. The original payment is fake or will be reversed, and your refund is real money lost. Never refund an overpayment until your bank confirms the money is genuinely in your account and cleared.

**The delivery agent scam.** A buyer insists on sending "their own dispatch rider" who will pay you on collection — and the rider arrives, collects the item, and disappears. Use your own rider, or meet the buyer yourself.

**The abroad buyer.** Someone claims to be abroad, wants to buy your item as a gift, and asks you to pay a courier fee or a customs fee first. This is almost always a scam. A genuine buyer pays for the item first.

## Step 10: Grow From Beginner to Steady Seller

Your first few sales are the hardest. After that, you have a system. Here is how to grow.

**Keep your best ads active.** Ads expire, so repost or renew them regularly. Your ads with the most views and enquiries are your winners — improve them first when you promote.

**Consider paid promotion once you know what sells.** A Jiji boost puts your ad at the top of search results for a few days. Test it on one proven product first: spend ₦2,000 to ₦5,000 on a boost and check whether the extra enquiries turn into sales. If the maths works, promote more. If it does not, fix the photos or the price before you spend again.

**Expand to your own channels.** Create an Instagram page or a WhatsApp Business catalogue showing your products, and direct happy Jiji buyers there for future purchases. A QR code linking to your catalogue works well on a shop banner or flyer, and you can create one free at joetech360.ng/tools/qr-code-generator.

**Track what works.** Each week, note which items sold, which ads got the most views, which prices attracted buyers, and which enquiries wasted your time. Over a month, a clear pattern appears: certain products, prices, and photos sell, and others do not. Spend your time and money on the winners.

**Stay consistent.** The sellers who earn steadily on Jiji are not the ones who post fifty ads in one day and disappear for a month. They post a few good ads every day, reply quickly, deliver reliably, and build a name buyers trust. In a marketplace where anyone can list, trust is the product that sells everything else.

## Conclusion

Selling on Jiji is a low-cost way to start an online business in Nigeria. You need no shop, no registration, and no capital beyond the items you already own or can source cheaply. What you do need is a good account, honest listings, clear photos, fair prices, fast replies, and safe payment habits.

Start small: list one or two items this week, learn from the enquiries you receive, and reinvest your first profits into better stock. With consistency, Jiji can grow from a side hustle into a reliable income — one honest sale at a time.
