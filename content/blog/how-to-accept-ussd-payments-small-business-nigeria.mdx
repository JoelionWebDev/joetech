A customer walks into a small shop in Surulere, picks up a pair of shoes worth ₦15,000, and reaches for her phone to pay. Her data is finished, the shop's Wi-Fi is down, and her banking app will not open. In the past, that sale was lost. Today, she dials a short code, types her PIN, and the money arrives in seconds. That is the power of USSD payments.

USSD, which stands for Unstructured Supplementary Service Data, is the menu system that appears when you dial a code like *737# or *894# on your phone. It works on any phone, from the cheapest feature phone to the newest smartphone, and it does not need internet.

This guide explains how to accept USSD payments for your small business in Nigeria. You will learn how USSD payments work, the different ways to set them up, how to choose the right provider for your business, how to take payments in your shop or online, and how to confirm every payment and protect yourself from fraud.

## What USSD Payments Are and How They Work

USSD is a text-based menu that runs over the mobile network, not the internet. When a customer dials a code on their phone, their network shows a menu on the screen. They select an option, enter an amount and a PIN, and the payment is made from their bank account to yours. The whole process takes less than a minute, and both sides get a confirmation message.

For your business, the customer pays by dialling a code that points to your account. There are two common ways this happens. The first is a bank USSD code, where the customer dials their own bank's code, selects "Transfer", and enters your account number and the amount. The second is a payment link from a provider like Paystack or Flutterwave, where the customer dials a code you give them and the payment is tied directly to your business.

The money usually arrives in your account within seconds, and you get an alert on your phone. Because USSD runs on the mobile network, it keeps working during a power cut, when your POS machine has no battery, or when the internet in your area is down.

USSD also reaches customers that card payments and bank apps cannot. A customer with a feature phone and no data can still pay you. If your customers include older people, market traders, or anyone who finds banking apps confusing, USSD is often the easiest way for them to pay you.

## Why USSD Makes Sense for a Small Business

The biggest advantage of USSD is that it removes friction at the point of sale. Every extra step between a customer deciding to buy and the money reaching you is a chance to lose the sale. A customer who has to download an app, remember a password, or wait for a slow network may simply walk away. With USSD, the customer dials a code they already know, enters a PIN, and the payment is done.

USSD is also cheap to set up. You do not need to buy a POS machine, pay a monthly rental fee, or install any equipment. If you already have a bank account and a phone, you can start accepting USSD payments today. For a new business with little capital, that matters.

It also helps you sell outside your shop. When a customer orders on WhatsApp, you can send them your account details and they can pay by USSD from home. When you sell at a trade fair or a pop-up market in Ikeja, you can take payments without carrying a POS machine or depending on the venue's internet.

Finally, USSD payments leave a clear record. Every payment comes with a bank alert that shows the amount, the sender's name, and the time. That record helps you track your sales and settle arguments about whether a payment was made. A cash sale leaves no trace; a USSD payment leaves proof.

## Ways to Accept USSD Payments

There are three main ways to accept USSD payments, and the right one depends on how you sell. The simplest is a direct bank transfer by USSD. You give the customer your account number and bank name, and they dial their own bank's code to send the money. For example, a GTBank customer dials *737#, selects transfer, and enters your account details. Every major Nigerian bank has a USSD code, and this method costs you nothing to set up.

The second way is through a payment provider such as Paystack or Flutterwave. These companies give you a payment link or a checkout page that includes USSD as an option. When a customer opens your link, they choose USSD, pick their bank, and receive a code to dial. The payment is confirmed automatically, and you can see it in your dashboard.

The third way is a dedicated merchant USSD code, where a provider gives your business its own short code that customers dial to pay you. This is more common for bigger businesses, because it can involve setup costs and monthly fees. For most small businesses, the first two options are enough.

Whichever method you choose, write your payment details clearly and keep them the same everywhere: on your shop wall, on your WhatsApp Business profile, on your Instagram bio, and on every receipt.

## How to Set Up USSD Payments Step by Step

First, decide which method fits your business. If you sell mainly in a physical shop, a direct bank transfer by USSD is enough. If you sell online through WhatsApp or Instagram, a payment link from Paystack or Flutterwave is better, because it confirms payments automatically and reduces the messages you have to answer.

Second, if you choose a payment provider, create an account with them. You will need your business name, a valid ID, your bank account details, and a phone number and email address you check regularly. The provider will verify your details, which usually takes a short time if your documents are clear. Make sure the bank account you link is in your name or your business name, because a mismatch can delay your payouts.

Third, set up your payment link or checkout page. Give it a clear name, such as your business name, and test it yourself before you share it with customers. Send the link to your own phone, open it, choose USSD, and follow the steps with a small test amount. Check that the money arrives and that you receive a notification. A link that fails on a customer's phone will cost you a sale and a customer.

Fourth, prepare a simple payment instruction that you can send to any customer. For a bank transfer, it should read something like: "Pay to: Adaeze Stores, GTBank, 0123456789. Or dial *737*2*0123456789*amount# on GTBank." Keep it short, and save it as a quick reply on WhatsApp Business so you can send it in one tap. Print the same details on a small card in your shop.

Fifth, train yourself and anyone who works for you on how to confirm a payment. The rule is simple: never hand over goods until you have seen the alert on your own phone or in your provider's dashboard. A screenshot from the customer is not proof.

## How to Take USSD Payments in Your Shop

In your shop, make paying by USSD easy and visible. Put your account details on a clear sign near the counter, big enough to read from a short distance. Include your business name, bank name, and account number, and the USSD code for the most common banks your customers use. A customer who can see the details does not need to ask, and the queue moves faster.

When a customer is ready to pay, tell them the exact amount and ask them to dial the code while you watch. Stand where you can see your own phone, because your alert is what matters. Do not rely on the customer's phone screen. Wait for your alert to arrive before you pack the goods. If the alert is delayed, ask the customer to wait a moment. Most alerts arrive within seconds, but network delays happen, and a minute of patience is better than a lost sale or a lost product.

Keep a small notebook at the counter for recording payments. Write the date, the customer's name, the amount, and the last few digits of the transaction reference. This takes seconds, and it saves you hours of confusion later when you are reconciling your sales at the end of the day. If a customer claims they paid and you have no alert, the notebook and your bank statement will settle the argument.

## How to Take USSD Payments Online and on WhatsApp

Most small businesses in Nigeria now take orders on WhatsApp, and USSD fits that flow well. When a customer orders, send them a clear message with the total amount, your account details, and the USSD instruction. Keep the message short so the customer can follow it without asking questions. For example: "Total is ₦22,500. Pay to Chidi Fabrics, Access Bank, 1234567890, or dial *901*2*1234567890*22500#."

If you use a payment link from Paystack or Flutterwave, send the link instead of your account number. The link shows the customer the amount and lets them choose USSD as their payment method. The advantage is that the provider confirms the payment automatically and sends you a notification, so you do not have to check your bank app for every order. This is especially useful when you receive many orders at once, such as after a Facebook Live sale or a viral post.

Set a clear rule for online orders: payment confirms the order. Tell the customer that you will only pack and dispatch their goods after the payment is confirmed. This protects you from customers who order and never pay, and it stops you from holding stock for people who are not serious. Give a deadline, such as payment within two hours, and be polite but firm about it.

Always reply when a payment comes in. Send a short confirmation: "Payment received, thank you. Your order will be delivered tomorrow." That message reassures the customer and starts the delivery conversation.

## How to Confirm Payments and Stay Safe from Fraud

Confirming payments properly is the most important habit in this whole guide. Never release goods based on a screenshot alone. Screenshots can be edited, and fake transfer alerts are a common scam in Nigeria. Always check your own bank alert, your bank app, or your payment provider's dashboard. If the money is not in your account, the payment has not happened, no matter what the customer's phone shows.

Watch out for the "wrong amount" trick. A customer pays ₦2,500 instead of ₦25,000 and shows you a screenshot quickly, hoping you will not check the figure. Always read the amount on your own alert before you hand over the goods. Also watch for payments sent to a different account: a fraudster may show you a genuine alert for a transfer to another person's account. Check that the account credited is yours.

Be careful with your account details. Only share your business account number, never your PIN, your card details, or any code sent to your phone. No bank or payment provider will ever ask you for your PIN or an OTP. If someone calls claiming to be from your bank and asks for those details, it is a scam. Hang up and call your bank yourself.

Keep your business money separate from your personal money. Open a separate account for your business, even if it is a personal savings account in your name to start with. When business and personal money mix in one account, it is hard to know whether you are making a profit.

## Common Mistakes Small Businesses Make with USSD

The first mistake is releasing goods before confirming payment. It happens when a shop is busy, the queue is long, and the seller trusts a screenshot. The rule must be absolute: no alert on your phone, no goods. The second mistake is using a personal account with a name that does not match your business. When a customer sees a different name on the transfer screen, they hesitate, and some will cancel the payment. Use an account in your business name, or at least tell customers in advance what name they will see.

The third mistake is not writing payment instructions down. A seller who recites a ten-digit account number from memory will eventually get a digit wrong, and the customer's money will go to a stranger. Print your details, save them as a quick reply, and let customers copy them. The fourth mistake is ignoring the fees. Some banks charge a small fee on USSD transfers, and some providers take a percentage of each payment. Know your fees and build them into your prices, so a ₦10,000 sale does not quietly become ₦9,700.

The fifth mistake is having no record of payments. A seller who relies only on memory cannot answer when a customer says "I paid you last week" or when the accounts do not balance at month end. Keep a notebook or a simple spreadsheet, and record every USSD payment the day it happens. The sixth mistake is failing to tell customers that USSD is an option. If your sign only says "Cash only" or your WhatsApp profile has no payment details, customers will assume you do not accept transfers. Advertise every payment method you accept.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need a special account to accept USSD payments?

No. Any Nigerian bank account can receive a USSD transfer, because the customer dials their own bank's code to send money to your account number. You do not need a special merchant account to start. If you use a payment provider like Paystack or Flutterwave, you will need to create an account with them and link your bank account, but that is free and takes a short time.

### How much does it cost to accept USSD payments?

Receiving a bank transfer by USSD is usually free for you as the seller, though the customer may pay a small transfer fee to their bank. If you use a payment provider, they charge a small percentage on each transaction, often around 1.5 percent. Check the current rate with your provider before you set your prices, so the fee does not eat into your profit.

### What happens if a customer's USSD payment fails?

Ask the customer to check their balance and try again, or to use another method such as a bank app or a transfer from a different bank. Sometimes the network is the problem, and the payment goes through a few minutes later. Never hand over goods until the money has arrived in your account. If the customer was debited but you received nothing, ask them to contact their bank with the transaction reference.

### Can customers pay by USSD if they use a different bank from mine?

Yes. USSD transfers work between any Nigerian banks. A customer with a Zenith Bank account can pay into your GTBank or Access Bank account by dialling their own bank's code and entering your account number. The transfer usually arrives within seconds, and both sides receive a confirmation message.

### Is USSD safe for my business?

Yes, when you follow the basic rules. Always confirm the payment on your own phone or dashboard before releasing goods, never share your PIN or OTP with anyone, and keep your business money in a separate account. The main risk is not the technology but carelessness: trusting a screenshot, or releasing goods before the alert arrives.
