Most Nigerian small business owners work very hard every day, but many never stop to ask a simple question: "How did my business actually do this month?" They know they were busy. They know money came in and money went out. But they do not know whether they made a profit, which products sold best, which customers came back, or what they should do differently next month.

That is like driving from Lagos to Abuja without checking your fuel gauge, your speed, or your map. You may arrive, but you will not know how much fuel you used, whether you took the best route, or whether you need to stop for repairs before the next journey. A monthly business review is your chance to check the gauge, look at the map, and plan the next leg of the journey.

A monthly review does not need to be complicated, and it does not need an accountant. With a notebook, your phone, and one or two hours at the end of each month, you can review your sales, your expenses, your customers, your stock, and your marketing, and set clear goals for the month ahead. This guide shows you how to do it step by step, in a way that fits a real Nigerian small business.

## What Is a Monthly Business Review?

A monthly business review is a regular meeting with yourself, or with your small team, where you look back at the month that just ended and plan the month ahead. You gather your numbers, compare them with the previous month, and ask honest questions about what worked and what did not.

It is not a tax filing or a formal report for a bank. It is a working session for you. The goal is not perfect paperwork. The goal is clear thinking. At the end of the review, you should be able to answer five questions: How much did I sell? How much did I spend? Did I make a profit? What sold best and who bought it? What will I do differently next month?

Many owners skip this step because they feel they do not have time, or because their records are messy. But a business without a review repeats the same mistakes every month. You keep stocking products that do not sell. You keep spending on adverts that bring no customers. You keep giving discounts that eat your profit. A monthly review breaks that cycle by forcing you to look at the facts.

## Gather Your Numbers Before You Start

A review is only as good as the records you bring to it. Before your review day, gather the information you need. You do not need fancy software. A notebook, a spreadsheet on your phone, or a simple app can work, as long as you record consistently.

Start with sales. Write down your total sales for the month. If you can, break them down by product or service, and by channel: walk-in customers, WhatsApp orders, Instagram orders, and any other channel. Note the number of orders, not just the total amount. Ten orders of N5,000 each tell a different story from one order of N50,000.

Next, gather your expenses. List everything you spent money on: stock or materials, transport and delivery, data and airtime, rent, electricity or fuel for your generator, staff wages, packaging, marketing, and any other cost. Be honest and include small costs. The small costs — POS charges, delivery bike fees, and airtime for customer calls — add up over a month, and many businesses underestimate them.

If you keep your records in a bank app, a POS report, or a notebook, bring them together for the review. If your records are incomplete, do not let that stop you. Use the figures you have, note what is missing, and commit to recording better next month. A review with imperfect numbers is far better than no review at all.

Finally, gather your stock information. What do you still have on hand? What is running low? What has been sitting unsold for weeks? A review is the best time to notice slow stock before it ties up your money for another month.

## Step One: Review Your Sales and Profit

The first step of your review is the simplest and the most important: work out whether you made money. Add up your total sales for the month, then subtract your total expenses. The result is your profit or loss for the month. Write it down in plain language: "In October, I sold N850,000 worth of goods, spent N620,000, and made a profit of N230,000."

Compare that figure with last month. Did sales go up or down? Did expenses go up or down? Did profit improve? Do not just look at sales alone. A month with high sales but even higher expenses can leave you worse off than a quieter month with tight costs. Profit is the number that matters.

Look at what sold best. List your top five products or services by sales, and your bottom five. Your top sellers deserve more attention, more stock, and more marketing next month. Your bottom sellers need a decision: lower the price, promote them differently, bundle them with a popular item, or stop stocking them. Keeping a product that never sells ties up money that could buy stock that does sell.

Check your sales by channel. Did most orders come from WhatsApp, Instagram, walk-in customers, or referrals? If one channel brings most of your sales, consider spending more time and effort there next month. If a channel you spend a lot of time on brings few sales, ask whether it is worth the effort or whether you need to change how you use it.

## Step Two: Review Your Expenses and Cash

Many Nigerian small businesses fail not because they do not sell, but because they do not control costs. Your review should look closely at where your money went.

Go through your expenses line by line. Sort them into groups: stock, transport, rent and power, staff, marketing, and other costs. Which group grew the most this month? Was the increase necessary, or could you have avoided it? For example, if transport costs doubled because you made many small trips to the market, you could save money next month by buying in bulk once a week.

Look for leaks. Are you giving too many free items to friends and family? Are you paying for subscriptions you no longer use? Are you losing money on delivery because you absorb the full cost? Small leaks are easy to ignore day by day, but they show up clearly in a monthly review.

Set a simple budget for next month based on what you learned. Decide how much you will spend on stock, transport, marketing and other costs. A budget does not need to be rigid, but it gives you a target to check against. Without a budget, spending drifts upward without you noticing.

## Step Three: Review Your Customers

Customers are the heart of your business, and your review should look at them closely. How many new customers did you gain this month? How many were repeat customers? Repeat customers are valuable because they cost less to serve and they often spend more. If your repeat rate is low, ask why. Are your prices too high? Is your delivery slow? Do customers forget about you after one purchase?

Read through your customer messages from the month. What did customers complain about? What did they praise? Complaints are free advice. If three customers complained about late delivery, delivery is a problem to fix. If many customers praised a particular product, that product deserves more promotion.

Check your follow-up. Did you message customers after delivery to confirm they were happy? Did you thank repeat customers? Did you ask happy customers for a review or a referral? A short follow-up message costs nothing and often leads to another order.

Think about your best customers. Who spent the most this month? Who referred others to you? Make a note to thank them personally next month, perhaps with a small discount or a free extra on their next order. Loyal customers are your best marketing, and a little appreciation keeps them coming back.

## Step Four: Review Your Stock and Operations

Stock is money sitting on your shelves. Your review should check whether that money is working for you or stuck.

List what you have in stock and how long it has been there. Items that have sat for two or three months without selling are a warning sign. Decide what to do with them: discount them, bundle them with a popular item, give them as a free gift with a larger order, or stop reordering them. Do not keep buying more of a slow product because you already have it.

Check what is running low. Your best sellers should never run out of stock at the moment customers want them. If you sold out of a popular item twice this month, order more next month, or order earlier. A stockout is a lost sale, and the customer may buy from your competitor instead.

## Step Five: Review Your Marketing

Marketing is often the first thing owners cut when money is tight, but a review helps you spend your marketing money wisely instead of cutting it blindly.

List everything you did to promote your business this month: Instagram posts, WhatsApp status updates, Facebook posts, YouTube videos, flyers, referrals, paid adverts, and any other effort. For each one, ask: did it bring enquiries or sales? You may not have exact numbers, but you can usually tell. If customers mention "I saw your post", that post worked. If you posted every day and got no new enquiries, something needs to change.

Compare your channels. A channel that takes an hour a day but brings no sales is less valuable than a channel that takes twenty minutes and brings regular orders. Spend your time where the results are. That does not mean abandoning a channel completely. It may mean posting less often there, or changing what you post.

Review your content. Which posts got the most likes, comments, shares and messages? Which got almost nothing? Make more of what worked and less of what did not. Your audience is telling you what they want. Listen to them.

Check your prices and offers. Did a discount or a promo bring new customers, or did it only reduce your profit from people who would have bought anyway? A promo that brings new customers who return is good. A promo that only rewards existing customers at a lower price may not be worth repeating.

## Set Goals for Next Month

A review that ends without goals is just a history lesson. The final step is to set clear, simple goals for the month ahead, based on what you learned.

Choose three to five goals, not twenty. A short list you can actually achieve is better than a long list you will forget. Make each goal specific and measurable. Instead of "Sell more", write "Sell 40 dresses, up from 30 last month." Instead of "Spend less", write "Keep transport costs below N30,000 by buying stock once a week." Instead of "Post more", write "Post on Instagram four times a week and share two customer reviews."

Write your goals down and keep them where you will see them. A note on your phone, a page in your notebook, or a paper on your shop wall all work. Share them with your staff or a trusted partner if that helps you stay accountable.

Break each goal into a few actions. If your goal is to sell 40 dresses, your actions might be: order 45 dresses from your supplier by the 5th, post three dress videos on Instagram, and message your last ten customers with a new arrival notice. Actions turn a goal into a plan.

Set a date to check your progress. Halfway through the month, spend fifteen minutes checking whether you are on track. If you are behind, you still have time to adjust. If you are ahead, you can aim higher. Do not wait until the next monthly review to discover you missed your goal.

## Common Mistakes in Monthly Reviews

The first mistake is doing the review only when there is a problem. If you review only when money is tight or a customer complains, you will always be reacting. A monthly review works best when it is a habit, done every month whether the month was good or bad.

The second is focusing only on sales and ignoring costs. A business that sells a lot but spends even more is not growing. Always look at profit, not just sales.

The third is setting vague goals. "Do better next month" is not a goal. A goal needs a number, a deadline, and an action. Without those, you cannot tell whether you achieved it.

The fourth is keeping the review in your head. Write it down. A written review can be compared with last month and next month. A review in your head is forgotten by next week.

The fifth is doing the review alone when you have staff. Your staff see things you do not. They know which customers complain, which products are hard to sell, and which tasks take too long. Invite their input, even if it is just a short conversation.

The sixth is never acting on what you learn. A review that identifies a problem but leads to no change is wasted time. End every review with at least one concrete change you will make next month, and check next month whether you made it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long should a monthly business review take?

One to two hours is enough for most small businesses. Gather your numbers before the session so you spend the time thinking and planning, not searching for receipts. As your records improve, the review will get faster.

### Do I need accounting software for a monthly review?

No. A notebook, a spreadsheet on your phone, or a simple app is enough to start. The important thing is to record sales and expenses consistently. You can move to accounting software later as your business grows.

### What if my records are messy or incomplete?

Start with what you have. Use your bank statements, POS reports, and your memory of the month to estimate the missing figures, and note that they are estimates. Then commit to recording better next month. A review with imperfect numbers is still useful, and it will motivate you to keep better records.

### Should I do a monthly review if my business is very small?

Yes, especially then. Small businesses have less room for mistakes. A monthly review helps you catch problems early, when they are still cheap to fix. Even a business with only a few sales a week benefits from knowing its profit and its best products.

### What numbers matter most in a monthly review?

Total sales, total expenses, profit, your best-selling products, and your number of new and repeat customers. If you track only those five, you will already understand your business far better than most small business owners.

### Can I do the review with my partner or staff?

Yes, and you should if you work with others. A short review meeting with your team builds shared understanding and accountability. Keep it focused, share the numbers honestly, and end with clear goals everyone understands.

## What to Do Next

1. Pick a fixed day for your monthly review, such as the last Saturday of the month, and put it in your calendar.
2. Gather your sales, expenses, customer notes, and stock list before the review day.
3. Work out your total sales, total expenses, and profit for the month, and compare them with last month.
4. List your top five and bottom five products, and decide what to do about the slow sellers.
5. Review your customers, noting complaints, praise, repeat buyers, and anyone you need to follow up with.
6. Review your marketing, noting which posts and channels brought enquiries and which did not.
7. Set three to five specific, measurable goals for next month, with clear actions and a mid-month check-in.

A monthly business review is a simple habit that separates businesses that grow from businesses that stay stuck. Set aside two hours at the end of this month and start next month with a clear picture of your business and a clear plan for growth.
