Every Lagos business owner who sells online knows the feeling. A customer in Lekki orders a dress, a customer in Ikeja orders shoes, and a customer in Surulere orders a cake. The products are ready and the money is in, and then comes the part that decides whether that customer ever orders again: delivery. In Lagos, delivery is not a small detail. It is the last thing your customer experiences, and it is what they will remember most. A parcel that arrives on time, in good condition, with a polite rider makes your business look professional. One that arrives late or damaged makes you look careless, even when the product was perfect.

Lagos makes delivery harder than most cities. Traffic on the Lekki-Epe Expressway, the Third Mainland Bridge, or the Apapa-Oshodi Expressway can turn a thirty-minute trip into three hours. Rain can flood a road in minutes. Addresses can be confusing, with streets that have no numbers and estates that need a gate pass. The businesses that deliver reliably in Lagos are not the ones with the fastest bikes. They are the ones with a clear system: the right rider, the right price, the right packaging, and a clear way of tracking every order. This guide gives you that system, whether you sell food, fashion, skincare, electronics, or cakes.

## Start With the Right Rider: How to Find Reliable Dispatch Riders in Lagos

Finding a reliable dispatch rider starts before you hand over your first parcel. Ask other business owners in your area who they use. A recommendation from a seller who has used the same rider for months is worth more than any advert. Ask in your WhatsApp business groups, at your market, or among sellers in your estate. Ask whether he arrives on time and communicates about delays. A rider with a good name among sellers is worth trying. You can also find riders through dispatch companies and apps, rider parks, and dispatch stands in busy areas such as Lekki Phase 1, Ikeja, Yaba, Surulere, and Victoria Island.

When you meet a new rider, ask for his full name, phone number, and where he is based. Ask whether he has a valid rider's permit and whether his bike is registered. Ask what areas he knows well. A rider who knows Lekki inside out may struggle in Ikeja, and a rider who knows the mainland may not know the island estates. Match the rider to the route, not just to the job. Before you trust a new rider with a valuable order, test him with a small one. Give him a low-value delivery to a nearby address and watch how he handles it. Does he confirm pickup and delivery, call the customer, and return payment-on-delivery money promptly? A rider who handles a small order with care has shown you how he will handle a big one. Keep a short list of the riders who pass the test, with their names, numbers, areas, and rates saved on your phone.

## Dispatch Rider vs Logistics Company: Which One Fits Your Business?

A single dispatch rider and a logistics company solve the same problem in different ways, and the right choice depends on how many orders you send and how far they travel. A freelance rider works for himself. You call him directly, agree a price, and he picks up and delivers. He is flexible and can learn your products and customers over time. For a small business sending a few orders a day within one area, a trusted freelance rider is often the best and cheapest option. The risk is that he is only one person. If his bike breaks down, if he is sick, or if he is already busy, your order waits.

A logistics company has many riders, a booking office or app, and a system for tracking parcels. You book through the company, they assign a rider, and they take responsibility if something goes wrong. This is better when you send many orders a day, when your orders go to different parts of Lagos, or when you need a receipt and a tracking number to share with your customer. The price is usually higher than a freelance rider, and you may not get the same rider twice, but you get backup. Many Lagos sellers use both: trusted freelance riders for regular routes, and a logistics company for busy days, far areas, and orders that need formal tracking. Whichever you choose, never rely on only one rider or only one company. Always have a backup name and number saved.

## How Delivery Pricing Works in Lagos

Delivery prices in Lagos are not fixed. They depend on distance, traffic, the size and weight of the parcel, and how urgent the order is. A short trip within Lekki Phase 1 or within Ikeja costs far less than a trip from Lekki to Ikorodu or from Surulere to Ajah. Island to island is usually cheaper than island to mainland, because crossing a bridge in Lagos traffic takes time and fuel. A light parcel costs less than a heavy box or a large cake, and an urgent order costs more than one that can wait until the next day.

Before you quote a delivery fee, know your rider's rates for the areas you serve most. Ask for a simple price list by area: Lekki, Victoria Island, Ikoyi, Ikeja, Surulere, Yaba, Maryland, Ikorodu, and Ajah, and write it down. Quote the fee clearly before you confirm the order. Never guess a delivery price: if you quote too low, you pay the difference yourself, and if you quote too high, the customer may cancel. Say clearly who pays for delivery, on your page, WhatsApp catalogue, or website. Most sellers charge it separately; some offer free delivery nearby or above a certain order amount. Either way, the customer must know before they pay. Do not compete on delivery price alone: a cheap rider who loses parcels or arrives late will cost you repeat orders, worth far more than the saving on one trip.

## Packaging Orders So They Arrive in One Piece

Good packaging is what stands between your product and a bumpy Lagos road. A dispatch bike goes over potholes, speed bumps, and rough estate roads, and the rider may have to brake suddenly in traffic. If your parcel is loosely packed, the product will move, knock against the box, and arrive scratched, crushed, or broken. Pack every order as if the road will be rough, because in Lagos, it often will be. Clothes can go in a strong branded bag or small box, sealed with tape. Shoes need a shaped box with paper inside. Liquids need bubble wrap, a taped cap, and padding to stay upright. Cakes need a firm box on a flat base, and a clear warning to the rider that it is fragile and must stay flat.

Label every parcel clearly, with the customer's full name, phone number, and address in large writing, plus your own business name and phone number as the sender. If the order is fragile, write FRAGILE in large letters on the box. If it must stay upright, write THIS SIDE UP with an arrow. Do not rely on the rider remembering instructions; a clear label speaks for you. Protect parcels from rain, which can start in Lagos without warning. Put the product inside a waterproof nylon bag before boxing it, use a sealed container for food, and wrap electronics in nylon. A small extra layer of nylon costs almost nothing, but it can save a product worth thousands of naira. Check the parcel with the rider before he leaves: sealed, labelled, address complete?

## How to Book, Track and Confirm Every Delivery

A delivery without a clear record is a delivery you cannot explain if something goes wrong. Every time you send an order, write down the details before the rider leaves: the date, the customer's name and phone number, the address, the product, the amount paid or to be collected, and the rider's name and phone number. A notebook, a Google Sheet, or a WhatsApp message to yourself will do. When a customer asks where their order is, you should be able to answer in seconds.

When you book a rider, give him all the information he needs in one message: the pickup address and time, the delivery address, the customer's name and phone number, and any special instruction, such as "call the customer before you arrive" or "the estate needs a gate pass". Do not split details across messages; a rider in traffic cannot scroll through a long chat. Ask the rider to confirm pickup, arrival, and delivery, and to photograph the parcel at the door where the customer agrees, as your proof of delivery. If you use a logistics company or a dispatch app, share the tracking link with the customer as soon as you have it. Then confirm delivery with the customer yourself, with a short message asking them to confirm they have checked the order.

## Handling Payment on Delivery Without Losing Money

Payment on delivery, often called POD, is popular with Lagos customers because it lets them pay when they see the goods. It is also the riskiest way to get paid, because the money passes through the rider's hands first. If you offer payment on delivery, set clear rules and follow them every time. First, agree the amount in writing before dispatch, including the delivery fee if the customer is paying it. If you send an invoice with the order, you can create a neat one quickly with Joetech's free invoice generator at /tools/invoice-generator. Second, agree how and when the rider will send you the money. The safest method is a bank transfer to your account as soon as the customer pays, with the rider sending you a screenshot of the transfer. Cash is slower and harder to trace, so prefer a transfer, immediately after delivery.

Third, only offer payment on delivery to customers you have a reason to trust, or for orders where the risk is small. For a first-time customer ordering an expensive item, ask for payment before dispatch, or at least a deposit. If a customer refuses to pay on delivery, or says they will pay later, the rider should not leave the parcel with them. Tell your rider this rule clearly: no payment, no parcel. The rider should bring the goods back to you, and you can then contact the customer to sort it out. Record every payment-on-delivery order: amount, date, rider, and when the money reached your account, and check your bank account each evening. If a rider is late sending money, call him the same day. A short delay may be innocent, but a pattern is a warning sign.

## When Deliveries Fail: Late, Wrong Address, Damaged Goods and No-Show Customers

Even with the best rider and the best packaging, some deliveries will fail, and what matters is how quickly and honestly you handle them. If a delivery is late, tell the customer before they ask: a short message saying the rider is held in traffic, with a realistic new time, keeps customers patient, while silence makes them angry. If the address is wrong, call the customer yourself, ask for a landmark or a live location on WhatsApp, and save the correct address for next time. If the customer does not answer, the rider should wait a few minutes, try again, and then return the parcel to you rather than leaving it with a stranger. If goods arrive damaged, apologise first, ask for a photo, find out whether the cause was packaging or handling, and offer a replacement, repair, or refund.

## Building a Rider Relationship That Lasts

A good dispatch rider is one of the most valuable people in your business, so treat him like a partner, not a servant. Pay him promptly and fairly, and never argue over a price you already agreed. Give him regular work, and he will prioritise your orders when he is busy. Speak to him politely, give clear instructions, and thank him when a delivery goes well. A small tip on a difficult trip costs little and is remembered. Do not overload his bike, and do not blame him for traffic on a last-minute trip. If he makes a mistake, talk about it calmly and agree how to avoid it next time. Riders stay with sellers who respect them, pay on time, and give steady work.

## Using Dispatch Apps and Logistics Platforms in Lagos

Dispatch apps and logistics platforms let you book a rider from your phone, compare prices, and track the parcel on a map. They are useful when your regular rider is busy, when an order goes to an area he does not know, or when a customer asks for a tracking link. To use one well, compare two or three platforms on the same route before you settle on a favourite, and check their reviews from other sellers. Read their policy on lost or damaged parcels, so you know what they will pay for and what you must cover yourself. Give the rider the same clear booking message you would give a freelance rider. Keep your freelance riders too: apps are a good backup, but a trusted rider who knows your business is best for regular orders.

## Common Mistakes That Cost Lagos Sellers Money

The most expensive delivery mistakes are simple ones. Sending an order without writing down the customer, the address, and the amount to collect leaves you with nothing to check when a customer complains. Guessing a delivery price instead of using a price list means you either lose money or lose the sale. Packaging an order loosely to save on materials means you pay for a replacement later. Relying on one rider with no backup means your orders stop the day he is sick or his bike breaks down. Letting a rider leave goods without payment, because he felt shy to insist, turns a sale into a loss. Staying silent when a delivery is late turns a small delay into an angry customer. Avoid these mistakes and most deliveries will go smoothly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does dispatch delivery cost in Lagos?

It depends on distance, traffic, parcel size, and urgency. A short trip within one neighbourhood costs far less than a trip from Lekki to Ikorodu or from the island to the mainland. Ask two or three riders for their rates on your common routes and quote from a written price list.

### How do I find a trustworthy dispatch rider in Lagos?

Start with recommendations from other sellers, then test a new rider with a small, low-value order. Watch whether he confirms pickup, calls the customer, delivers on time, and returns payment-on-delivery money promptly. Keep those who pass on a short list with numbers, areas, and rates.

### Should I use a dispatch app or a freelance rider?

Use a freelance rider for a few regular orders a day, and add a logistics company or dispatch app when you send many orders, when orders go to far areas, or when customers ask for tracking. Many sellers use both, with the app as backup.

### What should I do if my customer is not at home when the rider arrives?

Ask the rider to wait a few minutes and call the customer again, and send the customer a message yourself. If the customer still cannot be reached, the rider should bring the parcel back to you rather than leaving it with a stranger or a security guard without your permission.

## What to Do Next

First, save the numbers of two reliable riders and one logistics company on your phone today, and ask each of them for their delivery rates to Lekki, Ikeja, Surulere, and the other areas your customers live in. Write them into a simple price list.

Second, package your next order the way this guide describes: the right box or bag, padding for anything fragile or liquid, a waterproof layer against rain, and a clear label with the customer's name, phone, and address. Check it with the rider before he leaves.

Third, set your payment-on-delivery rule in writing and share it with your riders: the exact amount to collect, transfer immediately after delivery, and no payment means no parcel. Use it on every order, without exception.
