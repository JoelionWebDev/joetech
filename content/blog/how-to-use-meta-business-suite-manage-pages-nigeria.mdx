# How to Use Meta Business Suite to Manage Your Pages in Nigeria

If you run a small business in Nigeria, you probably juggle a Facebook Page, an Instagram account, WhatsApp messages, and maybe a second Instagram page for a side hustle. Switching between four apps to post, reply, and check results eats up hours every week — hours you could spend serving customers.

Meta Business Suite is the free tool that fixes this. It is Meta's official dashboard for managing Facebook Pages and Instagram business accounts from one place. You can schedule posts for both platforms at once, answer Facebook and Instagram messages in a single inbox, run and check ads, and see which posts are working — all without paying a kobo. Many Nigerian business owners have heard of it but never set it up, or they opened it once, felt confused, and went back to posting manually. This guide walks you through setup step by step, then shows you how to use it every day to save time and sell more.

## What Meta Business Suite Can Do for You

Before setup, it helps to know what you are getting. Business Suite is not a magic tool that creates customers by itself. It is an organiser: it gathers the jobs you already do on Facebook and Instagram into one screen so you do them faster and more consistently.

- **Post to Facebook and Instagram at the same time.** Write one post, attach one photo or video, and publish it to both platforms in one action. You can also schedule it for later.
- **One inbox for everything.** Facebook messages, Instagram DMs, and comments on both platforms appear in a single inbox. You reply once, from one place, instead of checking two apps.
- **Insights in plain numbers.** See which posts reached the most people, what time your audience is online, and which posts brought profile visits and messages.
- **Ads without confusion.** Create a simple boosted post or a basic ad, set a daily budget in naira, and watch the results in the same dashboard.
- **Team access without sharing your password.** Give a staff member or a social media manager access to your Page without handing over your personal Facebook login.
- **A content library.** All your photos and videos live in one place, so you can reuse a good product photo next month without scrolling through your phone gallery.

## Step 1: What You Need Before You Start

Setup takes about twenty minutes if you have everything ready. Gather these first:

- A personal Facebook account. Business Suite is built on top of Facebook, so you need one to log in. It does not need to be public or active — many business owners keep a quiet personal account just for this purpose.
- A Facebook Page for your business. If you do not have one yet, create it first at facebook.com/pages/create. Use your real business name, add your logo as the profile picture, and fill in your WhatsApp number, location, and opening hours.
- An Instagram business or creator account. If your Instagram is still a personal account, switch it in the app under Settings, then Account type and tools, then Switch to professional account. Choose Business, pick your category, and add your contact details. A personal Instagram account cannot be connected to Business Suite.
- Your business details: your WhatsApp number, your location (for example, Lekki, Lagos), and your opening hours. You will be asked for these during setup.
- A stable internet connection. Setup involves several loading screens, and a weak MTN or Airtel connection that drops halfway can force you to start again. Use Wi-Fi if you can.

## Step 2: Create Your Business Portfolio

Your business portfolio — Meta used to call it a Business Manager account — is the container that holds your Pages, Instagram accounts, ad accounts, and team members. One portfolio can hold several businesses if you run more than one brand, but most small businesses need only one.

Here is how to create it:

1. Go to business.facebook.com on your computer and log in with your personal Facebook account.
2. Click "Create Account" if you do not already have a portfolio.
3. Enter your business name exactly as customers know it — for example, "Ada's Cakes Lagos" — and your name and business email address. Use an email you actually check, such as info@yourbusiness.com.ng or your Gmail address.
4. Enter your business details: country (Nigeria), address, and phone number. You can skip some fields for now, but add your real details where asked — Meta may ask you to verify them later if you run ads.
5. Click Submit. Your portfolio is created, and you will land on the Business Suite home screen.

Do not create a second portfolio if you already have one from an earlier attempt. Having two portfolios with the same Page in both causes confusion and can lock you out of ad features. If you think you created one before, check business.facebook.com/settings first to see what already exists.

## Step 3: Add Your Facebook Page

If you created your Page from the same Facebook account, it may already appear in Business Suite. If not, add it manually:

1. In Business Suite, go to Settings at the bottom left, then Business Settings.
2. On the left menu, click Pages under Accounts, then Add, then Add a Page.
3. Type your Page name and select it from the list.
4. Click Add Page. You will need to be an admin of the Page to add it. If someone else created the Page, ask them to make you an admin first, or add the Page from their account.

Once added, your Page appears in the top-left switcher in Business Suite, and you can post as the Page from the dashboard.

A common problem in Nigeria is a Page created years ago by a friend, a former staff member, or a web designer who has disappeared. If you cannot get admin access, you cannot add the Page to Business Suite. In that case, your options are to contact the person who created it, or to start a fresh Page and direct customers to it. It is painful, but a Page you control is worth more than a Page with more followers that you cannot manage or advertise from.

## Step 4: Connect Your Instagram Account

Connecting Instagram is the step most people get stuck on, so follow it carefully.

1. In Business Settings, click Instagram Accounts under Accounts, then Add, then Connect your Instagram account.
2. Log in with your Instagram username and password. Make sure you are logging into the business account, not a personal one.
3. Allow the connection when Instagram asks. You may receive a confirmation code by SMS or email — enter it to finish.
4. Go back to Business Suite and check that your Instagram account appears in the switcher at the top left.

If the connection fails, check these three things first. Is your Instagram a business or creator account? Personal accounts cannot connect. Is your Instagram linked to the correct Facebook Page? In the Instagram app, go to Settings, then Accounts Centre, then Pages, and confirm the right Page is linked. And are you logged into the right accounts in your browser? Being logged into a different Facebook account in the same browser is the most common cause of failure. Log out of the wrong account and try again.

Once connected, posts you publish from Business Suite can go to Instagram and Facebook together, and Instagram DMs will appear in your Business Suite inbox.

## Step 5: Set Up Your Ad Account and Payment Method

You do not need an ad account to use Business Suite for posting and messages, but you will need one the first time you boost a post or run an ad. Setting it up now saves a scramble later.

1. In Business Settings, click Ad Accounts under Accounts, then Add, then Create a New Ad Account.
2. Name it after your business, set the time zone to Africa/Lagos, and set the currency to NGN (Nigerian naira).
3. Click Create, then assign yourself as the admin of the ad account.

To pay for ads, add a payment method under Business Settings, then Payments. Meta accepts Nigerian naira debit cards from banks such as GTBank, Zenith, Access, and UBA, as well as virtual dollar cards from services like Barter or Grey where available. Set a spending limit you are comfortable with. Under Ad Account Settings, you can set an account spending limit, for example ₦20,000, so an ad can never spend more than that without you raising the limit. This is the single most useful safety setting for a small business, and many owners skip it and regret it.

## Step 6: Invite Your Team Without Sharing Your Password

If a staff member, a family member, or a social media manager helps with your pages, give them access through Business Suite instead of sharing your Facebook password. Shared passwords are how Pages get stolen in Nigeria — someone leaves, keeps the login, and locks you out of your own business.

To add a person:

1. Go to Business Settings, then People under Users, then Invite People.
2. Enter their email address and assign them a role. An Employee can post and reply to messages but cannot change settings or add other people. An Admin can do everything, so only give admin access to someone you trust completely.
3. They will receive an email invitation. They accept it with their own Facebook account, and they can then work on your Page without ever seeing your password.

You can also give access to a partner agency or freelancer this way, and remove them in one click when the work ends. Review your People list every few months and remove anyone who no longer works with you.

## Your Daily Routine in Business Suite

Start with the inbox. Open Business Suite, click Inbox, and answer every unread message and comment. Nigerian buyers often message two or three vendors at the same time and buy from the one who replies first, so speed matters. Use saved replies for questions you answer every day — your prices, your location, your delivery areas. In the inbox, click the lightning icon to create saved replies such as "Our cakes start from ₦35,000. We deliver across Lagos. Would you like our price list?" One tap sends it, and you can edit it before sending if the customer asked something slightly different.

Next, check your notifications for comments that need a reply, especially questions about price and availability. A comment left unanswered is a customer lost to a competitor.

Then post or schedule your content. Click Create Post, write your caption, add your photo or video, and choose whether to post to Facebook, Instagram, or both. If you have a quiet hour in the morning, schedule the whole day's posts at once — one for late morning and one for the evening, when most Nigerian users are online. Business Suite's Planner view shows your scheduled posts on a calendar, so you can see the week at a glance and spot days with nothing planned.

Finally, glance at Insights. You do not need to study charts every day, but a quick look tells you which of yesterday's posts reached the most people and which brought messages. Do more of what works.

## Scheduling Posts Like a Professional

Consistency is what grows a Page, and scheduling is what makes consistency possible. A business that posts every day at the same times looks reliable, and the algorithm rewards regular posting with more reach.

Schedule posts for the times your audience is actually online. For most Nigerian consumer businesses, that is roughly 7:30 to 9:00 in the morning, 12:30 to 2:00 in the afternoon, and 7:00 to 9:00 in the evening. Check your own Insights under Audience to see when your followers are online, because a food vendor's audience may peak at lunchtime while a fashion seller's peaks in the evening.

When you schedule, write captions that end with a clear next step: "DM us to order," "Tap the WhatsApp link in our bio," or "Comment YES and we will send you the price list." A post without a next step gets likes; a post with a next step gets customers.

One warning: Business Suite scheduling works well for photos, videos, and carousels, but some features — such as certain music, stickers, and interactive elements — are only available when you post directly in the Instagram or Facebook app. If a post needs a special feature, publish it from the app instead of scheduling it.

## Reading Insights Without Getting Confused

Insights can look intimidating, but you only need four numbers.

Reach tells you how many people saw your post. If reach is falling week after week, your content may be getting repetitive, or you may be posting at the wrong times.

Engagement tells you how many people liked, commented, shared, or saved your post. A post with high engagement is a post your audience cares about — make more like it.

Profile visits and follows tell you whether your content is turning viewers into followers. If reach is high but follows are low, your profile may need work: a clearer bio, better pinned posts, or a WhatsApp link.

Messages started tells you how many people messaged you after seeing a post. For a business, this is the number that matters most, because messages are where sales happen. A post that brings ten messages is worth more than a post that brings a thousand likes.

## Running Your First Boosted Post

Boosting is the simplest form of advertising in Business Suite, and it is a good first step before you try full Ads Manager campaigns.

Pick a post that already performed well on its own — one with good likes, comments, and messages. A post that worked organically will usually work even better with a small budget behind it. Click Boost Post, then set your goal: more messages is usually the best goal for a Nigerian small business, because messages lead to sales. Choose your audience: people in Lagos, Abuja, or your city, in your customers' age range, with interests related to your product. Set a budget — ₦5,000 over three days is a sensible first test — and set an end date.

Before you confirm, check the preview. Read the caption once more for typos, and make sure your WhatsApp number or ordering instruction is in the post. Then let it run for the full three days before judging it. Ads need a day or two to find the right people, and stopping a campaign after six hours tells you nothing.

After it ends, check the results: how many messages did it bring, and how many of those became orders? Divide what you spent by the number of orders to get your cost per order. If you spent ₦5,000 and got five orders, each order cost you ₦1,000 to acquire. If your profit per order is ₦5,000, that is a good return. If it cost you ₦5,000 to get one order worth ₦2,000 profit, the ad lost money — change the audience, the photo, or the offer before spending again.

## Your Business Suite Checklist

Run through this list once, then keep it for a monthly check:

- Your business portfolio is created with your real business name and email.
- Your Facebook Page is added, with your logo, WhatsApp number, location, and hours filled in.
- Your Instagram business account is connected and linked to the right Page.
- Your ad account is set up in naira, with an account spending limit you chose.
- Your payment method is added and verified.
- Team members have their own access, and nobody shares your Facebook password.
- Your inbox is checked and cleared every day, with saved replies set up for common questions.
- Posts are scheduled at least three days ahead in the Planner.
- You check reach, engagement, follows, and messages once a week.
- You review your People list and remove anyone who no longer works with you.

Meta Business Suite will not run your business for you, but it will give you back the hours you currently lose switching between apps — and it will make you faster at the one thing that matters most, which is replying to customers before a competitor does. Set it up this week, use the daily routine for a month, and judge it by the only measure that counts: more conversations, more orders, and less time spent managing your pages.
