There was a time when "make us a video" meant a camera crew, a studio, editing suites, and a six-figure Naira budget. In 2026, a single person with a laptop can produce professional explainer videos, product demos, and social content in hours — because AI now covers the entire pipeline: script, storyboard, footage, voiceover, and edit.

But here is the uncomfortable truth: most AI-generated content converts nobody, because most of it is created without strategy. This guide is the full system for AI-generated video and visuals that actually convert. You get the current tool stack (Runway, Kling, Luma, CapCut AI, Midjourney, ElevenLabs), the end-to-end workflow, branding consistency, platform optimization, cost comparisons, quality control, ethical use, and specific strategies for Nigerian businesses.

The workflow pattern is always the same: **script with a chat AI → visual assets with Midjourney and video generators → narration with ElevenLabs → assemble and polish with CapCut.** Master that pipeline and you have a video production house in your pocket.

## Part 1 — Scripting: The Hook Decides Everything

A video converts only if it is watched, and it is watched only if the first 3 seconds earn attention. The script is where conversion is won or lost — and AI makes scripting fast without making it disposable.

**The script formula that converts (explainer or demo):**
1. **Hook (0–3 seconds):** the problem or the payoff in one line. "Your products are selling out and you still don't know your best customers." 
2. **Agitate (3–15 seconds):** one or two sentences making the pain vivid and specific to your audience.
3. **Reveal (15–40 seconds):** your product/service as the answer, shown clearly with features tied to benefits.
4. **Proof (40–60 seconds):** a result, a number, or a testimonial.
5. **Call to action (last 5 seconds):** one clear next step — "Click the link to order," "Send a WhatsApp message."

**The prompt that works:**

> "Write a 45-second explainer video script for [product/service]. Audience: [audience]. Goal: [goal]. Structure: hook (3s), pain (10s), solution with 3 features-as-benefits (20s), social proof (10s), and one clear call to action. Use a warm, confident tone. Include visual directions in brackets for each line."

**The human step:** read it aloud. If it does not sound like you would say it to a customer, edit it. AI writes a strong skeleton; your voice carries the authenticity that converts in African markets.

## Part 2 — Storyboarding and Generation: From Words to Frames

Once the script exists, turn it into visuals. The secret to good AI video is feeding it specific direction instead of vague wishes.

**The image generation workflow (Midjourney or similar):**
1. Break the script into scenes — one visual per sentence or beat.
2. For each scene, write a detailed prompt: subject, setting, action, style, lighting, and mood. "A young Nigerian woman in a bright modern kitchen, holding a phone showing an order confirmation, warm natural light, realistic, shallow depth of field."
3. Generate several variations per scene and pick the strongest.
4. **Maintain character and scene consistency** by reusing the same descriptive base between prompts — a fixed character description (name, appearance, clothing) across scenes keeps the video coherent.

**The video generation workflow (Runway, Kling, or Luma):**
1. Use your approved stills as image-to-video seeds so characters and settings stay recognisable.
2. Generate 2–4 second clips per scene (short clips are more reliable and cheaper than long ones).
3. Regenerate any clip where motion looks unnatural — never settle for a janky shot because "it's AI."
4. Assemble the clips in sequence; the editing tool handles the stitching.

**The quality rule:** spend 80% of your time on the script and the images, 20% on the video clips. Most AI video failures are script or image failures upstream, not generation failures.

## Part 3 — Voiceover and Sound: Audio Is Half the Video

Audio quality is the fastest shortcut to "professional." A great video with robotic voiceover reads as cheap.

**The ElevenLabs workflow:**
1. Choose a voice that matches your brand — warm for service businesses, energetic for consumer products, calm for finance.
2. Paste your script, adjust pacing, and regenerate lines until the delivery sounds natural (ask for a more conversational read if it sounds like a robot reciting).
3. **Clone your own voice** for authenticity: record 2–3 minutes of yourself speaking and let the tool clone it, then you can scale your own voice across all videos without re-recording.
4. Always keep a human ear on the final audio — the accent, emphasis, and Nigerian-English context should sound like you, not a generic American teleprompter.

**Sound design:** add light background music (CapCut's library) at low volume under the voice, and use short natural pauses. Silence between words at the right places signals professionalism.

## Part 4 — Editing and Branding Consistency

Branding consistency is what separates content that builds a brand from content that just fills a feed. Every AI video you ship should be recognisably yours within three seconds.

**The brand system for video:**
1. **Fixed colours:** your brand palette in titles, captions, and lower-thirds — the same green button on your website should be the same green in your video captions.
2. **Fixed fonts:** one title font, one caption font, always. Resist the urge to try a new font each week.
3. **Fixed opening and ending:** a 1-second branded intro and a consistent outro with your logo and your one call-to-action.
4. **Caption everything:** on mobile feeds, most people watch with sound off initially. Auto-captions from CapCut AI, corrected manually, catch the scrollers.

**The workflow:** build a CapCut project template with your colours, fonts, intro, and outro saved once. Every new video loads the template — consistency becomes automatic, and production time drops dramatically.

## Part 5 — Platform Optimization: One Video, Many Fits

The same video should be re-cut for each platform, because each platform has its own rules of attention.

**YouTube:** longer formats win here — 3–8 minute explainers and demos, with a strong title and thumbnail (your Midjourney thumbnails matter more than you think). Search-driven: title and description should include what people search for.

**TikTok and Instagram Reels:** 15–45 seconds, vertical, hook in the first 2 seconds, captions on. The goal is completion rate — cut ruthlessly. Save the full version; post the tightest re-cut.

**LinkedIn:** 60–90 second professional cuts, value-first (no hard sell), with a text post that carries the idea. LinkedIn rewards thought leadership over ads.

**WhatsApp Status:** ultra-short (10–30 seconds), direct offers with a clear action, posted to status and catalogue-synced. This is the African trust channel — a short personal-feeling status converts like nothing else.

**The re-cut workflow:** create the master video once, then ask CapCut AI (or a human editor) for platform-specific versions: vertical re-frame for Reels/TikTok, longer cut for YouTube, shortened for status. One production session feeds five channels.

## Part 6 — Measuring Performance: Did It Convert?

Production without measurement is expensive guessing. The metric is never "views"; it is the action at the end.

**What to track per video:**
- **Completion rate** (platform analytics) — did they watch to the CTA?
- **Click-through / messages** — how many took the action?
- **Orders or enquiries attributed** — ask customers "how did you find us?" and log it.
- **Cost per acquisition** if you boosted the post with ad spend.

**The weekly system:** log every video in a spreadsheet — platform, format, hook, views, completion, actions, revenue. At month end, ask AI: "Here is my video performance data. Which hook style, length, and platform performed best, and what should I make more of?" Let the data pick your next scripts. One weekly honest review beats a hundred unmeasured videos.

## Part 8 — Quality Control and Ethical Use

**Quality control checklist before publishing:**
1. Watch with sound off — are captions complete and correct?
2. Watch with sound on — is the voice natural and the music not overpowering?
3. Check hands and faces — AI still struggles with fingers, text on signs, and long-form consistency. Regenerate anything uncanny.
4. Check facts and numbers — AI video content that misstates a price or claim damages trust instantly.
5. Test on a slow connection — does it load and play on a mid-range Android on metered data? Optimise size.

**Ethical rules that protect your brand:**
- **Disclose AI use** where it matters (especially for influencers, news, or anything fact-adjacent). African audiences are sharpening their "is this real?" instincts — being honest is a differentiator.
- **Never fabricate testimonials** or fake people saying things they never said. This is the fastest way to destroy the trust your whole content strategy is building.
- **Respect likeness and IP:** do not generate a competitor's or a real person's likeness without consent.
- **Keep a human in charge of the offer:** the price, the promise, and the call-to-action must be approved by the person accountable for them.

## Part 9 — Nigerian Business Strategies That Convert

**Explainer video (service businesses):** a 60-second explainer for a home-services, logistics, or agency brand, posted to the website homepage and YouTube, starring an AI-animated or AI-visual version of the service in action. It converts because it makes an abstract service tangible — and it lives forever as SEO content.

**Product demo (e-commerce):** a 30-second demo showing the product being used and the delivery promise ("ordered today, delivered in Lagos by Friday"), run as a WhatsApp Status and a Reels ad. The specificity — city, time, price — is what converts.

**Social proof reel (trust building):** a short reel combining real customer photos and reviews with AI-generated smooth transitions and captions. Real proof assembled with AI polish is the strongest trust content in the African market.

**The campaign pattern that converts:** one strong video, one clear offer, one platform, three days. Run the offer video, measure the action, then re-cut and re-run for the next three days. Small, tested, consistent cycles beat one massive unreachable production.

## The Bottom Line

AI did not make video easy to make good — it made it easy to make, and the difference is strategy. Script with a hook, generate consistent visuals, use a natural voice, edit with your brand system, re-cut per platform, and measure the action, not the views.

For a Nigerian business, the formula is simpler than it sounds: one problem-focused explainer a week, delivered in your brand's colours and voice, pushed through WhatsApp Status, Instagram, and a landing page, tracked in a spreadsheet, and iterated from data. That single habit, run consistently, out-produces any video strategy this market has seen before.
