When a person in Lagos searches Google for "best salon near me", "caterer in Lekki", "plumber in Abuja", or "fashion designer in Port Harcourt", they do not read every result. They look at the businesses with the most stars and the most reviews, and they tap those first. A business with 4.8 stars and 60 reviews will get the call. A similar business with no reviews will be ignored, even if its work is better. Google reviews are not a vanity metric. They are a direct source of new customers, and they cost nothing to earn.

For a small business in Nigeria, reviews matter even more, because trust is a real concern. Many customers have been disappointed by a business they found online. They want proof that you are real, that your work is good, and that other people have used your service and been happy. A set of honest Google reviews provides that proof before you ever speak to the customer. This guide shows you how to set up your Google Business Profile correctly, ask customers for reviews at the right moment, respond to every review, and use reviews to improve your business and rank higher on Google.

## Step 1: Set Up Your Google Business Profile Correctly

You cannot receive Google reviews until your business appears on Google. The free tool for this is Google Business Profile, formerly called Google My Business. Search for "Google Business Profile" and create a profile for your business, or claim it if Google already lists you. You will need a Google account, which is free. Enter your business name exactly as customers know it, choose the correct category, such as "Hair Salon", "Restaurant", "Plumber", or "Fashion Designer", and add your address, phone number, and WhatsApp link.

Add your working hours and be honest about them. If you open at 9am and close at 6pm, write that. If you close on Sundays, write that. Add a short description of what you do, in plain language, and include the areas you serve, such as Lekki, Ikeja, Abuja, or Port Harcourt. Add clear photos of your shop, your products, and your finished work. A profile with photos looks real and trustworthy. A profile with no photos looks abandoned.

Google will ask you to verify your business, usually by sending a code to your address, phone, or email, or by asking you to record a short video of your shop. Follow the steps carefully and complete verification as soon as you can. Until your profile is verified, your reviews and your ranking will be limited. Once verified, your business can appear on Google Search and Google Maps, where customers in your area are already looking.

## Step 2: Create a Simple Review Link and Share It Everywhere

Once your profile is live, Google gives you a review link. You can find it in your Google Business Profile dashboard, under the option to ask for reviews. Copy that link and save it somewhere you can find it quickly, such as your phone notes app. This link takes a customer directly to the page where they can leave a review. Do not ask customers to search for your business on Google and find the review button themselves. Many will give up. A direct link removes every obstacle.

Share the link in every place a customer might see it. Add it to your WhatsApp Business profile and to your Instagram bio. Print it as a QR code and put it on your shop counter, your receipt, your business card, and your flyer. Send it to customers on WhatsApp after a good service, with a short message asking for a review. Add it to your email signature if you use email. If you have a website, add a "Review us on Google" button that links to it. The easier you make it, the more reviews you will receive. A customer who has to work hard to leave a review will not leave one, no matter how happy they are.

Test the link yourself from another phone. Tap it and check that it opens the review page for your business, not a general Google search. If the link is long and ugly, use a free link shortener to make it neat, or use the QR code instead. Keep the link the same for every customer, so you do not have to create a new one each time.

## Step 3: Ask at the Right Moment

Timing is the most important part of getting reviews. The best time to ask is when the customer is happiest, which is usually right after a good service. A customer who has just received a beautiful hairstyle, tasted delicious food, or seen a clean house is far more likely to leave a review than the same customer a week later, when the moment has passed. Ask in person, face to face, while the customer is still smiling.

Use a simple, polite script. For example, "Thank you so much. I am glad you liked it. Would you mind leaving us a quick review on Google? It takes less than a minute and it really helps our small business." Then send the link on WhatsApp immediately, while the customer is still in your shop. Do not ask a customer who is unhappy or who has complained about the service. Fix the problem first, and ask for a review another time, when they are satisfied.

Train your staff to ask too. If you run a salon, a restaurant, or a shop in Lagos or Abuja, your staff see customers every day. Teach them the same simple script, and remind them to ask after a good service, not at a busy or stressful moment. You can also ask after a delivery. When a customer in Port Harcourt receives an order and sends you a message saying "I love it", reply with thanks and the review link. That message of delight is a perfect moment to ask.

## Step 4: Make It Easy with a Short Message Template

Most business owners fail to ask for reviews because they do not know what to say, or because they feel awkward. A short template solves this. Save two or three message templates in WhatsApp Business, so you can send them in seconds without thinking about the wording each time.

Here is a template you can copy and adapt. "Hello Ada, thank you for choosing our salon today. We hope you love your new hairstyle. If you have a minute, please leave us a Google review using this link. Your feedback helps other customers find us. Thank you." Keep it short, friendly, and personal. Use the customer's name if you know it. Do not send a long message with many paragraphs. A customer who receives a long message may not read it to the end.

Send the message once, and only once, for each visit or order. Do not send it every day, and do not send it to the same customer after every small purchase. Asking too often will annoy customers and may make them ignore you. A good rule is to ask after a first visit, after a large order, or after a customer tells you they are happy. If a customer does not leave a review after your message, do not chase them. Thank them for their business and move on. They may leave a review later, on their own, when they have time.

## Step 5: Respond to Every Review, Good and Bad

Replying to reviews is as important as receiving them. When you reply, you show future customers that you care, and you show Google that your business is active. Reply to every review within a day or two, if you can. Thank the customer by name, mention something specific from their review, and invite them to return.

For a positive review, a simple reply works well. "Thank you, Chidi. We are glad you enjoyed the food at your event in Lekki. We look forward to catering for you again." This reply is warm, personal, and it tells future customers what you do. For a short review that only says "Good service", reply with thanks and a line about your business. Do not copy the same reply for every review. Change a few words each time so your replies look genuine.

For a negative review, stay calm and professional. Never argue, never insult the customer, and never write in anger. Reply publicly with an apology and an offer to fix the problem. For example, "We are sorry your order arrived late, Ada. That is not the standard we aim for. Please send us a WhatsApp message so we can make it right." Then try to resolve the issue privately on WhatsApp or by phone. A calm, helpful reply to a negative review can actually increase trust, because future customers see that you handle problems well. If a review is fake or abusive, you can report it to Google, but do not expect it to be removed quickly. Focus on earning more good reviews, which will push the negative one down.

## Step 6: Use Reviews to Improve Your Business

Reviews are not just for marketing. They are free feedback from your customers. Read every review carefully and look for patterns. If three customers mention that your shop is hard to find, improve your directions and add a landmark to your profile. If several customers praise one staff member, recognise that person and learn what they do well. If several customers complain about slow service on Saturdays, look at your staffing on Saturdays and fix it.

Share good reviews with your team. When a customer praises your business, show the review to your staff. It motivates them and shows them what good service looks like. Share good reviews on your WhatsApp Status, Instagram, and Facebook too, with the customer's permission if you use their name. A screenshot of a real Google review is powerful social proof, because it comes from Google, not from you. When you share a review, add a short caption in your own words, such as "Thank you, Ada, for your kind words about our Lekki salon." Do not share every review every day, or your followers will stop paying attention. Share one strong review each week, and vary the format by using a screenshot one week and a short video of your team reading the review the next week. This keeps the message fresh while still building trust with people who have not yet tried your business.

Set a simple goal for reviews. For example, aim for five new reviews this month, or ten new reviews in the next three months. Track your total and your average star rating in a notebook or a spreadsheet. If your rating drops below 4.0, look at the recent reviews and fix the problems they mention. If your rating is above 4.5, celebrate it and keep doing what works. A small business in Nigeria with a strong review record will stand out, because many competitors have no reviews at all.

## Step 7: Earn Reviews Honestly and Avoid Shortcuts

It is tempting to take shortcuts, but they will hurt your business. Do not pay people to write reviews, do not ask friends who have never used your service to write reviews, and do not write reviews for your own business. Google can detect fake reviews, and it can remove them and penalise your profile. A customer who discovers that your reviews are fake will lose trust in you completely. Honest reviews, even if there are fewer of them, are worth far more than many fake ones.

Do not offer a discount or a gift in exchange for a review. Google's rules do not allow businesses to pay for reviews, even with a discount, and a customer who feels bribed may leave a review that sounds false. Instead, offer excellent service and ask politely. That is the honest path, and it works over time.

Be patient. A new business may take three to six months to build a strong set of reviews. That is normal. Focus on serving each customer well, asking at the right moment, and responding to every review. The reviews will accumulate, and each new review will make the next customer more likely to choose you. A business with 20 honest reviews is already ahead of most small businesses in Nigeria.

## Step 8: Ask Again After a Second Good Experience

One review from a customer is good. A second review is not possible from the same person for the same business in the normal way, but a returning customer can update their review, and a new customer they refer can leave one too. This is why follow-up matters. After a customer has visited you twice and is clearly happy, thank them again and remind them that their review helped other people find you. You can say, "Thank you for coming back. Your last review really helped us. If you have any new feedback, you can update it on Google." Many customers will add a line about their latest visit, which keeps your profile fresh and shows Google that your business is still active.

You can also turn happy customers into referrers. When a customer tells you they enjoyed your service, ask them if they know anyone else who might need it. A short message such as "Thank you. If you know anyone in Lekki or Ikeja who needs a reliable caterer, please share our Google page with them" is polite and effective. Word of mouth in Nigeria is powerful, and a Google review makes that recommendation easy to pass on. A friend who receives your Google link can see your reviews, your photos, and your location in seconds, and decide to contact you without any extra effort from you.

Keep a simple list of customers who have left reviews, so you do not ask the same person again and again. A notebook or a spreadsheet with the customer name, the date, and whether they reviewed you is enough. This list also helps you spot your most loyal customers. Those are the people you can invite to a new product launch, a special offer, or a seasonal event, because they already trust you and are most likely to come back and to bring friends with them.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

The first mistake is having no Google Business Profile at all. If customers cannot find you on Google, they cannot review you. Set up and verify your profile before you do anything else.

The second mistake is asking for reviews only once, at launch, and then never asking again. Reviews should be a regular habit, not a one-time campaign.

The third mistake is ignoring negative reviews. A negative review left unanswered looks worse than the review itself. Reply calmly and offer to fix the problem.

The fourth mistake is using the same copied reply for every review. Customers can tell when a reply is copied. Write a short, personal reply each time.

The fifth mistake is making it hard to leave a review. If you do not share a direct link or a QR code, most customers will not bother. Make it easy, and ask at the moment they are happiest.

## Final Checklist

Before you start, check that your Google Business Profile is set up and verified, your business name, address, phone number, and hours are correct, you have clear photos on your profile, you have copied your review link and tested it, you have a QR code on your counter and your flyer, you have a short message template saved in WhatsApp Business, your staff know when and how to ask, and you have a habit of replying to every review within a day or two. If you can tick most of these boxes, you are ready. Start asking your happiest customers today, one at a time, and watch your reviews grow. In a crowded market in Lagos, Abuja, or Port Harcourt, a strong set of Google reviews is one of the cheapest and most powerful ways to win new customers without spending money on adverts.
