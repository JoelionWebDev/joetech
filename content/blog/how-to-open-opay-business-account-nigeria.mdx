If you run a small business in Nigeria, you need a simple way to receive money from customers. Bank transfers work, but they can be slow, they can fail, and they do not always give your customers a smooth experience. OPay, one of the most widely used payment platforms in Nigeria, offers a business account that lets you receive payments by transfer, by QR code, by payment link, and through a POS terminal if you need one. Many customers already have the OPay app on their phones, and many more are happy to transfer to an OPay account because it is fast and familiar. This guide walks you through opening an OPay business account step by step: what you need, how to register, how to verify your account, what it costs, and how to start receiving payments from customers in Lagos, Abuja, and across Nigeria.

## What Is an OPay Business Account?

An OPay business account is an account on the OPay platform that is set up for a business, rather than for personal use. It allows you to receive payments from customers, keep your business money separate from your personal money, and track your sales in one place. With a business account, you can generate payment links to send to customers on WhatsApp, display a QR code in your shop for customers to scan and pay, and view a clear record of every payment you receive. Some businesses also use OPay to pay suppliers, to transfer money to staff, and to manage daily cash flow. An OPay business account is not a bank account in the traditional sense, but it is licensed and regulated in Nigeria, and it is designed for the way Nigerian small businesses actually operate: fast payments, mobile-first, and easy to use on a phone. Whether you sell food, fashion, electronics, or services, a business account gives you a professional way to collect money, and it helps you keep records that a personal account cannot provide.

## Who Should Open One?

An OPay business account is useful for almost any small business that receives payments from customers. If you run a shop in a market in Lagos, a salon in Ikeja, a food business in Abuja, or an online store that sells on Instagram and WhatsApp, a business account will help you. It is especially useful if your customers often pay by transfer, because an OPay account number is easy to share and easy to remember. It is also useful if you sell at events, markets, or pop-up shops, where a QR code or a payment link is faster than writing down a bank account number. Freelancers, caterers, event planners, and service providers can also use a business account to receive deposits and full payments from clients. If you already have a bank account for your business, an OPay business account can work alongside it. You can receive payments on OPay and transfer the money to your bank when you need it. If you do not yet have a business bank account, an OPay business account can be a good first step, because it is faster to open than a traditional bank account and it does not require a large opening balance.

## Step 1: Gather the Documents You Will Need

Before you start the registration, gather the documents you will need so the process is smooth. You will need a valid means of identification, such as your National Identity Number, your Permanent Voter's Card, your international passport, or your driver's licence. You will also need your Bank Verification Number, which is the 11-digit number linked to your bank accounts in Nigeria. If you do not know your BVN, dial the BVN code on the phone number linked to your bank account, or ask your bank. You will need a phone number that is active and that you can receive SMS messages on, because OPay will send you a verification code. You will also need an email address that you check regularly. If you are registering as a registered business, you may need your business registration documents, such as your CAC certificate, and your business name. If you are registering as a sole trader or an individual running a small business, you may only need your personal identification and your BVN. Keep all of these ready before you open the app, so you do not have to stop halfway through to search for a document.

## Step 2: Download the OPay App and Create an Account

Download the OPay app from the Google Play Store if you use an Android phone, or from the Apple App Store if you use an iPhone. Make sure you download the official OPay app, and check the developer name and the number of downloads before you install it. Open the app and tap Sign Up or Register. Enter your phone number and create a password you will remember. OPay will send a verification code to your phone by SMS. Enter that code in the app to confirm your number. Next, create a transaction PIN, which is a four-digit number you will use to confirm payments and transfers. Choose a PIN that is not your date of birth and not a simple sequence such as 1234, and do not share it with anyone. Once your personal account is created, look for the option to upgrade to a business account or to register as a merchant. The exact wording in the app may change over time, but it is usually found in your profile, in settings, or under a section labelled Business or Merchant. Tap that option to start the business registration.

## Step 3: Complete the Business Registration Form

The business registration form will ask for details about you and your business. Enter your full name exactly as it appears on your identification document. Enter your business name, which is the name customers know you by. If your business is registered with the Corporate Affairs Commission, enter the registered name. If it is not registered, enter your trading name, such as "Ada's Kitchen" or "Tunde Fashion Hub". Choose your business category from the list provided, such as Food and Drinks, Fashion, Retail, Services, or Electronics. Choose the option that is closest to what you do. Enter your business address, which can be your shop address, your market stall location, or your home address if you work from home. Enter your email address and confirm it. The form may also ask for your BVN and your date of birth, so OPay can verify your identity. Fill in every field carefully, and double-check your spelling before you submit. A small mistake, such as a wrong digit in your BVN or a misspelt name, can delay your verification by days. If you are unsure about a field, read the help text in the app or contact OPay support before you submit.

## Step 4: Verify Your Identity and Your Business

After you submit the form, OPay will ask you to verify your identity. This usually involves uploading a clear photo of your identification document and taking a selfie so OPay can confirm that you are the person on the document. Take the photo in good light, make sure the document is not blurry, and make sure all four corners are visible. For the selfie, look directly at the camera, remove sunglasses and hats, and make sure your face is clearly visible. OPay may also ask you to confirm your BVN by entering it again or by completing a short verification step. Verification can take a few minutes, a few hours, or up to a day, depending on how busy the system is and whether your documents are clear. You will receive a notification in the app and by SMS when your verification is complete. If your verification fails, do not panic. Read the reason given, correct the problem, and submit again. Common reasons for failure are a blurry photo, a document that has expired, or a name that does not match the name on your BVN. If you keep having problems, contact OPay customer support through the app or visit an OPay agent or office near you for help.

## Step 5: Set Up How You Want to Receive Payments

Once your business account is verified, set up the ways you want to receive payments. The simplest option is your OPay account number, which you can share with customers on WhatsApp, on your Instagram bio, or on your invoices. Customers can transfer money to that number from any Nigerian bank or from another OPay account. The second option is a payment link. In the OPay app, you can create a link for a specific amount, such as ₦15,000 for a dress, and send it to a customer on WhatsApp. The customer taps the link, pays, and you receive a notification. The third option is a QR code. You can display your business QR code in your shop, at a market stall, or on a flyer, and customers can scan it with their OPay app or their bank app to pay you. The fourth option is a POS terminal, which OPay offers to some merchants. A POS terminal lets you accept card payments in person, which is useful if you run a physical shop. You can apply for a POS terminal in the app or through an OPay agent. Start with the account number, payment links, and QR code, because they cost nothing to set up and they cover most small business needs. Add a POS terminal later if you find that many of your customers prefer to pay by card.

## Step 6: Understand the Fees Before You Start

Every payment platform charges fees, and OPay is no exception. Fees can change over time, so always check the current fees inside the OPay app or on the OPay website before you rely on them. In general, receiving money by transfer to your OPay account is often free or very cheap, especially when the sender uses OPay. Transferring money from your OPay account to a bank account may attract a small fee, depending on the amount and the destination bank. Receiving payments through a payment link or a QR code may also attract a small percentage fee. POS transactions usually attract a small percentage fee as well. These fees are usually small, often less than one percent or a small fixed amount, but they add up if you process many payments, so factor them into your prices. For example, if you sell a product for ₦20,000 and the fee is ₦100, your real income is ₦19,900. That difference is small, but you should know about it. Do not set your prices based on the full amount if a fee will be deducted. Check your transaction history regularly to see exactly what fees you have paid, and if a fee looks wrong, contact OPay support with the transaction details.

## Step 7: Start Receiving Payments and Keep Good Records

Now you are ready to receive payments. Share your OPay account number or payment link with your next customer, and ask them to send you a screenshot or a confirmation message after they pay. When you receive a payment, check the OPay app to confirm it arrived before you deliver the goods or start the service. Do not rely on a screenshot alone, because screenshots can be edited. Open your OPay app, check your balance and your transaction history, and confirm the amount and the sender's name. Keep a record of every payment in your own spreadsheet or notebook, with the date, the customer's name, the amount, and what it was for. This record will help you at the end of the month when you check your income, and it will help you if a customer ever disputes a payment. If a customer pays the wrong amount, contact them immediately and sort it out while the details are fresh. If a payment fails or is delayed, check your network connection, check the app for a notification, and contact OPay support if the problem continues. A good habit is to check your OPay balance at the end of every day, just as you would count the cash in your shop.

## Step 8: Withdraw Your Money and Manage Your Balance

Money in your OPay account is useful, but you will need to move it to your bank account for rent, for large supplier payments, or for savings. To withdraw, open the OPay app, go to Transfer, choose Transfer to Bank, select your bank, enter your account number, enter the amount, and confirm with your transaction PIN. The money usually arrives in your bank account within minutes, but it can take longer if your bank is having network issues. Keep some money in your OPay account for daily business needs, such as paying a delivery rider or buying small stock, and transfer the rest to your bank regularly. Do not leave a large amount in your OPay account for a long time, just as you would not leave a large amount of cash in your shop overnight. Transfer money to your bank every Friday, keep your app updated, keep your phone locked, and never share your transaction PIN or login password with anyone. If your phone is lost or stolen, contact OPay immediately to secure your account.

## How to Use Your OPay Account to Grow Your Business

An OPay business account is more than a way to receive money. It can help you grow if you use it well. First, put your OPay number on all your marketing materials: your flyers, your WhatsApp status, your Instagram bio, and your business cards. The easier it is to pay you, the more likely a customer is to buy. Second, use payment links for specific products. A link that says "Pay ₦15,000 for this dress" is clearer than a general account number, and it reduces mistakes. Third, offer a small incentive for customers who pay through OPay, such as faster delivery or a small discount, if you can afford it. Fourth, use your transaction history to understand your business. Which days bring the most payments? Which products sell best? Which customers pay on time? That information can help you plan stock and promotions. Fifth, consider applying for an OPay POS terminal when your sales grow, so you can serve customers who prefer to pay by card.

## Common Problems and How to Fix Them

The most common problem is a failed verification, usually because a photo is blurry or a name does not match. Retake the photo in good light and check your spelling. The second problem is a payment that does not arrive. Ask the customer for the transaction reference, check your OPay transaction history, and contact OPay support if the money has left the customer's account but has not arrived in yours. The third problem is a forgotten transaction PIN. You can reset it in the app using your phone number and your BVN, but you will need to verify your identity again. The fourth problem is a locked account, which can happen if you enter the wrong PIN too many times or if OPay detects unusual activity. Contact OPay support and follow their instructions to unlock it. Do not try to create a second account to get around a lock, because that can cause more problems. The fifth problem is a customer who claims to have paid but has not. Always check your own app before you deliver goods. The sixth problem is network issues during a payment. Ask the customer to try again, or to use a different payment method, such as a bank transfer, if the problem continues.

## Final Thoughts

Opening an OPay business account in Nigeria is a straightforward process: gather your identification and your BVN, download the app, register, verify your identity, and set up the payment methods that suit your business. The whole process can take as little as a day if your documents are ready and clear. Once your account is open, use it properly: share your payment details clearly, confirm every payment in the app before you deliver, keep records of your sales, transfer money to your bank regularly, and keep your PIN and password safe. An OPay business account will not make your business successful on its own, but it will remove one of the biggest frustrations for Nigerian small businesses: getting paid quickly, safely, and professionally. Set it up today, test it with a small payment from a trusted customer or a friend, and start offering your customers an easier way to pay you. The easier you make it to pay, the easier you make it to grow.
