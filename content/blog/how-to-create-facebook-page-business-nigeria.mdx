If you run a business in Nigeria and you are not on Facebook, you are missing out on customers who are already there. Facebook remains one of the most used social platforms in the country. People search for tailors, caterers, phone repairers, salons, restaurants, and real estate agents on Facebook every single day. When someone hears about your business, one of the first things they do is check whether you have a Facebook Page.

This guide walks you through the whole process, step by step, from creating the Page to making it look professional enough that a stranger trusts you with their money. It also covers the common mistakes Nigerian business owners make, and how to avoid them.

## 1. Create the Page the Right Way

Start by logging into your personal Facebook account on your phone or computer. You cannot create a Page without a personal account, but do not worry — your personal posts and friends stay private, and your Page is a separate public space for your business.

On the Facebook app, tap the menu icon and look for "Pages", then tap "Create". On a computer, click "Pages" in the left menu, then "Create new Page". Facebook will ask for three things: the Page name, the category, and a short description. Use your exact business name, the one on your signboard and receipts, so customers can find you easily. For the category, pick the closest match, such as "Clothing Store", "Restaurant", "Beauty Salon", or "Local Service". If none fits perfectly, choose the nearest one — you can change it later.

The description is short, usually one or two sentences. Say what you sell and who it is for. For example: "We make ready-to-wear ankara dresses for women in Lekki and deliver nationwide." That is better than a vague line like "We sell quality products." Be specific, because people decide in seconds whether to follow you or scroll past.

## 2. Add a Profile Picture and Cover Photo

People judge a Page by its pictures before they read a single word. Your profile picture should be your business logo, or a clear photo of your product if you do not have a logo yet. Use a square image that is sharp and easy to recognise even when it appears small on a phone screen. Avoid blurry photos, screenshots, or pictures with too much text squeezed in.

Your cover photo is the wide banner at the top of your Page. Use it to show your best work: your shop front, your most popular products, or a photo of you serving a happy customer. If you offer a service, a photo of you at work builds trust faster than any written claim. Update this photo from time to time, for example when you launch a new product or run a promotion.

## 3. Fill In Every Detail on Your Page

An empty Page scares customers away. After creating the Page, go to the "About" section and fill in everything Facebook asks for: phone number, WhatsApp number, email address, location, opening hours, and your website or online catalogue link if you have one.

Add your WhatsApp number as the main contact button. In Nigeria, most customers prefer to chat on WhatsApp before buying, so make that button easy to find. Set the action button at the top of your Page to "WhatsApp" or "Send Message" so a visitor can reach you in one tap.

Write your opening hours honestly. If you open at 9am and close at 6pm, say so. If you work from home and deliver only, say that too. If you cannot reply instantly, set up an automatic reply that says when you will respond, for example: "Thank you for messaging us. We reply within 2 hours, Monday to Saturday."

Also add your location, even if it is just your area, such as "Lekki Phase 1, Lagos". Many customers search for businesses near them, and a Page with no location looks suspicious.

## 4. Make Your First Posts Before Inviting Anyone

Do not invite your friends to a Page with zero posts. An empty Page looks abandoned, and people will not follow it. Before you invite anyone, publish at least five to ten posts so that visitors see a living, active business.

Your first posts should answer the questions a new customer would ask. Post clear photos of your products or services with prices where possible. Post a short introduction about your business: who you are, what you sell, and why customers should choose you. If you have served customers before, post their feedback with their permission — real reviews are the strongest trust signal a small business can have.

Take your photos in good daylight, against a clean background. You do not need a professional camera; a phone camera and natural light are enough. Wipe the lens, hold the phone steady, and take several shots so you can pick the best one. A clear photo of a dress on a hanger sells better than a dark photo of ten dresses piled on a bed.

## 5. Post Regularly and Talk to Your Customers

A Page only works if you keep it alive. Posting once a month is not enough. Aim for three to five posts a week. You do not need to post every day, but you do need to be consistent. A customer who sees your Page active this week is far more likely to message you than one who sees your last post was three months ago.

Mix up your posts. Share new products, behind-the-scenes photos, short tips related to your trade, customer reviews, and special offers. Ask questions to start conversations, such as "Which colour do you prefer, blue or green?" People love to give their opinion, and every comment helps Facebook show your post to more people.

Reply to every comment and message, even the short ones. A quick "Thank you" or a helpful answer shows that a real person is behind the Page. When someone asks for a price in the comments, answer politely or invite them to WhatsApp, but never ignore them. Ignored customers rarely come back.

Avoid the temptation to buy followers or likes. Bought followers are mostly fake accounts that never buy anything, and they damage your Page because Facebook sees that your posts get no real engagement. Grow slowly with real customers instead.

## 6. Use Free Facebook Tools to Reach More People

Facebook gives Page owners free tools that many small businesses never touch. The first is Insights, which shows you how many people saw your posts, what time your followers are online, and which posts performed best. Check it once a week and post more of what works.

The second is the ability to share your Page link everywhere. Put your Facebook Page link on your WhatsApp status, your business card, your receipts, and your shop signboard. A customer who sees your Page link on a receipt can follow you in seconds.

You can also join Facebook Groups where your customers gather — for example, buy-and-sell groups in your area or groups for parents, brides, or homeowners, depending on what you sell. Share helpful posts there, not just adverts, and always follow the group rules. One helpful post in the right group can bring in more customers than a week of posting on your Page alone.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is creating a Facebook Page free in Nigeria?

Yes, creating and running a Facebook Page is completely free. You only pay if you choose to run paid adverts. You can post photos, reply to customers, and use Insights without spending a naira.

### Do I need a business name registered with the CAC to create a Page?

No. Facebook does not ask for a CAC certificate to create a Page. You can start with your trading name and register your business formally later as it grows. Use the name your customers already know you by.

### Can I use my personal Facebook profile to sell instead of a Page?

You can, but a Page is better. A profile has a friend limit, no business tools, and Facebook can restrict accounts that use a personal profile mainly for selling. A Page lets unlimited people follow you, shows your business details, and looks far more professional to new customers.

### How do I get my first followers?

Start with people who already know you: friends, family, and existing customers. Invite them from your Page, share the Page link on WhatsApp, and add the link to your receipts and business card. Then keep posting useful content so those followers share your posts with their own friends.

## Conclusion

Creating a Facebook Page for your business in Nigeria takes less than thirty minutes, but the difference it makes can last for years. It gives your business a public face, a place where customers can find you, see your work, and reach you on WhatsApp. The businesses that win on Facebook are not always the biggest ones — they are the ones that fill in their details, post clear photos, reply quickly, and show up consistently.

Set up your Page today, make your first posts, and invite your first followers. Then keep it active, one good post at a time.
