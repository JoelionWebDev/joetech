Before Zoom became a household name, a meeting with a client in Abuja or a supplier in London meant travel, traffic, and a full day gone. Now you can sit in your office in Lekki, open your laptop, and meet someone face to face in seconds. For a small business owner in Nigeria, that is a real advantage: you can pitch a client in another state, interview a staff member who lives far away, or hold a team meeting with people in three different locations — all without anyone spending money on transport.

Zoom is the most widely used video meeting tool in the world, and it is free for basic use. You do not need special equipment: a phone, a laptop, a camera and a microphone are enough. But there is a difference between using Zoom and using it well. A meeting where half the people cannot hear, the screen sharing fails, and nobody knows who is supposed to talk next wastes everyone's time and makes your business look unprofessional.

This guide shows you how to use Zoom for business meetings properly: setting it up, scheduling a meeting, running it smoothly, sharing your screen, recording it, and handling the problems that come up most often.

## What Zoom Is and What the Free Version Gives You

Zoom is a video conferencing app. You create a meeting, send people a link, and they join by clicking it — from a phone, a tablet or a computer. They do not need a Zoom account to join your meeting; they just need the link and the app, or even just a web browser.

The free version of Zoom is enough for most small businesses. It lets you hold meetings with up to 100 participants, share your screen, use the chat, and record to your own computer. The main limit is time: group meetings on the free plan end after 40 minutes. One-to-one meetings — just you and one other person — do not have that limit. If your meetings regularly run longer than 40 minutes with three or more people, you will need a paid plan, or you can simply start a new meeting when the first one ends and send the new link to everyone.

There are paid plans that remove the 40-minute limit and add features like cloud recording, but do not rush to pay. Start with the free version, learn how your business actually uses Zoom, and upgrade only when the limit genuinely gets in your way.

## Setting Up Zoom

### On Your Phone

Download the Zoom app from the Google Play Store or the Apple App Store — it is called "Zoom Workplace" in the stores. Open it and tap "Sign Up" to create a free account with your email address, or sign in with Google. Once you are in, you can start a meeting, join one, or schedule one for later. The phone app is fine for joining meetings and for quick calls, but for hosting an important business meeting, a laptop is better: the screen is bigger, screen sharing is easier, and you look more professional.

### On Your Computer

Go to zoom.us in your browser and click "Sign Up Free". Create your account with your business email address — this is better than a personal email, because the meeting invitations will come from your business address. After signing up, download the Zoom desktop app for Windows or Mac and install it. The desktop app is more reliable than joining from the browser, and it gives you all the controls in one place.

Once you are signed in, take a few minutes to set up your profile. Add your real name and your business name if you want it shown. Upload a professional profile photo — the same one you use on LinkedIn is a good choice. Check your audio and video settings before your first meeting, not during it.

### Test Your Camera, Microphone and Internet

Before any important meeting, test your setup. In the Zoom app, go to Settings, then Video, and you will see a preview of what your camera shows. Check that your face is well lit and centred, and that the background is tidy. Go to Settings, then Audio, and click "Test Speaker" and "Test Mic" — speak and listen to the playback to make sure your microphone works.

Your internet connection matters more than anything else. Zoom needs a stable connection. Wi-Fi is usually better than mobile data, but if your Wi-Fi is slow or unreliable, a good 4G connection can work. Close other apps that use the internet — YouTube, downloads, cloud backups — before an important meeting. If your video keeps freezing, turn your camera off and continue with audio only; a clear voice matters more than a frozen face.

## Scheduling a Meeting

There are two kinds of Zoom meetings: instant meetings, which you start right now, and scheduled meetings, which you plan in advance. For business, scheduled meetings are better, because the other person gets a proper invitation with the date, time and link.

### Schedule From the App

Open Zoom and click "Schedule". Give the meeting a clear title — "Website Project Discussion with Mrs Adeyemi" is better than "Meeting". Set the date, the start time and the duration. Zoom shows times in your computer's time zone, so if you are meeting someone in another country, double-check the time zone before you send the invite.

You can set a meeting password. Zoom adds one by default for scheduled meetings, and it is a good idea to keep it — it stops strangers joining your meeting from a shared link. You can also turn on the waiting room, which means people wait outside the meeting until you let them in. This is useful for client meetings: it stops someone joining early and seeing you unprepared, and it stops the wrong person joining by mistake.

Add the email addresses of the people you are inviting, or copy the meeting link and send it yourself on WhatsApp or email. WhatsApp is often the most reliable way to reach a Nigerian client — many people check WhatsApp faster than email. Send the link the day before and again an hour before the meeting as a reminder.

### Your Personal Meeting ID

Zoom gives you a Personal Meeting ID, a number that is always yours. You can use it for regular meetings, like a weekly team catch-up, so the link never changes. This is convenient, but be careful: if you share your Personal Meeting ID widely, anyone with it can try to join. For client meetings and one-off calls, use a fresh scheduled meeting with its own link and password instead.

## Joining and Starting a Meeting

When it is time to meet, open Zoom and click "Start" on your scheduled meeting, or click the link in your invitation. Join a few minutes early so you can check your camera and microphone before anyone else arrives. If you are the host, you control the meeting: you can mute people, let people in from the waiting room, and end the meeting for everyone.

If you are joining someone else's meeting, click the link they sent you. You may be asked to download the Zoom app if you do not have it, or you can join from your browser. Enter your name as you want it to appear — your real name, not a nickname or a phone model name. When you join, Zoom will ask whether to join with video and audio. Choose to join with video on for business meetings: people trust a face more than a black square with a name on it.

Mute yourself when you are not speaking, especially in a group meeting. Background noise — a generator, traffic, someone talking in the next room — is distracting for everyone. The mute button is at the bottom left of the Zoom window, and so is the video button. Learn where they are before the meeting starts, not during it.

## Running a Professional Meeting

### Before the Meeting

Send an agenda. Even a short one makes a big difference: "We will discuss the website design, the price, and the timeline." It tells everyone what to prepare and keeps the meeting focused. If you need the client to look at something before the meeting — a design, a document, a quotation — send it in advance, not during the call.

Prepare what you will share. If you plan to show your screen, open the document or website before the meeting starts so you are not searching for it while everyone watches. Close private tabs, messages and documents you do not want anyone to see. Turn on Do Not Disturb on your phone and your computer so notifications do not pop up on your shared screen.

### During the Meeting

Start on time. Starting ten minutes late because you were waiting for someone tells everyone your time does not matter. If someone is late, start without them and catch them up when they join.

Introduce people if they do not know each other, state the purpose of the meeting in one sentence, and say how long it will take. Keep the meeting moving: if a discussion goes off track, say so politely and bring it back.

### Screen Sharing

Screen sharing is Zoom's most useful feature for business. You can show a client your website design, walk a supplier through a spreadsheet, or present a proposal without emailing files back and forth. Click "Share Screen" at the bottom of the Zoom window, choose the window you want to share — a specific browser tab, a PowerPoint presentation, or your whole screen — and click "Share".

Share one window, not your whole screen, when you can. Sharing your whole screen risks showing notifications, private messages or other tabs by accident. When you finish showing something, click "Stop Share" so everyone sees your face again. Speak while you share: explain what you are showing as you go, and pause to ask if there are questions.

### Recording the Meeting

Recording a meeting is useful when you need to remember what was agreed — the price, the deadline, the next steps. On the free plan you can record to your computer: click "Record" at the bottom of the Zoom window. When the meeting ends, Zoom converts the recording and saves it on your computer, usually in a folder called Zoom inside your Documents.

Always tell people you are recording before you start. It is polite, it is professional, and it avoids problems later. A simple "I will record this meeting so we have a record of what we agreed — is that alright with everyone?" is enough. If anyone objects, do not record. Take notes instead.

## Common Zoom Problems and How to Fix Them

### "I can't hear anyone" or "Nobody can hear me"

This is the most common Zoom problem and it is almost always an audio setting. Check that you joined with audio — look for the microphone icon at the bottom left. If it has a red line through it, you are muted; click it to unmute. If that does not work, click the small arrow beside the microphone icon and check that Zoom is using the right microphone and speaker. If you are using earphones, make sure they are properly connected. Leaving the meeting and rejoining often fixes it.

### Video keeps freezing

Your internet connection is too slow for video. Turn off your camera and continue with audio. Close other apps using the internet. Move closer to your Wi-Fi router if you can. Audio-only is always better than a frozen, pixelated picture.

### The meeting ended after 40 minutes

That is the free plan's limit for group meetings. Either upgrade to a paid plan, or schedule your meetings in 40-minute blocks and send a new link when the first one ends. Some business owners plan their meetings around this: they keep client calls short and focused, which is good practice anyway.

### Someone joined who should not be there

This happens when a meeting link is shared too widely. End the meeting, and next time use a password and the waiting room, and do not post the link publicly. As the host, you can also remove a person from a meeting: click on Participants, find their name, and choose "Remove".

### Echo or feedback

Echo usually happens when someone joins the meeting twice — once on their laptop and once on their phone — or when their speakers are too loud and their microphone picks up the sound. Ask everyone to join from one device only, and to use earphones if they can.

## Zoom Etiquette for Business

A few small habits make you look professional on Zoom. Dress as you would for a face-to-face meeting — at least from the waist up, since that is what the camera sees. Sit facing a light source, not with a bright window behind you, or your face will be in shadow. Look at the camera when you speak, not at the little video of yourself. Keep your background tidy: a plain wall is better than a messy room, and Zoom lets you blur your background if you need to.

Be on time, mute when you are not speaking, and do not eat during a meeting. These are small things, but clients notice them, and they shape how people judge your business.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Zoom free for business meetings?

Yes, the free version covers most small business needs: meetings with up to 100 people, screen sharing, chat, and recording to your computer. The main limit is that group meetings end after 40 minutes. One-to-one meetings have no time limit. Paid plans remove the limit and add cloud recording and other features.

### Do clients need a Zoom account to join my meeting?

No. Anyone can join your meeting by clicking the link you send them. They may need to install the Zoom app on their phone or computer, or they can join from a web browser. They do not need to create an account or pay anything.

### How do I share my screen on Zoom?

During a meeting, click "Share Screen" at the bottom of the window, choose the window you want to show, and click "Share". Share one window rather than your whole screen so private notifications and tabs stay hidden. Click "Stop Share" when you are done.

### Can I record a Zoom meeting on the free plan?

Yes. On the free plan you can record to your computer by clicking "Record" during the meeting. The recording is saved on your computer when the meeting ends. Cloud recording — saving the video on Zoom's servers — is a paid feature. Always tell everyone you are recording before you start.

### Why does my Zoom meeting end after 40 minutes?

That is the time limit on the free plan for meetings with three or more people. You can start a new meeting with a fresh link, or upgrade to a paid plan if your meetings regularly run longer. One-to-one meetings on the free plan do not have this limit.

### Is Zoom safe to use for business?

Zoom is widely used by businesses, banks and schools. Keep your meetings safe by using a password, turning on the waiting room, not sharing meeting links publicly, and using your Personal Meeting ID only for regular meetings with people you trust. Keep the app updated so you get the latest security fixes.
