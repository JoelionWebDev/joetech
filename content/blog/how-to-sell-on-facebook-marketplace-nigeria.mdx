Facebook Marketplace is one of the easiest places to sell in Nigeria, and one of the most frustrating. It costs nothing to list, millions of Nigerians browse it every day, and a good listing can sell within hours. But most sellers know the other side of it too: listings that get hundreds of views and not a single message, buyers who ask the price that is already written in the description, people who promise to come and never show up, and the endless "is this still available?" messages that lead nowhere.

The difference between a listing that sells and a listing that sits is rarely the product. It is the photos, the price, the description, and how fast you reply. This guide covers all four, plus the practical realities of selling on Marketplace in Nigeria — payment, delivery, safety, and the scams to watch out for. Whether you sell fashion, phones, furniture, food items or fairly used goods, the same rules apply.

## Why Marketplace Works — and Why Most Listings Fail

Marketplace works because it meets buyers where they already are. Nigerians spend hours on Facebook every day, and Marketplace puts your product in front of them without adverts, without a website, and without them having to search for a shop. A buyer in Lekki looking for a fairly used iPhone, or a buyer in Ikeja looking for a sofa, opens Marketplace, types what they want, and sees what is near them. If your listing is there, clear and convincing, you are in the running.

Most listings fail for simple, fixable reasons. The photos are dark, blurry, or taken from too far away. The price is missing, or it is a placeholder price that has nothing to do with the real price. The description is one line — "call me" — with no useful information. Or the seller takes a day to reply, by which time the buyer has bought from someone else. None of these are hard to fix, and fixing them is what separates the sellers who make steady sales from the sellers who complain that Marketplace does not work.

## Photos That Make People Stop Scrolling

Photos are the most important part of your listing, because they are the first thing a buyer sees — and often the only thing they see before deciding to tap or scroll past. On Marketplace, your first photo is your shop window. If it is bad, nothing else matters, because the buyer will never read your description.

Take your photos in good, natural light. Daylight near a window or outside in the shade is far better than a dim room with a yellow bulb. Wipe your phone camera lens first — a smudged lens is the most common reason for blurry photos, and it takes two seconds to fix. Hold the phone steady, get close enough that the product fills most of the frame, and take the photo straight on, not at a strange angle.

Show the product from several angles. For a phone, show the front, the back, and the screen switched on. For a shoe, show both shoes, the soles, and any labels. For furniture, show the whole item, then close-ups of the material and any marks or damage. Honest photos of small faults build trust far more than hiding them — a buyer who discovers a scratch on arrival will demand a discount or walk away, while a buyer who saw the scratch in the photos has already accepted it.

Use all the photo slots Marketplace gives you. A listing with one photo looks like a seller who is hiding something; a listing with six or eight good photos looks like a seller who is proud of the product. Include a photo of the item in use or in context if it helps — a dress on a hanger and laid flat, a chair in a room, a blender with its parts laid out. And keep the background clean and uncluttered. A plain wall or a tidy table makes the product stand out; a messy room makes the buyer wonder how the product was treated.

## Pricing: The Number That Decides Everything

Price is the second thing a buyer looks at, and it is the reason most listings get views but no messages. The most common pricing mistake on Nigerian Marketplace is leaving the price blank, or entering a placeholder like ₦1, or writing "DM for price". Every one of these kills your listing. Buyers filter by price, so a listing with no real price often does not appear in their search at all. And buyers who do see it assume the seller is hiding the price because it is too high, or because the price depends on how much the seller thinks they can charge you.

Always put the real price. Research what similar items sell for on Marketplace before you list — search for the same product, look at five or ten listings, and note the range. Price yours fairly within that range, considering the condition of your item. A fairly used phone in good condition is not worth the price of a new one, and a buyer knows it. Overpricing is the most common reason a listing sits for weeks.

Decide your negotiation room in advance. Most Nigerian buyers will negotiate, and that is normal — it is part of how buying works here. So set your listed price slightly above the lowest amount you will accept, and know that lowest amount before the first message arrives. If your lowest price is ₦45,000, list at ₦50,000 and you have room to meet the buyer partway. If you list at ₦45,000 with no room to move, every negotiation becomes a loss, and you will end up either refusing every buyer or selling below what you wanted.

## Writing a Description That Answers Questions Before They Are Asked

A good description does one job: it answers the buyer's questions before they have to ask them. Every question a buyer has to ask in a message is a chance for the conversation to die. Every question your description already answers is a step closer to a sale.

Start with the essential facts, in the first two lines, because many buyers read only that far. What is it, exactly — brand, model, size, colour? What condition is it in — new, fairly used, barely used? What is the price, and is it negotiable? Where are you located? Those four facts decide whether the buyer keeps reading.

Then add the details that matter for your type of product. For electronics: does it come with a charger, a box, or accessories? How long have you used it, and does everything work? For clothing: what size is it, what material, and does it run small or true to size? For furniture: what are the measurements, and can it be dismantled for transport? For food items: what is the weight or quantity, and what is the expiry date? Think about what you would want to know before buying the same item, and write that down.

Be honest about faults. If a phone has a small crack on the corner, say so. If a bag has a faded spot, say so. Honesty in the description does two things: it filters out buyers who will not accept the fault, saving you wasted conversations, and it builds trust with the buyers who remain, because they know you are straight with them. A seller who discloses a fault is a seller a buyer can trust about everything else.

End the description with a clear next step: how to reach you, and what you need from the buyer. "Message me here on Facebook, or WhatsApp me on 080... Please tell me your location so I can confirm delivery cost." That tells the buyer exactly what to do, and it gives you the information you need to quote delivery without a long back-and-forth.

## Replying Fast and Closing the Sale

Speed is the most underrated selling skill on Marketplace. When a buyer messages you, they are usually messaging three or four sellers at the same time, comparing prices and replies. The seller who replies first, helpfully and completely, usually gets the sale. A reply that comes a day later often arrives after the buyer has already bought from someone else.

Turn on notifications for Facebook and Messenger, and check them regularly. When a message arrives, reply within minutes if you possibly can, and within an hour at the very latest. Your first reply should answer the buyer's question directly and completely — if they ask the price, give the price; if they ask for more photos, send them; if they ask about delivery, give them the delivery cost for their area. A short, vague reply like "DM me" or "call me" pushes the buyer away, because it asks them to do more work.

Have your standard answers ready. Most buyers ask the same five questions: the price, the location, the condition, whether delivery is available, and whether the price is negotiable. Prepare a short, friendly reply for each one, and keep the key facts — price, location, delivery cost — somewhere you can copy quickly. This is not about being robotic; it is about being fast and accurate, so the buyer gets what they need without waiting.

When a buyer is ready to buy, make the next step simple and clear. Confirm the price, confirm the delivery arrangement, and confirm the payment method. If the buyer is coming to collect, agree on a time and a place, and send your exact location. If you are delivering, confirm the address and the delivery fee before dispatching. A clear, confirmed plan prevents the misunderstandings that cause failed deliveries and wasted trips.

## Payment and Delivery: Getting Paid Safely

Payment is where Marketplace sales go wrong most often, so agree on it clearly before you hand anything over. For most small sellers in Nigeria, the safest arrangement is payment before delivery for items sent by rider, or payment on collection for buyers who come to you. Bank transfer is the most common method, and it has a useful feature: the buyer can send you a screenshot, and you can confirm the money in your account before dispatching the item. Never dispatch an item on the strength of a screenshot alone — confirm the money has actually arrived in your account, because screenshots can be faked.

Be very careful with payment on delivery for items sent by rider. If the buyer refuses the item or claims they have no cash, the rider returns with your product and you have paid for a wasted trip. If you do offer payment on delivery, agree the exact amount in advance, including the delivery fee, and make sure the rider knows the amount to collect.

For delivery, use a rider or logistics company you trust, and agree the fee with the buyer before dispatching. Delivery costs within Lagos vary by distance, so ask for the buyer's area first and quote the fee for that area. Package the item well — a damaged item on arrival becomes your problem, even if the rider caused the damage. And always get the buyer's phone number before dispatch, so the rider can call on arrival.

## Staying Safe and Avoiding Scams

Marketplace is full of genuine buyers, and it also attracts scammers. Knowing the common tricks will save you money and stress.

The most common scam is the fake payment alert. A buyer sends you a screenshot of a transfer, or an SMS that looks like a bank alert, but no money has actually arrived. The rule is simple: never release an item until you have checked your own bank account or banking app and confirmed the money is there. A screenshot is not proof. An SMS is not proof. Only your own account balance is proof.

Another common trick is the overpayment scam. A buyer "mistakenly" sends more than the price and asks you to refund the difference. The original payment is fake or will be reversed, and the refund you send is real money lost. If anyone overpays, do not refund anything until you have confirmed the original payment is genuine and settled in your account.

Be careful with buyers who want to move the conversation off Facebook immediately to a private channel, especially if they are in a hurry or their story does not add up. And never share sensitive information — your bank login, your card details, any code sent to your phone — with a buyer, for any reason. No genuine buyer needs those.

For physical meetings, meet in a safe, public place during the day — a busy area, a mall, or near your shop if you have one. Tell someone where you are going. If a buyer insists on meeting somewhere isolated or late at night, decline the sale. No sale is worth your safety. For high-value items, consider meeting at a police station or a bank branch, which many sellers in Lagos now do routinely.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need to pay to list on Facebook Marketplace?**

No. Listing on Facebook Marketplace is free. You can create a listing from the Facebook app or website, add photos and a description, and start receiving messages without paying anything. Facebook also offers paid boosts for listings, but these are optional — most small sellers sell well without them.

**Why does my listing get views but no messages?**

Usually the photos, the price, or the description. Check that your first photo is clear and well-lit, that you have entered the real price rather than a placeholder, and that your description answers the key questions: what it is, what condition it is in, where you are, and whether delivery is available. Also check how fast you reply — slow replies lose buyers to faster sellers.

**Should I write "DM for price" in my listing?**

No. Listings without a real price get fewer views, because buyers filter by price, and they get fewer messages, because buyers assume the price is hidden for a bad reason. Always put the real price in the listing, and say in the description whether it is negotiable.

**How do I avoid fake payment alerts?**

Never release an item based on a screenshot or an SMS. Check your own bank account or banking app and confirm the money has actually arrived before you hand over the item or dispatch it. If a buyer pressures you to release quickly, that is itself a warning sign.

**Can I sell on Marketplace if I do not have a shop?**

Yes. Most Marketplace sellers in Nigeria do not have a physical shop. You sell from your home, meet buyers in a safe public place, or deliver by rider. What matters is not a shop — it is clear photos, honest descriptions, fair prices and fast replies.

## What to Do Next

Improve your Marketplace selling today, in this order. First, open your current listings and check the first photo of each one — if it is dark, blurry or unclear, retake it in daylight and replace it. Second, make sure every listing shows the real price, and add one line to each description saying whether the price is negotiable and whether delivery is available. Third, rewrite your shortest descriptions to answer the buyer's key questions: what it is, what condition it is in, where you are, and how to reach you. Fourth, turn on Messenger notifications and commit to replying within an hour, with complete answers, not one-word replies.
