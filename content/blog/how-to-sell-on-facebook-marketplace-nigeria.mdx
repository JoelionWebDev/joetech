Facebook Marketplace is one of the easiest places for a Nigerian small business to find buyers. You do not need a website, a shop, or money for advertising to start. If you have a Facebook account and a phone, you can list a product in a few minutes and show it to people in Lagos, Abuja, Port Harcourt, Ibadan, or your town. Many buyers already use Facebook every day, so they do not need to download a new app or learn a new platform to see what you sell.

Sellers use Marketplace for almost everything: fairly used phones, clothes, shoes, furniture, cars, home appliances, baby items, and new products from small shops. A tailor in Surulere can sell ready-made dresses. A phone dealer in Computer Village can sell fairly used iPhones. A furniture maker in Onitsha can sell chairs and tables. A mother in Enugu can sell baby clothes her child has outgrown. The common thread is simple: a clear photo, an honest description, a fair price, and a safe way to meet the buyer.

This guide shows you how to sell on Facebook Marketplace in Nigeria step by step. You will learn how to take photos that sell, write listings buyers trust, price your products, respond to messages, negotiate without losing money, and meet buyers safely. You will also learn the common scams to avoid and how to turn one sale into repeat customers.


## Take Photos That Make Buyers Stop and Tap

On Marketplace, your photo is your shop window. Buyers scroll quickly through dozens of listings. A clear, bright photo makes them stop. A dark, blurry photo makes them scroll past. You do not need a professional camera. A phone camera is enough if you use good light and a clean background.

Take your photos in daylight, near a window or outside, if possible. Avoid yellow indoor light that makes colours look wrong. Place the product on a plain background, such as a white wall, a clean table, or a bedsheet. Remove clutter from the frame. If you are selling a dress, hang it or lay it flat so the shape is clear. If you are selling a phone, show the front, back, and sides. If you are selling furniture, show the whole item and a close-up of any damage. Honest photos build trust and reduce arguments later.

Take at least three photos for each listing. The first photo should be the best one, showing the whole product clearly. The second can show a detail, such as the fabric, the label, or the screen. The third can show the product in use, or the size compared with a common object. For a bag, show the inside. For a shoe, show the sole. For a TV, show it switched on. Do not use photos downloaded from the internet. Buyers will notice when the product they receive does not match a stock photo, and they will leave a bad review or report the listing.

Do not add too much text to your photos. A small watermark with your business name is fine, but a photo covered in words and phone numbers looks messy and is hard to read on a small screen. Let the photo speak, and put the details in the description. After taking the photos, check them on your phone before you upload. If a photo is blurry, retake it. A few extra minutes at this stage can save you days of unanswered listings.

## Write a Listing Buyers Can Trust

A good listing answers the questions a buyer will ask before they message you. Start with a clear title. Include the product, the brand if it matters, and a key detail. "Samsung Galaxy A54 - 128GB - Fairly Used" is better than "Phone for sale." "Wooden Dining Table with 4 Chairs - Lekki" is better than "Table." "Ladies Ankara Dress - Size 12 - New" is better than "Dress." A clear title helps buyers find your listing when they search, and it tells them immediately if the item is for them.

In the description, write the condition, the price, the location, and the delivery options. Be honest about the condition. If the item is new, say new. If it is fairly used, say fairly used and explain any faults. For example: "Fairly used iPhone 11, 64GB, battery health 85 percent, no cracks, comes with charger." That honesty may reduce the price you can ask, but it prevents returns and disputes. If a buyer discovers a fault you hid, they will not buy from you again and may warn others.

Add the details that matter for that product. For clothes, add size, colour, and material. For phones, add storage, battery health, and whether the box is included. For furniture, add dimensions and material. For cars, add year, mileage, and fuel type. For baby items, add age range and condition. Also state whether the price is fixed or negotiable. If you write "Price is ₦45,000, slightly negotiable," buyers know where they stand. If you write "DM for price," many buyers will scroll past, because they do not want to message just to learn the price.

Choose the right category and location. Facebook will suggest a category based on your title. Check that it is correct. A dress listed under electronics will not be found by the right buyers. Set your location accurately, using your area, not just Lagos or Abuja. A buyer in Ajah is more likely to message a seller in Ajah than a seller in Ikorodu, because collection is easier. Finally, read the listing once before you publish. Check the spelling, the price, and the phone number if you include one. A small mistake can cost you a sale.

## Price Your Products Fairly

Pricing is where many Marketplace sellers lose money or lose buyers. Price too high, and no one messages. Price too low, and you lose profit or attract buyers who do not value the product. Start by checking what similar items sell for on Marketplace. Search for the same product in your city and look at five to ten listings. Note the prices, the condition, and how long the listing has been up. A listing that has been up for three months at ₦80,000 is probably overpriced. A listing that sold quickly at ₦60,000 is a better guide.

Consider your costs. If you bought a fairly used phone for ₦70,000 and spent ₦5,000 on repairs, your cost is ₦75,000. You need to sell above that to make a profit. If you made a dress with fabric that cost ₦12,000 and your time and sewing cost another ₦8,000, your cost is ₦20,000. Do not price below your cost because you are in a hurry. A quick sale at a loss is not a good sale. If you need money fast, lower the price a little, but know your lowest acceptable price before you start negotiating.

Leave room to negotiate, but not too much room. Many Nigerian buyers will ask, "What is your last price?" If you list at ₦50,000 and your lowest price is ₦45,000, you have ₦5,000 to move. If you list at ₦50,000 and your lowest is ₦30,000, you have priced too high and buyers will feel cheated when you drop so far. A small, honest margin is better than a large, fake one. You can also offer a bundle price. For example, "One dress is ₦15,000, two dresses are ₦28,000." Bundles help you sell more and give the buyer a reason to buy two items at once.

Update your price if the item does not sell. If you have had many views but no messages after a week, the price may be too high or the photos may be weak. Try better photos first, then lower the price by a small amount. If you have had many messages but no sale, the price may be close but the negotiation is failing. Listen to what buyers offer. If five buyers offer ₦40,000 for an item listed at ₦50,000, the market is telling you the real price is around ₦40,000. You can accept it, or keep the item and wait for a buyer who will pay more.

## Respond Quickly and Negotiate Well

When a buyer messages you, speed matters. Many buyers message three or four sellers at once and buy from the first one who replies with a clear answer. Turn on Messenger notifications and reply as soon as you can, even if it is a short message: "Yes, it is still available. The price is ₦45,000. Are you in Lagos?" That reply keeps the conversation alive. If you are busy, reply when you can, but do not leave a buyer waiting for a day. By then, they will have bought elsewhere.

Answer questions directly. If a buyer asks, "Is it still available?" say yes or no, and add the price and location. If they ask for more photos, send them. If they ask about delivery, explain the cost and timing. Avoid one-word replies that end the conversation. A good reply is friendly, clear, and moves the sale forward. For example: "Yes, it is available. It is in Ikeja. Delivery to Lekki is ₦2,500, or you can collect from my shop. Would you like to collect today?"

Negotiation is normal on Marketplace. Stay polite and firm. If a buyer offers far below your price, do not get angry. Reply calmly: "Thank you, but my lowest price is ₦42,000 because it is in good condition and comes with a charger." That reply shows you have a reason for your price. If the buyer keeps pushing, you can offer a small discount or a free extra, such as a phone case, instead of dropping the price further. If the buyer is rude or abusive, end the conversation politely and block them if needed. No sale is worth abuse.

When you agree a price, confirm everything in writing on Messenger. Write: "Agreed price ₦42,000, collection at my shop in Ikeja tomorrow at 11 a.m., payment by transfer on collection." That written agreement protects both sides. If the buyer changes the price later or does not show up, you have a record of what was agreed. Do not agree to hold an item for a long time without a deposit. If a buyer asks you to hold a popular item for a week, ask for a small, non-refundable deposit, or tell them you will sell to the first person who pays. Holding an item for a buyer who never returns costs you other sales.

## Meet Buyers and Get Paid Safely

Safety is the most important part of selling on Marketplace. Always meet in a public, busy place during the day. A shopping mall, a bank premises, a police station, or your shop if it is in a busy area are good choices. Avoid meeting at night, in a quiet street, or at a stranger's house. Tell a friend or family member where you are going, who you are meeting, and when you expect to return. Take your phone, and keep it charged.

For small items, you can meet at a public place and let the buyer inspect the product. Let them test a phone, try on a shoe, or check a bag. A buyer who can inspect the item is more likely to pay happily and less likely to complain later. For large items such as furniture or a car, meet at your shop, your home with someone else present, or a public place with space. Do not let a stranger into your home alone. If the buyer wants to see a car, meet at a fuel station or a mall car park in daylight.

Payment should be confirmed before you hand over the item. If the buyer pays by transfer, check your bank app and confirm the money is in your account before you give them the product. Do not rely on a screenshot or a debit alert shown on their phone. Screenshots can be faked. Wait for your own bank alert or check your balance. If the buyer pays by cash, count the money carefully before you hand over the item. Check for fake notes if the amount is large. If the buyer uses a POS, wait for the successful receipt. For delivery, use a trusted dispatch rider and confirm payment before the rider leaves. If you use a payment link, confirm the payment in your payment dashboard, not just in a message.

## Avoid Scams and Follow Marketplace Rules

Scams are common on Marketplace, but most are easy to spot if you know the signs. Be careful of buyers who offer to pay more than your price, or who ask you to send the item before payment. Be careful of buyers who claim to be abroad and want to pay through a strange link or a third party. Be careful of anyone who asks for your bank login, your one-time code, or your card details. Facebook, your bank, and Paystack will never ask for those details on Messenger. Never share them.

Also be careful of fake buyers who send a fake payment alert and pressure you to release the item quickly. They may say, "I have sent the money, check your phone, I am in a hurry." Take your time. Check your own bank app. If the money is not there, do not release the item. A real buyer will wait for you to confirm. If a buyer becomes aggressive when you ask to confirm payment, walk away from the sale. That behaviour is a warning sign.

Follow Facebook's rules. Do not list prohibited items, such as weapons, drugs, or stolen goods. Do not list the same item many times in a short period, because Facebook may mark it as spam. Do not use misleading titles or photos. If Facebook removes your listing, read the reason and fix it before you relist. If a buyer reports you for a misleading listing, your account may be restricted. Honest listings protect your account and your reputation.

Keep a record of your sales. Write down what you sold, the price, the buyer name, and the date. That record helps you track your income, know which products sell best, and resolve disputes. If a buyer claims they paid for a different item, your record and Messenger chat will show the truth. Good records also help you plan what to list next.

## Turn One Sale Into Repeat Customers

Marketplace is not just for one-off sales. It can bring you repeat customers if you treat buyers well. After a sale, thank the buyer and ask if they are happy with the item. A short message, "Thank you for buying. I hope you enjoy the dress. Please let me know if you need another one," is enough. Do not send too many messages after the sale. One thank-you is friendly. Five messages are annoying.

Ask happy buyers to follow your business page or join your WhatsApp broadcast list, with their permission. You can say, "I post new items every week on my page. Would you like me to send you new arrivals on WhatsApp?" If they say yes, save their number and send them new products when they arrive. If they say no, respect it. A buyer who feels respected is more likely to return and to recommend you to friends.

List regularly. A seller who lists one item a month is easy to forget. A seller who lists new items every week stays visible. You do not need to list every day, but aim for a steady rhythm. When you sell an item, mark it as sold on Marketplace. That keeps your listings clean and tells Facebook you are an active, honest seller. When you get a new product, list it with good photos and a clear price, and share the listing in relevant Facebook groups if the group rules allow it.

Start with one item today. Choose a product you know well, take clear photos in daylight, write an honest description, set a fair price, and publish the listing. Reply quickly when buyers message, meet safely, and confirm payment before you hand over the item. Your first sale will teach you more than any guide. Your tenth sale will feel easy. Facebook Marketplace is free, local, and full of buyers who are already looking. Use it well, and it can become a steady source of income for your small business in Nigeria.
