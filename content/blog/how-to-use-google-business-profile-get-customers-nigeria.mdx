When a customer in Lekki needs a plumber, a tailor, a pharmacy or a restaurant, what does she do first? She opens Google, types "plumber near me" or "best restaurant in Lekki", and calls one of the first three businesses she sees. If your business is not on that list, you do not exist to her, no matter how good your work is.

Google Business Profile, formerly called Google My Business, is the free listing that puts your business on Google Search and Google Maps. It shows your name, address, phone number, opening hours, photos, reviews and a link to your website or WhatsApp. It costs nothing. It works on a cheap Android phone with MTN or Airtel data. And for a local business in Nigeria, it is often more powerful than a website or an Instagram page, because it reaches people at the exact moment they are ready to buy.

This guide walks you through setting it up properly, verifying it in Nigeria, and using it every week to bring in customers.

## What Google Business Profile Does for a Nigerian Business

Think of your profile as a free shop front on the busiest road in Nigeria. Google Search handles billions of searches, and a large share of them are local: "salon near me", "hotel in Ikeja", "fashion designer in Abuja", "phone repair in Computer Village". When someone searches like that, Google shows a map pack of three businesses at the top of the results, above the websites. Those three businesses get most of the calls.

A complete profile gives you five things:

1. **Visibility on Maps:** customers can find you, get directions, and see how far away you are.
2. **Trust before contact:** photos, reviews and opening hours help a stranger decide to call you instead of the next listing.
3. **Free calls and messages:** customers tap your phone number or WhatsApp link and reach you directly.
4. **Reviews that sell for you:** a business with 40 good reviews beats a business with none, even if the second one is better.
5. **Insights you can act on:** Google tells you how many people found you, what they searched for, and whether they called, visited your website or asked for directions.

## Step 1: Create Your Profile the Right Way

Go to business.google.com on your phone or laptop and sign in with a Google account. Use a business email if you have one, such as info@yourbusiness.ng, or a dedicated Gmail account you control. Do not use a personal account you might lose access to, and do not let an agent create it under their own email. Whoever owns the account owns the listing.

Enter your business name exactly as customers know it. If your signboard says "Adaeze Hair Studio", write "Adaeze Hair Studio". Do not add keywords to the name, such as "Adaeze Hair Studio Best Braids in Lagos". Google rejects that, and it looks spammy to customers. Keep the name clean and consistent everywhere: your profile, your Instagram bio, your website and your WhatsApp Business name should all match.

Choose the right category. This is one of the most important choices you will make, because Google uses it to decide which searches you appear for. Pick the category that describes your main service, not everything you do. A business that sells and repairs phones should pick "Mobile Phone Repair Shop" or "Mobile Phone Store", whichever brings in more money. You can add extra categories later, up to nine, but the first one matters most. Search Google for your category before you pick it, and see which words real businesses in your area use.

Add your location. If customers come to you, enter your full address: street number, street name, area, city and state. For example, "14 Admiralty Way, Lekki Phase 1, Lagos". If you run a home-based business and do not want your house address public, you can hide the address and list only your service area, such as Lekki, Victoria Island and Ajah. If you deliver or visit customers, such as a makeup artist or a plumber, choose the service-area option and list the areas you cover. Be honest about your area. Listing the whole of Lagos when you only serve Ikeja will bring you enquiries you cannot fulfil, and disappointed customers leave bad reviews.

Add your phone number and website. Use the number you actually answer, and the WhatsApp number if it is different. If you have a website, link it. If you do not, link your Instagram page or WhatsApp chat link for now, and add a proper website later. Add your opening hours, and keep them accurate. Nothing damages trust faster than a customer who drives to your shop on a Sunday because Google said you were open, and finds the gate locked.

## Step 2: Verify Your Business in Nigeria

Verification proves to Google that your business is real and that you control it. Until you verify, your profile will not appear properly in search results. Google offers several verification methods, and the one you get depends on your business type and location.

**Video verification** is now the most common method in Nigeria. Google will ask you to record a short video showing your shop sign, your street, your entrance, your work area and proof that you operate there, such as branded materials, equipment or a utility bill. Film it in daylight, keep it steady, and make sure your business name is visible. Do not edit the video or add music. Upload it through the Google Business Profile app or the verification page, and wait for the review, which usually takes a few days.

**Postcard verification** still appears for some businesses. Google posts a card with a five-digit code to your business address. It can take two to three weeks to arrive in Nigeria, and sometimes it never arrives. If you choose this method, check your address carefully and ask whoever receives post at your location to watch for it. Enter the code the day it arrives, because codes expire.

**Phone or email verification** is offered to some established businesses. If Google offers you a code by SMS or email, use it immediately, because it is the fastest option.

If verification fails, do not create a second profile. That creates a duplicate listing that confuses Google and splits your reviews. Read the reason Google gave, fix the problem, and try again. Common reasons include a business name that does not match the signboard, an address Google cannot find on Maps, or a video that does not clearly show the business. Once verified, add staff as managers, not owners. The owner account should stay with you.

## Step 3: Complete Every Section

A half-finished profile ranks badly and looks untrustworthy. Google rewards complete profiles, and customers trust them more. Work through every section.

**Description:** write two or three short paragraphs in plain language. Say what you do, who you serve, and what makes you different. A good description for a Lekki bakery reads: "Fresh cakes, pastries and small chops baked daily in Lekki Phase 1. We deliver across Lagos for birthdays, weddings and office events. Order on WhatsApp and get same-day delivery within Lekki." Avoid keyword stuffing and avoid copying another business's text.

**Services and products:** list every service you offer, with prices where possible. A salon can list "Braids from ₦15,000, Wig installation ₦10,000, Pedicure ₦8,000". Prices answer the question every customer asks first, and they filter out people who cannot afford you.

**Attributes:** tick the boxes that apply: wheelchair accessible, free Wi-Fi, outdoor seating, accepts card payments, women-led, and so on. These details appear in search filters and help customers choose you.

**Photos:** upload at least ten photos: your exterior, your interior, your team at work, and your best-selling items. Use clear daylight photos taken on a decent phone, and show real work, not stock images downloaded from the internet. Fake photos destroy trust. Add new photos every month, because fresh photos signal that the business is active.

**Logo and cover photo:** upload a clean logo and a cover image that shows your best work. A restaurant should show its best dish. A fashion designer should show a model wearing her best design. A hotel should show its cleanest room or its pool.

## Step 4: Get Reviews, and Reply to Every One

Reviews are the engine of Google Business Profile. They affect your ranking, and they affect whether a stranger trusts you enough to call. A business with 4.8 stars and 60 reviews will beat a business with no reviews almost every time, even if the second business is cheaper.

Start by asking your happiest customers. After a successful job, a good meal, or a delivery the customer loved, send a short WhatsApp message: "Thank you for choosing us. If you have a minute, please leave us a review on Google. It helps our small business a lot." Include your review link, which you can find in your profile dashboard under "Ask for reviews". Make it easy. Customers will not search for your listing themselves.

Ask at the right moment, when the customer is happiest: just after they collect their dress, just after the food is served, just after the repair is done. Do not ask a customer who complained. Fix their problem first, then ask later if they are satisfied.

Aim for a steady flow, not a flood. Five genuine reviews a week for two months is better than 40 reviews in one day, which looks suspicious to Google. Ask different customers, and never pay for reviews, never ask staff or family to write fake ones, and never copy reviews from another platform. Google removes fake reviews, and it can suspend profiles that use them.

Reply to every review, good and bad. Thank happy customers by name and mention the service they used. For a bad review, stay calm, apologise briefly, and invite the customer to contact you directly so you can make it right. Future customers read your replies more than the review itself. A calm, helpful reply to a complaint builds more trust than ten five-star reviews. Never argue with a reviewer in public, and never offer a bribe in exchange for removing a review. If a review is fake or written by a competitor, report it through your dashboard.

## Step 5: Post Updates Every Week

Google Business Profile has a posting feature that many Nigerian businesses ignore. It is free, it shows on your listing, and it tells Google your business is active. Post at least once a week.

Good posts include a new product, a promo with a clear end date, a seasonal offer, or a short tip related to your business. Keep each post short, add a clear photo, and add a call to action, such as "Call now" or "Order on WhatsApp". Google Posts expire after seven days, so set a weekly reminder. Monday morning works well: spend ten minutes writing one post and uploading one new photo. Always include the price or the date, because a post without either gets ignored.

## Step 6: Use Questions, Messages and Bookings

Your profile has a Questions and Answers section that anyone can use. Customers will ask the same questions you hear every day: "Do you deliver to Abuja?", "What time do you close on Sundays?", "Do you accept transfers?" Answer them yourself before customers ask, by posting the question and answering it from your owner account. This saves you time and gives customers instant answers.

Turn on messaging only if you can reply quickly, because Google shows your typical response time. If you take two days, turn it off and direct customers to WhatsApp instead. If you take bookings, link your booking system or add a simple "Reserve on WhatsApp" button, so a ready customer can act immediately.

## How Google Decides Who Ranks First

Google ranks local businesses using three factors. Understanding them tells you where to put your effort.

**Relevance** is how well your profile matches the search. A business with the right category, the right services, the right keywords in its description, and regular posts about those services will rank for those searches. If you want to rank for "wedding cakes in Abuja", your profile must say wedding cakes, list wedding cakes as a service, post about wedding cakes, and have reviews that mention wedding cakes.

**Distance** is how close you are to the searcher. You cannot change this, but you can work with it. If your shop is in Ikeja, you will rank better for searches made in Ikeja than for searches made in Lekki. That is why your service area and address must be accurate. A business that lists the whole of Lagos but sits in one corner will disappoint customers and earn bad reviews.

**Prominence** is how well known and trusted your business is. Google measures this through reviews, review quality, photos, website links, mentions on other sites, and how active your profile is. This is the factor you can influence most. More genuine reviews, better photos, regular posts, and mentions on directories and news sites all build prominence over time.

You cannot buy prominence with adverts alone. A business with no reviews and no photos will still lose to a business with 80 good reviews and a complete profile, even if the first one pays for ads.

## Common Mistakes Nigerian Businesses Make

Avoid these mistakes, because they cost you rankings and customers.

**Creating duplicate listings.** One business, one profile. If you have two profiles for the same shop, Google gets confused and customers split their reviews between them. Search for your business name first, claim the existing profile if one already exists, and delete or merge duplicates.

**Using a fake address or a virtual office you do not occupy.** Google can suspend your profile if it discovers the address is not real. Use your real shop, office or home base, and use the service-area option if you do not want the address public.

**Keyword-stuffing your business name.** Adding "best", "cheap" or a list of services to your business name violates Google's rules and can get your profile suspended. Keep the name real.

**Ignoring your profile for months.** A profile that has not been updated in six months looks closed, even if you are open. Google may mark it as possibly closed. Post weekly, upload new photos monthly, and update your hours for public holidays, Christmas, Sallah and Easter.

**Forgetting public holidays.** Nigeria has many public holidays, and customers search on those days. Update your holiday hours in advance, or Google will show your normal hours and customers will arrive to a locked gate. A two-minute update saves you a bad review.

**Leaving the wrong phone number.** If your number changes, update it everywhere the same day: Google, WhatsApp Business, Instagram and your website. A customer who calls a dead number will not try again. They will call your competitor.

## Measuring What Works

Check your profile insights once a week. Google shows you how many people found your profile, how they found you, what they searched for, and what they did next: called you, asked for directions, visited your website, or messaged you.

Look for patterns. If most people find you by searching your business name, new customers are not discovering you, and you need more reviews and better service keywords. Track calls too: 20 profile views and one call means your photos or reviews may be putting people off, while 20 views and eight calls means your profile is doing its job. Ask new customers how they found you for a month, and spend your time where it works.

Set a simple monthly target: ten new reviews, four posts, ten new photos, and a reply to every review within 24 hours. Hit that target for three months and you will see a real difference in calls and walk-ins.

## Conclusion: Your Free Shop Front on Google

Google Business Profile is the closest thing to free advertising a Nigerian business will ever get. It puts you on the map, in front of customers who are already searching, at the exact moment they are ready to buy. It costs no money, only attention: a complete profile, honest photos, steady reviews, weekly posts, and quick replies.

Set it up today. Verify it this week. Complete every section, ask your first five customers for reviews, and post your first update on Monday. Then keep going, week after week.
