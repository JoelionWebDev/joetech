You filmed a nice Reel. You typed a caption. Nothing happened. Then you scroll onto a video of someone holding a yam and talking — 200,000 views. What did they have that you didn't?

Not a better camera. A better script — and a caption that made the algorithm engage in the first 3 seconds.

In 2026 the free AI tools at your fingertips will write that script, adapt it per platform, and even generate caption text that keeps viewers watching to the end. Here's the exact playbook.

> The hook gets the view. The script keeps it. The caption turns it into a follow.

---

## Step 1: The hook formula — your first 3 seconds win or die

The algorithm only cares whether people stay to the end. Everything starts with what they hear in the first 1–3 seconds.

**The 4 hook formulas that work in Nigeria in 2026:**

1. **The cliff-drop (curiosity):** "I found the exact tool Lagos food vendors use to double their weekend orders."
2. **The anti-hook (reverse):** "Stop posting Reels at 3pm. Do this instead."
3. **The cost reveal:** "This simple WhatsApp tip saved my client ₦120,000 a month."
4. **The nameless stake:** "If you run a small business, don't post until you read the next 3 seconds."

**Prompt for AI:**
> "Write 8 hooks for a [Reel/Short/TikTok] about [topic], for [audience] in Nigeria. Keep each under 10 words and open with either a number, a cost, or a story referencing WhatsApp/Naira so it feels local."

Pick the 1 you'd reply to as a stranger. Your AI often gives 8; your job is to pick with your gut, not by number.

---

## Step 2: The 30–60 second script skeleton AI can fill fast

Short-form isn't an essay; it's a quick escalation. Give AI this scaffold:

**Skeleton (use as literal structure):**
1. **0–3s — Hook** (from step 1)
2. **3–10s — Contrast/Context:** "Now here's what people THINK vs what the data shows."
3. **10–35s — 3 rapid proofs or steps** (fast, numbered, "First… second… third…" with a quick visual each)
4. **35–55s — The value pull**: one line on the payoff of doing this ("Two weeks later the messages had tripled.")
5. **55–60s — Call-to-action**: "Follow for [topic]. Save this if you're planning one this month."

**AI prompt:**
> "Write a 30-second [Reel/Short] script about [topic] using this structure: hook (using one of the hooks: [paste]), the mistake vs the fix, three numbered steps, a concrete outcome in Naira, and an end-screen CTA 'Follow + Save'. Write in natural spoken Nigerian English, and note pauses as 'i can.' Add stage notes for cuts."

Your script comes out fast — but **verify every claim you let AI invent.** If it says "doubled orders," your answer needs a real example or it's a lie your page will pay for.

## Step 3: Captions that the algorithm actually feeds on

The caption is not for poetry — it works with the AI system shaping what your video is shown to. Use this structure in your caption block:

1. **First line = the hook written out** (repeat the spoken hook as text — a summary for the skimmers)
2. **Body = 2–4 lines of value** (short bullets, not paragraphs — "how to", "mistake to avoid")
3. **Keyword mention** — repeat what people actually type in search.
4. **Soft CTA** — "Follow for daily [topic] tips" or "Save this before your next shoot."
5. **3–5 relevant hashtags** — mix broad (wide reach) and niche (high intent). Skip the tag-pile routine; the algorithm has devalued "tags-stuffed."

Write caption text yourself — but let AI edit it:

> "Give me a first-line hook + 3 value bullets + CTA for a [Reel] about [X] for a [audience]. At least one line must mention '[keyword]'. Keep under 600 characters. Write like a friend advising — natural, not corporate."

You'll get an instantly more scrollable caption. **Rule:** never post a caption with 30 hashtags that reads like a billboard.

---

## Step 4: The AI caption that whispers to the watch time

Your caption and your script should pair like player and coach:

- When you say "step 2" on screen in the script, your caption repeats "3 steps outlined in the video" — the physical signal of watch time to the 30 seconds.
- Use "save this" + the number in the caption CTA, which correlates with the save action the algorithm reads as a strong signal.
- Put your keyword relation naturally: don't force keywords into every line; place two strong ones in the first sentence, the body, and the hashtag set.

> The goal isn't to be "found" — it's to be *finished*. People who finish your video are the ones who save it, follow you, and message you.

## A 30-minute AI content Saturday (repeatable)

1. **0–10 min:** Ask ChatGPT: "Give me 8 Reel/Shorts ideas for [niche] based on [this one premise]." Pick 4.
2. **10–20 min:** Generate a complete script + caption for each in Notion using the step 2 prompt.
3. **20–30 min:** Film all 4 in one take (your home, phone, natural light), edit all in CapCut at once using auto-captions, and schedule your 4 posts for the week.

The same 30 minutes compresses a full week's video plan and gives you time to smell your food. Perfect loops beat perfect videos.

## Watch out for these 5 pit falls

1. **Hook spam** — more hooks than value. One honest hook + honest payoff > five clickbaits.
2. **Scripts that sound "AI-ish"** — tell every AI tool: "Write like a Lagos business owner speaking, not a prospectus." Keep one or two of your own speaking rhythm phrases.
3. **Posting without a CTA** — every video asks for "save" / "follow"; otherwise the algorithm treats it as a clip, not content.
4. **Throwaway captions** — any video can outrank your shirt on Google-like search; captions that say nothing die.
5. **Post-everything-mania** — consistency wins, but 4 great posts a week open more doors than 10 forgettable ones.
