"How much is it?" If you run a small business in Nigeria, you hear this question every day — on WhatsApp, in your shop, in your comments, and in your DMs. And every time, you type out the same answer, or worse, you hesitate, wondering what price to quote this particular customer. That hesitation costs you sales. Customers who have to wait for a price, or who get a different price from the one their friend got, start to doubt you.

A simple price list solves all of this. It is a clear document that shows what you sell and what each item costs. It saves you from repeating yourself, it builds trust because every customer sees the same prices, and it helps you charge what your work is actually worth instead of discounting on the spot because you feel put on the spot.

## 1. List Everything You Sell

Start by writing down every product or service you offer, exactly as customers ask for it. Be thorough. A tailor's list might include a simple gown, an ankara dress, a two-piece set, a suit, and alterations. A caterer's list might include small chops per pack, a full tray of jollof rice, a full event package, and delivery. A phone repairer's list might include screen replacement, battery replacement, charging port repair, and software fixes.

Do not group things too broadly. "Dresses" is too vague — a customer cannot tell whether that means a simple shift dress or a detailed aso-ebi outfit. List each item separately with a clear name. If you offer variations, such as sizes or quantities, note them. For example: "Small chops (pack of 10)", "Small chops (pack of 50)".

While you list, also note anything that is seasonal or only available on order. Mark those clearly so customers are not disappointed. And if you have stopped selling something, leave it off the list entirely — an outdated price list is worse than no price list.

## 2. Work Out Your Prices Properly

This is the step most business owners rush, and it is the step that decides whether your business makes money. Your price must cover your costs and leave you a profit. Sit down with a pen and paper, or a simple spreadsheet, and for each item write down what it costs you to produce: materials, transport, packaging, and any helper you pay. Then add the value of your own time and skill.

Many Nigerian small businesses undercharge because they only count the cost of materials and forget their time. If you spend four hours making a dress and charge only for the fabric, you are working for free. Your time has a value. Decide on a fair amount for your labour and add it to every price.

Also look at what similar businesses charge, not to copy them, but to understand the market. If everyone else charges more than you for the same quality, you are probably undercharging. If you charge far more than everyone else, you need to be able to explain why — better materials, faster delivery, or a stronger guarantee.

Once you have your prices, round them to clean numbers. Prices like ₦4,500 or ₦15,000 are easier for customers to remember and pay than ₦4,350 or ₦14,750. Clean prices also make your list look tidy and professional.

## 3. Choose a Format That Customers Can Actually Use

Your price list needs to work where your customers are, and in Nigeria that mostly means WhatsApp. The most useful formats are a one-page document you can send as a PDF or image, and a short version you can paste into a WhatsApp message.

A simple table works best: one column for the item, one column for the details (size, quantity, or what is included), and one column for the price. Keep the font large enough to read on a phone screen, and avoid tiny text or cramped rows. Use your business colours if you have them, and put your business name, phone number, and WhatsApp number at the top.

You can build this in Google Docs, Microsoft Word, or Canva on your phone. In Google Docs, insert a table with three columns and fill it in. In Canva, search for a price list template and replace the text with yours. Either way, save the finished list as a PDF so the layout stays the same on every phone.

Keep a copy of the same list on your phone as a photo or PDF so you can send it in seconds whenever someone asks for prices. The faster you reply with a clear price list, the more likely that person is to buy.

## 4. Add the Details That Prevent Arguments

A price list is not only about prices — it is about setting expectations. Add a few short notes at the bottom that answer the questions customers always ask. State whether delivery is included or charged separately, and how much delivery costs to the main areas you serve. State your payment terms: do you take full payment upfront, or a deposit before you start work? State how long an order takes, for example "Orders take 3 to 5 working days."

If you offer discounts for bulk orders, say so clearly, with the exact quantities. "10% off orders of 50 packs or more" is clear. "Discount available" is not — it invites endless negotiation.

Also add a validity note, such as "Prices valid until 31 December 2026." Prices change, especially when the cost of materials rises, and a validity date gives you a natural reason to update your list without customers feeling cheated. When the date passes, review your costs, adjust where needed, and send the new list out.

These notes protect you. When a customer has seen your terms in writing before ordering, there is far less room for misunderstanding later.

## 5. Share Your Price List Everywhere It Matters

A price list sitting on your laptop helps nobody. Share it. Send it to every customer who asks for prices on WhatsApp instead of typing prices out one by one. Post it on your Facebook Page and pin it so new visitors see it first. Add it to your WhatsApp Business catalogue if you use WhatsApp Business. Print a copy and display it in your shop where customers can see it.

When you update your prices, tell your regular customers. A short WhatsApp broadcast saying "Our new price list is out — a few prices have changed due to rising material costs" is honest and professional. Customers respect a business that communicates changes clearly.

One more thing: use your price list as a selling tool, not just a document. When you post about a product on social media, mention its price from the list. Customers who already know the price before they message you are more serious buyers, and you will spend less time answering questions from people who were never going to buy at your price.

## 6. Review and Update Your Prices Regularly

Your price list is not a one-time document. Review it at least once every three months. Check whether your costs have changed, whether you are still making a profit on every item, and whether any item has become so unprofitable that you should raise its price or stop offering it.

Keep a simple record of what you sell at each price, even if it is just a notebook or a spreadsheet. Over time you will see patterns: which items sell well, which ones customers always haggle over, and which ones are worth promoting more. That information helps you decide where to focus your energy.

When you do raise a price, do it with confidence and explain it simply if asked: "The cost of materials has gone up, so our prices have been adjusted." Most customers understand this, especially when your quality and service stay strong. The customers who leave over a fair price rise were often the ones who cost you the most time anyway.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Should I put prices on my social media posts or keep them private?

Put them where you can. Public prices build trust and save you time, because only serious buyers message you. If you sell custom work where every job is different, give a starting price, such as "Dresses from ₦15,000", and explain that the final price depends on the design and fabric.

### What if customers say my prices are too high?

First, check that your prices are fair by comparing your costs and the market. If they are fair, stand by them and explain the value: your materials, your skill, your delivery, your after-sales care. You do not need every customer — you need the right customers, the ones who value what you offer.

### How often should I update my price list?

Review it every three months, and update it whenever your main costs change significantly. Always add a validity date to the list so customers know when it was last reviewed.

### Can I have different prices for different customers?

Avoid it. Charging different customers different prices for the same item destroys trust the moment they compare notes — and in Nigeria, they will. One clear price list for everyone is simpler, fairer, and better for your reputation.

## Conclusion

A simple price list is one of the most powerful tools a small business can have. It answers the question every customer asks, it saves you hours of repeated messages, it builds trust, and it helps you charge what your work is worth.
