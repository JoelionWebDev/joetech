Social media is rented land. An algorithm can change overnight, your reach can drop, and your followers may never see your posts. Your email list is different. It belongs to you. When someone gives you their email address, you can reach them directly, in their inbox, whenever you have something useful to say. That is why email marketing still works for Nigerian small businesses, even in a world dominated by WhatsApp and Instagram. Mailchimp is one of the easiest tools to start with: it has a free plan for small lists, simple templates, and clear reports that show you who opened your email and who clicked. This guide shows you how to set up Mailchimp, build a list the right way, send emails people actually open, and turn subscribers into repeat customers, whether you sell fashion in Lagos, food in Abuja, or services across Nigeria.

## Why Email Marketing Still Works in Nigeria

Many Nigerian business owners think email is dead because their customers live on WhatsApp. Email is not dead. It is quieter, but it is more reliable for certain jobs. A WhatsApp message is good for a quick chat, but an email is better for a price list, a new collection, a detailed offer, or a monthly update that people can save and come back to. Email also reaches customers who do not check your social media every day. A customer who bought from you six months ago may have forgotten you, but a short, useful email can bring them back. Email is also cheap. Sending an email to a thousand subscribers costs far less than running adverts to reach the same people, and you can measure exactly what happened: how many people opened it, how many clicked, and how many bought. For a business that wants repeat customers, not just one-time buyers, email is a steady, low-cost channel that works alongside WhatsApp and Instagram, not instead of them. The key is to send emails that are useful, short, and respectful of people's time.

## Step 1: Create Your Mailchimp Account and Set It Up Properly

Go to mailchimp.com and create a free account with your business email address. Use an email address that customers recognise, such as info@yourbusiness.com or your business Gmail, not a random personal address. Mailchimp will ask for your business name, your website address, and your physical address. Your physical address is required by law in many countries because marketing emails must include a real postal address in the footer. If you work from home, you can use a business address, a shop address, or a virtual office address if you have one. Do not skip this step, or your emails may be marked as spam. Next, set your sender name to your business name or your own name, so people recognise you in their inbox. "Ada's Kitchen" is clearer than "noreply" or a string of numbers. Set your reply-to address to an inbox you actually check, because some customers will reply to your emails with questions or orders. Finally, choose your time zone and set it to West Africa Time, so your emails are scheduled correctly for your Nigerian audience. Take ten minutes to complete this setup properly. A sloppy setup will cause problems later that are harder to fix.

## Step 2: Build Your Email List the Right Way

Your list is your most valuable asset in email marketing, so build it honestly. Never buy an email list. Bought lists are full of people who never asked to hear from you, they will mark your emails as spam, and Mailchimp can suspend your account for using them. Build your list from real customers and real interested people. Start with the people you already know: past customers, WhatsApp contacts who have bought from you, and people who have asked about your products. Ask for their permission before you add them. A simple message works: "Hello, we are starting a short email update with new products and special offers. May we add your email?" Most happy customers will say yes. Next, add a signup form to your website, your Instagram bio link, and your WhatsApp catalogue if you use one. Offer a small reason to join, such as early access to a new collection, a 10 percent discount on the first order, or a useful guide related to your business. A fashion store can offer a style guide. A food business can offer a recipe. A service business can offer a checklist. Collect only what you need: a name and an email address are enough to start. You can ask for a phone number later if you need it, but every extra field reduces the number of people who sign up.

## Step 3: Organise Your List With Tags and Groups

A list of a thousand names is not very useful if you treat everyone the same. A customer in Lagos who buys dresses does not need the same email as a customer in Abuja who buys shoes. Mailchimp lets you organise your contacts with tags and groups, so you can send the right message to the right people. Create tags for simple things: "Customer", "New Subscriber", "Lagos", "Abuja", "Bought Dress", "Bought Shoes", "Interested in Wholesale". Tag people when they join, and tag them again when they buy. You can also create groups for interests, such as "New Collections", "Discounts Only", or "Events". When you send an email, you can choose to send it to everyone, or only to a tag or a group. Sending a targeted email to 200 interested people will often bring more sales than sending a general email to 2,000 uninterested people. It also reduces unsubscribes, because people only receive emails that are relevant to them. Spend a few minutes tagging your contacts each week. That small habit makes your emails far more effective over time, and it helps you understand who your best customers really are.

## Step 4: Create Your First Campaign in Mailchimp

A campaign in Mailchimp is simply an email you send. To create one, go to Campaigns, click Create, choose Email, and pick Regular as the type. Give your campaign a clear internal name, such as "December New Collection", so you can find it later. Next, choose your audience: the list you built, and any tags you want to include or exclude. Then choose a template. Mailchimp has ready-made templates, or you can start with a simple layout. For a small business, a simple layout is best: a header with your business name, one main image, a short message, and a clear button. Do not use a template with ten sections and five images. Your customers read email on their phones, often on a slow connection, so keep it light and fast. Add your logo at the top if you have one, and keep your colours consistent with your brand. If you use bright blue on your website, use the same blue in your emails. Consistency helps people recognise you instantly, even before they read your name. Save your campaign as a draft, and do not rush to send it. A careful draft is better than a rushed email with a mistake.

## Step 5: Write Subject Lines People Actually Open

Your subject line decides whether your email is opened or ignored. Most people scan their inbox quickly and delete anything that looks like spam. Keep your subject line short, clear, and honest. Aim for fewer than 50 characters so it fits on a phone screen. Tell the reader what is inside: "New Ankara Styles Just Arrived" is better than "Check This Out Now". "Your 10 Percent Discount Ends Friday" is better than "Amazing Offer Inside". Use the person's name if you have it: "Ada, your favourite dresses are back in stock" feels personal. Avoid words that trigger spam filters, such as FREE in capital letters, excessive exclamation marks, and promises that sound too good to be true. Do not lie in your subject line. If you promise a discount, the email must contain that discount. A misleading subject line may get one open, but it will cost you trust and future opens. Test two subject lines on a small part of your list if you can, and use the winner for the rest. Mailchimp can do this for you with a simple A/B test. Over time, you will learn which style your audience responds to, and your open rates will improve.

## Step 6: Write Emails That Are Short, Useful and Human

The body of your email should be short, useful, and written like a human, not like a corporate announcement. Start with a warm greeting and get to the point in the first two sentences. Your readers are busy. They do not want a long essay. They want to know what is new, what it costs, and what to do next. Use short paragraphs, simple language, and one main idea per email. If you have a new collection, show three or four items with prices, not twenty. If you have a discount, state the discount, the deadline, and how to claim it. Always include a clear call to action: a button that says "Shop Now", "Order on WhatsApp", "Book Your Slot", or "View the Collection". Make the button easy to tap on a phone. Add your WhatsApp number and your website link in every email, because many Nigerian customers will prefer to continue the conversation on WhatsApp. End with a friendly sign-off and your business name. Before you send, read the email out loud. If it sounds stiff or confusing, rewrite it in simpler words. An email that sounds like it was written by a real person will always perform better than one that sounds like a robot.

## Step 7: Send a Welcome Email Automatically

A welcome email is the most important email you will ever send, because it goes to people at the exact moment they are most interested in you: right after they sign up. Set up an automated welcome email in Mailchimp so it sends itself without you lifting a finger. Go to Automations, choose the welcome email option, and write a short message that thanks the new subscriber, tells them what to expect, and gives them a reason to stay. For example: "Welcome to Ada's Kitchen. Thank you for joining. You will receive a short email every Friday with our new menu, special offers, and early access to weekend orders. As a thank-you, here is 10 percent off your first order this week. Reply to this email or message us on WhatsApp to order." Include a button that links to your WhatsApp or your shop. A welcome email also helps your deliverability, because it teaches email providers that your emails are wanted. People who open the welcome email are more likely to open your future emails. Check your welcome email every few months and update it if your offer or your schedule changes. A stale welcome email with an expired discount looks careless and wastes a good first impression.

## Step 8: Send Regularly, But Do Not Spam People

Consistency matters more than frequency. Pick a schedule you can keep, and stick to it. For most Nigerian small businesses, one email a week or one email every two weeks is enough. A fashion store can send a weekly update on new arrivals. A food business can send a weekly menu. A service business can send a useful tip every two weeks. Do not send an email every day unless you have a very good reason, such as a short sale or a launch week, because daily emails will annoy people and cause unsubscribes. Send your emails at a time when your audience is likely to read them. Late morning, around 10am, and early evening, around 6pm, often work well in Nigeria, when people are on their phones during a break or after work. Avoid sending at 2am, when your email will be buried by morning. If you have a big announcement, such as a new product or a price change, send a separate email for that, but do not send three emails in one day. Respect your subscribers' inbox, and they will respect your emails by opening them. If someone unsubscribes, let them go gracefully. A smaller list of interested people is worth more than a large list of annoyed people.

## Step 9: Read Your Reports and Improve Every Time

After you send an email, Mailchimp will show you a report. Focus on four numbers. Open rate is the percentage of people who opened your email. A rate between 15 and 25 percent is a reasonable range for many small businesses, but your own trend matters more than any single number. Click rate is the percentage who clicked a link inside your email. That shows whether your content was interesting enough to act on. Unsubscribe rate shows how many people left your list after that email. A small number is normal, but a sudden spike means the email was not relevant or was sent too often. Bounce rate shows how many emails could not be delivered, usually because the address is wrong or the inbox is full. Clean your list regularly by removing addresses that bounce repeatedly. Compare your reports over time. Which subject lines got the highest open rates? Which emails got the most clicks? Which products got the most orders? Use those answers to plan your next email. If a weekly menu email always performs well, keep sending it. If a long, text-heavy email always performs poorly, make your next one shorter. Email marketing improves with practice, and your reports are your teacher.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid With Mailchimp

The most common mistake is buying a list or adding people without permission, which leads to spam complaints and account problems. The second is sending emails that are all sales and no value. If every email is "Buy now, buy now", people will stop opening them. Mix in useful content: a tip, a behind-the-scenes story, a customer photo, or a short guide. The third mistake is using a subject line that does not match the email inside. The fourth is sending emails with huge images that take too long to load on a Nigerian mobile connection. Compress your images before you upload them. The fifth is forgetting to test your email before you send it. Always send a test email to yourself, open it on your phone, check the links, and check the spelling. A broken link or a wrong price in a sent email is embarrassing and costly. The sixth is ignoring your reports. If you never look at your open rates, you will keep repeating the same mistakes. The seventh is giving up after one or two emails. Email marketing takes time to build. Give it at least three months of regular sending before you judge it.

## Final Thoughts

Email marketing with Mailchimp is not complicated, but it does require consistency and respect for your subscribers. Start with a proper account setup, build a list from real customers who have given you permission, organise that list with tags, and send short, useful emails on a regular schedule. Write subject lines that are honest, write emails that sound human, set up a welcome email that works while you sleep, and read your reports after every send. Over time, your list will grow, your open rates will improve, and your emails will bring you repeat customers who already trust you. That is the real value of email: it turns one-time buyers into loyal customers who come back again and again. Set up your Mailchimp account today, send your first welcome email, and start building the list that will serve your business for years to come.
