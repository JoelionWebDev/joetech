There is a difference between a photo of a document and a scan of a document, and everyone who receives them knows it instantly. A photo is tilted, shadowy, with the edges of the paper cut off and the text hard to read. A scan is straight, evenly lit, with clean edges and sharp text — it looks professional, it prints well, and it is accepted by offices, schools, banks and government agencies that would reject a crooked photo.

The good news is that you do not need a scanner to produce a scan. Your phone, with the right free app and a few simple techniques, can produce scans that are good enough for almost any purpose: job applications, school registrations, business documents, receipts, certificates, and contracts. This guide shows you how — from choosing an app, to setting up the shot, to saving and sharing the result as a proper PDF.

## Photo vs Scan: Why It Matters

A photo captures whatever is in front of the camera, including the table, your shadow, the tilt of your hand, and the glare from the light. A scanning app does something smarter: it finds the edges of the document, straightens it, corrects the perspective, evens out the lighting, and sharpens the text. The result looks like the document was fed through a machine, not photographed by hand.

That difference matters in practice. An employer reviewing your CV and certificates will judge the documents you send. A blurry, tilted photo suggests carelessness, even if the document itself is perfect. A clean scan suggests a careful, professional person. A bank or a government office may reject a poor photo outright and ask you to resend it, which costs you days. A good scan gets accepted the first time.

There are also practical advantages. A scan saved as a PDF is a single file that contains all the pages in order, which is what most online forms ask for. A set of separate photos is harder to upload, harder to organise, and harder for the receiver to read. Learning to scan properly with your phone is a small skill, but it is one you will use for the rest of your life.

## Choosing a Scanning App

Your phone may already have a scanner built in, and that is the best place to start. On many Android phones, the Google Drive app has a scan feature: open it, look for the scan or camera option, and point your phone at the document. On iPhones, the Notes app has a document scanning feature built into the camera option, and the Files app can scan as well. These built-in tools are free, they work well, and they save the result as a PDF. Before downloading anything new, check whether your phone already has one of these.

If your phone does not have a built-in scanner, or you want more control, there are free scanning apps available in your app store. The well-known ones all do the same basic job: they detect the document edges, straighten the image, improve the contrast, and save as PDF. When choosing one, look for an app that lets you save and share without a watermark, and that does not demand a subscription before it will let you export your scan. Read the reviews and check what is actually free — some apps scan for free but charge to save as PDF, which defeats the purpose.

Whichever app you choose, install it before you need it, and do one practice scan. The worst time to learn a new app is when a deadline is an hour away and a form is waiting for your documents. A ten-minute practice run with an old receipt or a page from a book will teach you everything you need to know, and it will make the real scan calm and quick.

## Setting Up: Light, Surface and Position

Good scans start before you open the app. The three things that matter are light, surface, and position, and getting them right takes less than a minute.

Light is the most important. You need bright, even light across the whole document, with no shadows and no glare. Daylight is the best light there is, so scan near a window during the day if you can. If you are scanning at night, use a bright room light, and position yourself so that your body and your phone do not cast a shadow on the page. Avoid direct sunlight on glossy paper, because it creates bright spots that wash out the text. And never use your phone's flash for documents — it creates a harsh bright patch in the middle of the page and dark corners everywhere else.

The surface matters too. Place the document on a flat, plain, dark surface if you can — a dark table, a dark cloth, or a plain floor. A dark background helps the app find the edges of a white page, which is the step that makes the scan straight. Make sure the surface is clean and flat, and smooth out any folds or curls in the paper. A curled page will scan with distorted text, so press it flat, or place a heavy book on it for a few minutes first.

Position your phone directly above the document, parallel to the page, not at an angle. Hold it with both hands if you can, or rest your elbows on the table to steady yourself. The whole document should be visible in the frame, with a small margin of background around all four edges — the app needs to see the edges to straighten them. Take a breath, hold still, and take the shot. If the app has an automatic capture mode, it will take the photo when it detects the document; if not, tap the shutter yourself when the document looks straight and fully in frame.

## Scanning Step by Step

Here is the process, from start to finish, for a single document.

First, prepare the document. Remove it from any plastic sleeve or file, because plastic creates glare. Smooth it flat. If it is a multi-page document, put the pages in order and keep them in a neat pile beside you.

Second, open your scanning app and choose to create a new scan. Point the phone at the first page. Most apps will show a frame or a coloured outline around the document when they detect its edges. Wait until that outline sits neatly on the edges of the page, then let the app capture it, or tap to capture. Check the result immediately: is the whole page visible? Is the text sharp? If not, retake it now — it is much easier to retake a page immediately than to discover the problem after you have scanned ten pages.

Third, add the remaining pages the same way. Most apps let you add page after page to a single scan, which is exactly what you want for a multi-page document. Keep the pages in order, and check each one as you go. If a page comes out badly, delete just that page and retake it, rather than starting the whole document again.

Fourth, review and adjust. Once all pages are captured, most apps let you adjust the edges of each page by dragging the corners, and let you choose a colour mode. For a document with text, the black-and-white or greyscale mode usually gives the sharpest, cleanest result and the smallest file. For a certificate or a document with a coloured stamp or logo, use colour mode so those details remain visible. Crop tightly so there is no wasted background around the page, but do not crop so tightly that you cut off the edges of the text.

Finally, save the scan as a PDF. Give it a clear name that describes what it is — "Igono-Joel-CV.pdf" or "Business-CAC-Certificate.pdf" — not "Scan 1" or "Document". A clear name helps you find the file later, and it looks professional to the person who receives it. Check the file size too: a scan of a few pages should be a reasonable size for emailing and uploading. If it is very large, your app may have an option to reduce the quality slightly, which is fine for most purposes.

## Scanning Different Types of Documents

Not all documents scan the same way, and a few small adjustments make a big difference for each type.

Certificates and licences are often printed on thick, glossy paper, which reflects light. Scan these in soft daylight, away from direct sun, and tilt the phone very slightly if glare appears — but keep the tilt minimal, because the app needs a fairly straight view to correct the perspective. If a certificate has a gold seal or a coloured stamp, scan in colour mode so those details are preserved.

Receipts and small slips are awkward because they are narrow and often curled. Flatten them under a heavy book for a few minutes first. Place them on a dark surface so the app can find their edges easily. If the text is faint — thermal receipts fade over time — scan in black-and-white mode, which often makes faded text sharper and easier to read.

Identity cards and small cards need a close, steady shot. Place the card on a dark surface, hold the phone close enough that the card fills most of the frame, and make sure the text and the photograph are both sharp. Scan the front and the back as two pages of the same PDF, so the whole card is in one file.

## Organising, Saving and Sharing Your Scans

A good scan is only useful if you can find it when you need it. Build a simple habit: every scan goes into one place, with a clear name, on the same day you create it.

Keep your scans in a dedicated folder — in your Google Drive, in your phone's file app, or in the scanning app itself. Name each file clearly, with the document type and your name or your business name, and the year if it matters. A folder full of files called "Scan 1", "Scan 2" and "Document" is a folder you will dread opening. A folder with "CV-Igono-Joel-2026.pdf", "CAC-Certificate.pdf" and "Rent-Agreement-2026.pdf" is a folder that works for you.

Back your important scans up. If your scans live only on your phone, a lost or broken phone means lost documents. Saving them to Google Drive or another cloud service means they are safe even if the phone is not. This is especially important for documents that are hard to replace, like certificates and identity documents.

When sharing a scan, use the right method for the purpose. For a job application or an official form, attach the PDF to an email, or upload it through the form as requested. For a quick share on WhatsApp, a PDF works better than a photo, because it opens cleanly and prints well. Before sending an important scan, open the PDF yourself and check every page: is it the right document, are all pages there and in order, and is the text readable? A thirty-second check saves the embarrassment of sending the wrong file, or a file with a page missing.

## Common Problems and Quick Fixes

If your scans come out blurry, the usual cause is movement or poor light. Hold the phone with both hands, rest your elbows on the table, and make sure the room is bright. Wipe the camera lens. If the app has a manual focus option, tap on the text on the screen to focus on it before capturing.

If the scan is tilted or cut off, the app probably could not find the edges of the page. Use a darker background, make sure the whole page is in the frame with a margin around it, and flatten any curled corners. If the app still struggles, capture the page as a plain photo first, then adjust the edges by hand in the app's editing screen.

If the text looks washed out or the page looks grey instead of white, switch to black-and-white mode, which increases contrast and makes text sharper. If the page looks too dark, move closer to the light or scan near a window. If there is a shadow across the page, change your position so your body is not between the light and the document.

If the file is too large to email or upload, check whether your app has a quality or compression option, and reduce it slightly. A scan does not need to be maximum quality for most purposes — a clear, readable scan at a moderate size is better than a huge file that will not upload. As a rough guide, a few pages of text should be well under the size limit of most email and form uploads.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is a phone scan accepted for official purposes?**

In most cases, yes. A clear phone scan saved as a PDF is accepted by employers, schools, banks and many government offices for applications and registrations. For the most formal purposes — such as a court document or a certified copy — an office may ask for an original or a certified photocopy, so check the requirements of the specific office before you rely on a scan alone.

**Which app should I use to scan documents?**

Start with what your phone already has: the scan feature in Google Drive on Android, or the document scanner in the Notes app on iPhone. Both are free and produce good results. If you need more features, there are free scanning apps in your app store — choose one that lets you save and share as a PDF without a watermark and without a subscription.

**How do I scan several pages into one PDF?**

Most scanning apps let you add pages to a single scan. Capture the first page, then use the add-page option to capture the next one, and continue until all pages are done. Then save the whole thing as one PDF, with the pages in order. Check the order before saving, because it is easy to add a page out of sequence.

**Why do my scans look blurry?**

The usual causes are a dirty camera lens, a moving phone, or poor light. Wipe the lens, hold the phone with both hands and rest your elbows on the table, and scan in bright, even light near a window. Tap on the text on the screen to focus before capturing, and retake any page that is not sharp.

**Can I scan a document at night?**

Yes, if the light is bright and even. Use a well-lit room, position yourself so you do not cast a shadow on the page, and avoid using the phone's flash, which creates a harsh bright patch. Daylight is better, so if the document is important, it is worth waiting until morning.

## What to Do Next

Learn to scan properly today, in this order. First, check whether your phone already has a built-in scanner — in Google Drive on Android, or in the Notes app on iPhone — and do one practice scan of an old receipt or a page from a book. Second, practise the setup: bright, even light near a window, a flat dark surface, the phone held directly above the page with both hands. Third, scan one real document you need — your CV, a certificate, or a business document — save it as a clearly named PDF, and open it to check every page is straight, complete and readable. Fourth, save it to a cloud folder with a clear name, so you can find it again and it is safe even if your phone is lost.
