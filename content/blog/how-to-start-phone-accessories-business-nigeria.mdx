Almost every adult in Nigeria owns a phone, and many own two. Students, traders, office workers, drivers, and parents all use phones every day for calls, WhatsApp, banking, photos, and business. Phones need chargers, cases, earphones, screen guards, power banks, and cables, which break, get lost, or wear out.

That is why selling phone accessories is one of the most popular small businesses in Nigeria, from Computer Village in Ikeja to markets in Onitsha, Abuja, and Ibadan. Demand is large, products are small and easy to carry, and you can start with modest money.

This guide explains how to start a phone accessories business in Nigeria step by step. You will learn which products sell best, where to buy them, how much capital you need, where to sell, how to price your goods, and how to use WhatsApp and Instagram to find customers. If you want a business you can start quickly and grow steadily, this is a practical choice.

## Why Phone Accessories Sell So Well in Nigeria

The first reason is the size of the market. Nigeria has over 200 million people, and most adults use a mobile phone. Smartphones are common, even in smaller towns. Each phone owner needs at least a charger, a case, and a screen guard. Many also want earphones, a power bank, and extra cables.

The second reason is repeat buying. A charger cable can spoil in three months. A phone case can crack when the phone falls. A screen guard can scratch and need replacement. An earphone can stop working on one side. Customers do not buy these items once in a lifetime. They buy them many times. A customer who buys a charger from you today may return in two months for a power bank, and in four months for a new case.

The third reason is low cost per item. Many accessories cost between ₦500 and ₦5,000 to buy from a supplier. That means you can stock many different items with a modest budget. You do not need to spend ₦500,000 on a single product, as you might with phones or laptops. You can start small, test what sells, and buy more of the winners.

The fourth reason is that accessories are easy to display and sell. They are small, light, and colourful. A small table, a display board, or a WhatsApp catalogue can show dozens of products. Customers can see, touch, and test many items quickly. That makes selling faster than selling large, heavy goods.

## Choose the Right Products to Sell

Not all phone accessories sell equally well. Some are in high demand every day. Others sell slowly and tie up your money. Start with the products Nigerians buy most often.

Chargers and cables are the top sellers. Every phone owner needs a charger, and cables spoil often. Stock chargers for Android phones with USB-C and micro-USB, and chargers for iPhones with Lightning cables. Also stock fast chargers, car chargers, and multi-port chargers for people who charge several devices. Test every charger before you sell it, because a faulty charger can damage a customer's phone and your reputation.

Phone cases and covers are also top sellers. Nigerians love to protect their phones and to change their look. Stock cases for popular phone brands: Samsung, Tecno, Infinix, Itel, Xiaomi, and iPhone. Stock different colours and styles: clear cases, leather cases, rugged cases, and fashionable printed cases. Cases for the newest phone models sell fastest, so keep up with new releases.

Screen guards and tempered glass are essential. Many customers buy a new phone and immediately buy a screen guard. Stock guards for popular models, and learn how to fix them neatly without bubbles. A neat installation is a service customers will pay for, often ₦500 to ₦1,500 extra, and it brings them back when they need a replacement.

Earphones, earbuds, and Bluetooth speakers sell well, especially to young people. Stock wired earphones at low prices, and wireless earbuds and small speakers at higher prices. Be honest about sound quality and battery life. Do not claim a ₦5,000 earbud sounds like a ₦50,000 one. Customers will test it and know the truth.

Power banks are important in Nigeria because power supply is unstable. Many people carry a power bank to charge their phones during the day. Stock power banks in different capacities, such as 10,000 mAh and 20,000 mAh. Explain the capacity clearly, and warn customers against fake power banks that claim a high capacity but deliver very little. Your honesty will build trust.

Other useful products include memory cards, phone stands, and smartwatches. Add these gradually, once your main products are selling well.

## Where to Buy Your Stock in Nigeria

Your profit depends on where you buy. Buy from the right supplier at the right price, and you will earn a good margin. Buy from an expensive middleman, and your profit will disappear.

Computer Village in Ikeja, Lagos, is the largest phone accessories market in Nigeria. Hundreds of shops sell chargers, cases, screen guards, and power banks at wholesale prices. You can compare prices from several shops in one visit. Go early in the morning, when shops open, and take time to walk around before you buy. Ask for wholesale prices, not retail prices, and tell the seller you will be a regular buyer.

In the South-East, Onitsha Main Market and markets in Aba also have large phone accessories sections with competitive prices. In Abuja, Wuse Market and Banex Plaza are good sources. In Ibadan, Dugbe and Bodija markets have suppliers. In Port Harcourt, Mile 1 and other markets serve the area. Choose the market closest to you to save transport costs, unless the price difference in Lagos is large enough to justify the trip.

You can also buy from online wholesale suppliers and importers, but be careful. Some online sellers advertise low prices and deliver fake or faulty goods. Buy small quantities first to test a new supplier. Check the goods carefully when they arrive. If the quality is good, order more. If it is poor, do not order again.

When you buy, check for original and quality products, not only the cheapest. A charger that costs ₦800 but spoils in one week will cost you customers. A charger that costs ₦1,500 and lasts a year will bring customers back. Ask the supplier about warranty and returns. Some suppliers will replace faulty items within a few days. That policy protects you and your customers.

## How Much Capital Do You Need?

You can start a phone accessories business at different levels, depending on your money and your plan.

A small roadside or table start can cost between ₦50,000 and ₦150,000. This buys a small stock of fast-selling items: a few dozen chargers, cables, cases, screen guards, and earphones. It also covers a table, a display board, and a small sign. This level is good for testing the business in a busy area without risking too much money.

A small kiosk or shop start can cost between ₦200,000 and ₦500,000. This buys a wider stock, including power banks, earbuds, and speakers, plus rent for a few months in some areas, a display shelf, and a signboard. This level suits a seller who wants a fixed location customers can find easily.

A well-stocked shop can cost between ₦600,000 and ₦1,500,000. This buys a large range of products for many phone models, a good display, a POS or bank transfer setup, and enough stock to serve many customers daily. This level is for a seller who already knows the market and wants to grow quickly.

Do not borrow a large amount to start. Begin with the level you can afford, sell, and reinvest your profit into more stock. Many successful sellers in Computer Village started with a small table and grew over years. Your growth should come from profit, not from debt.

Keep your business money separate from your personal money, even if it is only a separate wallet or a separate bank account. Each evening, write down what you sold, what you spent, and what profit you made. This habit will show you which products earn the most and which ones are slow. It will also stop you from spending your capital on personal needs without noticing.

## Choose a Good Location

Location matters greatly for phone accessories. The best locations are where many phone users pass daily.

A spot inside or near Computer Village is ideal if you can afford it, because customers go there specifically to buy phone items. Competition is high, but demand is also high. A spot near a busy market, a bus stop, a school, or a university is also good, because students and workers buy accessories often.

A shop near an office area in places like Ikeja GRA, Lekki, Victoria Island, or Central Area Abuja can work well, because office workers buy chargers, power banks, and earphones, and they can pay higher prices for quality. A shop in a residential estate is good for repeat customers, especially families who need chargers and cases for several phones.

If you sell online, your location matters less, but you still need a place to store stock safely and to meet customers who want to collect orders. A small room at home can serve as your store, as long as it is dry, secure, and organised. Many successful WhatsApp sellers in Nigeria run their business from home and deliver by rider.

Before you rent a shop, check the rent, the foot traffic, and the competition. Visit the area at different times of day. Count how many people pass. Check how many accessory sellers already work there. If there are already ten sellers on one street, you may need a different street or a special offer, such as free screen guard installation or a tested-charger guarantee, to stand out.

Agree rent terms in writing, even for a small kiosk. Know the monthly rent, the agent fee, and any service charge. Start with a rent you can pay from your weekly profit, not from hope. A good location with a rent you cannot afford will close your business faster than a modest location with a rent you can pay.

## How to Price Phone Accessories

Pricing accessories is a balance. Price too high, and customers will buy from the next seller. Price too low, and you will earn little and may not be able to restock.

Start by knowing your cost for each item, including transport. If a charger costs you ₦2,000 from the supplier and ₦100 for transport, your true cost is ₦2,100. If you sell it for ₦2,500, your profit is ₦400. That may be acceptable if you sell many chargers daily. If you sell it for ₦3,500, your profit is ₦1,400, but you may sell fewer. Test different prices and watch what customers accept.

As a guide, many sellers in Nigeria use these rough price ranges, though prices vary by city, brand, and quality. A standard Android charger: ₦3,000 to ₦6,000. A fast charger: ₦5,000 to ₦12,000. A charging cable: ₦1,500 to ₦3,500. A phone case: ₦2,000 to ₦6,000. A tempered glass screen guard: ₦1,500 to ₦3,500, plus installation. Wired earphones: ₦2,000 to ₦5,000. Wireless earbuds: ₦8,000 to ₦25,000. A 10,000 mAh power bank: ₦8,000 to ₦15,000. A 20,000 mAh power bank: ₦15,000 to ₦30,000.

Use round numbers that are easy to pay by transfer. Prices like ₦3,000, ₦5,000, and ₦10,000 are easier than ₦3,270 or ₦5,480. Customers paying by transfer or POS will appreciate a clear, round price.

Offer a small discount for customers who buy several items. For example, "Buy a charger and a case together, and I will give you the screen guard for ₦1,000 instead of ₦2,000." This encourages larger orders and helps you sell more per customer. Make sure the bundle still leaves you a profit.

Be honest about original versus quality copies. Some accessories are original from the phone maker, some are good-quality copies, and some are cheap fakes. Tell the customer which one you are selling and what it can do. A customer who buys a good copy knowingly will be happy. A customer who pays an original price for a fake will never return, and will warn others.

## Sell on WhatsApp, Instagram, and in Person

You need more than a good location to sell well. You need to reach customers where they spend time: on their phones.

Set up a WhatsApp Business account with your business name, a clear profile photo, your location, and your opening hours. Add your products to the WhatsApp catalogue with clear photos and prices. Take your own photos in good light, not blurry supplier photos. Show the product clearly, and add the phone models it fits. For example, "Samsung A54 clear case – ₦3,500."

Post on WhatsApp status daily, but do not post too much. Two or three good posts a day are enough: a new product, a customer review, or a special offer. Post in the morning when people check their phones, and in the evening when they are free. Use short captions with the price and your location. For example, "Original fast charger for Samsung and Tecno. ₦6,000. Ikeja. DM to order."

In person, offer a service that online sellers cannot easily match. Test every charger before the customer leaves. Fix screen guards neatly and for free when the customer buys from you. Check that the case fits the phone model. These small services build trust and bring repeat customers.

## Avoid Fake Products and Protect Your Reputation

Fake products are a serious problem in the phone accessories market in Nigeria. Many customers have bought a charger that spoilt their phone battery, a power bank that did not charge, or earphones that stopped working in one day. They are careful, and they will test you.

Buy from suppliers you trust, and test products yourself before you sell them. Charge your own phone with a new charger for a week. Use a power bank yourself. Listen to earphones yourself. If a product fails your test, do not sell it. Return it to the supplier if you can, or stop buying that brand.

Give a simple guarantee where you can. For example, "If this charger has a fault within seven days, bring it back and we will exchange it." A short guarantee costs you little, because good products rarely fail in seven days, but it gives customers confidence to buy. Write the guarantee clearly, and honour it when a customer returns.

Do not sell a fake as an original. If a customer asks, "Is this original Samsung charger?" and it is a good copy, say so. Say, "This is a high-quality copy, sir. It charges fast and works well, but it is not the original Samsung charger. The original is ₦12,000, this one is ₦5,000." The customer can then choose. Honesty like this builds a reputation that lasts for years.

## Your First 30 Days: A Simple Plan

In week one, research your market and buy your first stock. Visit Computer Village or your nearest wholesale market, compare prices from three suppliers, and buy a small stock of chargers, cables, cases, screen guards, and earphones for popular phone models. Set up your WhatsApp Business account and catalogue.

In week two, start selling and learn what customers ask for most. Note which phone models are most common, which products sell fastest, and which prices customers accept. Fix screen guards neatly, test chargers, and ask every happy customer to tell a friend.

In week three, review your sales and restock the winners. If Samsung cases sell fast, buy more Samsung cases. If a particular earphone sells slowly, do not buy more of it. Add one new product, such as power banks, if your customers are asking for it. Post your best products on WhatsApp status and Instagram daily.

In week four, set a goal for the next month. It could be to earn a set profit per week, to gain 20 repeat customers, or to add delivery by rider to nearby estates. Write the goal down, track your sales daily, and review your prices. If a product is not selling, lower the price slightly or replace it with a better product.

Starting a phone accessories business in Nigeria does not require a huge shop or a large loan. It requires a good understanding of what phone users need, a reliable supplier, fair prices, honest products, and the discipline to serve customers well every day. Start small, test your products, keep good records, and let your customers' trust grow your business. The phone in almost every Nigerian's pocket is a daily reminder that your market is already there, waiting for a seller they can trust.
