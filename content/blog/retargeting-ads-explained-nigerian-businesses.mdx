You have seen it yourself. You visit a website to check a price, you get distracted, you leave, and then you start seeing that same business in your social feed and around the internet. That is retargeting, and it is one of the most useful tools a small business can use.

Most people do not buy or enquire on their first visit to a website. They are comparing options, checking prices, waiting for payday, or simply busy. Retargeting gives you a fair second chance to reach them after they have already shown interest, instead of spending all your advertising money chasing complete strangers.

This guide explains retargeting in plain English, the main types worth knowing, and a simple setup outline you can follow or hand to whoever runs your ads.

## What Retargeting Actually Is

Retargeting, also called remarketing, is advertising shown to people who have already interacted with your business in some way. They may have visited your website, viewed a service page, watched your video, or engaged with your social media page.

It works through a small piece of tracking code on your website, often called a pixel, or through engagement audiences built inside an advertising platform such as Meta or Google. When someone visits your site, they are added to an audience list. You can then show ads specifically to that list.

Think of it this way. Cold advertising is like calling out to strangers on the street. Retargeting is like following up with someone who already walked into your shop, looked around, and left without buying. The second conversation is usually much easier, because the person already knows who you are.

## Why Warm Visitors Are Easier to Win Back

A first-time visitor has questions. Is this business real? Can I trust them? Is the price fair? Will this solve my problem? A returning visitor has already started answering those questions.

That is why retargeting often performs better than cold ads for the same business. You are not starting from zero. You are reminding, reassuring, and giving a clear next step to someone who already showed intent.

Retargeting also helps you stay visible while people compare options. In Lagos and across Nigeria, customers routinely check several businesses before deciding. If your business disappears after the first visit while a competitor keeps showing up, the competitor has an advantage that has nothing to do with quality. Retargeting keeps you in the conversation.

## The Main Types of Retargeting

Not all visitors are equal, so it helps to group them. Here are the most useful audiences for a typical Nigerian service or product business:

- **All website visitors:** People who visited any page on your site in a recent period. This is the broadest audience and a good starting point.
- **Service or product page viewers:** People who viewed a specific service, product, or pricing page. They showed stronger interest than someone who only saw your homepage.
- **People who started but did not finish:** People who began an enquiry form, a booking step, or a checkout, but did not complete it. These are often your warmest prospects.
- **Past customers:** People who already bought from you. Retargeting can remind them about repeat purchases, new services, or seasonal offers.
- **Social and video engagers:** People who watched your videos, liked or commented on your posts, or visited your social profile. They may never have reached your website at all.

You do not need all five audiences on day one. Start with website visitors and people who started but did not finish, then add others as you learn what works.

## A Simple Retargeting Setup Outline

You do not need a complicated setup to start. Here is a practical outline:

1. **Install the tracking pixel.** Add the Meta pixel and, if you run Google Ads, the Google tag to your website. Test that they are firing correctly before you spend anything.
2. **Build your audiences.** Create audiences for website visitors, service page viewers, and people who started an enquiry or checkout. Set a sensible time window, such as the last 30 days, so your list stays fresh.
3. **Exclude people who already converted.** There is no point showing a "buy now" ad to someone who just bought. Exclude recent buyers or completed enquiries from that campaign, or show them a different message instead.
4. **Create a dedicated ad.** Do not reuse your cold ad word for word. Speak to someone who already visited: remind them what they viewed, answer a common objection, and offer a clear next step.
5. **Send them to the right page.** If they viewed a service page, send them back to that service page, not your homepage. Every extra click is a chance to lose them again.
6. **Set a frequency limit.** Decide how often someone should see your ad. You want to stay visible, not to follow people around all day. A modest frequency over a few weeks is usually enough.
7. **Review weekly.** Check which audience and which message brings enquiries, and pause what clearly does not work. Retargeting still needs attention; it is not a set-and-forget machine.

## What to Show Different Visitors

The biggest retargeting mistake is showing everyone the same generic ad. Match the message to what the person already did:

- Someone who only visited your homepage may need a simple reminder of what you do and why businesses choose you.
- Someone who viewed a service page may need proof: what is included, how the process works, and how to start.
- Someone who started an enquiry but stopped may need reassurance: a simpler next step, a way to ask questions on WhatsApp, or answers to common concerns.
- A past customer may respond best to a new service, a seasonal offer, or a reminder to reorder.

Keep the ad short, clear, and honest. Retargeting works best when it feels like a helpful reminder, not pressure.

## Mistakes to Avoid

- **Running retargeting forever without an end date.** Audiences go stale. Refresh your time windows and pause audiences that stop responding.
- **Showing ads too often.** If people feel stalked, your brand suffers. Frequency limits exist for a reason.
- **Sending everyone to the homepage.** Always match the landing page to the ad and to what the visitor viewed before.
- **Forgetting exclusions.** Exclude buyers from "buy now" campaigns, or you will waste money and annoy customers.
- **Targeting an audience that is too small.** If only a handful of people visited your site, retargeting will barely run. In that case, focus first on bringing more visitors to your site, then retarget them.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is retargeting creepy?**

It can feel that way if it is done badly, with ads shown far too often or for months on end. Done well, with a frequency limit and a fresh time window, most people experience it as a reminder from a business they already visited. Keep it respectful and it stays helpful.

**Do I need a large budget for retargeting?**

Retargeting audiences are usually smaller than cold audiences, because they only include people who already interacted with you. That means you can often start with a modest budget and scale only when you see enquiries coming in. Start small, learn, then increase.

**How long should someone stay in a retargeting audience?**

It depends on how long your customers usually take to decide. For many services, a window of around 30 days is a sensible starting point. For higher-priced services where people take longer, a longer window may make sense. Review your results and adjust.

**Can I retarget people who only engaged on social media, without a website?**

Yes. Advertising platforms let you build audiences from video viewers, profile visitors, and people who engaged with your posts. A website gives you more options, but social engagement audiences are a useful starting point.
