There is a particular sound that many Nigerian online sellers know well. It is the sound of a customer typing for a long time in a WhatsApp chat, then going quiet. The seller waits. The customer never replies. The sale is gone, and the seller never finds out why.

Often the reason is simple: typing is hard work. A customer on a bus in Lagos traffic, or a busy mother with a baby in one arm, or a trader standing in a market in Onitsha, does not want to type a long message asking about sizes, colours, delivery, and payment. They want to talk. That is why voice notes have become one of the most powerful selling tools on WhatsApp in Nigeria. A short voice note feels like a real conversation. It carries your tone, your warmth, and your confidence. A typed message cannot do that.

If you sell on WhatsApp, and most Nigerian small businesses do, learning to use voice notes well can change your results. This guide explains why voice notes work, when to send them, what to say, how to record them properly, and how to use them for follow-ups, negotiations, and after-sales care without annoying your customers.

## Why Voice Notes Work So Well in Nigeria

The first reason is trust. Online shopping in Nigeria carries a real fear of being scammed. A customer who has never met you is being asked to send money to a stranger for a product they have only seen in photos. A voice note changes that calculation. When a customer hears a real human voice, friendly and confident, answering their question directly, the seller stops being a faceless account and becomes a person. That shift matters more than any discount.

The second reason is speed. Many customers are slow typists, or they are busy, or they are not comfortable writing in English. A voice note lets them ask a full question in ten seconds instead of typing for two minutes. It also lets you answer in ten seconds instead of typing a long reply. In a busy market where you may be chatting with twenty customers at once, that saved time adds up to real money.

The third reason is tone. Text is easy to misunderstand. A short reply like "Yes, it is available" can sound cold or rude in text, even when you meant it warmly. In a voice note, the same words sound friendly. Tone also helps with negotiation. When a customer asks for a discount, a warm voice saying "I can do a small discount for you because you are a returning customer" lands very differently from the same sentence typed out.

The fourth reason is accessibility. Nigeria has many languages, and many customers are more comfortable speaking than writing. A customer can send you a voice note in Pidgin, Yoruba, Igbo, or Hausa, and you can reply in the same language. That comfort makes them more likely to buy, and more likely to come back. If you sell to customers across the country, this is a genuine advantage.

Finally, voice notes stand out. Most sellers reply with typed text, often copied and pasted. A personal voice note feels different. It tells the customer that you took the time to speak to them directly. In a crowded market, that personal touch is often what makes the customer choose you over the next seller.

## When to Send a Voice Note, and When Not To

Voice notes are powerful, but they are not right for every moment. Knowing when to use them is as important as knowing how to record them.

Send a voice note when a customer asks a question that needs a warm or detailed answer. For example, when they ask about the quality of a product, how it works, or whether it will suit them. A voice note lets you explain properly and add reassurance. Send one when a customer sounds unsure or hesitant. If they have asked the price and then gone quiet, a short, friendly voice note can reopen the conversation without pressure.

Send a voice note when you want to explain something complicated. Delivery arrangements, how to measure for a dress, how to use a product, or how to pay in instalments are all easier to explain by voice than by text. Send one when you are thanking a customer after a purchase, or checking that a delivery arrived safely. That small gesture turns a one-time buyer into a returning customer.

Send a voice note when a customer sends you one first. This is simple courtesy. If they took the time to speak to you, reply in the same way. Matching their style shows respect and keeps the conversation natural.

Do not send a voice note as your very first message to a new customer who has only sent a short text, unless they have asked a question that suits it. Some people prefer text, and an unexpected voice note from a stranger can feel intrusive. Start with text, and switch to voice once the conversation is flowing.

Do not use voice notes for information the customer needs to keep and refer back to, such as an account number, a delivery address, a price list, or an order confirmation. Voice notes disappear into the chat history and are hard to search. Always send prices, account details, addresses, and order summaries as text, even if you also explain them by voice. The customer needs to be able to copy an account number. They cannot copy it from a voice note.

Do not send long voice notes. A voice note that runs for three minutes feels like a lecture, and most customers will not listen to the end. Keep your voice notes short, ideally under one minute, and never longer than two minutes unless the customer has asked for a detailed explanation.

## How to Record a Good Voice Note

A good voice note is clear, warm, and to the point. It does not need professional equipment. Your phone microphone is enough, but a few simple habits will make a big difference to how you sound.

First, find a quiet place before you record. Background noise is the biggest enemy of a good voice note. A generator humming, a television playing, children shouting, or traffic noise all make you hard to hear and make you sound unprofessional. Step into a quiet room, or at least move away from the noise, before you press record. If you are in a market or on the road, wait until you are somewhere quieter, or keep the note very short and apologise for the noise at the start.

Second, hold the phone at a steady distance from your mouth. Too close and your voice sounds harsh and breathy. Too far and you sound distant and quiet. About fifteen centimetres from your mouth is usually right. Speak at a normal volume, as if you are talking to someone sitting across a table from you.

Third, smile when you speak. It sounds strange, but it works. A smile changes the shape of your mouth and the tone of your voice, and the listener can hear it. A voice note recorded with a smile sounds warm and welcoming. One recorded while you are tired or irritated sounds flat, and the customer will notice, even if they cannot explain why.

Fourth, slow down slightly. Many people speak too fast when they record, especially when they are nervous or in a hurry. A slightly slower pace sounds more confident and is easier to understand, especially for customers listening on a noisy bus or with a weak earpiece.

Fifth, plan what you are going to say before you press record. A short pause to think is better than a rambling note full of "erm" and "you know". Know your main point, say it, add one helpful detail, and finish with a clear next step. For example: greet the customer by name, answer their question, mention the price or the delivery option, and ask a simple question that moves the sale forward, such as "Would you like me to reserve one for you?"

Finally, listen to your own voice notes sometimes. Play one back and ask yourself honestly: would I buy from this person? If you sound rushed, bored, or unclear, adjust. Small improvements in how you sound will show up in your sales.

## What to Say: Scripts You Can Adapt

You do not need to memorise scripts, but having a structure for common situations helps you sound confident and consistent. Here are simple patterns you can adapt to your own products and your own voice.

When a customer asks for the price, do not just state the number and stop. Greet them, confirm the product, state the price clearly, mention what is included, and add a gentle next step. For example: "Good afternoon, Ada. Thank you for asking. The dress you saw is forty-five thousand naira, and that includes delivery within Lagos. It comes in sizes eight to sixteen, and we have your size in stock. Would you like me to hold one for you while you decide?" That is warm, complete, and it moves the conversation forward.

When a customer says your price is too high, do not argue and do not immediately drop the price. Acknowledge their concern, explain the value briefly, and offer something reasonable. For example: "I understand, and thank you for telling me. This one is made with quality fabric that lasts, which is why it costs a bit more than the regular ones. I can offer you a small discount if you order today, or I can show you a similar style at a lower price. Which would you prefer?" That response respects the customer, defends your price without being defensive, and gives them a choice.

When a customer has gone quiet after asking for the price, a short voice note can reopen the conversation. Keep it light and helpful, not pushy. For example: "Hello, good morning. I just wanted to check in and see if you had any more questions about the bag. It is still available, and I am happy to help with anything you need. No pressure at all." That message reminds them you exist, answers the silence kindly, and gives them an easy way to reply.

When a customer has paid, send a voice note to confirm the order and explain what happens next. For example: "Thank you so much for your order. I have received your payment, and your package will be sent out tomorrow morning. I will share the delivery details with you as soon as it leaves. Thank you for trusting us." That message removes anxiety, and it is the kind of thing customers remember and tell their friends about.

When a delivery has arrived, check in. A short voice note asking if everything is fine, and whether the product is as expected, shows care. If there is a problem, you hear about it early and can fix it. If everything is fine, you can gently ask for a review or a referral.

## Voice Notes in Pidgin and Local Languages

One of the most underused advantages of voice notes is language. Many Nigerian customers are far more comfortable speaking than writing, and they are far more comfortable in their own language than in formal English. A seller who can switch to Pidgin, or to the customer's language, instantly feels closer and more trustworthy.

If a customer sends you a voice note in Pidgin, reply in Pidgin. If they greet you in Yoruba, Igbo, or Hausa, greet them back in that language, even if you switch to English for the details. That small gesture shows respect and builds a connection that no discount can buy.

This also applies to your marketing. A short voice note status, or a voice note in a broadcast, spoken in warm, natural Pidgin, often gets more attention than a polished written advert. It sounds like a real person talking to real people, not like a company broadcasting at them.

Be careful, though, not to assume a customer's language. Let them lead. If they write in English, reply in English. If they speak Pidgin, match them. The goal is to make the customer comfortable, not to show off how many languages you speak.

For businesses that sell across the country, consider having team members who can handle voice notes in different languages. Even a basic ability to greet a customer and answer common questions in their language makes a difference. Customers remember the seller who spoke to them like a neighbour, not like a stranger.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long should a selling voice note be?

Keep it under one minute in most cases, and never longer than two minutes unless the customer has asked for a detailed explanation. A short voice note respects the customer's time and keeps their attention. If you have a lot to say, break it into a short voice note plus a text message with the details, such as prices and account numbers.

### Should I send voice notes to new customers I have never spoken to?

It is better to start with text and switch to voice once the conversation is flowing, unless the customer sends you a voice note first. A voice note from a stranger can feel intrusive to some people. Once you have exchanged a few messages and the customer seems comfortable, a voice note will feel natural and welcome.

### What if a customer prefers text and does not like voice notes?

Respect that. Some customers are in places where they cannot listen to a voice note, such as at work, in church, or on a quiet bus. Others simply prefer reading. If a customer always replies in text, reply in text. The goal is to communicate in the way the customer prefers, not the way you prefer.

### Can I use voice notes for marketing, not just replies?

Yes, but carefully. A short voice note sent to your broadcast list or posted as a status can work well, especially for announcing a new product or a promo. Keep it short, friendly, and useful. Do not send marketing voice notes to people who have not bought from you or asked to hear from you, and do not send them too often. Once or twice a week is enough.

### How do I handle a customer who sends very long voice notes?

Listen to them, and reply to the main points. If a customer sends a three-minute voice note, they have taken time to explain something, and they deserve a proper reply. You can reply with a shorter voice note that addresses their main question, and a text message for any details they need to keep. If long voice notes are a regular problem, you can gently guide the customer by asking a specific question that they can answer briefly.

## What to Do Next

1. **Record three practice voice notes today.** Answer your three most common customer questions out loud: your price, your delivery arrangement, and your location. Listen to each one and check that it is clear, warm, and under one minute. Save the best versions as a guide for how you want to sound.

2. **Set a simple rule for your chats this week.** Reply by voice when a customer asks a question that needs warmth or explanation, and reply by text for prices, account numbers, addresses, and order confirmations. Try this for one week and notice which conversations move faster and which customers reply more warmly.

3. **Start a follow-up routine with voice notes.** Each morning, pick three customers who asked for a price in the last two days and have not replied. Send each one a short, friendly voice note that adds something useful, such as a new colour, a delivery option, or a gentle reminder that the item is still available. Track how many reply, and how many of those replies turn into orders.
