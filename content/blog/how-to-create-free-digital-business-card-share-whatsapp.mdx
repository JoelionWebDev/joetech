You meet someone at a market, at a wedding, at a church programme, or at a business event. They like what you do. They ask for your card. You reach into your pocket and find nothing — or you hand over a paper card that will be lost by tomorrow, buried in a bag, or thrown away with the week's receipts.

Now imagine instead that you send them a link on WhatsApp. They tap it and see your name, your photo, your business, your services, your phone number, your WhatsApp button, your location, and your catalogue — all on one neat page, on their phone, ready to save. That is a digital business card, and it costs nothing to create.

A digital business card is simply a small web page that holds your business details in one place. It replaces the paper card with something that cannot be lost, cannot run out, and can be updated whenever your number, your prices or your services change. This guide shows you how to create one for free and share it on WhatsApp so that it actually brings you customers.

## Why a Digital Card Works Better Than Paper

A paper card has one job: to carry your phone number from your hand to someone else's pocket. It does that job badly. People lose paper cards, they forget whose card it was, and they have to type the number in by hand before they can call you. Every extra step loses customers.

A digital card does the same job and more. The customer does not type anything — they tap to call, tap to chat on WhatsApp, tap to see your location, tap to view your catalogue. Your card also stays current. Change your number or add a new service, and everyone who has your link sees the new version. A paper card, once printed, is frozen.

There is also the cost. A pack of printed cards costs money every time you reprint, and you run out at the worst moment — at an event, when you have just met your best lead of the month. A digital card never runs out. You can share it a thousand times a day for free.

None of this means paper cards are useless. A paper card still has its place at formal events and in shops. But if you only have one card, make it digital first, and print paper later.

## What to Put on Your Digital Business Card

A good digital card is short. It answers four questions in a few seconds: who are you, what do you do, how do I reach you, and why should I trust you? Anything that does not answer one of those questions can be left out.

**Your name and a clear photo.** Use a good photo of yourself, or your business logo if you are a bigger brand. People remember faces. A blurry photo, or no photo at all, makes you forgettable.

**Your business name and what you do, in one line.** Not a paragraph. "Adaeze — Tailor, Lekki. Ankara and corporate wear, ready in 5 days." That is enough for someone to know whether they need you.

**Your contact buttons.** A WhatsApp button is the most important one for a Nigerian business, because that is where the conversation will happen. Add a call button, and your email if you use it. Make the buttons big and obvious — the customer should not have to hunt for them.

**Your services and prices, briefly.** Three to five services, with starting prices if you have them. This is what turns a contact into a customer: the person can see what you offer and whether they can afford it before they even message you.

**Your location and working hours.** "Lekki Phase 1, Lagos. Open Monday to Saturday, 8am to 6pm." This stops the most common question you will ever be asked on WhatsApp.

**Your social proof.** A link to your Instagram or Facebook page, your Google reviews if you have them, or a line about your experience. One or two strong lines is enough. If you have a website, link it.

**Your catalogue or portfolio.** If you sell products, link your WhatsApp catalogue or your product page. If you offer a service, add two or three photos of your best work. A tailor's card should show finished outfits. A caterer's card should show food. A designer's card should show designs.

## How to Share Your Card on WhatsApp

Creating the card is only half the job. Sharing it well is the other half.

**Share it in your WhatsApp bio area.** WhatsApp does not have a proper bio link like Instagram, but you can put your card link in your business profile. Open WhatsApp Business, go to your business profile, and add your card link in the website or description field. Anyone who views your profile can tap it.

**Send it instead of typing your number.** When someone asks for your contact, do not type your number. Send your card link with a short message: "Here is my card — tap to chat, see my services and my work." It is faster for you and more useful for them.

**Use it in groups — carefully.** Do not spam groups with your card link. That annoys people and gets you removed. Instead, when someone in a group asks for a service you offer, reply with your card and a helpful sentence. That is welcome. A random advert is not.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

The first mistake is putting too much on the card. A card with twenty services, five phone numbers, three addresses and a life story is not a card — it is a website, and nobody reads it. Keep it short. Your card's job is to start a conversation, not to close the sale.

The second mistake is using a bad photo. A dark, blurry or unprofessional photo makes people doubt the business before they read a word. Take a new photo in good light if you have to. It takes two minutes and it is worth it.

The third mistake is dead links. A WhatsApp button that opens the wrong number, an Instagram link that goes nowhere, a catalogue link that is broken — each one tells the customer you do not check your own work. Test every link on your card once a month.

The fourth mistake is forgetting to update the card. You change your prices, you move shop, you add a service — and your card still shows the old details. Update it the same day something changes. A digital card is only better than paper if you keep it current.
