When a customer in Lagos needs a plumber, a caterer, a tailor or a web designer, what do they do first? Many of them open Google on their phone and type a few words: "plumber near me", "best caterer in Lekki", "affordable web designer Lagos". The businesses that appear at the top of those results get the calls. The businesses that appear on page three rarely get seen at all.

Search engine optimisation, usually shortened to SEO, is the work of helping your website appear higher in those Google results. It is not magic, and it is not only for big companies with large budgets. It is a set of practical habits: using the words your customers actually search for, writing clear pages about what you do, making your website fast and easy to use on a phone, and showing Google that your business is real, local and trustworthy.

This guide explains basic SEO in plain language for a Nigerian small business owner. You do not need to know how to code. You do not need expensive software. You need a website, a little time each week, and the willingness to fix one thing at a time. By the end, you will know how to choose keywords, improve your pages, show up in local search, and measure whether your work is paying off.

## What SEO Is and Why It Matters for a Small Business

SEO is simply making your website easy for Google to understand and easy for customers to use. When Google understands what your business does and where you are based, it can show your website to the right people at the right moment, when they are already looking to buy.

That timing is what makes SEO so valuable. A person who sees your Instagram post may or may not need your service that day. A person who searches Google for "emergency electrician in Ikeja" needs one right now. They are ready to call, ready to ask for a price, and ready to book. If your website appears in that search, you have a real chance of winning the job without paying for an advert.

SEO also keeps working after you do the work. An advert stops the moment you stop paying for it. A good page that ranks well on Google can bring visitors for months, sometimes for years, with only small updates. That makes SEO one of the best long-term investments a small business can make, especially when advertising budgets are tight.

But SEO takes time. You will rarely see results in a week. Most small business websites need several weeks or a few months of steady work before rankings improve noticeably. That is normal. Google needs time to discover your changes, compare your site with others, and decide where you belong. The businesses that win at SEO are usually the ones that keep going when the early results are slow.

## Start by Understanding What Your Customers Search For

Good SEO starts with your customers, not with Google. Before you change anything on your website, spend time thinking about the exact words people type when they need what you sell.

Start by writing down the services and products you offer, in the words your customers use. A customer rarely searches for "integrated facility management solutions". They search for "cleaning company in Lekki" or "office cleaning Lagos price". A baker's customer searches for "birthday cake in Abuja" or "small chops for party Lagos", not for "artisanal confectionery services". Use simple, everyday language, because that is what your customers type.

Next, add your location to those phrases. Most Nigerian small businesses serve a particular city or area. A hair salon in Yaba serves people in Yaba and nearby Surulere, not people in Kano. A restaurant in Port Harcourt serves people in Port Harcourt. Location words such as Lagos, Abuja, Port Harcourt, Ibadan, Lekki, Ikeja, Wuse, Garki, Surulere and Yaba help Google show your website to people near you, and they help customers know at a glance that you can serve them.

Make a list of ten to twenty phrases like this. Include a mix of short phrases, such as "tailor in Lekki", and longer, more specific phrases, such as "affordable Ankara tailor for wedding in Lekki". The longer phrases usually have less competition, and the people who search for them often know exactly what they want. A person searching for "wedding tailor Lekki Ankara" is much closer to booking than a person searching for just "tailor".

You can also learn from your customers directly. Ask the next five customers who call or message you how they found you and what they searched for. Ask your WhatsApp contacts what words they would type to find a business like yours. Their answers are often more useful than any tool, because they show you real language from real buyers in your area.

## Fix Your Page Titles and Descriptions

Your page title is the blue clickable line people see in Google results. Your meta description is the short grey text underneath it. Together, they decide whether someone clicks on your website or scrolls past it to the next result. They are small, but they matter a great deal.

Every page on your website should have its own unique title. Your homepage title should say what your business does and where you are based. For example, a good homepage title is "Catering Services in Lagos | Fresh Food for Weddings and Events". A poor title is just "Home" or just your business name with no description. Nobody searches for "Home", and a business name alone tells a stranger nothing about what you do.

Keep your titles short enough to display fully in search results, usually under 60 characters. Put the most important words near the start. If you are a plumber in Abuja, start with "Plumber in Abuja" rather than burying it at the end. If you sell shoes in Lagos, start with the type of shoes and the city. This helps both Google and the person scanning the results to understand your page quickly.

Your meta description should be one or two sentences that explain what the page offers and why someone should click. It does not directly raise your ranking, but a clear description brings more clicks, and more clicks send a good signal to Google. For a catering page, a good description might be: "Fresh Nigerian food for weddings, birthdays and office events in Lagos. Call or chat on WhatsApp for a free quote today." That tells the reader what you offer, where you work, and what to do next.

Use the same approach on your service pages. If you offer three services, give each one its own page with its own title and description, rather than listing all three on one crowded page. A page about "House Cleaning in Lekki" can rank for cleaning searches. A page about "Office Cleaning in Lagos" can rank for office searches. Separate pages let Google understand each service clearly, and they let customers land directly on the service they need.

## Write Helpful Pages That Answer Real Questions

Google ranks pages that help people. That means your website needs real content: clear pages about your services, your prices where possible, your location, and the questions customers ask you again and again.

Start with your main service pages. Each page should explain the service in plain language, say who it is for, show examples of your work, and tell the reader how to contact you. A tailor's page might explain the types of clothes made, the fabrics used, how measurements are taken, how long an order takes, and the starting price. A cleaning company's page might explain what is included in a home cleaning visit, how long it takes, and which areas of Lagos it covers. Use headings to break the page into sections, so a visitor can scan it quickly on a phone.

Add a page that answers common questions. Customers in Nigeria ask the same things again and again: How much does it cost? Do you deliver? How long will it take? Do you work on weekends? What areas do you cover? How do I pay? Write a short answer to each question on your website, using the same words your customers use when they ask. These answers help your visitors, and they also help Google match your page to the questions people type into search.

Consider adding a simple blog or a guides section to your website. You do not need to post every day. One helpful article a week, or even two a month, is a good start. Write about topics your customers care about: how to choose a wedding caterer in Lagos, what to check before hiring a plumber, how to care for Ankara fabric, or how to plan a small event on a budget. These articles bring in visitors who are searching for answers, and some of those visitors become customers. They also show Google that your website is active and useful.

When you write, use your keywords naturally. Mention "catering in Lagos" where it fits, but do not repeat it in every sentence. Google is good at spotting pages that stuff keywords in awkwardly, and readers find them unpleasant to read. Write for a real person first, and let the keywords appear where they belong. A good test is to read your page out loud. If it sounds natural, it is probably fine. If it sounds like a robot wrote it, rewrite it.

## Make Your Website Fast and Easy to Use on a Phone

Most Nigerians browse the internet on a phone, often on mobile data that is not always fast. If your website takes a long time to load, or if it is hard to read on a small screen, visitors will leave and go to a competitor. Google also pays attention to this. Websites that load quickly and work well on phones tend to rank better than slow, awkward ones.

Test your website on your own phone. Open it on mobile data, not only on Wi-Fi, and see how long it takes to appear. Try to tap the buttons, read the text, and find your phone number or WhatsApp link. If you have to pinch and zoom to read anything, or if a button is too small to tap, your website needs fixing. Ask a friend to try it too, on a different phone, and tell you honestly what was confusing.

Images are the most common cause of slow websites. Photos taken on a modern phone can be very large, and uploading them directly to your website without compressing them will slow every page down. Before you upload a photo, reduce its file size with a free compression tool or an image plugin. Aim for clear photos that load quickly, rather than the largest possible file. You can also use the right format: JPG for photographs, and PNG only when you need a transparent background or sharp text.

Check that your website uses HTTPS, which is the secure version of a website address that starts with https:// and shows a padlock in the browser. Most hosting companies provide a free security certificate, and your developer can switch it on if it is not already active. A website without HTTPS may show a "not secure" warning, which puts visitors off and hurts your ranking.

## Show Up in Local Search With Google Business Profile

Local search is one of the easiest wins for a Nigerian small business, and it is free. When someone searches for "barber near me" or "pharmacy in Ikeja", Google often shows a map with a short list of nearby businesses before it shows normal website results. That map section is powered largely by Google Business Profile, and your business should be there.

If you have not done so already, create or claim your Google Business Profile at business.google.com. Add your correct business name, your category, your address, your phone number, your WhatsApp number if you use one for business, your opening hours, and a link to your website. Make sure the name, address and phone number match exactly what is on your website. Google checks for consistency, and mismatched details can confuse it.

Add clear photos of your shop, your products, your team and your work. A restaurant should show its food and its seating area. A salon should show its chairs, its styles and its products. A shop should show its shelves and its best-selling items. Photos help customers choose you over a competitor with an empty profile, and they help Google understand that your business is active and real.

Ask happy customers to leave a review on your Google profile. Reviews are one of the strongest signals in local search, and they also persuade new customers to trust you. After a good job, ask the customer politely: "If you are happy with the work, please leave us a short review on Google. It helps our small business a lot." You can share a direct link to your review page on WhatsApp to make it easy. Do not offer payment for reviews, and never write fake reviews for your own business. Google can detect them, and real customers can usually spot them too.

Keep your profile updated. If your opening hours change for a public holiday, update them. If you move to a new shop, update your address the same week. If you add a new service, add it to your profile. Post a short update or offer on your profile from time to time, just as you would on social media. An updated profile ranks better and looks more trustworthy than one that has been untouched for a year.

## Measure What Is Working and Keep Improving

SEO without measurement is guesswork. You need to know whether people are finding your website, which pages they visit, and which searches bring them to you. Two free tools from Google make this possible: Google Search Console and Google Analytics.

Google Search Console shows you which search phrases bring people to your website, how often your pages appear in results, and how often people click. It also warns you about problems, such as pages Google cannot read, broken links, or pages that load slowly on phones. Set it up for your website if you have not done so already. Your developer can help you verify your site, and the setup takes only a short time. Check it once a week and note which phrases are bringing visitors and which pages are getting the most clicks.

Google Analytics shows you what visitors do once they arrive: how long they stay, which pages they visit, and whether they contact you. You can see whether most visitors come from Google, from social media, or from typing your address directly. You can also see whether they use a phone or a computer. This information helps you decide where to spend your effort. If most visitors leave your homepage after a few seconds, the page may need clearer text or faster loading. If a particular blog article brings many visitors, write more articles on similar topics.

## A Simple SEO Checklist to Start This Week

- Write down ten to twenty phrases your customers actually search for, with your city included.
- Give every important page a unique title under 60 characters and a clear one-to-two sentence description.
- Write one helpful service page or guide that answers a real customer question.
- Compress your images, test your site on a phone, and check that it uses HTTPS.
- Create or claim your Google Business Profile and add photos, hours and correct contact details.
- Ask three happy customers for a Google review this week.
- Set up Google Search Console and check it once a week.
- Repeat one small improvement every week for the next three months.

Basic SEO is not a mystery. It is clear titles, helpful pages, a fast phone-friendly website, a complete local profile, honest reviews, and steady measurement. Start with the checklist above, fix one item at a time, and give Google time to notice. Your website will not jump to the top overnight, but with consistent work, more of the right customers in Lagos, Abuja, Port Harcourt and beyond will find you exactly when they need what you sell.
