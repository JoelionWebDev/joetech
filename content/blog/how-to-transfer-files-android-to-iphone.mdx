Moving files between Android and iPhone used to be a headache. The two systems were built by different companies, they do not talk to each other the way two Android phones or two iPhones do, and the simple Bluetooth sharing that works between two Android phones does not work with an iPhone. Many people in Nigeria discover this the hard way: they buy a new iPhone, or a friend with an iPhone wants the photos from a party, and suddenly nothing seems to work.

The good news is that there are now several reliable ways to move your files, and most of them are free. Which one you choose depends on what you are moving and how much of it there is. Moving everything to a brand-new iPhone is different from sending a few photos to a friend, and sending one document is different from moving ten years of contacts. This guide covers the main methods, step by step, so you can pick the one that fits your situation.

## Method 1: Move to iOS — Best for a Brand-New iPhone

If you have just bought an iPhone and you are setting it up for the first time, Apple's own app, Move to iOS, is the best option. It moves your contacts, messages, photos, videos, web bookmarks, email accounts and calendars from your Android phone to your new iPhone over a direct Wi-Fi connection between the two phones. It does not use your mobile data, and it does not need the internet at all.

### Before You Start

There are a few conditions. First, your iPhone must be new or freshly reset — Move to iOS only works during the iPhone's initial setup, on the screen that asks you to transfer your data. If you have already finished setting up your iPhone and you are using it, this method will not work unless you erase the iPhone and start again, which most people do not want to do. So do this first, before you set up the iPhone.

Second, both phones need to be charged, or plugged in. The transfer can take a while if you have a lot of photos, and you do not want either phone dying halfway through. Third, turn off mobile data on the Android phone, because the transfer creates its own Wi-Fi connection and mobile data can interfere with it. Fourth, make sure the Android phone has enough free space for the app and that the iPhone has enough free space for everything you are moving.

### The Steps

On your Android phone, open the Google Play Store and download "Move to iOS" — it is free, made by Apple. Open it, tap "Continue", agree to the terms, and tap "Next" on the "Find Your Code" screen.

On your iPhone, go through the setup steps until you reach the "Transfer Your Apps and Data" screen, or the "Apps and Data" screen on older iOS versions. Tap "From Android". The iPhone will show a six-digit or ten-digit code.

On your Android phone, enter that code. The iPhone will create a temporary Wi-Fi network and the Android phone will join it. A screen will appear on the Android phone asking what you want to transfer: Contacts, Message history, Photos and Videos, Files and folders, and more. Tick the ones you want and tap "Continue" or "Next".

Now wait. Do not use either phone while the transfer runs. The time it takes depends on how much you are moving — a few hundred photos might take ten minutes, while thousands of photos and videos can take an hour or more. When the transfer is finished, tap "Done" on the Android phone and "Continue" on the iPhone to finish setting it up.

### What Move to iOS Does Not Move

Move to iOS moves a lot, but not everything. It does not move your Android apps — it will suggest the iPhone versions of free apps you had, but you will need to download them again from the App Store. It does not move music files stored outside the usual folders, and it does not move WhatsApp chats by itself — WhatsApp has its own separate transfer process, which is covered below. Paid apps do not carry over either; if you bought an app on Android, you will need to buy it again on iPhone if there is a paid iPhone version. Your photos and videos move at full quality, which is good, but it also means the transfer takes longer.

## Method 2: Google Drive — Best for a Few Files or Documents

Google Drive is the simplest option when you want to move a handful of files, documents or photos and you do not want to install anything new. Most Android users already have Google Drive on their phone, and it works on iPhone too.

On your Android phone, open Google Drive, tap the plus sign, and choose "Upload". Select the files you want to move — photos, videos, PDFs, documents, anything. Wait for the upload to finish. You need internet for this, so use Wi-Fi if the files are large, because uploading videos over mobile data will eat your data bundle quickly.

Then, on your iPhone, download Google Drive from the App Store, sign in with the same Google account, and your files will be there. You can view them, download them to the iPhone, or just leave them in Drive and open them whenever you need them.

Google Drive gives you 15 GB of free storage, shared with Gmail and Google Photos. That is enough for documents and a good number of photos, but if you are moving years of videos you may run out of space. In that case, move the files in batches and delete them from Drive after downloading them to the iPhone, or use one of the other methods below.

This method is also useful when the two phones are not in the same place. If your business partner is in Abuja and you are in Lagos, you can upload a file to Drive and they can download it on their iPhone immediately. That is why Google Drive is the go-to option for sending documents to clients who use iPhones.

## Method 3: Google Photos — Best for Moving All Your Photos

If photos and videos are the main thing you want to move, Google Photos is the cleanest method. It is designed for exactly this.

On your Android phone, open Google Photos, sign in with your Google account, and turn on backup. The app will upload your photos and videos to your Google account. This can take a long time if you have thousands of photos, so plug the phone in, connect to Wi-Fi, and let it run — overnight is a good time.

On your iPhone, download Google Photos from the App Store and sign in with the same Google account. All your photos will appear. You can view them there, or you can save selected photos to the iPhone's own Photos app by opening a photo, tapping the share button, and choosing "Save to device". Google Photos also lets you free up space on the old Android phone afterwards by deleting the copies that have been backed up.

One thing to note: Google Photos backup is free only up to your 15 GB Google storage limit. Photos backed up in original quality count against that limit. If you have a lot of photos, you may need to buy extra Google storage or use the "Storage saver" quality option, which compresses photos slightly but uses less space.

## Method 4: WhatsApp — Best for Sending a Few Photos or a Document

WhatsApp is the method most Nigerians already use without thinking about it, and it works fine between Android and iPhone. Open a chat with the person (or with yourself, using the "Message yourself" chat), tap the attachment button, choose the photos, videos or documents you want to send, and send them.

There is one important catch: when you send a photo or video on WhatsApp as a normal photo or video, WhatsApp compresses it to save data, so the quality drops. The photo still looks fine on a phone screen, but if you want to print it or use it for business, the quality loss matters. The fix is simple: send photos and videos as documents. In WhatsApp, tap the attachment button, choose "Document", then "Browse other docs" or "Choose from gallery", and select the file. It sends at full original quality, with no compression. The file size limit for documents on WhatsApp is large enough for most photos and short videos.

WhatsApp is not a good method for moving hundreds of photos at once — it is slow and fiddly. Use it for a few files, and use Google Photos or a computer for a big move.

## Method 5: Move Your WhatsApp Chats

Your WhatsApp chats do not move with Move to iOS or Google Drive, because WhatsApp stores its own backup separately. If your chats matter to you — and for many business owners, years of customer chats are valuable — move them properly.

WhatsApp now supports moving chats from Android to iPhone directly. On your Android phone, open WhatsApp, go to Settings, then Chats, then "Move chats to iOS". On your iPhone, during setup, when you use Move to iOS, WhatsApp will appear as one of the transfer options. Follow the prompts on both phones and keep them close together. Both phones need to be on the same Wi-Fi network, and the iPhone needs to be new or freshly reset, just like the main Move to iOS transfer.

If your iPhone is already set up, the direct transfer will not work. In that case, you can export individual important chats instead: open the chat, tap the name at the top, scroll down to "Export Chat", and choose whether to include media. This sends the chat history as a file you can email to yourself or save in Google Drive. It is not as neat as a full transfer, but it saves the important conversations.

## Method 6: Use a Computer in the Middle

If you have a laptop or desktop computer, it can act as a bridge between the two phones. Connect your Android phone to the computer with a USB cable, open it like a storage drive, and copy the folders you want — DCIM for photos and videos, Downloads for documents, and so on — onto the computer. Then connect the iPhone and use iTunes on Windows, or Finder on a Mac, to copy the files onto the iPhone.

On a Windows computer, you may need to install iTunes from the Microsoft Store to transfer files to an iPhone. On a Mac, Finder handles it directly. For photos specifically, you can also use the Windows Photos app or the Mac Photos app to import from the Android phone and then sync to the iPhone.

## Method 7: Email for Small Files

For one or two small files, email still works. Attach the file to an email, send it to yourself, and open it on the iPhone. Most email services limit attachments to around 25 MB, so this is only for small documents and photos.

## Which Method Should You Use?

Here is a quick way to decide. If you just bought a new iPhone and have not set it up yet, use Move to iOS — it moves the most in one go. If you only need your photos, use Google Photos. If you need to send a few documents or photos to someone else's iPhone, use Google Drive or WhatsApp, sending photos as documents to keep the quality. If your WhatsApp chats matter, use WhatsApp's own move feature during setup. If you have a huge collection and no good internet, use a computer as the bridge.

Whatever method you choose, do not delete anything from your old Android phone until you have checked that the files actually arrived on the iPhone. Open a few photos, check your contacts, open a document. Only when you are sure everything is there should you wipe or sell the old phone. Keep the old phone for a few days after the move as a backup, just in case something is missing.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I transfer files from Android to iPhone with Bluetooth?

No, not directly. iPhones do not accept file transfers over Bluetooth from Android phones the way two Android phones do. You need to use one of the methods in this guide instead — Move to iOS, Google Drive, Google Photos, WhatsApp, a file-sharing app, or a computer.

### Does Move to iOS move my WhatsApp chats?

Not on its own. WhatsApp chats need WhatsApp's own transfer, which runs alongside Move to iOS during the iPhone's first setup. On Android, go to WhatsApp Settings, then Chats, then "Move chats to iOS", and follow the prompts. If your iPhone is already set up, you can only export individual chats instead.

### How do I transfer photos from Android to iPhone without losing quality?

Use Google Photos with backup set to original quality, send the photos as documents on WhatsApp instead of as photos, or use Google Drive or a computer. Normal WhatsApp photo sending compresses images and reduces quality, so always choose the document option when quality matters.

### Can I use AirDrop from Android to iPhone?

No. AirDrop only works between Apple devices — iPhone, iPad and Mac. Android phones cannot send or receive AirDrop. Cross-platform alternatives like Send Anywhere or LocalSend work on both Android and iPhone and do a similar job over local Wi-Fi.

### How long does it take to transfer everything to a new iPhone?

It depends on how much you are moving. A few hundred photos and your contacts might take ten to twenty minutes. Several thousand photos and videos can take an hour or more. Move to iOS and Google Photos both take longer with more data, so start the transfer when you have time and keep both phones charged.

### Will my Android apps work on iPhone?

No. Android apps and iPhone apps are different. Move to iOS will suggest the iPhone versions of your free apps, and you can download them from the App Store, but you will need to sign in to each one again. Paid apps you bought on Android will need to be bought again on iPhone if there is a paid version.
