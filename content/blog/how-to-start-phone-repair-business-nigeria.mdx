Every day in Nigeria, thousands of phones crack, stop charging, lose their screens, or refuse to switch on. People depend on their phones for work, banking, school, and staying in touch with family. When a phone breaks, most people do not rush to buy a new one. They look for a good repairer they can trust. That is why a phone repair business remains one of the most practical small businesses you can start in Nigeria today.

You do not need a degree to succeed here. You need skill, the right tools, honest pricing, and a good location. Many successful phone technicians in Nigeria started as apprentices in Computer Village in Ikeja, or in a small shop in their neighbourhood, and grew into shop owners with staff and regular customers. This guide walks you through the whole journey, step by step.

## Why a Phone Repair Business Works in Nigeria

Nigeria has one of the largest mobile phone markets in Africa. Almost every adult owns a phone, and many people own two. Phones are used heavily every day, which means they break often. Screens crack when phones fall. Charging ports wear out from daily use. Batteries swell after years of charging. Water damage, software problems, and faulty buttons are common too.

Because new phones are expensive, repair is usually the first choice. That creates steady demand all year round. Unlike seasonal businesses, phone repair does not depend on Christmas or a farming season. You can also start small, from a table in a busy market, a corner of an existing shop, or from home, then rent a proper shop as your income grows. You can add related services over time, such as selling phone accessories, unlocking phones, flashing software, and selling fairly used phones.

## Step 1: Learn the Skill Properly

Do not skip this step. A phone repair business lives or dies on the quality of your work. One bad repair can destroy your reputation in a neighbourhood. One good repair can bring you many new customers through referrals.

### Learn through apprenticeship

The most common and reliable way to learn phone repair in Nigeria is through apprenticeship. Find an experienced technician in a busy area and learn under them for six months to one year. In places like Computer Village in Ikeja, Lagos, there are many masters who take apprentices. You will learn by watching, then by practising on old phones, then by handling real customer phones under supervision.

During apprenticeship, learn how to diagnose a fault correctly, how to talk to customers, how to price a job, and where to buy genuine spare parts. Fees vary by location and by the reputation of the master. In Lagos, fees can range from about ₦100,000 to ₦300,000 for a full training period, sometimes paid in parts. In smaller towns, the fee may be lower. Ask clearly what the fee covers, how long the training lasts, and whether you will receive a certificate or a recommendation at the end.

### Learn through a training school

If apprenticeship does not suit you, there are phone repair training schools in Lagos, Abuja, Port Harcourt, and other major cities. A structured course usually runs for one to three months and covers hardware repair, software flashing, screen replacement, charging port repair, battery replacement, and water damage treatment. Some schools also teach board-level repairs, which earns higher fees.

Before you pay, visit the school, ask to see their workshop, and talk to past students if you can. A good school should have enough practice phones, working tools, and a teacher who actually repairs phones for a living. Avoid schools that only show videos without giving you real phones to practise on.

### Practise on old phones first

Buy old or faulty phones and practise on them at home. Old Infinix, Tecno, Samsung, and iPhone models are good for practice because spare parts are widely available. Practise opening phones without breaking the casing, replacing screens, soldering charging ports, and replacing batteries.

### Learn software skills too

Hardware is only half of the job. Many customers come with software problems: a phone that hangs, a forgotten password, a phone that needs flashing, or an app that keeps crashing. Learn how to flash common phone brands, remove viruses, back up customer data safely, and update phone software. These services are quick, need little capital, and bring in regular income.

## Step 2: Decide Your Business Model

Before you spend money, decide how you want to operate. The first model is a shop-based repair business. You rent a shop in a busy area and customers bring their phones to you. This is the most trusted model because customers know where to find you. The second is a mobile or home-service model, where you go to the customer's home or office. This works well if you already have a network of customers, but it is harder to build trust at the start. The third is a market-stall or shared-shop model. You rent a small space inside a busy phone market or share a shop with another trader. This keeps rent low while giving you access to foot traffic. For most beginners, a small shop or a shared space in a busy area is the best choice.

## Step 3: Work Out Your Startup Capital

How much you need depends on your location, your tools, and whether you rent a shop.

Tools are your first big cost. Screwdrivers, tweezers, spudgers, a hot air rework station, a soldering iron, a multimeter, a power supply, and a microscope or magnifying lamp can cost between ₦150,000 and ₦400,000 depending on quality. Do not buy the cheapest tools you can find. Cheap screwdrivers strip screws and damage phones. Buy mid-range tools that will last.

Spare parts are your second cost. Start with fast-moving parts: screens for popular Tecno, Infinix, Samsung, and iPhone models, batteries, charging ports, charging flex cables, and back covers. A starting stock can cost between ₦200,000 and ₦500,000. Buy from trusted suppliers and keep records of what sells fastest so you do not tie up money in slow-moving parts.

Shop rent varies widely. In a busy Lagos market, a small shop can cost ₦300,000 to ₦800,000 per year. In smaller towns, the same size shop may cost ₦100,000 to ₦250,000 per year. Many landlords ask for one or two years upfront, so confirm the payment terms before you commit. Add money for a workbench, chairs, a glass display counter, and basic shop fitting, which may cost ₦100,000 to ₦200,000.

A realistic total for a small starter shop is between ₦700,000 and ₦1,500,000. If that is too much at once, start smaller, as a mobile technician or in a shared space with about ₦300,000 to ₦500,000 for tools and parts, then save towards your own shop. Keep a separate record of every naira you spend at the start, using a notebook or a simple spreadsheet on your phone.

## Step 4: Buy the Right Tools

Your tools are your workshop. Buy them once, buy them well, and take care of them.

- Precision screwdrivers for different phone models
- Plastic opening tools, spudgers, and suction cups
- Tweezers, straight and curved, for handling small parts
- A hot air rework station for chips and ports
- A soldering iron and good solder for board work
- A digital multimeter for testing batteries and circuits
- A DC power supply for testing phones without a battery
- A microscope or magnifying lamp for small board components
- An ultrasonic cleaner for water-damaged phones
- A screen separator machine for frequent screen replacements
- An anti-static mat and wrist strap to protect phone boards

Buy your tools from trusted sellers in Computer Village or other major phone markets, and test a tool before you pay if possible. Keep your tools organised on your workbench. As you earn more, upgrade gradually. A better hot air station, a good microscope, and a proper screen separator will let you handle more complex jobs and charge higher fees.

## Step 5: Source Spare Parts You Can Trust

Spare parts quality is one of the biggest challenges in Nigerian phone repair. There are original parts, good-quality copies, and very poor copies that fail after a few weeks. Your reputation depends on choosing well.

Build relationships with two or three trusted suppliers in Computer Village, Ikeja, or in the main phone market in your city. In Onitsha, the main market has many phone parts dealers. In Abuja, Wuse Market and Banex Plaza are popular. Buy small quantities first, test the parts, and only increase your orders when you are sure of the quality.

Learn to tell the difference between grades of screens. A cheap screen may look fine on the day of repair but develop touch problems later. A better screen costs more but lasts longer and produces fewer complaints. Explain the difference to your customers and let them choose.

Always keep fast-moving parts in stock. If a customer comes with a broken Tecno Spark screen and you have to tell them to come back in three days because you have no screen, they may go to another shop and never return. Keep a simple stock list on paper or on your phone, and order before you run out completely. Negotiate with your suppliers, and do not buy parts you do not need just because they are cheap. Dead stock is money sitting on a shelf.

## Step 6: Choose a Good Location

Location can make or break a phone repair business. The best locations are places where people already go with phone problems: busy markets, areas near schools, busy junctions, and streets with many phone accessory shops.

Look for a shop with good foot traffic, easy access, and security. Customers want to leave an expensive phone with someone they trust. A shop in a dark, hidden corner will struggle, even if the rent is cheap. A small shop in a busy area is better than a big shop nobody can find.

Before you rent, visit the area at different times of day. Watch how many people pass by and count how many phone shops are already there. Competition is not always bad. In a phone market, customers come because they know many repairers are there. But if there are already ten repairers on one small street, you will need a clear reason for customers to choose you, such as faster service, better prices, or a specialisation. Check the power situation too. Phone repair needs steady electricity, so if the area has poor power supply, budget for a small generator or a solar setup.

## Step 7: Set Your Prices Honestly

Pricing is where many new technicians make mistakes. Price too low and you cannot buy good parts or pay rent. Price too high and customers will go elsewhere. Study what other repairers in your area charge for common jobs. Screen replacement, battery replacement, charging port repair, and software flashing have fairly standard price ranges in each city. Use those prices as a guide, then adjust for the quality of parts you use and the complexity of the job.

Always diagnose before you quote a price. Open the phone, check the fault, and tell the customer what is wrong and how much it will cost before you start work. Do not quote a low price to attract the customer and then add extra charges halfway through the job. That destroys trust. Give the customer options where possible, for example a standard screen at one price and a higher-quality screen at a higher price, and explain the difference clearly. Write the agreed price on a receipt so there is no argument later. Keep a price list in your shop for common services. A printed list makes you look professional and helps your apprentice or staff quote correctly when you are not around.

## Step 8: Register Your Business and Keep Simple Records

You do not need to register on the first day, but as soon as you have a shop and regular customers, register your business name with the Corporate Affairs Commission. A registered name makes your shop look more serious, helps you open a business bank account, and makes it easier to work with corporate customers or suppliers who need an invoice.

Open a separate bank account or mobile money wallet for the business. Do not mix business money with personal money. Use OPay, PalmPay, or your bank app to receive payments, and keep a record of every payment you receive.

Keep a job register. For every phone you receive, write down the customer's name, phone number, phone model, fault, agreed price, deposit paid, and date. Give the customer a receipt with the same details. Track your income and expenses weekly so you can see whether your business is growing or whether you are spending your capital without realising it.

## Step 9: Attract Customers and Build Trust

In phone repair, trust is your best advertisement. Most customers come through referrals. If you fix one person's phone well, they will tell their friends, family, and colleagues. Start by telling everyone you know that you repair phones.

Use WhatsApp well. Create a WhatsApp Business account with your shop name, services, location, and opening hours. Post your work, such as before-and-after photos of a repaired phone, with the customer's permission. When a customer messages you, reply quickly and politely. Use Google Business Profile to list your shop so people searching for a phone repairer near them can find you, and ask happy customers to leave a review.

Offer a short warranty on your repairs, for example one or two weeks on screen replacements and charging port repairs, covering faults caused by your work, not new damage by the customer. Write the warranty terms on the receipt. Be honest about what you cannot fix. If a phone has a serious board problem you cannot handle, refer the customer to a specialist rather than experimenting on their phone.

## Step 10: Handle Customers Professionally

How you treat customers matters as much as your technical skill. Greet customers warmly and listen carefully when they describe the problem. Explain the fault in simple language, give a realistic repair time, and do not promise thirty minutes for a job that needs three hours. Protect customer data: never go through a customer's photos, messages, or files, and warn them before any reset so they can back up important files. Handle complaints calmly. If a repair fails within warranty, check the phone again without arguing and fix it if the fault was caused by your work or a faulty part.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

Do not start without proper training. Watching a few videos online is not enough. Real phones are delicate, and one mistake can damage a customer's phone beyond repair. Do not buy fake parts to save money. Cheap parts lead to repeat complaints, refunds, and a bad reputation. It is better to charge a fair price for a good part than to charge a low price for a part that fails in two weeks. Do not take on jobs you cannot do. It is better to refer a complex board repair to a specialist than to damage the phone and lose the customer. Do not spend your capital on personal needs. Keep business money separate and pay yourself a fixed amount each week or month, like a salary.

To grow later, train apprentices, sell accessories such as chargers, earphones, cases, and screen guards, and consider selling tested fairly used phones with a short warranty. You can also specialise, for example as the best iPhone repairer in your area, or the best at board-level repairs. Specialisation lets you charge higher fees because customers seek you out for difficult jobs other repairers cannot handle.

## Final Checklist Before You Open

Before you open, run through this checklist. Have you completed your training and practised enough to handle common repairs confidently? Do you have your basic tools and a starting stock of spare parts? Have you chosen a location with good foot traffic and steady power? Do you have a price list, a job register, and receipts? Do you have a WhatsApp Business account and a simple way for customers to find you and pay you?

If you can answer yes to most of these questions, you are ready to start. You do not need to have everything perfect on day one. A phone repair business rewards skill, honesty, and patience. The first months may be slow as you build a name. Keep showing up, keep learning new phone models, and keep treating every customer fairly. Over time, your reputation and your customer base will grow, and your small repair shop can become a steady source of income.
