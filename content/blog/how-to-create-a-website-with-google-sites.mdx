Every business needs a place on the internet where customers can find it, but many small business owners in Nigeria assume that building a website means hiring a developer and spending a lot of money. That is true for a custom website, but it is not the only option. Google Sites is a free tool from Google that lets you build a simple website by dragging and dropping — no coding, no hosting fees, no technical background required. If you can use Google Docs, you can build a Google Site.

A Google Sites website is not the right choice for every business. It will not run an online store with a shopping cart, and it does not offer the design freedom of a professionally built site. But for a small business that needs a clean online presence — your services, your prices, your contact details, a few photos — it is a genuine, working website that you can build yourself in an afternoon. This guide walks you through the whole process, from opening Google Sites to publishing your finished site, and explains honestly where its limits are.

## What Google Sites Is and What It Can Do

Google Sites is a website builder that comes free with a Google account. You do not need to buy hosting or a domain name to start, and you do not install anything — it runs entirely in your web browser. You build your site by adding sections, text boxes, images and buttons, and moving them around on the page. When you are done, you click Publish and Google gives your site a web address that anyone can visit.

A typical small business site built on Google Sites has a home page with a short introduction and a photo, a page describing your services or products, a page with your prices, an about page, and a contact page with your phone number, WhatsApp link and location. You can also add a photo gallery, embed a Google Map showing where you are, embed a YouTube video, and add a contact form that sends enquiries straight to your email. For many small businesses — a tailor, a caterer, a salon, a school, a church, a consultancy — that is everything a customer needs.

Google Sites also works well with the rest of Google's tools. You can embed a Google Form for orders or bookings, a Google Calendar showing your availability, a Google Sheet with your price list, or a Google Doc with your brochure. Because everything lives in your Google account, you can update your site from any device, and more than one person can edit it if you share it with them. Your site is also hosted on Google's servers, so it loads reliably and comes with a secure connection automatically.

## What You Need Before You Begin

All you need is a Google account — the same Gmail account you use for email — and a web browser on a computer. Google Sites works best on a laptop or desktop computer, because dragging sections around is much easier with a mouse than on a phone screen. You can view and make small edits on a phone, but do the main building work on a computer if you can borrow one.

Before you open Google Sites, gather your content. Write down in a notebook or a document: your business name, a one or two sentence description of what you do, the list of your services or products with prices, your phone number and WhatsApp number, your email address, your location, and your opening hours. Also gather a few good photos of your work, your shop, your products or yourself. A website with real photos of real work is far more convincing than one with only text. Make sure your photos are clear, well lit, and show what you actually do.

Think about your site's structure too. Most small business sites need five or six pages at most: Home, About, Services or Products, Prices, Gallery, and Contact. Write the headings for each page before you start building, so you are not staring at a blank screen wondering what to write. Keep the writing short and plain — customers scan websites, they do not read them like novels. One short paragraph and a few bullet points per page is usually enough.

## Creating Your Site Step by Step

Go to sites.google.com and sign in with your Google account. Click the plus button or the "Blank" option to start a new site. Google will open the editor with an empty page. The first thing to do is give your site a name: click on the title at the top left, where it says "Untitled site", and type your business name. This name appears in the browser tab when people visit your site, so use your real business name, not a nickname.

Next, choose a theme. On the right side of the editor, click the Themes tab. You will see several ready-made designs with different colours and fonts. Pick one that suits your business — a calm, professional look for a consultancy or law firm, something warmer for a food or fashion business. Do not spend too long on this; you can change the theme later and all your content will stay in place. You can also adjust the colours and fonts to match your brand if you have one.

Now build your home page. The editor shows a header area at the top of the page, where you can add a large title and a short line of text — for example, your business name and a line like "Quality catering for weddings and events in Lagos". You can add a background image to this header, such as a photo of your best work. Below the header, click the plus button to add a section, and choose a layout: a text box, an image, or a text-and-image combination. Type your introduction into the text box — two or three sentences about what you do and who you serve. Add a button that links to your contact page or your WhatsApp number, so visitors can reach you in one click.

## Adding Pages and Content

Your home page is only the start. To add more pages, click the Pages tab on the right side of the editor, then the plus button at the bottom. Give each page a clear name — "Our Services", "Prices", "About Us", "Contact Us" — and Google Sites will add it to your site's menu automatically. Visitors will see these page names at the top of your site and can click between them.

On each page, use the same building blocks. For a Services page, add a section for each service with a short description and its price. For a Prices page, you can add a table: click the insert option, add a table, and list your items and prices in neat rows. For a Gallery page, add an image carousel or a grid of photos so visitors can browse your work. For an About page, write a few sentences about your experience and why customers should trust you, and add a photo of yourself or your team if you are comfortable with that.

To add an image, click the plus button and choose Image, then upload a photo from your computer or pick one from Google Drive. After the image is on the page, click it to resize it or add a caption. Keep your images reasonably sized — very large photos slow your site down — and use your own photos rather than pictures copied from the internet, which may belong to someone else. If you need extra images, use a free stock photo site and choose pictures that genuinely match your business.

You can also embed useful tools. To add a map of your location, insert a Google Map and type your business address. To take enquiries, insert a Google Form — you can create a simple form with fields for name, phone number and message, and the answers will arrive in your email and in a spreadsheet. To add a video, insert a YouTube video of your work or a short introduction. Each of these takes only a few clicks, and they make your site far more useful to visitors.

## Settings, Preview and Publishing

Before you publish, check your site's settings. Click the gear icon at the top of the editor to open Settings. You can add a short description of your site, which search engines may show when people find you on Google. You can also add a small logo or icon, called a favicon, which appears in the browser tab next to your site's name. These small details make your site look more professional.

Now preview your site. Click the eye icon at the top of the editor to see how your site will look to visitors. Check it on a computer screen, a tablet and a phone — Google Sites lets you switch between these views in the preview. Read every page carefully and look for spelling mistakes, photos that are the wrong way round, and buttons that do not go anywhere. Ask a friend or family member to look at it too; a fresh pair of eyes catches mistakes you have read past ten times.

When you are happy, click Publish at the top right. Google will ask you to choose a web address for your site. The free address will look something like sites.google.com/view/yourbusinessname. You can change the last part to something short and easy to remember, but only if it has not been taken by someone else. Click Publish again and your site is live — anyone with the address can visit it. You can keep editing after publishing; just click Publish again after each change to make the update live.

If you already own a domain name, such as yourbusiness.com.ng, you can connect it to your Google Site in the settings under Custom Domain. This makes your address shorter and more professional. The process involves changing a setting with your domain provider, so if you find that step confusing, ask the person who helped you buy the domain, or keep the free Google address for now — it works perfectly well.

## The Honest Limits of Google Sites

Google Sites is a good starting point, but you should know its limits so you are not disappointed later. The design options are limited: you cannot change the layout freely the way a developer can, and every Google Site looks somewhat similar. If your business depends on standing out visually — a high-end fashion brand, for example — a custom website will serve you better in the long run.

Google Sites also cannot run a real online store. You cannot add a shopping cart, take payments directly on the site, or manage stock. The workaround is to list your products with prices and add a WhatsApp button or an order form, so customers message you to buy. That works well for many small businesses, but if you want customers to pay on the site itself, you will need a different platform.

There are other limits worth knowing. Google Sites does not let you add your own analytics tracking in the same flexible way as other platforms, and its search engine optimisation options are basic — you can add page titles and descriptions, but you have little control beyond that. The site is also tied to your Google account: if you lose access to that account, you lose the ability to edit your site. Keep your account secure with a strong password and two-factor authentication, and make sure more than one trusted person knows how to reach it if needed.

None of this makes Google Sites a bad choice. It makes it a first step. Many businesses start with a free Google Site, use it to get customers and prove the business works, and later invest in a professional website once the money is coming in. A simple live website that customers can find beats a perfect website that does not exist yet.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Google Sites really free?

Yes. Building and publishing a site on Google Sites costs nothing, and Google hosts it for you at no charge. You only pay if you choose to buy your own domain name to use as a custom address, and even that is optional — the free address Google gives you works fine.

### Do I need to know how to code to use Google Sites?

No. Google Sites is designed for people with no coding experience. You build pages by choosing sections, typing text, uploading images and clicking buttons. Everything is done with the mouse, and you can see the result as you work. If you can use Google Docs or send an email, you can build a Google Site.

### Can customers find my Google Site on Google Search?

Yes, your site can appear in Google search results, though it may take some days after publishing before Google lists it. To help, give your pages clear titles that describe your business and location, write a short description in the site settings, and share your site's link on your WhatsApp status, Facebook page and business cards so people visit it. A site that people visit and link to is easier for Google to find.

### Can I add a contact form to my Google Site?

Yes. The easiest way is to create a Google Form with the questions you want — name, phone number, message — and then insert that form into your contact page. When a visitor fills it in, the answers are sent to your email and saved in a spreadsheet in your Google Drive. You can also simply put your phone number and a WhatsApp link on the page, which many Nigerian customers prefer.

### What is the difference between Google Sites and a professional website?

A professional website is built for your business from scratch, so it can look exactly how you want, include features like online payments, and be optimised to rank well on Google. Google Sites is a free template-based tool with limited design and features. Think of it as the difference between a ready-made suit and one sewn by a tailor: the ready-made one is affordable and works well, but the tailored one fits perfectly.

### Can I move my Google Site to another platform later?

There is no automatic way to move a Google Site to another platform — you would rebuild the content on the new platform. This is not a big problem if your site is small, since you can copy your text and download your photos. It is one more reason to treat Google Sites as a starting point: build it, use it, and when your business grows, move to a professional website and take your content with you.

### How long does it take to build a website with Google Sites?

If you have your text and photos ready, you can build a simple five-page site in one afternoon — usually three to five hours for a first attempt. Most of that time goes into writing your content and choosing photos, not into learning the tool. Take your time with the words and the pictures; they matter more than any setting in the editor.

Share the link with two or three people you trust, ask them what is unclear, fix those things, and publish again. Within a week you will have a real website that customers can find.
