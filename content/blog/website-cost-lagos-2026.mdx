"₦50,000 or ₦2,000,000 — why such a huge range?"

We hear this question almost every week. A business owner searches for "website cost Lagos" and finds prices ranging from the price of a decent lunch to the cost of a used car. Both numbers are real quotes from real developers in Lagos. So which one is right for your business?

The short answer: it depends on what you are buying. Here is the long answer.

## The 5 Factors That Determine What You Actually Pay

### 1. Number of Pages

A single-page landing page is fundamentally different from a 15-page business website with service pages, an about section, a blog, and a careers page. More pages means more design, more content, more development, and more testing.

A 5-page site (Home, About, Services, Contact, Blog) is the most common starting point for Lagos small businesses. A 15+ page site for a larger company or an e-commerce store with product pages is a different project entirely.

### 2. Custom Design vs Template

A pre-built template that gets customised with your colours and logo is significantly cheaper than a fully custom design built from scratch. Templates work well for many businesses, but if you need a unique brand identity, a specific user flow, or complex functionality, custom design is the way to go.

The difference is usually 2-3x in cost. We use templates as starting points for budget-conscious clients and go fully custom for brands that need to stand out.

### 3. E-commerce and Payment Integration

Adding an online store changes everything. You need product pages, a shopping cart, a checkout flow, payment gateway integration (Paystack, Flutterwave, or direct bank transfer), inventory management, and order tracking.

A basic e-commerce site costs roughly double what a comparable informational site costs. A full-featured online store with multiple payment options, automated invoicing, and inventory sync can cost 3-4x more.

### 4. Ongoing Maintenance and Hosting

Many first-time buyers forget that a website has ongoing costs. Quality hosting (not the cheapest shared plan), a custom domain name (renewed annually), SSL certificate, security updates, and regular backups all add up.

At Joetech, we are transparent about these costs upfront because we do not want you to be surprised six months later. A well-maintained site stays fast, secure, and visible on Google. A neglected site becomes a liability.

### 5. Content — Copywriting, Photography, and Brand Assets

A website needs words and images. Professional copywriting that sells your services, original photos of your team and workspace, and polished brand assets (logo, colour palette, typography) all cost money.

Many developers do not include content in their quotes. When we quote a project, we break down what is included and what is optional, so you know exactly what you are paying for.

## Why It Is Not Just a Build Cost

When someone quotes you ₦80,000 for a website, here is what is probably not included:

- A custom domain name (₦8,000 — ₦15,000/year)
- Reliable hosting (₦50,000 — ₦150,000/year for quality)
- SSL certificate (often free, but some hosts charge)
- Ongoing security updates and backups
- SEO setup (meta tags, schema markup, Google Search Console)
- Mobile responsiveness testing
- Post-launch support

We have taken over sites built on ₦80,000 budgets. They were slow, insecure, broke on mobile, and invisible on Google. The owner ended up paying more to fix the problems than they would have paying for quality work upfront.

## Red Flags to Watch For

- **A quote that seems too cheap** — Ask what is excluded. If the answer is vague, walk away.
- **No portfolio of real Lagos/Nigerian sites** — Anyone can show you beautiful international templates. Ask to see sites they have built for businesses like yours.
- **No discussion of maintenance** — If they do not bring up hosting, updates, and support, they are either inexperienced or planning to disappear after launch.
- **"One week delivery" for a complex site** — Quick turnarounds are possible for simple sites. A custom e-commerce store takes time. Rushed work cuts corners.
