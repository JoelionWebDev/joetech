Three business owners in Lagos asked for a website in the same month. A salon owner in Surulere paid ₦90,000. A pharmacy owner in Ikeja paid ₦380,000. A law firm on Victoria Island paid ₦1,400,000. All three got "a website." None of them got the same thing.

That is the first fact to understand about Lagos website prices in 2026: the number alone tells you almost nothing. The route you take — do it yourself, hire a freelancer, or hire an agency — decides what the number includes, how long it takes, and who you call when something breaks on a Saturday. This article walks all three routes with real Lagos naira figures, then covers the extras that catch first-time buyers by surprise.

## Route one: do it yourself

DIY is the cheapest route in cash and the most expensive route in time. A business owner who builds their own site typically spends ₦0 to ₦120,000 in the first year. The money goes to a domain name (₦10,000–₦40,000 a year for a .com), hosting (₦30,000–₦80,000 a year for a decent shared plan), and possibly a premium template or a builder subscription (₦0–₦60,000). The tools themselves — WordPress, Wix, Squarespace — are either free or cheap. The real cost is the 20 to 40 hours you will spend learning the builder, writing the words, finding the pictures, and fixing the layout that looks fine on your laptop and broken on your phone.

DIY suits one kind of Lagos business well: a small operation with more time than money, a simple offer, and an owner who is comfortable with technology. A hair vendor in Yaba selling five products through WhatsApp can build a one-page site in a weekend and it will do the job. A tailor in Surulere with a lookbook and a booking form can manage it too, if she is patient.

It suits another kind badly: any business where the website has to persuade a stranger to part with serious money. A law firm, a school, a hotel — these buyers judge you by the site before they ever call. A DIY site built at 2am after a long day rarely clears that bar. It also has a hidden cost that shows up months later. When the site breaks, or Google stops showing it, or a customer says the contact form never delivered their message, there is nobody to call. You are the developer now.

A fair rule: if your website needs to earn trust on its own, DIY is a stepping stone, not a destination. Build it, learn what you want, then pay someone to build it properly when the money comes in.

## Route two: the freelancer

The freelancer is the middle route, and in Lagos it is the most common one. A solid freelancer charges ₦150,000 to ₦600,000 for a small business website. The range is wide because the work is wide. At the lower end, around ₦150,000 to ₦250,000, you are buying a clean template-based site of five or so pages: home, about, services, contact, and a blog or gallery. At the upper end, ₦400,000 to ₦600,000, you are buying custom design, a booking form or an online store, proper SEO setup, and a freelancer who will still answer your messages a month after launch.

Where do you find them? Referrals first — ask a business owner whose site you like who built it. Then Twitter and LinkedIn, where Lagos developers show their work in public. Upwork and similar platforms work too, but a local freelancer you can meet in Ikeja or Lekki has one advantage no platform gives you: you can sit in the same room and look at the site together.

The freelancer route has real strengths. You deal with one person, decisions are fast, and the price is usually half of an agency's for similar work. The pharmacy in Ikeja that paid ₦380,000 got a seven-page site with a product catalogue, a WhatsApp order button, Google Maps, and basic SEO — built by one developer in three weeks, with two rounds of changes included. That is a good freelancer outcome, and it is typical of the route when it works.

The risks are personal. A freelancer is one person, and one person gets sick, travels, or takes on too much work. Ask three questions before you pay anything. Can I see three live sites you built for Lagos businesses like mine? What exactly is included — design, content, hosting, support — and what costs extra? And who owns the domain and the site files?

Be cautious below ₦100,000. At that price a freelancer is either very new and building a portfolio, or selling you a template with your logo pasted in and no support after. The first case can be a bargain if you go in with open eyes. The second case is the site that costs ₦90,000 to build and ₦250,000 to fix.

## Route three: the agency

An agency is a team, and you pay for the team. A small Lagos agency charges ₦350,000 to ₦1,500,000 for a business website, and ₦1,500,000 to ₦3,500,000 for custom work like a full e-commerce store, a booking platform, or a multi-branch site. The law firm on Victoria Island that paid ₦1,400,000 got custom design, fifteen pages of content written for them, a client portal, SEO setup, and a support contract. That is the top of the small-business range, and for a firm whose clients pay millions in fees, it was a reasonable spend.

What the extra money buys is not just design. It buys a process. An agency will usually start with a proper conversation about your business, produce a design for you to approve before anything is built, test the site on real phones, and hand it over with training. It buys reliability too. If one designer is away, another picks up the work. If the site goes down, there is a support line, not a single WhatsApp number that may or may not reply.

The agency route suits businesses where the website is a serious sales channel, or where the owner has no time to manage a project. A hotel in Lekki, a school with a hundred parents checking the site, a real estate company listing properties — these businesses need the site to work every day, and they need someone accountable when it does not.

The risk with agencies is paying agency prices for freelancer work. A small "agency" of two people working from a flat in Yaba can charge ₦900,000 and deliver what a ₦300,000 freelancer would have built. Protect yourself the same way: see live sites they built, ask who exactly will work on your project, and get the scope in writing.

## The Lagos extras nobody quotes you

Whatever route you take, three extras decide the final bill, and they are almost never in the first quote. Lagos buyers who budget for them are the ones who do not feel cheated at handover.

Photography is the first. A website full of stock photos of smiling strangers looks like every other website. Real photos of your shop, your food, your team, your building — those are what make a Lagos business believable online. A proper half-day shoot with a Lagos photographer costs ₦80,000 to ₦250,000, depending on the photographer and the number of shots. A hotel in Lekki that paid ₦150,000 for a shoot got room photos, food photos, and staff photos that now carry the whole site.

Content is the second. The words on the site are a separate job from building it, and developers who do not write will either leave you with placeholder text or charge for writing as an add-on. Professional copywriting for a five-page site runs ₦50,000 to ₦150,000 in Lagos. The cheaper alternative is writing it yourself, which is honest work but slow — most projects stall here, not in the coding. When a developer quotes you, ask one direct question: does this include writing my pages, or do I supply the text? The answer changes the price by six figures, and it is better to know now than in week three.

Delivery is the third, and it is the one people never think to ask about. Delivery means: who owns the domain, who holds the hosting account, who holds the passwords, and will someone show you how to update the site yourself? A proper handover takes an hour — a walkthrough on a video call, a short written guide, and the login details in your hands. Some developers charge for training as an add-on, ₦25,000 to ₦50,000. Some disappear after launch and charge you ₦15,000 every time you want a phone number changed. Agree the handover before you pay the deposit, and put it in writing: domain in your name, hosting account in your email, passwords handed over at launch.

There is a smaller Lagos extra worth naming: the meeting itself. Lagos runs on relationships and traffic in equal measure. A developer who will come to your shop in Ikeja or your office in Lekki, look at the business, and talk through the site in person is worth more than one who only works over WhatsApp from another state.

## How to choose, with your actual budget

If you have under ₦100,000, build it yourself or hire a beginner freelancer for a simple one-page site — and go in knowing it is a starting point, not a finished asset.

If you have ₦150,000 to ₦400,000, a freelancer is usually your best value. You get a proper multi-page site, a real person to work with, and money left over for photography or content.

Whatever your budget, get the quote in writing, with the scope, the timeline, the payment stages, and the handover spelled out. Pay in stages — half to start is normal, the balance on launch, never all of it upfront. And keep the Lagos rule in mind: a developer with three live sites for businesses like yours, who will meet you and answer questions, is worth more than a beautiful promise from someone you have never seen.
