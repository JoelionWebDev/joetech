A customer finds your Instagram page from a friend's story or a hashtag. They tap your profile, and they have about five seconds to decide whether to follow you, message you, or leave. Your last nine posts tell them something, but your Highlights tell them everything: what you sell, what it costs, how to order, and whether other Nigerians trust you. If your Highlights are empty, messy, or full of random reposts, that customer leaves — and you never know they were there.

Instagram Highlights are the circles sitting just below your bio. They are saved Stories that never disappear after 24 hours, and they work like a shop window that never closes. In Nigeria, where most small business sales start on Instagram and finish on WhatsApp, Highlights do the job of a salesperson: they answer questions, show proof, and guide a stranger from curiosity to payment, even at 2am while you are asleep.

This guide shows you how to use Instagram Highlights to sell more in Nigeria — what categories to create, what to put inside each one, how to design covers that look professional, and how to keep them fresh so they keep converting visitors into customers.

## What Instagram Highlights Are and Why They Sell

Highlights are collections of Stories you choose to keep on your profile permanently. Ordinary Stories vanish after a day. A Highlight stays until you remove it. Each Highlight has a cover image and a name, and it can hold as many Story slides as you want. You can have several Highlights side by side, each one covering a different topic.

Why do they matter for sales? Because Nigerian customers buy from people they trust, and trust needs evidence. A new visitor cannot tell from one post whether you are reliable. But a Highlight full of customer reviews, delivery videos, and unboxing clips answers that question in seconds. Highlights also answer the questions every buyer asks before messaging you: How much is it? Do you deliver to Abuja? How do I pay? Can I pay on delivery? When those answers sit visibly on your profile, fewer people message just to ask, and more people message ready to buy.

Highlights also save you time. Instead of answering "How do I order?" twenty times a day on WhatsApp, you point people to your "How to Order" Highlight. Instead of explaining your delivery areas again and again, your "Delivery" Highlight does it for you.

## Fix Your Profile Before You Build Highlights

Highlights sit on your profile, so the profile itself must be ready first. If your bio is confusing or your profile picture is blurry, even perfect Highlights will struggle. Start with a clear profile photo — your logo, sharp and readable at small size, or a clean photo of you if you are the face of the brand. Avoid a photo with tiny text nobody can read on a phone.

Write a bio that says what you sell, who it is for, and where you deliver, in two or three short lines. For example: "Quality female shoes in Lagos | Sizes 37–43 | Nationwide delivery | DM or WhatsApp to order." Add your location, such as Lekki, Lagos, and a WhatsApp link so a visitor can reach you in one tap.

Switch to a professional or creator account if you have not already. It is free, and it gives you insights that show how many people view your Highlights and tap through to your WhatsApp link. That data tells you which Highlight sells and which one nobody opens. Also turn on Story archiving in your settings. Without it, your Stories disappear after 24 hours and you cannot add them to Highlights later. Go to Settings, then Privacy, then Story, and make sure saving to archive is switched on.

## Plan Your Highlight Categories First

Do not create Highlights at random. Plan them like the sections of a shop. Most Nigerian small businesses need between five and eight Highlights. Fewer than that leaves questions unanswered. More than that looks cluttered, and visitors will not tap through them all.

Here are the categories that work for most businesses selling on Instagram in Nigeria. Start with "Shop" or "Available" — your current products, with prices clearly shown. Follow with "Prices" or "Price List" if your catalogue is large. Add "Reviews" for screenshots of happy customers and their messages. Add "How to Order" with the exact steps, from choosing an item to paying and receiving delivery. Add "Delivery" with your areas, fees, and delivery times — Lagos, Abuja, Port Harcourt, nationwide. Add "About" or "Meet Us" so people see the human behind the business. If you run sales or new arrivals often, add "New In" and update it weekly.

A food vendor in Surulere might use: Menu, Prices, Reviews, How to Order, Delivery, and Events. A fashion seller in Lekki might use: New In, Dresses, Shoes, Reviews, Price List, How to Order, and Delivery. Choose the set that matches what your customers ask about most. If you are unsure, check your WhatsApp and Instagram DMs from the last two weeks and list the questions that repeat. Those questions become your Highlights.

## How to Create a Highlight, Step by Step

Creating a Highlight takes less than a minute once your Stories are archived. Open your profile and tap the plus sign or the "New" circle under your bio. Instagram will show your archived Stories. Pick the ones you want in this Highlight, tap Next, and give it a short name — two words at most, because long names get cut off on a phone screen. Then tap Add, and your Highlight is live.

You can also create one from a Story you just posted. While viewing your own Story, tap the Highlight button at the bottom and choose an existing Highlight or create a new one. To add more slides later, open the Highlight, tap the three dots, and choose Edit Highlight. From there you can add or remove slides and change the cover. To delete a Highlight, open it, tap the three dots, and choose Delete Highlight. To reorder your Highlights, you cannot drag them directly, but adding a new Story to an older Highlight moves it to the front, so you can control the order that way.

A practical tip: create your Highlights from Stories that are already well designed, with clear text and good photos. If your old Stories are messy or hard to read, make fresh ones. A Highlight is a permanent part of your profile, so it deserves better than a rushed Story from three months ago. Take an hour, create clean Story slides for each category, and build your Highlights from those.

## Design Covers That Make People Tap

The cover is the first thing a visitor sees, and it decides whether they tap or scroll past. A good cover is simple, bold, and easy to read at a tiny size. Use one strong colour that matches your brand — bright blue, deep green, rich purple — with a short word or a simple icon on it. Avoid covers with long sentences, small text, or busy photos. At the size they appear on a phone, nobody can read them.

Keep all your covers in the same style. Same background colour, same font, same layout, different word or icon. That consistency makes your profile look professional and trustworthy, like a real brand. You can design covers for free in Canva on your phone: search for Instagram Highlight cover templates, pick one style, change the colours to your brand colours, and export each cover as an image.

Your cover words should be short and clear: Shop, Prices, Reviews, Order, Delivery, About, New In. Avoid clever words that confuse people. A visitor should understand each Highlight without tapping it. If you sell in Nigeria, keep the words simple and direct — your customers are scrolling fast, often on a bus or during a lunch break, and clarity beats cleverness every time.

## What to Put Inside Each Highlight

A Highlight with a beautiful cover and nothing useful inside is worse than no Highlight at all. Each one should answer a question and move the visitor closer to buying. Here is what to put inside the most important ones.

Your Shop or Available Highlight should show your products with prices on every slide. One product per slide, a clear photo, the name, the price in naira, and the sizes or options available. Update it when items sell out, and remove sold-out products so customers do not try to order what you no longer have. Nothing frustrates a buyer more than paying for an item that is out of stock.

Your Reviews Highlight is your most powerful sales tool. Save screenshots of WhatsApp chats where customers say they love the product, photos of customers wearing or using what they bought, and short video clips of unboxings or deliveries. Blur or crop out private details like phone numbers and addresses before posting. Add new reviews regularly — a Reviews Highlight with two screenshots from last year looks abandoned. Aim to add at least one fresh review every week.

Your How to Order Highlight should show the exact steps, one per slide: choose your item, send a screenshot on WhatsApp, confirm availability, pay via bank transfer or Paystack, and receive your delivery. Say clearly whether you accept pay on delivery — many Nigerian buyers ask this first.

Your Delivery Highlight should list where you deliver, the fees, and how long it takes. For example: delivery within Lagos takes one to two days and costs from ₦2,000 depending on location; Abuja and Port Harcourt take two to three days; nationwide delivery is available. Add photos or short clips of packaged orders and riders collecting them — proof that deliveries actually happen.

Your About Highlight should show the human side. A short video of you in your shop, your team packing orders, or your products being made. Nigerian customers buy from people they feel they know. A face, a voice, and a real workspace build more trust than a hundred product photos.

## Use Highlights to Answer Questions Before They Are Asked

Every question a customer asks before buying is a small obstacle between you and a sale. Highlights remove those obstacles in advance. Go through your DMs and write down the ten questions you answer most often. Then check: does a Highlight answer each one? If not, make a Story slide that answers it and add it to the right Highlight.

Common questions for Nigerian businesses include: What is your price? Do you deliver to my area? How long will delivery take? Can I pay on delivery? Is this item still available? What is your return policy? Do you have this in another colour or size? Are you real — can I trust you? Each one deserves a clear answer on your profile.

Your Prices Highlight can also reduce haggling. When prices are public, customers who cannot afford an item simply move on, and customers who can afford it message you ready to pay. Some sellers fear that showing prices will drive people away. In practice, hiding prices drives away the serious buyers, who assume the price is high or that they will be cheated. Transparency builds trust, and trust builds sales. If your prices change with the market, update the Highlight the same day you change them.

## Turn Highlight Viewers Into Buyers

Highlights alone do not sell. You must guide people to them and give them a reason to act. Mention your Highlights in your captions and Stories: "Prices are in our Price List Highlight," or "See real customer reviews in our Reviews Highlight." When someone asks a question in your DMs, answer it, then add, "You can also see more in our Delivery Highlight." Each mention teaches followers that your Highlights hold the answers.

Use your Highlights to support promotions too. Running a weekend sale? Add the sale items and prices to a Sale Highlight and mention it in a Story with a link sticker to WhatsApp. Launching a new product? Add it to New In and announce it. When a customer buys from you, ask them to tag you in their Story, then repost it to your Reviews Highlight. That cycle — sell, get tagged, repost, sell more — is one of the cheapest marketing engines a small business can run.

Make the path to payment as short as possible. Your How to Order Highlight should end with a clear call to action: "Tap the WhatsApp link in our bio to order now." Test the journey on a friend's phone — if it takes more than three taps to reach your WhatsApp, simplify it. ## Keep Your Highlights Fresh

A Highlight that never changes slowly stops working. Products sell out, prices change, delivery fees move with fuel costs, and old reviews lose their power. Set a weekly routine — fifteen minutes every Sunday evening, for example — to update your Highlights. Remove sold-out items from Shop, add new arrivals, add the week's best reviews, and check that every price is still correct.

Pay attention to what your insights tell you. Instagram shows how many people view each Highlight and how many tap forward or exit. If a Highlight has many views but people exit quickly, its slides may be boring, too long, or hard to read. Shorten it, improve the photos, or put the most important slide first. If a Highlight has few views, its cover may not be clear enough, or it may sit too far to the right where nobody scrolls.

Delete slides that no longer help you. A promotion from six months ago, a product you no longer stock, a price that has changed — all of these make your business look careless. A tidy profile with five current Highlights sells more than a cluttered one with twelve stale ones. Freshness is a signal: it tells visitors that your business is active, reliable, and paying attention.

## Mistakes That Make Highlights Useless

The first mistake is having no Highlights at all. An empty space under your bio tells visitors there is nothing to see, and they move on. Even three simple Highlights — Shop, Reviews, and How to Order — are far better than none.

The second mistake is using Highlights as a dumping ground for random reposts, memes, and personal content. Every Highlight on a business profile should serve a buyer. Keep personal posts on your personal account, or in a single clearly labelled Highlight if you must. A business profile that mixes jokes, reposts, and products confuses visitors about what you actually sell.

The third mistake is walls of text. A Story slide with ten lines of small writing will not be read, especially on a phone with low brightness or a cracked screen. Keep each slide to a few words, a price, and a clear photo. If something needs a long explanation, break it into several slides, one idea per slide.

The fourth mistake is hiding your prices — hidden prices cost you serious buyers. The fifth is never updating: a Highlights section still advertising a Christmas sale in June tells visitors you have abandoned the page. And the sixth is forgetting the call to action — every Highlight should end with a next step, such as messaging you on WhatsApp.

## Start Building Your Highlights Today

You do not need a designer, a big budget, or a new phone to make Highlights that sell. Open Instagram now and plan your five to eight categories based on the questions your customers ask most. Write your bio clearly, switch on Story archiving, and design simple covers in your brand colours. Then create clean Story slides — one product per slide, prices in naira, real reviews, honest delivery details — and build each Highlight from them.

Give yourself one focused hour today to set up the basics: Shop, Reviews, How to Order, and Delivery. Then add one new review and one new product to your Highlights every week. In a month, your profile will look completely different — like a shop that is open, organised, and ready for business.

In Nigeria, where most customers discover businesses on Instagram and pay on WhatsApp, your Highlights are your shop window, your salesperson, and your customer service desk all in one. Build them well, keep them fresh, and they will sell for you every hour of the day — including the hours you are asleep. Start today, and let your profile do more of the selling for you.
