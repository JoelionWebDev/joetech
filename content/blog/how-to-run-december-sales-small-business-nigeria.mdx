December is the biggest shopping month of the year in Nigeria. Salaries and bonuses land, Nigerians abroad come home for Detty December, families buy new clothes, food, gifts and decorations, and businesses buy hampers and gifts for their clients and staff. For a small business, December can be the month that makes the whole year — or the month where a badly planned promo burns through your stock and your profit and leaves you with nothing to show for it.

A December sales promo is not just about cutting prices. It is about planning the right offers, pricing them so you still make money, promoting them where your customers actually are, and being ready to deliver when the orders come in. This guide walks you through the whole process, from planning in November to counting your profit in January.

## Why December Is Different

December shopping in Nigeria follows a rhythm, and understanding it will help you time your promo. Early December is when organised buyers shop: companies buying hampers and corporate gifts, families buying clothes and food for Christmas, and people buying gifts to send to relatives. Mid-December is the peak: last-minute shoppers, partygoers buying outfits for Detty December events, and travellers buying gifts to take home. The week between Christmas and New Year is quieter for most businesses, except for food, drinks, and party supplies, which keep selling until the 1st of January. The first week of January brings a second wave: New Year sales, as people spend gift money and businesses clear leftover stock.

Your promo should match this rhythm. Start early enough to catch the organised buyers, peak in mid-December when spending is highest, and plan a clearance or New Year offer for the last days of the month. A promo that starts on the 20th of December misses the best two weeks of the month.

## Plan Your Promo in November

The best December promos are planned in November. Start by setting a clear goal. Do you want to clear old stock? Do you want to win new customers? Do you want to increase your sales by a set amount, such as 50 percent more than a normal month? A clear goal shapes every other decision. If your goal is to clear stock, deep discounts on old items make sense. If your goal is to win new customers, a smaller discount plus a bonus gift works better. If your goal is to increase sales, a bundle offer or a buy-one-get-one deal may work better than a flat discount.

Next, choose what to put on promo. Not everything needs a discount. Pick your best sellers, your slow-moving stock, and a few items that make good gifts. A fashion seller might discount last season's styles, bundle a dress with a bag, and offer a gift set. A food seller might offer a Christmas hamper, a bulk discount on soups, and a party pack for events. A skincare brand might offer a gift set, a buy-two-get-one-free deal, and a discount on its most popular product. Choose items you have enough stock of, because nothing kills a promo faster than a popular offer selling out on day one.

Work out your numbers before you announce anything. For each item on promo, write down its cost, its normal price, its promo price, and your profit at the promo price. If the profit is too thin, change the offer: reduce the discount, add a cheaper item to the bundle, or raise the normal price slightly before the promo, which is honest only if the normal price was already planned to rise. Never run a promo at a loss to "attract customers" unless you have a clear plan for how those customers will make you money later. A customer who buys once at a loss and never returns is not a win.

Set a budget for your promo. This includes the cost of any adverts, the cost of packaging and gifts, the cost of extra delivery, and the cost of any staff help you need. A small business can run a good December promo on a budget of ₦20,000 to ₦100,000, mostly spent on WhatsApp and Instagram adverts, flyers, and packaging.

## Choose Offers That Protect Your Profit

The most common December offer is a percentage discount, such as 10, 20, or 30 percent off. Percentage discounts are simple and customers understand them, but they can eat your profit quickly. A 30 percent discount on a product with a 40 percent margin leaves you with almost nothing. Use percentage discounts carefully, and only on items with a healthy margin, or on slow stock you need to clear.

Bundle offers often work better. Instead of discounting one item, sell two or three items together at a special price. A skincare brand can bundle a cleanser, a cream, and a soap for ₦25,000 instead of ₦30,000. A fashion seller can bundle a dress, a bag, and a scarf. A food seller can bundle a hamper with rice, oil, and a gift item. Bundles increase the average amount each customer spends, and they let you include a slow-moving item alongside a popular one.

Buy-one-get-one-free is powerful, but only if the numbers work. It works best on low-cost items with high margins, such as snacks, accessories, or small beauty products. Do not offer buy-one-get-one-free on your most expensive product unless you have worked out the numbers and you are sure you still make a profit.

Free gifts work well in December, because Nigerians love a gift. A small free item with every order over a set amount — a free sample, a small pouch, a Christmas card — costs you little and makes the customer feel valued. A gift with purchase also gives you a reason to raise the minimum order value, which increases your sales.

Free delivery is another strong offer, especially in Lagos where delivery costs are a common reason customers abandon an order. Offer free delivery within your city for orders above a set amount, such as ₦30,000 or ₦50,000. The extra sales usually cover the delivery cost, and customers who would have hesitated will place the order.

Limited-time offers create urgency. A flash sale for 48 hours, a weekend-only discount, or a "first 20 customers" offer gives people a reason to buy now instead of waiting. Make the deadline real — if you say the offer ends on Sunday, end it on Sunday.

## Price Your Promo Honestly

Nigerian customers are smart, and they remember prices. If you raise your prices in November and then offer a "50 percent discount" in December that brings the price back to where it was, customers will notice, and they will tell their friends. Fake discounts destroy trust, and trust is harder to rebuild than profit.

Price your promo honestly. Show the old price and the new price, and make sure the old price was the real price you sold at before the promo. If you are offering a bundle, show the total value of the items and the bundle price. If you are offering a gift, say what the gift is worth. Honest pricing builds a reputation that lasts beyond December, and it is the reason customers come back in January.

Keep your prices simple. Round numbers are easier to remember and easier to pay: ₦10,000, ₦15,000, ₦25,000, ₦50,000. Avoid prices like ₦13,750 that are hard to transfer and hard to remember.

## Promote Your Promo Where Your Customers Are

A promo nobody hears about is a waste of stock and effort. Start promoting at least a week before your promo begins. Build excitement with a countdown on your WhatsApp status and Instagram stories: "Something big is coming on the 1st of December", with a hint of what it is. When the promo starts, announce it clearly, with the dates, the offers, and how to order.

WhatsApp is your most powerful promo channel. Send your promo flyer to your broadcast list, post it on your status every day, and update your catalogue with the promo prices. Use WhatsApp Business labels to track who has seen the promo, who has ordered, and who needs a reminder. A personal message to a regular customer — "Hello Mrs Ade, our December sale starts on Monday, and your favourite soup is 20 percent off" — works better than a generic broadcast to a thousand people.

Instagram is your second channel. Post a clear, attractive flyer with the promo details, and pin it to the top of your profile. Post reels showing the products on offer, the hampers being packed, and customers receiving their orders. Use festive hashtags such as #DettyDecember, #ChristmasInLagos, #LagosChristmas, and #DecemberSales, along with your usual product hashtags. Run a small advert with a budget of ₦2,000 to ₦5,000 a day, targeting people in your city, and send them to your WhatsApp.

Do not forget offline promotion. A banner outside your shop, flyers in your estate, and a mention at your church or community meeting can bring customers that social media never reaches. Tell your neighbours, your market women, and your regular customers about the promo, and ask them to share it. Word of mouth is still the most trusted advertising in Nigeria.

Partner with other businesses. A fashion seller can partner with a makeup artist, a food seller with a drinks seller, a skincare brand with a spa. Cross-promote each other's December offers to your customers, and offer a joint bundle. Partnerships cost nothing and they introduce you to customers you would never reach alone.

## Prepare Your Stock, Your Team, and Your Delivery

A successful promo can triple your normal orders, and if you are not ready, that success becomes a disaster. Before your promo starts, check your stock. Count what you have, order what you need, and make sure your suppliers can deliver on time. December is the busiest month for suppliers too, and prices rise as Christmas approaches, so order early. If you sell food, buy your ingredients in bulk in late November, when prices are lower, and store them properly.

Prepare your packaging. Buy enough packs, bags, boxes, ribbons, and stickers for the extra orders. A December order should look festive: a ribbon, a Christmas sticker, or a small card with a thank-you message costs little and makes the customer feel special. Customers who receive a beautifully packed order in December will remember you in January.

Plan your delivery. Talk to your dispatch riders in advance, agree on prices, and let them know you expect more orders. Have a backup rider in case your main rider is busy or unavailable. For orders outside your city, confirm your courier's December schedule, because some couriers stop accepting parcels a few days before Christmas. Tell your customers the last date you can guarantee delivery before Christmas, and stick to it. An order that arrives on the 27th of December, when the customer needed it on the 23rd, is a failed order.

Get help if you need it. A promo that doubles your orders also doubles your work: more packing, more messages, more deliveries. Ask a family member, a friend, or a neighbour to help for a few days, and pay them fairly. If you have staff, brief them on the promo offers, the prices, and the delivery promises, so that every customer gets the same information, no matter who they talk to.

## Handle Orders, Payments, and Customer Service

During a promo, your phone will be busier than usual, and mistakes are more likely. Set up a simple system before the promo starts. Use WhatsApp Business labels to track each order from enquiry to delivery. Keep a notebook or a spreadsheet of every order: the customer's name, what they ordered, how much they paid, and whether it has been delivered. Check it every evening, so that no order is forgotten.

Take payment before you dispatch. For promo orders, ask for full payment upfront, or at least a deposit of 50 percent, before you pack and send the goods. This protects you from customers who order during a promo and disappear, and it gives you cash to buy more stock. Use Paystack or Flutterwave payment links, or a bank transfer to your business account, and always confirm the payment in your own bank app before you send the goods. For December orders, keep your invoices clear and professional — a free invoice generator at joetech360.ng/tools/invoice-generator can help you create neat invoices with your business name, the items, and the total, so customers have a proper record of what they paid for.

Reply to messages quickly, even during a promo. If you are overwhelmed, send a short message saying you have seen their message and will reply shortly. Customers who feel ignored will buy from a competitor, and December is the month when every competitor is also running a promo.

Handle complaints gracefully. During a busy promo, something will go wrong: a delivery will be late, a size will be wrong, a hamper will be missing an item. When it happens, apologise, fix it quickly, and offer a small gesture, such as a discount on the next order or a free delivery. A customer whose complaint was handled well in December is more likely to return in January than a customer who never had a problem at all.

## Measure Your Results and Plan for January

When the promo ends, do not just move on. Sit down and work out what happened. How much did you sell? How much did it cost you, including the discounts, the adverts, the packaging, and the delivery? How much profit did you make? Which offers sold best, and which ones did not sell at all? Which channel brought the most orders — WhatsApp, Instagram, or a referral? How many new customers did you win, and how many of them ordered more than once?

Write the answers down. They are the most valuable thing your December promo produces, because they tell you what to do next year. If bundles sold better than percentage discounts, plan more bundles next year. If Instagram adverts brought few orders, spend that money on WhatsApp next year. If a particular product sold out in two days, order more of it next December.

Follow up with your new customers in January. Send a thank-you message, ask how they enjoyed the product, and offer them a small discount on their next order. January is a quiet month for most businesses, and a message to a December customer can bring you a January sale.

## Common December Promo Mistakes

The first mistake is discounting too deeply. A 50 percent discount sounds exciting, but if it wipes out your profit, you have worked hard for nothing. Discount with discipline, and protect your margin.

The second mistake is starting too late. A promo that starts on the 20th of December misses the best weeks of the month. Plan in November, announce in late November, and start in early December.

The third mistake is running out of stock. A popular offer that sells out on day one disappoints customers and wastes your advertising. Order early, order enough, and have a plan for what to offer when a popular item runs out.

The fourth mistake is promising delivery you cannot keep. If your courier stops taking parcels on the 22nd, do not promise Christmas delivery on the 23rd. Be honest about your last delivery date, and say it clearly in your promo.

The fifth mistake is forgetting to follow up. A promo that wins a hundred new customers is wasted if you never speak to them again. Keep their details, thank them, and invite them back in January.

## Your December Promo Timeline

Late November: set your goal, choose your offers, work out your numbers, order your stock, and prepare your packaging and flyers.

First week of December: launch your promo. Announce it on WhatsApp, Instagram, and Facebook. Start your adverts. Post daily.

Mid-December: this is your peak. Push your best offers, run a flash sale for a weekend, and remind customers of your last delivery date before Christmas.

Week of Christmas: fulfil your orders, deliver on time, and thank your customers. Share photos of your deliveries and your happy customers.

Last week of December: run a New Year offer or a clearance sale on leftover stock, and start planning your January follow-up.

December rewards the businesses that plan. It punishes the ones that discount without thinking, run out of stock, or promise what they cannot deliver. Plan your promo, price it honestly, promote it well, and be ready when the orders come. If you do that, December will not just be your busiest month — it will be your most profitable one, and the customers you win will still be buying from you long after the Christmas lights come down.
