# How to Use Hashtags to Get Customers in Nigeria

You post a beautiful photo of your product on Instagram, write a good caption, and then... nothing. A few likes from friends, maybe one comment from your cousin, and no new customers. The problem is rarely your product. The problem is that nobody outside your small circle ever saw the post.

That is exactly what hashtags fix. A hashtag is a free doorway into a room full of strangers who are already interested in what you sell. When someone in Lagos searches for #LagosFashion or taps #MadeInNigeria, your post can appear in front of them — even if they have never heard your business name. Used well, hashtags are one of the cheapest customer-acquisition tools a Nigerian small business has. But most Nigerian businesses use hashtags badly. They copy thirty random tags from a competitor, use tags with tens of millions of posts where their content drowns in seconds, or use tags nobody searches for, like #BestQualityEver. This guide shows you how to do it properly: how to find the right hashtags for your business, how many to use, where to put them, and how to turn the people who find you through hashtags into paying customers.

## What a Hashtag Actually Does

A hashtag turns an ordinary word into a clickable search term. When you write #AbujaBakers under a photo of your cake, your post joins a public collection of every other post that used the same tag. Anyone who searches for or follows that tag can see your post, even if they do not follow your account.

A tag like #NaijaFood puts your sign on a busy street with thousands of people walking past. A tag like #LekkiCakes puts your sign on a smaller street, but nearly everyone walking there is looking for exactly what you sell. The smartest strategy uses both kinds of streets: a few busy ones for reach, and several small, specific ones for buyers.

Hashtags work on Instagram, TikTok, Facebook, X (Twitter), LinkedIn, and YouTube. The rules differ slightly on each platform, but the core idea is the same everywhere: the right tag puts your content in front of people who are already interested.

## Step 1: Know Exactly Who You Want to Reach

Before you write a single hashtag, write down who your customer is. Hashtag strategy fails when it is aimed at "everybody." Everybody is nobody.

Answer these questions in one or two sentences each:

- What do you sell, in plain words? Not "premium lifestyle solutions" — say "ready-to-wear Ankara dresses for working women."
- Who buys it? Age, gender, location, income level. A ₦150,000 bridal dress and a ₦8,000 Ankara top have very different buyers.
- Where are they? A Lekki bakery serves Lekki, Ikoyi, and Victoria Island. It does not need customers in Kano.
- What words would they type when searching? Would they type #LagosTailor or #FashionDesignerLagos? Would a buyer search #SkinCareNigeria or #GlowUpLagos?

Your answers become the foundation of your hashtag list. A business selling handmade soaps in Abuja should be found under tags like #AbujaSkincare, #NaturalSoapNigeria, and #MadeInAbuja — not under generic tags like #Beauty or #Soap, where your post competes with millions of posts from around the world.

Write your customer profile on paper or in your phone notes before moving on. Every hashtag you choose later must pass one test: would my real customer search for this or follow this?

## Step 2: Build Your Hashtag Bank

A hashtag bank is a saved list of tags you have researched and tested, grouped by type. Building one takes an afternoon, but it saves you time on every post for months. Create it in your phone's notes app or a Google Doc, with four groups.

### Group A: Location tags

These tell the platform where your customers are. Examples include #LagosBusiness, #AbujaVendors, #MadeInLagos, #PortHarcourt, #Lekki, #Ikeja, #Enugu, #Ibadan, #ShopInLagos, and #NigerianBusiness. Location tags are the most important group for any business that serves a specific city, because a customer in Lekki cannot buy from a vendor who only delivers in Abuja — and you do not want to waste reach on people who cannot buy from you.

### Group B: Niche tags

These describe exactly what you sell. A baker would use #LagosBaker, #NigerianCakes, #CakeVendorLagos, #BirthdayCakesLagos, and #CupcakesInLagos. A fashion designer would use #AnkaraStyles, #NaijaFashion, #LagosTailor, and #ReadyToWearNigeria. Niche tags have fewer posts than broad tags, which means your post stays visible longer and reaches people with real buying intent.

### Group C: Broad tags

These are the big, popular tags that bring volume: #MadeInNigeria, #Naija, #ShopNigeria, #NigerianVendor, #SupportSmallBusiness, and #BuyNigerian. Your post will sink fast in these feeds, but they still bring occasional discovery, and they signal to the algorithm what your content is about. Use a few, never all.

### Group D: Branded tags

Create one unique tag for your own business, such as #CakesByAda or #AdaMadeIt. Encourage happy customers to use it when they post photos of your product. Over time, your branded tag becomes a gallery of social proof that new customers can browse before buying. Keep it short, easy to spell, and unique enough that nobody else is already using it.

Aim for a bank of about 60 to 90 tags total across the four groups. That sounds like a lot, but you will never use them all at once — you will pick 15 to 25 per post from the bank, rotating them so your posts do not look identical to the algorithm.

## Step 3: Research Before You Copy

Never copy another business's hashtags blindly. A tag that works for a big brand with 200,000 followers may be useless for you, because their audience finds them anyway. Research each tag yourself with these simple checks.

First, search the tag on Instagram or TikTok and look at the number of posts. Tags with fewer than 5,000 posts may be too quiet — almost nobody searches them. Tags with more than 5 million posts are so crowded that your post disappears in minutes. The sweet spot for a small Nigerian business is usually between 10,000 and 500,000 posts: busy enough that people search it, quiet enough that your post stays visible for hours.

Second, look at the top nine posts under the tag. Are they from businesses like yours, or from something completely different? A tag like #Lagos might show you travel photos, news clips, and memes — your cake photo will look out of place there, and the people browsing it are not looking to buy cakes. A tag like #LagosBaker shows exactly your kind of content, which means the people browsing it are exactly your kind of customer.

Third, check whether the tag is being used by spammers. If the recent posts under a tag are mostly "follow for follow" accounts, betting adverts, or unrelated content, drop the tag. Spammy tags attract the wrong audience and can hurt your reach.

## Step 4: How Many Hashtags to Use

There is no single magic number, and the right number depends on the platform.

On Instagram, you can use up to 30 hashtags, but using all 30 is not always best. Most small businesses get the best results with 15 to 25 well-chosen tags. Fewer than 10 usually limits your reach; more than 25 starts to look spammy and can dilute the effect of your best tags. On Reels, 5 to 15 strong tags often work well because Reels already get wide distribution.

On TikTok, 3 to 5 hashtags per video is the usual advice. TikTok's algorithm relies more on the video content and captions than on hashtags, so a few precise tags — for example #LagosFood, #NaijaFoodVendor, and #SmallBusinessNigeria — work better than a long list.

On Facebook, use 2 to 5 hashtags at most. Facebook users do not search hashtags the way Instagram users do, and a long block of tags looks messy on Facebook.

On X (Twitter), 1 to 2 hashtags per post is enough. More than that reduces engagement.

On LinkedIn, 3 to 5 professional hashtags work best, such as #NigerianBusiness, #SMENigeria, and #DigitalMarketingNigeria.

The pattern is clear: the more visual and discovery-driven the platform, the more hashtags help. Instagram and TikTok reward them; Facebook, X, and LinkedIn reward restraint.

## Step 5: Where to Place Your Hashtags

On Instagram, you have two choices: in the caption or in the first comment. Both work for reach. Putting them in the first comment keeps your caption clean and readable, which looks more professional. Putting them in the caption means they are always attached to the post, even if the comment gets buried. Many Nigerian businesses put two or three key tags in the caption and the rest in the first comment. Either way, add the comment immediately after posting — within the first minute if possible — because the algorithm starts distributing your post right away.

On TikTok, put hashtags at the end of your caption, after a short sentence that describes the video. Do not hide them in a comment; TikTok reads the caption for context.

On Facebook and LinkedIn, put hashtags at the end of the post text, not in a comment, because comments there are less visible.

One rule applies everywhere: never put hashtags in the middle of a sentence. Writing "We sell the best #cakes in #Lagos" makes your caption hard to read and looks amateurish. Write the sentence cleanly, then add the tags at the end.

## Step 6: Mix Broad, Niche, and Local Tags in Every Post

A good hashtag set is a mix, not a pile of similar tags. For each post, pick roughly:

- 3 to 5 broad tags for volume, such as #MadeInNigeria or #ShopNigeria.
- 7 to 10 niche tags that describe your product precisely.
- 4 to 6 location tags that pin you to your city and neighbourhood.
- 1 branded tag of your own.

Here is an example for a Lekki baker posting a birthday cake:

#LagosBaker #BirthdayCakesLagos #CakeVendorLagos #NigerianCakes #LekkiCakes #CakesInLagos #MadeInLagos #LagosFood #NaijaBaker #MadeInNigeria #ShopNigeria #CakesByAda

Notice how the set moves from very specific (Lagos baker, birthday cakes in Lagos) to broad (Made in Nigeria). A buyer searching any of these can find the post, and the mix tells the algorithm exactly what the post is about and who should see it.

Rotate your sets. If you use the exact same 20 tags on every post, Instagram may treat your posts as repetitive and reduce their reach. Keep three or four different sets in your hashtag bank and rotate them across the week.

## Step 7: Use Hashtags on Reels and TikTok Videos

Short video is where hashtags pay off fastest in Nigeria right now. A single Reel or TikTok video can reach thousands of people who have never seen your page, and hashtags help the platform decide who those people should be.

When you post a Reel, add hashtags that describe both the content and the customer. A tailor showing a finished outfit could use #AnkaraStyles, #LagosTailor, #NaijaFashion, #MadeInLagos, and #FashionReels. A food vendor filming a cooking process could use #LagosFood, #NaijaFood, #FoodVendorLagos, and #StreetFoodNigeria.

Add one trending tag only if it genuinely fits your video. Jumping on a trending hashtag that has nothing to do with your business brings views from people who will never buy, and it teaches the algorithm to show your content to the wrong audience. Relevance beats virality every time for a business that needs customers, not just views.

Also, speak or write your location in the video itself. Saying "We deliver across Lekki and Ikoyi" in the first three seconds tells both the viewer and the platform who the video is for, and it works together with your location hashtags.

## Step 8: Turn Hashtag Visitors Into Customers

Getting discovered is only half the job. A person who finds you through a hashtag is a stranger. You have a few seconds to convince them to stay, follow, and eventually buy. Here is how to convert that attention.

First, make your profile ready before you chase reach. Your bio must say what you sell, where you are, and how to order — in one line each. A bio that says "Cakes, pastries and small chops. Lekki, Lagos. DM or WhatsApp 0803 123 4567 to order" converts far better than a bio that says "God's favourite baker, living my best life." Add a WhatsApp link in your bio so a visitor can reach you in one tap; many Nigerian buyers will not send an Instagram DM and wait — they want WhatsApp.

Second, pin your best posts. Pin three posts that show your best work, your prices, and your customer reviews. A hashtag visitor who lands on your profile should immediately see proof that you are real and good at what you do.

Third, reply fast. When a hashtag visitor comments or sends a DM, reply within minutes, not hours. Nigerian buyers often message three or four vendors at once and buy from the one who replies first. If you cannot be online all day, set up a quick reply or an auto-response that says when you will respond, and keep your WhatsApp Business greeting message switched on.

Fourth, post your prices or price ranges. Many Nigerian businesses hide their prices and lose customers who do not want to ask. You do not need to list every price, but a post that says "Ready-to-wear dresses from ₦25,000" or "Cakes from ₦35,000" filters in serious buyers and saves you from answering the same question fifty times.

Fifth, follow up. If someone likes several of your posts or watches your stories after finding you through a hashtag, send them a polite DM: "Thank you for checking out our page. We deliver across Lagos. Let me know if you would like our price list." One short, friendly message can turn a silent visitor into a customer. Do not spam people who never engaged with you — follow up only with people who showed interest.

## Step 9: Track What Works and Drop What Does Not

Hashtag strategy is not guesswork. Instagram gives you free data on every post. After posting, tap "View insights" and look at the number of accounts reached through hashtags. If a post reached 500 people through hashtags, note which tags you used. If another post reached only 20, note that too. Over a month, a clear pattern will emerge: some tags consistently bring reach, and some bring nothing.

Keep a simple weekly log. It can be a notebook or a phone note with four columns: date, post, hashtags used, hashtag reach. Review it every Sunday. Drop any tag that has brought zero reach in three weeks. Promote the tags that bring the most reach into your main rotation. Test two or three new tags each week to keep discovering better ones.

Also watch for the metric that matters more than reach: DMs and orders. A tag that brings 1,000 views but zero messages is less valuable than a tag that brings 100 views and five DMs. When a new customer messages you, ask politely how they found you. If they say "I saw your hashtag" or "I searched for cakes in Lekki," you will know which tags are producing buyers, not just viewers.

## Your Hashtag Checklist

Before you post anything this week, run through this list:

- I know who my customer is and where they live.
- My hashtag bank has location, niche, broad, and branded tags.
- Every tag in my bank has been checked for size, relevance, and spam.
- Each post uses a mix of broad, niche, and local tags — 15 to 25 on Instagram, 3 to 5 on TikTok.
- My tags are in the caption or first comment, added immediately after posting.
- My bio says what I sell, where I am, and how to order, with a WhatsApp link.
- My three pinned posts show my best work, my prices, and my reviews.
- I reply to DMs and comments within minutes.
- I check insights every Sunday and drop tags that bring no reach.
- I rotate my hashtag sets so no two posts use the identical list.

Hashtags will not fix a bad product or a neglected page. But if your product is good and your page is ready, hashtags are the cheapest way to put that product in front of strangers who are already looking for it. Start with your hashtag bank today, post consistently, track what works, and let the tags do the quiet work of bringing customers to you — one search at a time.
