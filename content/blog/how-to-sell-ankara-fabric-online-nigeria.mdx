Ankara is more than fabric in Nigeria. It is what people wear to weddings, church, owambes, naming ceremonies, birthdays and office Fridays. It is what aso ebi groups buy in bulk, what tailors turn into senator wears and gowns, and what fashion designers build whole collections around. If you can source good Ankara and sell it well online, you have a business with demand in every month of the year.

This guide shows you how to start and grow an online Ankara and fabric business in Nigeria — from choosing your fabrics and finding suppliers, to pricing per yard, photographing your materials, taking orders on WhatsApp and Instagram, collecting payments, and delivering to customers across the country.

## Why Ankara Sells So Well Online

Ankara demand is constant and it comes in waves that you can plan around. December is the biggest season, because of Christmas, weddings and Detty December parties. Then comes the wedding season, then Easter, then Sallah, then the steady flow of birthdays and owambes every weekend. A fabric vendor who is stocked and visible before each wave will sell; a vendor who waits until the wave arrives will miss it.

The margins are good when you buy well. A 6-yard Ankara piece you buy for ₦12,000 can sell for ₦18,000 to ₦25,000 depending on the design, the quality and the brand. Lace, Swiss voile and senator materials carry even bigger margins. And because fabric does not expire, unsold stock is not a loss the way unsold food is — you can sell it next month, or next season, as long as you store it properly.

## Step 1: Decide What Fabrics You Will Sell

Do not try to stock everything. A vendor who stocks Ankara, lace, Swiss voile, senator material, chiffon, silk, velvet, adire, aso oke and damask all at once will tie up all her money in stock that moves slowly. Start with one or two lanes and become known for them.

Here are the main lanes in the Nigerian fabric market:

- **Ankara wax prints:** The most popular lane. Sold in 6-yard pieces, with prices in Lagos typically from ₦12,000 to ₦30,000 per piece depending on the brand and design. Target, Vlisco, Hollandaise and quality Chinese wax prints are the names customers ask for.
- **Lace:** For weddings, aso ebi and luxury outfits. Prices run from ₦25,000 to ₦120,000 per 5 yards depending on the design, the stones and the brand. Lace carries high margins but needs more capital.
- **Swiss voile and senator materials:** Popular with men and for classy native wears. Prices run from ₦15,000 to ₦60,000 per 4 or 5 yards.
- **Adire and tie-and-dye:** Handmade, unique, and increasingly popular with fashion designers. Prices run from ₦8,000 to ₦25,000 per piece.
- **Aso oke:** Woven by hand, used for traditional weddings and ceremonies. Prices run from ₦30,000 to ₦150,000 per set depending on the quality and the weaving.
- **Ready-made aso ebi packages:** Curated sets of the same fabric in different colours for groups. One group order can be 20 to 50 pieces at once.

Choose based on three things: how much capital you have, who your customers are, and what you can source reliably. If your audience is young women in Surulere and Yaba, Ankara wax prints and adire will move fastest. If your audience is brides and wedding groups in Lekki and Victoria Island, lace and aso ebi packages will sell better.

## Step 2: Find Reliable Fabric Suppliers

Your supplier decides your quality, your price and your reputation. Buy badly once, and the complaints will follow you for months. Never buy a large quantity from a supplier you have not tested.

### Where fabric vendors source stock

- **Balogun market, Lagos:** The biggest fabric market in West Africa. You can touch the fabric, check the print quality, compare designs side by side, and negotiate face to face. Many successful online fabric vendors started here, buying a few pieces at a time.
- **Onitsha main market:** Another major source, especially for Ankara and lace at wholesale prices. Worth a trip if you are based in the East or you buy in large quantities.
- **Kano and Kaduna:** Important sources for adire, aso oke and locally woven fabrics. If you sell traditional fabrics, build relationships with weavers and suppliers here.
- **Direct importers and agents:** Many established vendors import wax prints directly. The prices are lower, but you need to factor in shipping, customs and the risk of a supplier who sends a different design from the one you ordered.

### How to test a supplier

Buy a small quantity first — two or three pieces. Wash a sample and check whether the colours run. Check the edges of the print: are they sharp or blurry? Check the feel of the cotton: is it soft and strong, or stiff and thin? Hold it up to the light: can you see through it too easily? Ask direct questions: Is this original wax or a copy? What is the yardage, exactly 6 yards or less? A good supplier answers clearly. A supplier who dodges questions is a warning sign.

Build relationships with two or three good suppliers rather than depending on one. If one supplier runs out of a popular design, the other may have it. And always keep records of what you bought, from whom, and at what price.

## Step 3: Price Your Fabrics So You Make Money

Pricing fabric is where many new vendors fail. They price by copying a big vendor on Instagram, or by guessing, and then discover they are selling at a loss after transport and delivery costs.

### Work out your true cost

Your cost is not just the price you paid the supplier. Add transport to the market, the cost of carrying the goods, packaging, delivery, data and your time. A 6-yard Ankara piece that costs ₦12,000 at Balogun might cost you ₦14,500 by the time it is photographed, packaged and ready to send.

Use this formula:

**Selling price = supplier cost + transport + packaging + delivery allowance + your profit**

Aim for at least 30 to 40 percent gross margin. If your total cost is ₦14,500, your price should be ₦20,000 to ₦22,000. Customers will pay for fabric whose colours are rich, whose print is sharp, and whose quality matches what you showed them. They will not come back to a vendor whose Ankara faded after one wash.

### Publish a clear price list

Publish prices for your main products and pin them in your WhatsApp Business catalogue and Instagram highlights. Be specific: name the fabric, the brand, the yardage and the price. For example:

- Ankara wax print, 6 yards, Target — ₦20,000
- Ankara wax print, 6 yards, premium — ₦28,000
- Swiss voile, 5 yards — ₦35,000
- Lace with stones, 5 yards — ₦65,000
- Adire, 5 yards — ₦15,000

State whether the price is per piece or per yard, and state it clearly. Confusion about yardage is one of the biggest causes of disputes in the fabric business. If you sell per yard, say so. If a piece is 6 yards, say so. And never advertise a price you cannot honour — a customer who sees ₦15,000 on your page and is told ₦22,000 in the chat will leave and tell her friends.

## Step 4: Set Up Your Online Shopfront

Your customers will find you on Instagram, TikTok and WhatsApp. Each one has a job to do, and you need all three working together.

### Instagram

Your Instagram page is your shop window. Use a business account with a clear name, for example "Ankara by Ada — Lagos." Your bio should say what you sell, where you are based, and how to order: "Quality Ankara, lace and aso ebi in Lagos. Nationwide delivery. Order via WhatsApp. Link below." Put your WhatsApp link in your bio so a customer can tap once and start chatting.

Your feed should be full of your actual fabrics. Post every new design you stock, laid flat or draped, with the name, the yardage and the price in the caption. Post videos of the fabric being unfolded, draped and held up to the light.

### TikTok

TikTok is where many Nigerian fabric vendors find new customers today. Short videos of new arrivals, fabric hauls, styling ideas and aso ebi inspiration go viral easily. You do not need expensive equipment — a phone, good light and a clean background are enough. Post at least three times a week and use sounds that are trending in Nigeria.

### WhatsApp Business

Download WhatsApp Business and set up a business profile with your shop name, area, opening hours and a clear profile photo. Your catalogue is your price list: add every fabric with a good photo, the exact name, the yardage, the price and a short description. "Ankara wax print, Target, 6 yards, ₦20,000." Set up quick replies for the questions you answer every day: "/prices" sends your price list, "/delivery" sends your delivery areas and fees, "/payment" sends your account details.

## Step 5: Photograph Your Fabrics Properly

Fabric is a visual product. Nobody can touch your Ankara from a photo, so the photo and video have to do all the selling. Good content will bring you orders; bad content will quietly kill your business.

Photograph in daylight, near a window, against a clean background. Lay the fabric flat so the full print is visible, then drape it so customers can see how it falls. Photograph close-ups of the print so customers can see the sharpness of the design and the richness of the colours.

Always show the real fabric. Never use another vendor's photos, and never post a photo of a premium Vlisco print when you are selling a cheaper copy. The customer will open the package, see the difference, and demand a refund — or worse, post about it. Honest photos build trust, and trust is what brings repeat customers and aso ebi groups.

Take a short video of every new design you stock. A 20-second clip of the fabric being unfolded and draped is worth more than ten still photos. Post these videos on your status, your feed and TikTok on the same day the stock arrives.

## Step 6: Take Orders and Payments Safely

Orders will come from Instagram DMs, TikTok comments, WhatsApp chats, status replies and referrals. You need one simple order process that works every time.

Ask every customer for the same details: name, the fabric they want, the design or colour, the number of yards or pieces, the delivery address with a landmark, and their phone number. Confirm the order back to them in one message: "Confirmed: 2 pieces Ankara wax print, Target, 6 yards each, ₦40,000 + delivery to Ikeja (₦2,500) = ₦42,500. Please send payment to confirm your order."

### Payment before dispatch

For new customers, collect full payment before you send the fabric. This is standard in the Nigerian fabric business. Fabric is expensive and a cut piece cannot be resold easily. Accept transfers to your bank account, and consider a Moniepoint or Opay account for fast confirmation alerts. Always confirm when you have seen the money: "Payment received, thank you! Your fabric will be dispatched today."

Be very careful with payment scams. Common tricks include fake transfer alerts and screenshots of payments that never arrived. Check your own account balance before dispatching anything — never trust a screenshot alone.

## Step 7: Deliver Fabrics Safely Across Nigeria

Fabric is light, valuable and easy to damage if it is packed badly. Good packaging and a trusted rider make all the difference.

Fold each piece neatly and pack it in a clean nylon bag, then in a sturdy branded bag or box. Label the package with the customer's name and the design, so there is no confusion when you have ten orders going out on the same day.

For deliveries within Lagos, Abuja or Port Harcourt, use a dispatch rider you trust. Agree the fee with the customer before you dispatch, and add it to the total. A typical short delivery within Lekki might cost ₦2,000 to ₦3,500. For deliveries outside your city, use a trusted logistics company: GIG Logistics, DHL, or a reliable inter-state bus service for customers who prefer it. Always share the tracking details with the customer and follow up until the package arrives.

Tell the customer the delivery fee upfront, before they pay. Nothing annoys a customer more than a surprise delivery charge after payment.

## Step 8: Market Your Fabrics Every Day

You do not need paid adverts to start. You need consistency and visibility in the places your customers already are.

Post on your WhatsApp Status at least once a day: a new design, a restock announcement, a customer wearing your fabric, or a short video of a new arrival being unfolded. The best times are the morning, around 7 a.m. to 8 a.m., and the evening, when people are scrolling after work.

On Instagram, post a Reel at least three times a week. New arrivals, styling ideas, and aso ebi inspiration perform very well. Use stories for polls and questions: "Which colour for this weekend's owambe — blue or burgundy?" Customers love to vote, and every vote pushes your page to more people.

Plan your stock around the calendar. Stock heavily before December, before wedding season, and before Sallah and Easter. Announce your new arrivals early, so aso ebi groups can plan their orders with you instead of buying from someone else.

## Step 9: Keep Customers and Tailors Coming Back

Getting an order is hard. Keeping a customer is cheaper and more profitable. A customer who buys fabric for every family event is worth more than 20 one-time buyers.

After delivery, send a short follow-up message: "Hello Mrs Ade, hope you love the fabric. Thank you for shopping with us!" That single message makes people feel valued. If there was a problem, they will tell you privately instead of posting about it.

Keep a simple record of your customers: their names, what they bought, their preferred colours and designs, and when they last ordered. Before December and wedding season, send a friendly message to past customers: "Hello! New Ankara designs just arrived. Would you like me to send you photos?"

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Buying a large quantity from an untested supplier.** Test with two or three pieces first, always.
- **Advertising premium fabric and delivering a copy.** Your reputation will not survive it.
- **Dispatching before confirming payment in your own account.** Never trust a screenshot alone.
- **No price list, or unclear yardage.** Customers who have to ask for every price will buy from a vendor who shows prices.
- **Poor photos and videos.** Dark, blurry photos of fabric will kill your sales.
- **No records.** If you do not write down what you sold and what you spent, you will not know whether you are making money.
- **Ignoring customer messages.** Fabric customers ask many questions before they buy. Slow replies send them to another vendor.

## How Much Can You Realistically Make?

It depends on your capital, your niche and your consistency, but here is a realistic example. If you sell 60 pieces of Ankara a month at an average price of ₦20,000, that is ₦1,200,000 in monthly sales. If your average cost per piece is ₦13,000, your gross profit is ₦7,000 per piece, or ₦420,000 a month before transport, delivery, data and packaging costs.

## Your First Month Action Plan

Do not wait for everything to be perfect. Start small and start this month.

1. **Week 1:** Choose your fabric lane. Visit Balogun or your nearest market and test two or three suppliers with small purchases.
2. **Week 2:** Set up Instagram, TikTok and WhatsApp Business. Photograph and film your first stock in daylight.
3. **Week 3:** Post daily on your status and at least three Reels. Send your price list to 30 contacts personally.
4. **Week 4:** Take your first paid orders. Confirm payment in your account before dispatching. Follow up with every customer.

If you sell only ten pieces in your first month, that is a win. You have proof that people will pay for your fabrics.
