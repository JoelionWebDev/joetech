Every successful freelancer remembers their first international client. That first yes, first deposit in dollars, first moment of realizing your skills are valuable on the global market.

Getting that first client is the hardest part. Without testimonials or a track record, you are asking someone to trust you with their project. Building that trust requires strategy.

This article covers the tactics that help Nigerian freelancers land their first international client.

## Building Trust Without a Track Record

Every freelancer starts with no reviews. The key is to demonstrate capability through means other than past client work.

Build a portfolio site that showcases your skills. Include personal projects, open-source contributions, and sample work. A well-designed portfolio with clear case studies signals professionalism even without client references.

Create a strong LinkedIn profile. International clients search LinkedIn for freelancers. Your headline should describe what you do and who you serve, not just your job title. Full-Stack Developer Specializing in React and Node.js for Early-Stage Startups is better than Developer at XYZ Company.

Contribute to open source. Visible contributions on GitHub demonstrate your coding ability and work ethic. Clients can see your code quality, commit history, and collaboration skills.

## Finding Prospects

LinkedIn is the best platform for finding international clients. Search for founders, CTOs, and hiring managers at companies that hire remote developers. Filter by industry, company size, and location.

Connect with decision-makers, not recruiters. Send a personalized connection request. Reference something specific about their company or work. Generic requests are ignored.

Job boards like We Work Remotely, Remote OK, and Dynamite Jobs list remote freelance positions. Apply selectively to positions that match your skills. A tailored application beats twenty generic ones.

Upwork and Toptal remain valid platforms despite their competition. On Upwork, target projects with clear scope and reasonable budgets. Apply with personalized proposals that reference the client's specific requirements.

## Writing Proposals That Get Responses

The first line of your proposal determines whether the client reads further. Start with a statement that shows you understand their problem. I see you need a developer who can integrate Paystack into your React application. I have done exactly that for three businesses this year.

Demonstrate relevant experience explicitly. Link to a similar project or describe a comparable problem you have solved. If you do not have direct experience, explain how your skills transfer.

Keep it concise. Clients read dozens of proposals. Long proposals are skipped. Cover who you are, why you are qualified, how you would approach the project, and what the next step should be.

Include a call to action. End with a clear next step. Would you be available for a brief call this week to discuss the project further?

## Pricing in USD

Price your services in USD. Nigerian freelancers who price in Naira leave money on the table. The global market rate for your skill is in dollars. Charge accordingly.

Research market rates for your skill level and location. Entry-level developers charge $15 to $30 per hour. Mid-level developers charge $30 to $60. Senior developers charge $60 to $150.

Do not underprice yourself to win your first client. Low pricing attracts difficult clients who do not value your work. Price at a fair rate and justify it with your portfolio and communication.

## Handling International Payments

Set up payment accounts before you start searching for clients. Payoneer provides a virtual US bank account that clients can pay into. Funds are withdrawn to your Nigerian bank account within one to three days.

Wise offers a similar service with competitive exchange rates. You can hold multiple currencies and convert when rates are favorable.

Include your payment terms in your proposal or contract. Fifty percent upfront and fifty percent on completion is standard for first-time client relationships.

## Delivering Excellence

Your first international client is your most important client. Delivering exceptional work leads to testimonials, referrals, and repeat business.

Communicate proactively. Provide regular updates before the client asks. Flag issues early. Overcommunicate timelines and progress.

Deliver on time or early. Reliability is the quality clients value most. Being consistently reliable earns trust that leads to higher rates and more work.

Ask for a testimonial and referral after successful delivery. A detailed testimonial from your first client is the key to landing your second.

## Conclusion

Landing your first international client requires strategy, persistence, and professionalism. Build a portfolio that demonstrates capability. Find prospects on LinkedIn and job platforms. Write proposals that show understanding. Price fairly and professionally. Set up payment infrastructure. Deliver exceptional work.

The first client is the hardest. Each subsequent client gets easier as your reputation grows.
