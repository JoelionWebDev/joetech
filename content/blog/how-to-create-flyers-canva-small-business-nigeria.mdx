Walk through any busy area in Lagos, Abuja, or Port Harcourt and you will see flyers everywhere: pasted on shop walls, shared on WhatsApp Status, posted on Instagram, and handed out at bus stops. Some flyers make you stop and read. Most do not. The difference is rarely money. A salon owner in Lekki, a caterer in Ikeja, a tutor in Abuja, or a fashion designer in Surulere can all design a flyer that looks professional, using Canva, a free design tool that works on a phone or a laptop.

Canva is popular with Nigerian small businesses because it is simple. You do not need design training. You choose a template, change the words and pictures, and download your flyer in minutes. The free plan is enough for most flyers, and you can design on your phone while you are in traffic or waiting for a customer. This guide shows you how to use Canva step by step, from planning your message to sharing the finished flyer where your customers will actually see it.

## Step 1: Plan Your Flyer Before You Open Canva

A good flyer starts on paper, not on a screen. Before you open Canva, answer four questions. First, what is the one action you want people to take? Do you want them to call you, send a WhatsApp message, visit your shop, or register for a class? A flyer with one clear action works far better than one that asks people to do five different things. Second, who is the flyer for? A flyer for students in Yaba will look different from a flyer for brides in Lekki or for busy mothers in Abuja. Third, what is your offer? Is it a discount, a new service, a free consultation, or an event date? Fourth, what must the flyer say, and what can you leave out?

Write your message in this order. Start with a short headline that states the benefit, such as "Learn Tailoring in 8 Weeks" or "Fresh Small Chops for Your Party". Add one or two lines that explain the offer, such as the price, the location, or the date. Then add your call to action, such as "WhatsApp 0803 123 4567 to book your slot". Keep the total words under 50 if you can. People in Nigeria see dozens of flyers every day on their phones. A flyer packed with long paragraphs will be skipped. A flyer with a bold headline, a clear offer, and one phone number will be read.

## Step 2: Set Up Canva and Choose the Right Size

1. **Create a free account.** Go to canva.com or download the Canva app from the Play Store or App Store. Sign up with your Google account or your email address. The free plan gives you thousands of templates, photos, and design elements. You do not need the paid plan to make a good flyer.
2. **Choose the right size.** The size depends on where the flyer will be used. For WhatsApp Status and Instagram Stories, use 1080 by 1920 pixels, which is the tall phone-screen size. For an Instagram post or a Facebook post, use 1080 by 1080 pixels, which is square. For a printed flyer to share in your shop or at an event, use A5 size, which is half of an A4 sheet and is cheap to print. In Canva, tap "Create a design", type the size you need, and start from a blank page or a template.
3. **Learn the basic layout.** Canva has a simple layout. Templates and elements are on the left side on a laptop, or at the bottom on a phone. You tap an element to add it, drag it to move it, and use the corners to resize it. Take ten minutes to tap around and try things before you design your real flyer. This practice will save you time later.

Use Wi-Fi when you can, because Canva loads images and templates from the internet and can use a lot of data. If you are on MTN or Glo mobile data, load the template once, then work offline as much as possible. Save your work often, because Canva saves automatically when you are online, but a network drop can still lose a recent change.

## Step 3: Pick a Template That Fits Your Business

Canva has thousands of flyer templates. Search for your business type, such as "salon flyer", "food flyer", "fashion flyer", "event flyer", or "school flyer". You will see many options. Choose a template with a clear layout, not the busiest one. A good template has space for a headline at the top, a photo in the middle, and contact details at the bottom. If a template has too many words and decorations, it will be hard to adapt.

Look for a template that matches the feeling of your business. A children's party business should use bright colours and playful shapes. A law firm or a consulting business should use calm colours and clean lines. A food business should use warm colours and a large photo of the food. Do not copy a template exactly. Change the colours, the photo, and the words so the flyer looks like your business, not like everyone else who used the same template.

If you cannot find a template you like, start from a blank design. Choose a background colour, add a large headline, add one photo, and add your contact details. A simple blank design often looks cleaner than a complicated template. Keep a note of the templates you like, so you can reuse them for future flyers and keep a consistent look for your brand.

## Step 4: Choose Colours and Fonts That People Can Read

Colour and font choices make the difference between a flyer that looks professional and one that looks messy. Use two or three colours at most. Pick one main colour that matches your brand, one second colour that goes well with it, and white or black for text. If your business already has a logo, use the colours from the logo. A food business in Lagos might use orange and dark green. A beauty salon might use pink and gold. A tech repair shop might use blue and white. Avoid using red text on a green background, or yellow text on a white background, because these combinations are hard to read.

Use two fonts at most. One bold font for the headline, and one simple font for the rest of the text. Canva has many fonts, but simple ones work best. A headline font should be large and bold, so it can be read from a distance or on a small phone screen. The body text should be smaller but still easy to read. Do not use a fancy script font for important details such as your phone number or your price, because people may not be able to read it. Test your flyer by looking at it on your phone from arm's length. If you cannot read the headline and the phone number easily, make them bigger.

Leave empty space on your flyer. This is called white space, and it helps the important parts stand out. A flyer with no empty space looks crowded and confusing. A flyer with space around the headline and the phone number looks calm and professional. Resist the urge to fill every corner with text, stickers, and decorations.

## Step 5: Add a Strong Photo

A flyer with a real photo of your product or service will always perform better than one with no photo or a poor photo. Use a photo you took yourself if you can, because customers trust real photos more than stock photos. Take the photo in good daylight, near a window or outside in the morning, and clean the background so the product stands out. A plate of food on a clean table looks far better than the same food on a cluttered table. A finished hairstyle against a plain wall looks far better than one in a busy salon.

If you do not have a good photo of your own, use a free photo from Canva's library. Search for your product, such as "Nigerian food", "braided hair", "men's fashion", or "birthday cake". Choose a photo that looks like what you actually sell. Do not use a photo of a luxury product if you sell affordable products, because customers will be disappointed when they arrive. Crop the photo to fit the space on your flyer, and make sure it is large enough to see clearly on a phone screen. One large, clear photo is better than four small, blurry photos.

If you sell a service rather than a product, use a photo of the result. A cleaner can show a sparkling living room. A tutor can show a happy student or a whiteboard with clear writing. A makeup artist can show a finished face. People buy results, so show the result on your flyer.

## Step 6: Write Copy That Makes People Act

Good flyer copy is short, specific, and honest. Start with a headline that promises a benefit, not just a business name. "Fresh Cakes Baked Daily in Lekki" is better than "Ada's Cakes". "Pass Your JAMB With Our Weekend Classes" is better than "Bright Future Academy". Your headline should make a person in your target audience stop scrolling and think, "That is for me."

Follow the headline with two or three short lines that give the key facts. Include the price or the starting price, the location, and the date if it is an event. For example, "Cakes from ₦15,000. Lekki Phase 1. Order 24 hours ahead." This is far more useful than a long paragraph about your passion for baking. End with a clear call to action and one contact method. "WhatsApp 0803 123 4567 to order" is clear. "Call or WhatsApp or DM or visit our website or come to our shop" is confusing. Pick the contact method your customers already use most, which in Nigeria is usually WhatsApp, and make it the biggest contact detail on the flyer.

Check your spelling and your phone number twice before you download the flyer. A wrong digit in a phone number means lost customers. Ask a friend to read the flyer and tell you if anything is unclear. If they cannot tell what you sell, what it costs, and how to reach you within five seconds, rewrite the flyer.

## Step 7: Download, Print, and Share Your Flyer

When your flyer looks right, download it from Canva. For sharing on WhatsApp, Instagram, and Facebook, download it as a JPG or PNG file, which is a standard image file that opens easily on any phone. For printing, download it as a PDF in print quality, which keeps the text sharp. Check the file size before you share it. A very large file will be slow to send on mobile data and may fail on a weak network. If the file is too large, download it at a slightly lower quality or crop the image.

For printed flyers, take the PDF to a print shop in your area. In Lagos, printing is available in Ikeja, Yaba, Surulere, and Lekki. A5 flyers are cheap to print in bulk, and you can print 100 copies for a small amount. Ask the print shop to show you one copy before they print the full batch, so you can check the colours and the text. If the colours look dull, it may be a printer issue, not a design issue. For sharing online, post the flyer on your WhatsApp Status, your Instagram feed and Stories, your Facebook page, and in relevant WhatsApp groups where your customers are. If you need a QR code on your flyer that links to your WhatsApp chat, Joetech's free QR code generator at joetech360.ng/tools/qr-code-generator can create one in seconds.

Do not post the same flyer every day in the same group, or people will start to ignore it. Post it once, then post it again after a few days with a different caption. Change the caption each time. One day, talk about the price. Another day, talk about a customer who enjoyed the service. Another day, share a behind-the-scenes photo. The flyer stays the same, but the story around it changes.

## Step 8: Reuse Your Design and Keep Your Brand Consistent

Your first flyer is the hardest. Your second flyer should take half the time, because you can copy the first design and change the words and the photo. In Canva, open your finished design, tap the three dots, and choose "Make a copy". Change the headline, the offer, and the photo, but keep the same colours, fonts, and layout. This consistency helps customers recognise your flyers instantly, even before they read your business name.

Create a simple brand kit for yourself, even on the free plan. Write down your main colour, your second colour, your headline font, and your body font, and use the same choices every time. Save your logo in Canva so you can add it to every flyer in seconds. Save your best flyers in a folder called "My Flyers" so you can find them quickly. Over time, this consistency builds trust. A customer who sees the same colours and style again and again will remember your business when they need your service.

Review your flyers every few months. Which ones brought the most calls or messages? Which ones were ignored? Keep the style of the ones that worked, and change the ones that did not. Ask customers how they found you, and note which flyer or post brought them in. This simple feedback tells you more than any design theory.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

The first mistake is putting too much text on the flyer. A flyer is not a brochure. If you have a lot to say, put the details on your WhatsApp catalogue or your website, and keep the flyer short.

The second mistake is using a blurry or dark photo. A poor photo makes a good business look careless. Take a new photo in daylight rather than using an old, dark one.

The third mistake is forgetting the location or the price. A customer who likes your flyer but cannot tell where you are or what you charge will move on to a competitor who states it clearly.

The fourth mistake is using too many colours and fonts. A flyer with five colours and four fonts looks untidy. Two colours and two fonts are enough.

The fifth mistake is never updating your flyer. A flyer with an old price, an old phone number, or a Christmas offer in June will damage trust. Check your flyers every month and update them when prices or details change.

## Final Checklist

Before you share your flyer, check that you have planned one clear action, chosen the right size for where it will be used, picked a clean template, used two or three colours and two fonts, added one strong photo, written a short headline with a clear offer and price, included one contact method, checked your spelling and phone number, downloaded the right file type, and saved a copy so you can reuse the design. If you can tick most of these boxes, your flyer is ready. Design your next flyer today, share it where your customers already are, and watch which version brings the most messages. With Canva and a little practice, you can produce professional flyers for your small business in Nigeria without paying a designer for every single one.
