You did the work. You delivered on time. The client is happy. Then you send the invoice — and wait. Days pass. Weeks pass. You send a reminder, and you feel awkward about it, as if you are the one doing something wrong.

## Why Invoices Matter More Than You Think

An invoice is not just a bill. It is a business document that does several jobs at once: it tells the client exactly what they owe, what it is for, and when it is due; it creates a record you can both refer to if there is ever a disagreement; and it shows that your business is organised and professional, which builds trust.

Many Nigerian small businesses still send invoices as a plain WhatsApp message — "Please pay 150k for the website" — with no breakdown, no due date, and no reference number. That is not an invoice; it is a request. And requests are easy to ignore. A proper invoice is harder to ignore, because everything the client needs to pay is right there in front of them.

## What Every Professional Invoice Must Include

A good invoice answers every question the client might ask before they ask it. Here is the full checklist:

### 1. Your Business Details

Your business name, phone number, email address, and office address if you have one. If you have a logo, include it — it makes the invoice instantly recognisable and more professional. The client should be able to tell at a glance who the invoice is from.

### 2. The Word "Invoice"

Label the document clearly as an invoice at the top. If it looks like a quote or a receipt, the client may not realise payment is due. A clear title removes all doubt.

### 3. A Unique Invoice Number

Give every invoice its own number — for example, INV-001, INV-002, and so on. This helps you track payments, helps the client reference the payment, and makes your business look organised.

### 4. The Date and the Due Date

Always show the date the invoice was issued and the date payment is due. A due date is essential. Without one, the client decides when to pay — and that is usually "later." Common payment terms are 7, 14, or 30 days from the invoice date. Choose a term that fits your work, state it clearly, and stick to it.

### 5. The Client's Details

The client's name or business name, and their contact details. This confirms the invoice is for the right person and helps the client's accounts department process it.

### 6. A Clear Description of the Work

List each item or service separately, with a short description, the quantity, the unit price, and the line total. For example: website design (5 pages) — ₦250,000; logo design — ₦50,000; one year of website hosting — ₦30,000. A client who can see exactly what they are paying for is far less likely to query the invoice. Vague lines like "services rendered — ₦300,000" invite questions and delays.

### 7. The Total, Including Any Tax

Show the subtotal, any tax or VAT that applies, any discount, and the final total in bold. The total should be impossible to miss. If your price includes tax, say so. If tax is added on top, show it as a separate line so there is no confusion.

### 8. Your Payment Details

Include your bank name, account name, and account number, and any other payment method you accept. If you expect a transfer, write the account details clearly and double-check every digit. A wrong account number is one of the most common reasons payments get delayed.

### 9. Payment Terms and Notes

A short note at the bottom can cover anything else the client needs to know: your payment terms, a thank-you message, or what happens if payment is late. Keep it polite and professional.

## How to Word Your Invoice

The tone of an invoice should be professional and polite, never aggressive. You are asking for money you have earned, and there is nothing awkward about that. Use simple, direct language. "Payment due by 21 November 2026" is clearer than "We would be grateful if payment could be made at your earliest convenience." The second version sounds polite but gives no deadline at all.

## Send the Invoice Promptly

One of the simplest ways to get paid faster is to send the invoice as soon as the work is done — the same day, if possible. Many small businesses finish a job on Monday and send the invoice on Friday, or forget entirely until the end of the month. Every day of delay pushes payment further away. Make invoicing part of finishing the job: the moment the work is delivered and accepted, the invoice goes out.

Also, send the invoice to the right person. If you dealt with a manager but the accounts department handles payments, ask for the accounts contact and copy them in. An invoice sitting in the wrong inbox is an invoice that never gets paid.

## Follow Up Without Damaging the Relationship

Even with a perfect invoice, some clients pay late. Following up is part of running a business, not a sign of conflict. The key is to follow up early, politely, and consistently.

Here is a simple follow-up rhythm:

1. **A few days before the due date:** a friendly reminder. "Hello, just a quick note that Invoice INV-014 for ₦330,000 is due on 21 November. Thank you!"
2. **On the due date:** a short message confirming the invoice is now due, with the payment details attached again.
3. **A few days after the due date:** a polite but firmer message. "We have not yet received payment for Invoice INV-014. Could you please confirm when it will be made?"
4. **A week or more overdue:** a phone call, followed by a written message summarising what was agreed on the call.

Never apologise for asking for your money. Phrases like "Sorry to disturb you" make it sound as if you are in the wrong. You are not. Be warm, be clear, and be consistent.

## Common Invoice Mistakes to Avoid

- **No due date.** Without a deadline, payment always drifts.
- **No invoice number.** Tracking becomes impossible once you have more than a few clients.
- **Vague descriptions.** "Services rendered" invites questions. Itemise everything.
- **Wrong or missing payment details.** Check your account number every time.
- **Sending the invoice late.** Send it the day the work is done.
- **Only sending it on WhatsApp with no document.** A proper invoice document — PDF or printed — is taken more seriously than a chat message.
- **No follow-up.** Many clients pay only after a reminder. Silence is not a strategy.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How soon should I send an invoice after finishing work?

The same day, if possible. Sending the invoice immediately keeps the work fresh in the client's mind and starts the payment clock right away. Waiting even a few days pushes payment back by the same amount.

### What payment terms should I use?

For most small businesses in Nigeria, 7 to 14 days is reasonable for smaller jobs, and 30 days is common for larger corporate work. State the term clearly on the invoice and, ideally, agree it with the client before you start the work.

### What if a client refuses to pay?

First, check whether the problem is the invoice itself — a missing detail, a wrong amount, or an unclear description. If the invoice is correct, follow up in writing, keep a record of every message, and consider pausing further work until payment is made. For large amounts, a formal letter from a lawyer is often enough to resolve the matter without going to court.
