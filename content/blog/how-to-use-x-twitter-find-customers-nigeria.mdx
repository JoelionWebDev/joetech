A customer in Abuja tweets at 9 p.m.: "I need a reliable website designer in Lagos. Who do you recommend?" Within an hour, ten people reply, tagging designers they trust. One of those designers gets a DM, a call, and a ₦350,000 project. She did not run an advert. She was simply present, helpful and trusted on X when the question was asked.

That is how X, formerly Twitter, works for Nigerian businesses. It is fast, public, and driven by conversation. People ask for recommendations, complain about bad service, praise good service, and share useful tips — all in real time. If your business is visible and helpful there, customers will find you. If you are invisible, or if you only post adverts, they will scroll past you.

This guide shows you how to use X to find customers in Nigeria: setting up a profile that builds trust, posting content people actually share, joining conversations, using Spaces and trends wisely, and turning followers into buyers without sounding like a billboard.

## Why X Works for Nigerian Businesses

X is different from Instagram and Facebook. On Instagram, people follow beautiful pictures. On Facebook, people follow friends and groups. On X, people follow ideas, opinions, news and useful information. A small account with a good post can reach thousands of strangers in a day, because X shows posts to people who do not follow you if the post is interesting.

Nigerians use X to talk about money, business, jobs, phones, food, football, politics, and daily life in Lagos, Abuja, Port Harcourt and beyond. That means your customers are already there, talking about the problems your business solves. A tailor, a web designer, a food vendor, a real estate agent, a makeup artist, a tutor, a cleaner, and a logistics company can all find customers on X, if they speak the language of the platform.

X is also fast. A tweet about a flash sale, a new product, or an open slot for tomorrow can bring orders the same day. A helpful thread can be retweeted by a big account and bring you hundreds of new followers overnight. No other platform in Nigeria gives a small business that kind of speed for free.

But X rewards consistency and honesty. An account that posts once a month will not grow. An account that only posts "Buy from us" will be ignored. An account that shares useful tips, answers questions, and talks like a real person will grow steadily, and its followers will become customers.

## Step 1: Set Up a Profile That Builds Trust

Before you post, fix your profile. When someone sees your tweet and clicks your name, they decide in three seconds whether to follow you, message you, or leave. Make those three seconds count.

Use a clear profile picture: your face, or your logo on a clean background. A blurry photo, a cartoon, or no photo at all makes people suspicious. If you are a personal brand, use your face. People buy from people they can see. If you are a company account, use a sharp logo.

Write a bio that says what you do, who you help, and where you are. "I design websites for small businesses in Lagos. Founder, Joetech. Lekki." That is clear in one line. Avoid vague bios like "Entrepreneur | Dream chaser | DM for business." They tell the reader nothing.

Add your location, Lagos, Abuja, or Port Harcourt, and a link to your website or WhatsApp. A link in your bio is the easiest way for a new follower to become a customer. If you have a website, link to it. If you do not, link to your WhatsApp.

Pin a tweet to the top of your profile. Your pinned tweet should be your best introduction: what you sell, your prices or starting prices, how to order, and a photo or video of your work. A new visitor who reads your pinned tweet should understand your business in 30 seconds.

Use one account for your business, and keep it focused. Mixing business tweets with personal arguments, politics, or controversial posts can scare away customers. You can have a personal account for personal opinions, and a business account for your work. If you use one account for both, keep the personal posts light and kind.

## Step 2: Know Who You Are Talking To

You cannot find customers on X if you do not know who they are. A web designer looking for startup founders should post differently from a food vendor looking for office workers in Ikeja.

Write down three things about your ideal customer: what they need, where they are, and what they worry about. A small business owner in Lekki who needs a website worries about cost, trust, and whether the designer will deliver. A mother in Surulere who orders food worries about taste, hygiene and delivery time. A student in Ibadan who needs a laptop worries about price and originality.

Once you know that, your posts become easier. Instead of posting "We sell websites," you post "A 5-page business website in Lagos costs ₦350,000. Here is what is included, and what to ask before you pay." That post speaks directly to the worry of your ideal customer, and it will be saved and shared.

Search X for the words your customers use. Search "need a website designer Lagos," "best tailor in Abuja," "where can I buy," "recommend a good," and "who sells." You will see real questions from real people, in their own words. Those questions are your content ideas for the next month.

## Step 3: Post Content That People Share

On X, you have a short time to catch attention. A good post is short, clear, and useful or entertaining. A bad post is long, vague, or a pure advert.

Share useful tips in your field. A web designer can post "5 things to check before you pay a website designer in Nigeria." A skincare seller can post "3 mistakes that make your cream stop working." A food vendor can post "How to keep jollof rice fresh for a party." Tips position you as an expert, and experts get hired.

Share your work. Post a photo of a website you just finished, a dress you just sewed, a meal you just delivered, or a room you just cleaned. Add a short caption: what it is, who it is for, and how much it costs. Real work, posted regularly, builds a portfolio that anyone can scroll through.

Share customer stories, with permission. "Mrs Ade from Ikeja ordered 30 lunch packs for her office. Here is what she said." A short testimonial with a photo is more convincing than a long sales pitch.

Share behind-the-scenes moments. A short video of you packing an order, designing a logo, or cooking in your kitchen makes your business feel real. Nigerians buy from people they feel they know, and behind-the-scenes posts create that feeling.

Use a mix of formats: short text posts, photos, videos, polls, and threads. A poll — "Would you pay ₦3,500 for a lunch pack delivered to your office in Lekki? Yes or No?" — is a quick way to learn what your customers think, and it brings replies. A thread — five to eight tweets that explain one topic step by step — is a good way to show expertise and get retweets.

Post at times when Nigerians are online: early morning, 7 a.m. to 9 a.m., lunchtime, 12 p.m. to 2 p.m., and evening, 7 p.m. to 10 p.m. WAT. Test different times and see when your posts get the most replies. Consistency matters more than perfection: two or three good posts a day, every day, will grow your account faster than ten posts in one day and silence for a week.

## Step 4: Join Conversations, Do Not Just Broadcast

X is a conversation, not a television channel. If you only post and never reply, you will grow slowly. The fastest way to find customers is to join conversations where your customers already are.

Reply to questions in your field. When someone asks "Who can design a logo in Lagos?" reply helpfully, with a link to your work or a clear price. Do not just say "DM me." Say what you do, how much it costs, and why you are a good choice. A helpful reply can be seen by hundreds of people who read the original question.

Reply to big accounts in your niche, with useful additions. If a popular business account tweets about pricing, add a thoughtful reply with your own experience. If your reply is good, people will click your profile. This is one of the fastest ways for a small account to be discovered.

Quote-tweet, do not just retweet. When you share someone else's post, add your own thought: "This is true. In my work with small businesses in Lekki, I see the same mistake every week. Here is how to avoid it." A quote-tweet with your own insight is more valuable than a silent retweet.

Be kind and professional in replies. Never insult a competitor in a public reply, never argue with a stranger for hours, and never use a trending tragedy to promote your business. Nigerians remember rude or insensitive posts, and screenshots last forever.

## Step 5: Use Threads, Spaces and Trends Wisely

Threads are one of the best tools on X for a Nigerian business. A thread is a series of connected tweets that explain one topic in depth. A good thread can be retweeted hundreds of times and bring you followers for days.

Write threads about topics your customers care about. "How to start a POS business in Lagos: a thread." "7 mistakes small businesses make on Instagram." "What I charge for a website and why." Start with a strong first tweet that promises a clear benefit, then write each tweet as one clear point. End with a call to action: "Follow me for more tips" or "DM me if you need a website."

X Spaces are live audio conversations. You can join a Space about business, marketing, or your industry, listen, and speak when you have something useful to add. Speaking well in a Space can bring you followers who heard your voice and trusted your knowledge. You can also host your own Space once a week, for 30 minutes, answering questions about your field. Keep it simple, invite your followers, and announce it a day before.

Trends can help, but use them carefully. A trend about a football match, a new phone, or a Nollywood film can bring attention if you connect it naturally to your business. A trend about a tragedy, a disaster, or a sensitive political issue should never be used to sell. If you are not sure, do not use it.

Hashtags on X are less important than they used to be, but one or two relevant hashtags can help. Use #Lagos, #Nigeria, or a hashtag for your industry, such as #WebDesign or #MadeInNigeria, when it fits. Do not add ten hashtags to every post. It looks like spam and reduces engagement.

## Step 6: Turn Followers Into Customers

Followers are not customers until they buy. You need a clear path from a tweet to a sale, and you need to make it easy.

State your prices, or at least your starting prices, in your pinned tweet and on your website. Many Nigerians will not DM a seller who hides prices, because they assume the price will be high or inconsistent. A clear starting price — "Websites from ₦350,000" or "Lunch packs from ₦3,500" — removes that fear.

When someone replies or DMs you, reply quickly. On X, speed matters. A person who asks for a price at 10 a.m. may have found another seller by 2 p.m. if you do not reply. Set up notifications for DMs and replies, and check them at least three times a day.

Move serious buyers to WhatsApp. X is good for discovery, but WhatsApp is better for closing a sale, sharing photos, confirming payment, and arranging delivery. Say in your DM: "Thank you for your message. Please chat with me on WhatsApp on 0816 709 9131 so I can send you photos and confirm your order." That is a natural next step.

Use a simple offer to turn a follower into a first buyer. A first-time discount, a free consultation, a free sample, or a limited-time price can give a follower a reason to buy today instead of "sometime." Make the offer clear, give it a deadline, and honour it.

Keep a list of people who showed interest but did not buy. A polite follow-up a few days later — "Hello, just checking if you still need a website. I have a slot open this week" — can turn a forgotten conversation into a sale. One follow-up is enough. Do not pester people.

## Step 7: Grow Steadily Without Buying Followers

Never buy followers. Bought followers are fake accounts that never buy, never reply, and damage your credibility. A brand that checks your account will see thousands of followers and almost no engagement, and they will know. Grow slowly and honestly instead.

Follow people in your niche: customers, competitors, suppliers, and business accounts in Lagos, Abuja and Port Harcourt. Many will follow you back if your profile is clear and your posts are useful. Unfollow accounts that never engage after a while, so your timeline stays useful.

Engage every day for at least 20 minutes. Reply to five posts, like ten posts, and retweet two useful posts from others. Engagement is the currency of X. The more you engage genuinely, the more X shows your posts to others.

Track what works. Once a week, check which of your posts got the most likes, replies, retweets and profile visits. Was it a tip, a photo of your work, a price breakdown, or a customer story? Do more of what worked, and less of what did not. You do not need expensive analytics tools. X's built-in analytics, free on the platform, will show you enough.

Be patient. A new account may take two or three months to reach its first 1,000 real followers. That is normal. The followers you gain by being helpful are far more valuable than followers gained by a viral joke that has nothing to do with your business. A thousand followers who need what you sell are worth more than ten thousand followers who do not.

## Mistakes That Hurt Nigerian Businesses on X

- **Only posting adverts.** If every post is "Buy from us," people will mute you.
- **Hiding your prices.** "DM for price" on every post frustrates buyers and reduces enquiries.
- **Arguing in public.** A long argument with a stranger can be screenshotted and shared, and it scares away customers.
- **Copying another account's tweets word for word.** People notice, and it damages trust.
- **Posting at random times and then disappearing.** Consistency builds an audience. Silence loses it.
- **Using a trend about a tragedy to sell.** It is insensitive, and it will be remembered.
- **Ignoring DMs and replies.** A slow reply loses the sale to a faster seller.
- **Promising what you cannot deliver.** A customer who was disappointed on X will complain on X, publicly, to thousands of people.

## A Simple Daily X Routine

You do not need to spend all day on X. Thirty minutes a day, done well, is enough.

1. **Morning (10 minutes):** Post one useful tip or one photo of your work. Reply to any DMs and mentions from overnight.
2. **Midday (10 minutes):** Reply to three posts in your niche, and search for one question you can answer.
3. **Evening (10 minutes):** Post one more update — a customer story, a behind-the-scenes video, or a question for your followers. Check your analytics for the day.

Do that five days a week, and in three months you will have a profile full of useful posts, a growing list of real followers, and a steady flow of DMs from people who found you through a reply, a thread, or a recommendation.

X rewards businesses that show up, help people, and talk like real humans. It punishes businesses that only shout "Buy from us." If you set up a clear profile, post useful content, join conversations, reply quickly, and make it easy to buy, X will become one of your best sources of customers in Nigeria — not just for a week, but for as long as you keep showing up.
