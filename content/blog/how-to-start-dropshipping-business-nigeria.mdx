Dropshipping is a way of selling products online without keeping stock in your house or shop. When a customer orders from you, you pass the order to your supplier, who sends the product directly to the customer. You never touch the product, and you do not pay for it until the customer has paid you. Your profit is the difference between what the customer pays you and what you pay the supplier, minus delivery and advertising costs.

This model appeals to many Nigerians because the starting cost is low. You do not need to rent a shop in Lagos, buy goods in bulk from Balogun Market, or risk your money on stock that may not sell. A student in Abuja, a young mother in Port Harcourt, or a worker in Ikeja can start with a phone, a WhatsApp Business account, and a small budget for adverts. That low barrier is also the danger. Dropshipping is not free money, and many beginners lose money because they choose the wrong product, trust the wrong supplier, or spend too much on adverts before they understand their market.

This guide gives you an honest, practical path: how to choose a niche, find a supplier you can trust, set up your selling channel, price your products, handle payments and delivery in Nigeria, and avoid the mistakes that sink most new dropshippers.

## Step 1: Understand How Dropshipping Really Works in Nigeria

In the classic model, a customer orders on your store, you order from a supplier overseas, and the supplier ships the product to the customer. In Nigeria, that model has real problems: international shipping can take three to six weeks, customs can delay a parcel, and a customer who paid ₦15,000 will not happily wait a month for a phone case. Many successful Nigerian dropshippers use a local version instead. They find suppliers in Nigeria, often wholesalers in Lagos, Onitsha, or Kano, or importers with a warehouse in Lagos, who can deliver within one to five days. Others import a small test batch themselves, then dropship from that local stock through a supplier who holds it.

Your job as the dropshipper is to find customers, take orders, and provide good service. The supplier's job is to hold the product and deliver it. You need a clear agreement on who does what. Who pays for delivery? What happens if the product is damaged? What happens if the customer returns it? Get these answers in writing on WhatsApp before you take your first order. A supplier who cannot answer these questions clearly is not ready to work with you.

You should also understand your costs. Your selling price must cover the supplier price, delivery, advert costs, payment charges, and your profit. If you buy a product for ₦5,000, sell it for ₦8,000, and spend ₦2,000 on delivery and ₦1,500 on adverts per sale, you have lost money. Always calculate the full cost before you set a price, not after.

## Step 2: Choose a Niche and Products That Sell

A niche is the type of product you focus on. Beginners often try to sell everything, from shoes to electronics to kitchen items, and end up selling nothing well. A focused niche is easier to promote, because you learn what your customers want, which adverts work, and which suppliers are reliable. Good niches for Nigerian dropshipping include phone accessories, beauty and skincare products, fashion items such as watches and bags, home and kitchen gadgets, baby products, and fitness items.

Choose products that meet five tests. First, the product should solve a clear problem or satisfy a clear desire. A phone stand, a waist trainer, a mini blender, or a LED light solves a problem people understand in seconds. Second, it should be light and easy to deliver. Heavy or fragile products increase delivery costs and breakage. Third, it should have a good profit margin. Aim for a product you can sell for at least double what you pay the supplier, so that delivery and adverts do not wipe out your profit. Fourth, it should not be too common. If every vendor on Instagram sells the same item, you will compete on price alone. Fifth, it should be a product you can explain and defend. If a customer asks how it works, you should be able to answer confidently.

Test one or two products at a time, not ten. Pick a product, create a simple advert, and see if people ask about it. If you get many enquiries but few orders, the price or the trust may be the problem. If you get almost no enquiries, the product or the advert may be wrong. Learn from each test before you spend more money.

## Step 3: Find a Reliable Supplier

Your supplier is your business partner, even if you never meet them in person. A bad supplier will ruin your reputation with late deliveries, wrong items, and poor packaging. Finding a good one takes time, and it is worth that time.

1. **Start with local wholesalers.** Visit markets in your city, such as Balogun, Computer Village in Ikeja, or Trade Fair in Lagos, and ask wholesalers if they deliver to customers on your behalf. Many do, even if they do not advertise it. Ask for their price list on WhatsApp, their delivery time to Lagos, Abuja, and Port Harcourt, and their policy on damaged or wrong items.
2. **Check online suppliers carefully.** You can find suppliers on Instagram, Jumia, Konga, and Alibaba. For any supplier you find online, check their reviews, ask for a video of the product, and place a small test order for yourself first. If a supplier asks you to pay a large registration fee before you can see a price list, walk away. Real wholesalers earn money from selling products, not from registration fees.
3. **Test before you sell.** Order the product for yourself. Check the quality, the packaging, and how long delivery takes. Take your own photos and videos of the product you received, so your adverts show the real item, not a picture copied from the internet. If the quality is poor, find another supplier. Your customers will blame you, not the supplier, when a product disappoints.
4. **Agree the terms in writing.** Confirm the price, the delivery fee, the delivery time, and what happens on returns. Save the chat. This record protects you if there is a disagreement later. Keep a list of two or three backup suppliers for your best product, so one supplier going out of stock does not stop your business.

## Step 4: Set Up Your Selling Channel

Most Nigerian dropshippers start on WhatsApp and Instagram, because that is where their customers already are. You do not need a website on day one, but you do need a clear, trustworthy place where customers can see products and order.

Set up a WhatsApp Business account with a clear business name, a profile photo of your product or logo, a short description of what you sell, and your location. Use the catalogue feature to list each product with a clear photo, a price, and a short description. Use quick replies for common questions such as delivery time and payment details. Use labels to track orders as new, paid, sent, or delivered.

Set up an Instagram business account with the same name and the same profile photo. Post clear photos and short videos of your products, and use your bio to tell people what you sell and how to order. Link your WhatsApp number in your bio so a customer can tap and chat with you in seconds. Post regularly, at least three or four times a week, and reply to comments and messages quickly. A customer who waits hours for a reply will often buy from someone else.

When you are ready, you can add a simple online store or a website with product pages and a checkout. A store looks more professional and can take orders while you sleep, but it also needs more work to set up and promote. Start with WhatsApp and Instagram, prove that people will buy, and add a store when sales are steady.

## Step 5: Price Your Products Correctly

Pricing is a calculation, not a guess. Write down every cost for one order. Start with the supplier price. Add the delivery fee from the supplier to the customer. Add the payment charges for the method the customer uses. Add a share of your advertising cost for each sale. The total is your true cost. Your selling price must be higher than this total by enough to leave a profit worth your time.

For example, if a watch costs ₦6,000 from your supplier, delivery costs ₦2,000, payment charges are ₦200, and adverts cost you about ₦1,000 per sale, your true cost is ₦9,200. Selling the watch for ₦10,000 leaves only ₦800 profit, which is too small. Selling it for ₦13,000 leaves ₦3,800 profit, which is more reasonable. Do this calculation for every product before you advertise it. Many dropshippers discover too late that their best-selling product was their least profitable one.

Be honest about delivery fees. Some sellers advertise free delivery and then add the cost to the product price. Others charge delivery separately and state it clearly. Either approach can work, but the customer must know the total cost before they pay. Surprise charges at the point of payment are a common reason for abandoned orders in Nigeria. State your delivery time honestly too. If delivery takes three to five days, say so. A customer who expects two days and waits five will be unhappy, even if the product is good.

## Step 6: Handle Payments Safely

Payment is the most sensitive part of dropshipping in Nigeria, because you are asking a stranger to pay you before they receive a product. Most Nigerian customers prefer to pay on delivery, but that option is risky for a dropshipper. A customer can refuse the parcel, and you will have paid the supplier and the delivery cost for nothing. Many experienced dropshippers therefore ask for full payment before delivery, or at least a deposit, and they build trust so customers feel safe paying first.

Build trust in visible ways. Use a business account with a real name and photo. Post reviews from real customers, with their permission. Post videos of yourself packing orders or showing the product. Reply to messages promptly and politely. Use a payment link from a recognised Nigerian payment provider, which looks more professional than asking for a transfer to a personal account with no business name. Check the provider's current fees before you choose one.

Keep clear payment records. For every order, record the customer name, phone number, product, amount paid, date, and delivery address in a spreadsheet or a notebook. Send a confirmation message after payment, with the order details and the expected delivery time. Send another message when the product is sent, with the delivery tracking details if available. These messages reduce anxiety and reduce the number of "where is my order" messages you receive.

## Step 7: Manage Delivery and Customer Service

Delivery is where many Nigerian dropshipping businesses win or lose customers. Lagos traffic, poor addressing, and network problems can all cause delays. Choose a delivery partner with a good record in the areas you serve. Ask other sellers which dispatch companies they use and what they charge. Test the delivery service with your own order before you rely on it for customers.

Give the customer a realistic delivery time and keep them updated. If a parcel is delayed, tell the customer before they ask you. A short message such as "Hello, your order is on the way but traffic has delayed the rider. It should arrive tomorrow morning. Sorry for the delay" is far better than silence. Most customers will accept a delay if they are informed. They will not accept being ignored.

Handle problems calmly and quickly. If a product arrives damaged or wrong, apologise, ask for a photo, and offer a replacement or a refund according to the agreement you made with your supplier. Do not argue with the customer or blame the supplier in your reply. Your customer bought from you, and your response is what they will remember and tell others about. Keep a record of problems and which products cause them most often. If one product is returned again and again, stop selling it.

## Step 8: Promote Your Products Without Wasting Money

You need a steady flow of new customers, but you do not need a large advertising budget to start. Begin with free promotion. Post your products on WhatsApp Status, Instagram, Facebook, and TikTok. Use short videos that show the product in use. A video of a mini blender making a smoothie sells far better than a still photo of the blender in a box. Ask happy customers to send you a photo or a short video of the product, and share it with their permission. This is called social proof, and it is very powerful in Nigeria, where people trust recommendations from other buyers.

When you start paid adverts, start small. Spend ₦5,000 to ₦10,000 on one product and one advert, and watch the results for three to five days. Count how many people messaged you, how many ordered, and how much each sale cost you in adverts. If the advert brings sales at a profit, you can increase the budget slowly. If it brings many messages but no orders, the price, the product, or the trust may be the problem, not the advert. Do not increase the budget until the small test is profitable.

Avoid copying another seller's advert word for word. Use your own photos, your own words, and your own prices. Customers in Lagos, Abuja, and Port Harcourt see the same copied adverts many times a day. An original advert with a real photo of the product you hold in your hand will stand out.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

The first mistake is choosing a product because you like it, not because customers want it. Always test demand before you spend money on adverts.

The second mistake is trusting a supplier without testing them. Order the product yourself first, check the quality, and confirm delivery time before you advertise it.

The third mistake is pricing without counting delivery and advert costs. A product that looks profitable can lose money once all costs are included.

The fourth mistake is promising fast delivery you cannot control. Be honest about delivery time, and update the customer if there is a delay.

The fifth mistake is spending a large amount on adverts before you have proven that people will buy. Start with a small test, learn from it, and scale only what works.

The sixth mistake is poor customer service. In dropshipping, your service is your main advantage, because many sellers offer the same products. Reply quickly, be polite, and solve problems fast.

## Final Checklist

Before you launch, check that you have chosen one focused niche, tested one or two products, found a supplier you have ordered from yourself, agreed prices, delivery, and returns in writing, set up WhatsApp Business and Instagram with a clear catalogue, calculated the full cost and set a profitable price, set up a safe payment method, chosen a reliable delivery partner, and planned a small advertising test. If you can tick most of these boxes, you are ready to take your first order. Start small, learn from every order, and grow slowly. Dropshipping in Nigeria can work, but it works for sellers who treat it like a real business, with real suppliers, real costs, and real customer service, not for those looking for money without effort.
