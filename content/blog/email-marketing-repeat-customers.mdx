Every time you hear someone say "email marketing is dead," a smart business owner quietly adds another ₦100,000 to their monthly revenue from email.

Email marketing is not dead. It is not even close to dead. In Nigeria, where WhatsApp and social media dominate the conversation, email is actually an underutilized channel — which means it is a massive opportunity for businesses that use it well.

Here is why email marketing matters more than ever for Nigerian businesses, and exactly how to build a system that turns one-time buyers into loyal repeat customers.

## Why Email Marketing Beats Social Media

### You Own Your Email List

Your Instagram followers are not yours. They belong to Instagram. If the algorithm changes, your reach drops. If your account gets suspended, you lose everyone. Your email list, on the other hand, is yours. You can export it, import it to any platform, and reach your subscribers whenever you want.

### Email Has Higher Engagement

The average open rate for emails in Nigeria is 20-30%. The average engagement rate for organic Instagram posts is under 5%. Even with the spam and promotional filters, email delivers 4-6x better engagement than social media for most businesses.

### Email Drives More Sales

Email marketing has an average ROI of ₦4,200 for every ₦100 spent, according to industry data. That is higher than any other marketing channel including social media ads, search ads, and influencer marketing. For Nigerian businesses, the ROI is often even higher because the channel is less crowded.

### Email Enables Personalization

You can segment your email list by purchase history, location, interests, and behaviour. A customer who bought a dress last month gets different emails from a customer who browsed but did not buy. Social media does not offer this level of targeting without paid ads.

## Step 1: Build Your Email List

You cannot do email marketing without an email list. Here is how to build one ethically and effectively.

### Capture Emails on Your Website

Every visitor to your website is a potential email subscriber. Give them a reason to share their email address.

**Strategies that work for Nigerian businesses:**
- **Newsletter signup** — "Get weekly tech tips and business insights delivered to your inbox." Place this in your website footer, sidebar, and as a pop-up (but make the pop-up easy to dismiss).
- **Lead magnet** — Offer a free resource in exchange for an email address. A makeup artist could offer "Free Bridal Makeup Checklist." A web developer could offer "Website Launch Checklist." A restaurant could offer "Free Birthday Meal Coupon."
- **Discount incentive** — "Sign up for 10% off your first order." This is the most common and effective email capture strategy for e-commerce businesses.
- **Content upgrade** — When someone reads a blog post, offer a related free download. "Download the PDF version of this guide" is simple and effective.

### Collect Emails Offline

Do not limit yourself to online channels. Collect emails in your physical location too:
- Add an email signup field to your paper forms
- Ask for email addresses at the point of sale
- Run an in-store promotion that requires email signup
- Collect business cards at networking events and add people to your list

### Import Existing Customer Emails

If you already have customers who have purchased via bank transfer, WhatsApp, or in person, add them to your email list. This is not spam — they have already done business with you and would benefit from hearing about new products, offers, and updates.

### Important: Get Permission

Under Nigeria's Data Protection Regulation, you must have consent to send marketing emails. Your signup process should make it clear what subscribers will receive and how often. Every email must include an unsubscribe link.

## Step 2: Choose an Email Marketing Platform

You do not need an expensive enterprise tool to start. These platforms work well for Nigerian businesses:

**Mailchimp:** Free for up to 500 subscribers and 1,000 emails per month. Easy to use with drag-and-drop email builder, templates, and basic automation. The free plan is sufficient for most small businesses starting out.

**MailerLite:** More affordable than Mailchimp for growing lists. The free plan includes up to 1,000 subscribers. Strong automation features even on the free plan. Clean, modern templates.

**ConvertKit:** Designed for creators and content businesses. Excellent automation and segmentation. Starts at about ₦15,000/month for 1,000 subscribers. Best if you plan to build sophisticated email sequences.

**Brevo (formerly Sendinblue):** Good for transactional emails (order confirmations, shipping notifications) in addition to marketing emails. Pricing is based on emails sent rather than subscriber count.

**Benchmark Email:** Solid option with good deliverability in Nigeria. Competitive pricing and easy-to-use templates.

## Step 3: Write Emails People Actually Open

Getting subscribers is only half the battle. Getting them to open your emails is where the real work begins.

### Write Great Subject Lines

Your subject line determines whether your email gets opened or deleted. Here are proven patterns:

- **Curiosity gap:** "The one thing most Lagos businesses get wrong about websites"
- **Direct benefit:** "5 ways to double your repeat customers this month"
- **Personalized:** "Igono, your 10% discount expires tomorrow"
- **Urgency:** "Last chance: free consultation slots for July"
- **Numbers and lists:** "3 email templates that increased our sales by 40%"
- **Question:** "Are you making this website mistake?"

Avoid all-caps, excessive exclamation marks, and spammy words like "free money" or "limited time offer" — these trigger spam filters.

### Write Emails That Get Read

Once they open, your email needs to deliver value quickly.

**Structure your email:**
1. Greeting (keep it personal)
2. Hook (first sentence that makes them want to continue)
3. Value (the main content — tip, offer, story, or information)
4. Call to action (what you want them to do next)
5. Signature (your name and business)

**Email length:** Keep it concise. 100-300 words is ideal. If you need to write more, use a short email with a link to a blog post or video.

**Write like a human:** Nigerian consumers respond to authentic, conversational emails. Write as if you are emailing a friend or client, not a mass audience. Use "you" and "I" instead of "customers" and "we."

### Send at the Right Time

Email open rates vary by day and time. For Nigerian audiences, these patterns generally hold:
- Tuesdays, Wednesdays, and Thursdays have the highest open rates
- Tuesday to Thursday mornings (8 AM — 11 AM) perform best
- Weekends have lower open rates for business emails
- Sundays evenings can work well for consumer-focused content

Test different times and track your open rates to find what works for your specific audience.

## Step 4: Set Up Automated Email Sequences

Automation is where email marketing becomes a revenue machine. Instead of sending individual emails, you set up sequences that trigger automatically based on subscriber actions.

### Essential Sequences for Nigerian Businesses

**Welcome Sequence (3-5 emails)**

Sent automatically when someone subscribes. This is your chance to make a great first impression.

- Email 1 (immediate): Thank you, what to expect, link to your best content or product
- Email 2 (day 2): Your story — why you started your business, your values
- Email 3 (day 4): Your most popular product or service
- Email 4 (day 7): Social proof — testimonials, reviews, case studies
- Email 5 (day 10): Special offer for new subscribers

**Abandoned Cart Sequence (3 emails)**

Sent when a customer adds items to their cart but does not complete checkout. This single sequence can recover 10-15% of lost sales.

- Email 1 (1 hour after abandonment): "You left something in your cart" — show the items, make it easy to return to checkout
- Email 2 (24 hours after): "Still thinking about it?" — include a testimonial or review of the product
- Email 3 (72 hours after): "Last chance" — offer a small discount or free shipping to close the sale

**Post-Purchase Sequence (3-5 emails)**

Turn one-time buyers into repeat customers.

- Email 1 (immediate): Order confirmation and thank you (transactional, not marketing)
- Email 2 (3 days after): "How to get the most out of your purchase" — tips, usage guide, care instructions
- Email 3 (7 days after): Request a review or testimonial
- Email 4 (14 days after): Related product recommendations
- Email 5 (30 days after): "We miss you" — special offer for next purchase

**Re-engagement Sequence (2-3 emails)**

For subscribers who have not opened your emails in 3 months.

- Email 1: "Are you still there?" — ask if they want to continue receiving emails
- Email 2: "We have new things" — showcase your best recent content or products
- Email 3: Final attempt with a compelling offer. If no response, remove them from your list

## Step 5: Measure and Improve

Email marketing improves with data. Track these key metrics:

**Open rate:** Percentage of subscribers who open your email. A good open rate for Nigerian businesses is 20-30%. If yours is below 15%, work on your subject lines.

**Click-through rate (CTR):** Percentage of subscribers who click a link in your email. A good CTR is 2-5%. If yours is lower, work on your content and calls to action.

**Conversion rate:** Percentage of subscribers who complete your desired action (purchase, signup, download). This varies by offer but 1-3% is typical for promotional emails.

**Unsubscribe rate:** Percentage of subscribers who unsubscribe. Anything under 0.5% per email is normal. If higher, review your email frequency and relevance.

**Revenue per email:** Total revenue attributed to an email campaign divided by number of emails sent. This is the ultimate measure of email marketing ROI.

## Common Email Marketing Mistakes

### Buying Email Lists

Never buy an email list. Purchased lists are full of people who did not consent to receive your emails. You will get spam complaints, high unsubscribe rates, and potentially get your email platform account suspended. Build your list organically.

### Sending Too Frequently

Sending daily emails often leads to fatigue and unsubscribes. Weekly or bi-weekly is ideal for most businesses. If you have valuable content, twice per week can work. Test and adjust.

### Only Sending Sales Emails

If every email is a sales pitch, subscribers will lose interest. Follow the 80/20 rule: 80% of your emails should provide value (tips, education, entertainment, stories), and 20% can be direct promotions. When you do send a promotional email, subscribers are more receptive because you have built goodwill with valuable content.

### Not Segmenting Your List

Sending the same email to everyone is inefficient. Segment your list by:
- Purchase history (first-time buyer vs repeat customer)
- Interests (what content they engaged with)
- Location (Lagos customers may get different offers from Abuja customers)
- Engagement level (active subscribers vs inactive ones)

Segmented campaigns consistently outperform non-segmented ones by 30-50% or more.

### Ignoring Mobile Optimization

Over 70% of emails in Nigeria are opened on mobile devices. Use a responsive email template, keep subject lines under 40 characters, use a single-column layout, and make buttons large enough to tap with a thumb.

## Getting Started Today

To start your email marketing journey:

1. Sign up for a free MailerLite or Mailchimp account
2. Add a signup form to your website
3. Import any existing customer email addresses (with consent)
4. Write and send your first welcome email
5. Set up your welcome sequence (3-5 emails)
6. Set up your post-purchase sequence (3-5 emails)
7. Schedule one educational email per week for the next 4 weeks
8. Track your open rates, click rates, and unsubscribes
9. Adjust based on data
