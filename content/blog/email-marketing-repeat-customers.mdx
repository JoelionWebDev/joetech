Kemi sells hair products from a shop on Allen Avenue in Ikeja. Leave-in conditioner, hair growth oil, edge control, braiding hair. Her shop is called Kemi's Hair Hub, and her problem is one every Lagos trader knows: the first sale is easy, the second sale is where the money hides, and most of her customers never come back for it.

She checked her records one slow Monday in October. In the previous six months, 412 people had bought from her. Only 96 of them had bought twice. That means 316 people paid her once, took their products home, and vanished. Some found another shop. Some finished the product and forgot where they bought it. Some just needed a reminder. Kemi was spending money every month chasing new customers on Instagram while the people who already trusted her with their money were quietly drifting away.

She did not need more ads. She needed three kinds of email, sent to the right people at the right moment: win-back emails for customers gone quiet, replenishment reminders for customers whose products should be running out, and VIP emails for the small group who buy the most. She built all three in one week, using a free MailerLite account and the sales book she already kept. Within two months, her repeat orders rose from 96 to 141. Here are the three emails she actually sends.

## Know Who Went Quiet, and When

Before writing anything, Kemi sorted her buyers by their last order date. Her leave-in conditioner, ₦8,500 a bottle, lasts the average customer about five weeks. Her hair growth oil, ₦6,000, lasts about four weeks. Her edge control, ₦4,500, lasts about six weeks. So a customer who bought the leave-in on 1 October should be running out around 5 November. If Kemi hears nothing by 12 November, that customer has either bought somewhere else or forgotten her entirely.

She drew three lines. A customer who has not ordered in 45 days is "due a nudge." A customer who has not ordered in 90 days is "gone quiet." A customer who has bought four times or more, whatever the date, is a VIP. She tagged all three groups in MailerLite, using the order dates from her sales book. The tagging is the part people skip, and it is the part that makes everything else work: the email only feels personal when it arrives at the moment the customer is actually thinking about the product.

## The Win-Back Email: One Honest Chance

The win-back email goes to the "gone quiet" group — people who have not ordered in 90 days. Kemi sends it once, then waits two weeks, then sends one shorter follow-up. If there is still no reply, she stops chasing. She does not delete them. She just stops emailing them about buying.

The mistake most businesses make with win-back emails is begging. "We miss you so much, please come back, here is 50% off." It reads like desperation, and customers can smell it. Kemi's version does something different. It reminds the customer what she bought, asks a genuine question, and makes one clear offer.

Here is the first win-back email, exactly as Kemi sends it. The customer in this example is Ngozi, who bought a leave-in conditioner and an edge control on 12 August and has not been back since.

---

**Subject: Ngozi, did the leave-in work for your hair?**

Hi Ngozi,

It is Kemi, from Kemi's Hair Hub on Allen Avenue.

You bought our leave-in conditioner and edge control back in August, and I have been wondering how they worked for you. The leave-in is the one most of my customers either love or find too heavy, and I honestly want to know which one it was for you.

If it worked, I have kept a bottle aside for you at the same ₦8,500, and I will add a small bottle of our hair growth oil, ₦6,000, for ₦3,000 if you order before Friday. If it did not work, reply and tell me. I can suggest something lighter that suits your hair better.

Either way, thank you for buying from us in August. I hope the hair is growing well.

Kemi
Kemi's Hair Hub, Allen Avenue, Ikeja
WhatsApp: 0803 555 0147

---

Read that email again and notice what is missing. There is no "WE MISS YOU!!!" in capital letters. There is a real question, a real memory of what Ngozi bought, and an offer that only makes sense if she comes back. The discount is small and specific — ₦3,000 off the oil — not a desperate blanket cut.

The follow-up, sent two weeks later to anyone who did not reply, is shorter. The subject is "Should I stop emailing you, Ngozi?" It says she will stop sending offers but keep Ngozi on the list for hair tips, and the leave-in is ₦8,500 and one WhatsApp message away. People who ignore a discount will answer a polite goodbye, and half the time the reply turns into an order.

Kemi's numbers: she sent 74 win-back emails in October. Nineteen replied, eleven ordered, spending an average of ₦11,200 each. The emails cost her nothing but an evening.

## The Replenishment Reminder: "Your Cream Should Be Finishing"

The replenishment reminder is the quiet earner of the three. It goes to customers whose product should be running out, based on the date they bought it and how long the product lasts. Kemi sends it a few days before the product should finish, not after, because a reminder that arrives when the bottle is already empty is a reminder to buy from whoever is closest, and that might not be her.

She sets the timing by product. Leave-in conditioner: 35 days after purchase. Hair growth oil: 28 days after purchase. Edge control: 40 days after purchase. She worked these out by asking twenty regulars how long a bottle lasted them, and taking the middle answer.

Here is the replenishment email, exactly as Kemi sends it. The customer is Adaeze, who bought a hair growth oil on 3 October.

---

**Subject: Adaeze, your hair oil should be finishing this week**

Hi Adaeze,

Quick one from Kemi at Kemi's Hair Hub.

You bought our hair growth oil on 3 October. Most people finish a bottle in about four weeks, so yours should be running low right about now.

If you want another one, it is still ₦6,000, and delivery within Ikeja is free this week if you order before Saturday. Just reply to this email or send me a WhatsApp message and I will have it sent out the same day.

One small thing: shake the bottle well before each use. A few customers told me they forgot, and the oil works much better when it is properly mixed.

Kemi
Kemi's Hair Hub, Allen Avenue, Ikeja
WhatsApp: 0803 555 0147

---

Notice the detail at the end. "Shake the bottle well before each use" is not a sales line. It is a useful tip, and it is the reason this email does not feel like an advert. Adaeze reads it, thinks Kemi actually cares whether the oil works, and reorders. Kemi sends about thirty of these reminders a month, and roughly a third turn into orders within a week — about ₦74,000 a month she used to lose to forgetfulness.

## The VIP Email: Treat the Best Buyers Like They Matter

The third group is the smallest and the most valuable. Kemi has 31 VIP customers — people who have bought four times or more. One of them, Mrs. Balogun, has spent ₦186,000 in the shop since February. These people do not need a discount to come back. They need to feel that Kemi knows who they are.

The VIP email goes out once a month, and it never looks like the emails everyone else gets. The VIPs hear about new products three days before the rest of the list. They get first pick of limited stock. Twice a year, Kemi sends them a small physical gift with their order — a satin bonnet that costs her ₦2,500 to buy in bulk, wrapped in tissue paper with a handwritten note.

Here is the VIP email Kemi sent in November, when a new shea butter hair mask arrived and she had only 20 jars.

---

**Subject: For my regulars first: the new hair mask (20 jars only)**

Dear Mrs. Balogun,

I am writing to you before I tell anyone else.

A new shea butter hair mask arrived in the shop this morning. It is ₦9,500 a jar, and I only have 20 jars. I am not putting it on Instagram until Friday, and I am not telling the general list until Thursday. You are hearing about it today because you have been buying from us since February, and I wanted you to have first pick.

If you want one, reply to this email or WhatsApp me and I will keep a jar aside for you until Wednesday evening. No payment needed now. Just let me know.

Thank you for sticking with this small shop for so long. It genuinely means a lot to me.

Kemi
Kemi's Hair Hub, Allen Avenue, Ikeja
WhatsApp: 0803 555 0147

---

That email is doing three things at once. It gives Mrs. Balogun early access, which is a real benefit. It gives her a deadline, Wednesday evening, which is polite rather than pushy. And it thanks her by name, for something specific — buying since February — which no automated template can fake. Fourteen of the twenty jars were claimed by VIPs within a day.

The lesson is that VIP treatment is not about the discount. Kemi rarely discounts for VIPs at all. It is about order: who hears first, who gets first pick, who gets thanked by name. People who spend the most want to be treated like they spend the most.

## Making It Run Without Eating Your Week

All three emails run on simple rules inside MailerLite, and Kemi set them up in a single afternoon with her nephew, who handles the shop's Instagram. The win-back fires automatically when a customer crosses 90 days without an order. The replenishment reminder fires at 28, 35 or 40 days depending on the product, using the purchase date Kemi records in her sales book. The VIP email is the only one she writes by hand each month, because it needs her voice and her judgment about what is worth announcing early.

She checks the results on the first Monday of every month. How many win-backs replied? How many replenishment reminders turned into orders? How many VIPs claimed the early offer? October's numbers told her the replenishment reminders were earning more than the win-backs, so in November she tightened the timing — oil reminders moved from 30 days to 28, and orders went up again.

She also watches the unsubscribe rate. So far it has stayed under one in a hundred, which tells her the emails read like messages from a shop owner, not a marketing machine.

Your first sale gets a customer through the door. These three emails decide whether she ever comes back through it. Start with the replenishment reminder — it is the easiest to set up and it pays first. Then add the win-back. Then pick your twenty best customers and write them a VIP email this week. You already know their names. They are in your sales book.
