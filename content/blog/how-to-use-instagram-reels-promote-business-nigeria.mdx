A customer in Lekki is scrolling Instagram at night. She is not looking for your business. She is looking for entertainment. Then a short video appears: a baker in Abuja decorating a cake, a tailor in Yaba turning fabric into a dress in 30 seconds, a skincare seller in Port Harcourt showing a real result. She watches it twice, follows the page, and sends a WhatsApp message. That is the power of Instagram Reels.

Reels are short vertical videos, usually 15 to 90 seconds long. Instagram shows them to people who do not follow you yet, making them one of the best free ways for a small Nigerian business to reach new customers. You do not need a professional camera, a studio, or a large advertising budget. A phone, good light and a clear idea are enough to start.

This guide shows you how to use Reels properly. You will learn what to post, how to film with your phone, how to write captions that sell, how often to post, and how to turn viewers into paying customers.

## Why Reels Work for Nigerian Small Businesses

Reels work because people prefer watching to reading. A photo of a dress tells a customer what it looks like. A 30-second Reel shows how it moves, how it fits, and how happy the customer looks wearing it. A photo of food tells a customer what it looks like. A Reel shows the steam, the plating, and the first bite. Video builds trust faster than a photo alone.

Reels also reach people beyond your followers. When you post a photo, Instagram mainly shows it to people who already follow you. When you post a Reel, Instagram can show it on the Reels feed and the Explore page to people who have never heard of you. For a small business in Lagos or Abuja, that free reach is valuable. One good Reel can bring in more enquiries than a week of regular posts.

Reels are also cheap to make. You can film in your shop, your kitchen, your studio or your home. You can use natural daylight from a window instead of expensive lights. You can edit inside the Instagram app for free. The main cost is your time, and even that can be kept low once you have a simple routine.

Finally, Reels help customers remember you. People forget most of the posts they scroll past, but they remember a face, a voice and a useful tip. If you show up regularly with helpful, entertaining Reels, your business stays in their mind when they are ready to buy.

## Step 1: Set Up Your Instagram Page Properly

Before you post your first Reel, make sure your page is ready for new visitors. A Reel can bring a thousand people to your page in one day. If your page is confusing, they will leave without following or buying.

Start with your bio. In two lines, say what you sell, who it is for, and where you are. For example: "Handmade cakes in Lekki, Lagos. Orders via WhatsApp. Delivery across Lagos." Add your WhatsApp link so a visitor can message you in one tap. Test the link yourself to be sure it works.

Use a clear profile photo. For a personal brand, use a clear photo of your face. For a shop, use your logo. Make sure the photo is not blurry and can be recognised at a small size.

Turn your account into a business or creator account if you have not already done so. This is free and it gives you insights that show how many people watched your Reels, how many followed you, and which Reels performed best. You will need these numbers later to know what to post more of.

Pin your three best posts to the top of your profile. These could be your best-selling product, a customer review, or a Reel that explains what you do. A new visitor should understand your business in ten seconds.

Clean up your highlights. Create highlights for prices, reviews, delivery information, and how to order. When a customer watches a Reel and visits your page, they should be able to find the price and ordering steps without sending you ten questions.

## Step 2: Choose Reel Ideas That Sell

The biggest mistake small businesses make is posting Reels that only say "Buy from me." People scroll past adverts. They stop for useful, interesting or entertaining videos. Your Reels should give value first and sell second.

Here are proven Reel ideas that work for Nigerian businesses. Use the ones that fit your business, and repeat the ones that get the best response.

Show the process. Film yourself making the product, preparing the food, sewing the dress, fixing the phone, or setting up the event hall. People love watching work happen. A 30-second time-lapse of a cake being decorated or a wig being installed can get far more views than a plain product photo.

Show the result. Film a before-and-after. A dirty sofa before cleaning and clean after. A plain face before makeup and glamorous after. An empty room before decoration and beautiful after. Before-and-after videos are simple and they prove your skill without you having to say a word.

Answer a common question. If customers always ask how to care for your product, make a Reel about it. "How to keep your braids fresh for three weeks." "How to store your cake so it stays soft." "Three mistakes to avoid when buying a wig." These videos position you as an expert and they save you from answering the same question many times.

Show customer reviews. With the customer's permission, film a short clip of them talking about their experience, or show a screenshot of their WhatsApp message while you talk over it. Real reviews build trust faster than anything you say about yourself.

Show your prices and packages. Many Nigerian customers do not message a seller because they fear the price will be too high. A Reel that says "Our cakes start at ₦15,000" or "Full glam makeup is ₦30,000 in Lekki" removes that fear and brings in serious buyers.

Share a day in your life. A short, honest video of your working day helps customers connect with you as a person. Show your shop opening in the morning, your first customer, your lunch break, and your closing time. Keep it simple and real.

## Step 3: Film Good Reels With Your Phone

You do not need a professional camera. A phone made in the last few years is good enough. What matters more is light, sound and stability.

Film in good light. Natural daylight is best. Film near a window during the day, or outside in the shade. Avoid filming with a bright window behind you, because your face will look dark. Avoid yellow indoor bulbs if you can. If you film at night, use a small ring light, which you can buy in Lagos markets or online for a few thousand naira.

Film vertically. Reels are vertical videos, so hold your phone upright. Film in the highest quality your phone allows, and clean your camera lens before you start. A dirty lens makes the whole video look dull.

Keep the camera steady. Rest your phone on a table, a stack of books, or a cheap tripod. Shaky video looks unprofessional and makes people scroll away. If you are moving around, walk slowly and keep your elbows close to your body.

Record clear sound. If you are speaking, film in a quiet place. Turn off the fan and television. Speak clearly and a little louder than normal. If the background noise is too much, add music and use text on the screen to explain what is happening instead of speaking.

Keep your Reels short at first. Aim for 15 to 30 seconds. A short Reel is easier to film, easier to edit, and more likely to be watched to the end. Instagram notices when people watch a Reel to the end, and it shows that Reel to more people. Once you are comfortable, you can try longer Reels of 45 to 90 seconds for tutorials.

Film more clips than you need. If you are showing a process, film each step separately. You can cut the boring parts later. It is better to have too much footage than to realise you missed a key step after you have finished.

## Step 4: Edit Simply and Add Text

You do not need complicated editing. Instagram's own editor, or a free app like CapCut, is enough. Keep your edits clean and fast.

Cut out pauses, mistakes and boring parts. The first three seconds are the most important. Start with the most interesting moment, a clear promise, or a question. "Watch how this ₦20,000 fabric becomes a party dress" is stronger than "Hello everyone, welcome to my page." If you do not hook the viewer in three seconds, they will scroll away.

Add text on the screen. Many people watch Reels without sound, especially in public places or at work. Text tells them what the video is about even when the sound is off. Use large, clear text that is easy to read on a phone. Keep each line short, with no more than a few words per line.

Add music that fits. Instagram offers a large library of trending sounds. Using a trending sound can help Instagram show your Reel to more people. Choose music that matches the mood of your video. A calm song fits a skincare routine. An energetic song fits a fashion transformation. Do not use music so loud that it drowns out your voice.

Add captions if you speak. Captions help people who watch without sound, and they help people who do not hear well. Instagram can add automatic captions, and you can edit them if they are wrong.

## Step 5: Write Captions That Turn Viewers Into Customers

The video gets the attention. The caption does the selling. A good caption is short, clear and ends with a clear next step.

Start the caption with the main point. Do not hide the price or the offer at the end of a long paragraph. "Braids for ₦25,000 in Lekki. Book on WhatsApp" is clear. A customer who reads the first line already knows what you offer and what to do next.

Explain the benefit in one or two sentences. Tell the customer what they will get, not just what the product is. "This wig is easy to install, lasts for months with proper care, and comes in three colours" is more useful than "Wig for sale."

End with a call to action. Tell the viewer exactly what to do. "Send us a WhatsApp message to order." "Tap the link in our bio to book." "Comment PRICE and we will send you the details." One clear action is better than three different actions.

Use hashtags, but do not overdo it. Five to ten relevant hashtags are enough. Use a mix of general and local tags, such as #LagosBusiness, #MadeInNigeria, #LekkiSalon, #NigerianFashion or #AbujaBaker, depending on your business. Avoid using 30 hashtags that have nothing to do with your video. Instagram may treat that as spam.

Reply to comments quickly. When someone comments on your Reel, reply within a few hours. A reply shows Instagram that your Reel is engaging, and it shows the commenter that you care. Ask a question in your caption to encourage comments, such as "Which colour do you prefer, red or blue?" or "Have you tried this before?"

## Step 6: Post Regularly and at the Right Time

Consistency matters more than perfection. Posting three Reels a week, every week, will bring better results than posting ten Reels in one week and then disappearing for a month. Choose a schedule you can keep. For most small businesses, three to four Reels a week is a good target.

Post when your customers are online. For many Nigerian businesses, good times are early morning before work, lunchtime, and evening after work, roughly 7am to 9am, 12pm to 2pm, and 7pm to 9pm. Test different times and check your Instagram insights to see when your own followers are most active. Your customers may be different from the general pattern, so trust your own data.

Do not post and disappear. After you post a Reel, stay online for 30 minutes to reply to comments and messages. Early engagement helps Instagram show your Reel to more people. Share the Reel to your Stories as well, so followers who missed it in their feed can still see it.

## How to Know What Is Working

Check your Instagram insights every week. Look at four numbers for each Reel: views, likes and comments, shares and saves, and new followers. Views tell you how many people saw the Reel. Shares and saves tell you that people found it useful enough to keep or send to someone else. New followers tell you that the Reel brought in people who want to hear from you again.

Pay special attention to shares and saves. A Reel with many shares is being recommended by viewers to their friends. That is free advertising. A Reel with many saves is useful, and people will return to it later. Make more Reels like the ones that get shares and saves.

Look at watch time if you can. If most people leave after three seconds, your opening needs work. If they watch to the end, your idea and editing are working. ## Common Mistakes to Avoid

The first mistake is posting only adverts. If every Reel is "Buy this, buy that," people will stop watching. Mix your content. For every sales Reel, post two helpful or entertaining Reels. The helpful Reels build the audience that the sales Reels then convert.

The second mistake is copying another business exactly. You can learn from other businesses, but your customers chose you for your own style, your own voice, and your own products. Use trends as a starting point, then make them your own. A trend that works for a fashion seller in Lagos may not work in the same way for a food seller in Enugu.

The third mistake is poor sound or poor light. A great idea filmed in a dark room with noisy background sound will not perform well. Take five minutes to find good light and a quiet place before you film.

The fourth mistake is ignoring the caption and the call to action. A beautiful video with no price, no location and no next step leaves the customer confused. Always tell the viewer what to do next.

The fifth mistake is giving up too early. Your first five Reels may get few views. That is normal. Instagram needs time to learn who likes your content, and you need time to learn what your audience likes. Keep posting, keep improving one thing at a time, and judge your progress after a month, not after a day.

## Turning Viewers Into Paying Customers

Views alone do not pay your bills. You need a clear path from a Reel to a sale. Make that path as short as possible. A viewer should be able to go from your Reel to your WhatsApp chat in two taps. Check that path yourself. Watch your Reel, tap your profile, tap the WhatsApp link, and see if it opens correctly.

When a viewer messages you, reply fast. A customer who saw your Reel today may buy today, or they may forget by tomorrow. A fast reply, a clear price, and a simple ordering process will close the sale. Use saved replies in WhatsApp Business for common questions about price, delivery and payment, so you can answer quickly without typing the same message every time.

## Final Thoughts

Instagram Reels are a free and powerful way to promote a small business in Nigeria, but they only work if you use them with a plan. Set up your page properly, choose ideas that give value, film with good light and clear sound, edit simply, write captions that tell people what to do next, and post regularly. Check your insights every week, make more of what works, and fix what does not.

You do not need to be perfect. Your first Reels will not be your best Reels, and that is fine. Start with what you have, film in your shop or home, and improve a little with each video. Over time, your Reels will bring in new followers, new enquiries, and new customers. In a crowded market, the business that shows up on video, again and again, is the business that customers remember when they are ready to buy.
