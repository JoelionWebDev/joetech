Ask a Nigerian business owner for their price list, and you will often get one of three answers: "DM me for price", a long chat message with prices buried in the middle, or a blurry photo of a handwritten list. None of these help a customer decide quickly. The customer who has to ask for every price will often give up and buy from a competitor whose prices are clear.

A price list is one of the simplest and most powerful tools a small business can have. It answers the first question every customer asks — "How much is it?" — before they even ask it. A clear price list saves you time, because you stop typing the same prices again and again. It builds trust, because a business that shows its prices looks honest. And it helps customers buy more, because when they can see everything you offer and what it costs, they can plan their order.

But most price lists fail because they are hard to read, out of date, or missing the information a customer needs. A list with tiny text, no product descriptions, no delivery fees, and prices from six months ago will confuse customers and cost you sales.

This guide explains how to create a price list customers actually read. You will learn what to include, how to organise it, how to format it for WhatsApp, print, and your shop, how to price your items honestly, and how to keep the list updated so customers can always trust it.

## Why a Price List Matters More Than You Think

A price list saves you time every single day. If you sell food, clothes, hair, or cakes, you probably answer the same price questions dozens of times a week. A customer asks the price of jollof rice, you reply. Another asks the price of a wig, you reply. A clear price list that you can send in one tap ends that repetition. You send the list once, and the customer finds the answer themselves.

It also helps customers decide faster. When a customer can see all your products and prices together, they can compare options and make a choice without waiting for your reply. A customer who sees that a small cake is ₦15,000 and a large cake is ₦25,000 can decide immediately. A customer who has to ask for each price will take longer, and may lose interest while waiting.

A price list builds trust. In Nigeria, many customers are suspicious of businesses that hide their prices, because they worry the price will change depending on who is asking. A business that shows its prices openly looks confident and honest. Customers are more likely to buy from a business they trust, and a price list is one of the easiest ways to earn that trust.

## Decide What Goes on Your Price List

Before you design anything, decide what belongs on the list. Start with your products or services, and write down every item you sell regularly. If you sell food, list each dish, each size, and each pack. If you sell hair, list each length, each style, and each colour. If you offer services, list each service and what it includes.

Group your items into clear categories. A food vendor might have categories such as Rice Dishes, Soups, Small Chops, and Drinks. A fashion seller might have Dresses, Tops, Trousers, and Accessories. A salon might have Hair, Nails, and Makeup. Categories help a customer find what they want quickly, instead of scrolling through a long, random list.

For each item, decide what details a customer needs. At minimum, they need the name of the item and the price. But a little more detail helps them choose. For food, add the portion size or the number of pieces. For clothes, add the sizes available. For hair, add the length and the texture. For services, add what is included and how long it takes. A customer who knows exactly what they are getting is more likely to buy.

Think about what not to include. Do not include your cost price, your supplier's name, or internal notes. Do not include prices for items you are not sure about. And do not include a long story about your business on the price list itself. The list should be about the customer's choice, not your history. Your story belongs in your bio or on your website.

## Price Your Items Honestly Before You List Them

A price list is only as good as the prices on it. Before you write a single price, make sure each one is honest and profitable. That means knowing what each item costs you, including the ingredients or the purchase price, the packaging, the transport, and your time. A price that does not cover your costs will lose you money on every sale, no matter how many customers you attract.

Be consistent in how you show prices. If you write ₦5,000 for one item, do not write "5k" for another and "N5000" for a third. Use the same format throughout the list, so customers can scan it quickly. Always include the naira symbol, so there is no doubt about the currency.

Show the price for each size or option, not just the cheapest one. If a cake costs ₦15,000 for a small size, ₦25,000 for a medium, and ₦40,000 for a large, list all three. A customer who only sees the cheapest price may feel misled when they discover the size they want costs more. Showing all the options builds trust and helps the customer choose.

Include delivery fees separately, and be clear about them. If delivery in Lekki is ₦1,500 and delivery to Ikeja is ₦4,000, say so on the list or in a note at the bottom. A customer who sees a food price of ₦4,000 and then discovers a delivery fee of ₦4,000 at checkout will feel cheated. A customer who sees both prices upfront can decide whether the total is worth it.

## Organise Your List So Customers Can Scan It

A price list is not a document to be read from start to finish. It is a document to be scanned. A customer will look at it for a few seconds, find the category they want, and check the price. Your job is to make that scan easy.

Use clear headings for each category, in a larger or bolder font than the items underneath. A heading such as "Rice Dishes" or "Hair" should stand out, so a customer can jump to the right section. Leave a small space between categories, so the list does not look like one long block of text.

Keep each line short. An item name, a short description, and the price is enough. For example, "Jollof Rice and Chicken (1 plate) — ₦4,000" is clear. "Jollof Rice, cooked with fresh tomatoes and peppers, served with grilled chicken and a side of salad, available in small, medium and large portions, prices start from..." is too long for a price list. Save the long description for your menu or your website.

Add a short note at the bottom for the important details: your delivery areas, your delivery fees, your order cut-off time, and how to pay. A note such as "Delivery available in Lekki, Ajah and Yaba. Order before 10 a.m. for lunch delivery. Payment by transfer or Paystack." answers the questions a customer will ask next, without cluttering the main list.

## Design a Price List That Looks Professional

A price list does not need to be fancy, but it needs to be clean and readable. Start with your business name at the top, in a clear font, with your logo if you have one. Add the date or the month, such as "Price List — November 2026", so customers know it is current. A list with no date looks old, even if the prices are new.

Use your brand colours, but use them sparingly. A coloured heading or a coloured line under your business name is enough. A price list with a bright background, coloured text, and coloured prices is hard to read. Black or dark text on a white or light background is the easiest to read, and it prints well too.

Check the list on your phone before you share it. Most of your customers will see it on a phone, so open the file on your phone and check that the text is readable without zooming, that nothing is cut off, and that the prices line up correctly. If the list is a PDF, make sure the file is not too large, so it downloads quickly on a slow connection.

If you want a designed version, you can create it in Canva, using a simple template with your brand colours and logo. A Canva design is good for sharing on WhatsApp status or Instagram, while a simple document or PDF is better for sending to a customer who wants to read it carefully. Keep both versions, and make sure the prices match.

## Format Your Price List for WhatsApp, Print, and Your Shop

One price list does not fit every place. A list that works as a PDF may be too long for a WhatsApp message, and a list that works on a screen may be too small to read on a printed sheet. Create the right format for each place your customers will see it.

For WhatsApp, create a short version. A long PDF can be sent as a file, but many customers will not open it. A short text version, with your most popular items and prices, can be pasted directly into a chat. Keep it to ten or fifteen items, with clear lines and prices. Save it in your notes app, so you can copy and paste it in seconds when a customer asks.

For WhatsApp Business, add your prices to your catalogue. Each product in your catalogue can have a photo, a name, a price, and a description. When a customer opens your catalogue, they can see your prices without asking. Update the catalogue whenever your prices change, so it always matches your price list.

For print, create a clean, one-page or two-page version. Use a larger font, because a printed list will be read from a distance in a shop or at a market stall. Print it on good paper, laminate it if it will be handled often, and place it where customers can see it. A printed list in a shop saves your staff from answering the same questions all day.

## Keep Your Price List Updated and Honest

A price list is only useful if it is accurate. In Nigeria, prices change often, and a list that is out of date will cause problems. A customer who orders at an old price and is then asked to pay a new price will feel cheated, even if the increase is fair. A customer who sees a product on your list that you no longer stock will lose trust in the whole list.

Set a routine for updating your list. Check your prices at least once a month, and update the list whenever a supplier changes a price, or whenever you add or remove a product. When you update the list, change the date at the top, so customers know it is current. A list dated "November 2026" tells a customer the prices are fresh. A list with no date, or a date from six months ago, tells them the opposite.

Remove items you no longer sell, and add new items as soon as you start selling them. A customer who sees a new product on your list may order it the same day. A customer who asks for a product on your list and is told you do not sell it anymore will wonder what else on the list is wrong.

## Common Mistakes People Make with Price Lists

The first mistake is hiding prices and writing "DM for price". Some business owners think hiding the price will make customers message them, and that a chat will lead to a sale. In practice, it does the opposite. Most customers will not message a business just to ask a price, because they do not want to be pressured. They will buy from a competitor whose price is clear.

The fourth mistake is forgetting delivery and extra charges. A price list that shows only the product price, and hides the delivery fee, the service charge, or the packaging cost, will lead to arguments at checkout. Show the full cost, or clearly state that delivery is charged separately, with the fees listed. Honesty about the total price builds trust.

The fifth mistake is never updating the list. A price list from January, still being shared in November, will have wrong prices, missing products, and items you no longer sell. Set a monthly reminder to review your list, update the prices, remove old items, add new ones, and change the date. A current list is a trustworthy list.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Should I show my prices publicly, or ask customers to DM me?

Show your prices publicly. A clear price list saves you time, builds trust, and helps customers decide faster. Customers who have to message you just to ask a price often give up and buy elsewhere. The few customers who prefer to negotiate will still message you, even when your prices are public.

### How often should I update my price list?

Check your prices at least once a month, and update the list whenever a supplier changes a price or you add or remove a product. In periods when prices change quickly, you may need to update more often. Always change the date at the top of the list when you update it, so customers know it is current.

### What format is best for a price list: PDF, image, or text?

Each format has a use. A PDF is best for a detailed list that a customer can save and read carefully. An image is best for sharing on WhatsApp status or Instagram. A short text version is best for pasting into a chat. Keep a PDF and a designed image, and make sure the prices match in both.

### How do I handle customers who say my prices are too high?

Stay calm and explain what the price includes. A customer may not know the cost of your ingredients, your packaging, or your delivery. Explain the quality, the portion size, or the service that justifies the price, and offer a smaller or cheaper option if you have one. Do not lower your price out of fear, because a price that loses you money will close your business.

### Can I have different prices for different customers?

Avoid it. Charging different customers different prices for the same item destroys trust, and in a small market, customers talk to each other. If you want to reward loyal customers, offer a clear discount, a loyalty offer, or a bulk price that is available to everyone. A public, consistent price list is fairer and easier to manage.

## What to Do Next

1. Write down every product or service you sell regularly, grouped into clear categories.
2. Check the true cost of each item, including ingredients, packaging, and transport, and set a price that leaves a profit.
3. Organise the list with your most popular items first, clear headings, and prices lined up in a column.
4. Add your business name, your logo, the date, and a short note about delivery fees and how to pay.
5. Create a clean PDF version for sending to customers, and a short text version for WhatsApp chats.
6. Add your prices to your WhatsApp Business catalogue, so customers can see them without asking.
7. Share the list on your WhatsApp status and social media, and print a copy for your shop or stall.
8. Set a monthly reminder to review the list, update the prices, remove old items, and change the date.
