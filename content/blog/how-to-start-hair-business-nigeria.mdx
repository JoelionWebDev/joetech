Hair is one of the most reliable businesses in Nigeria. Women buy wigs, bundles, and braiding hair all year round, for work, weddings, church, parties, and everyday style. A customer who buys a good wig today will return for another colour, another length, or a replacement in a few months. That repeat demand is why hair vendors in Lagos, Abuja, Onitsha, and Port Harcourt build loyal customer bases and steady incomes, even starting from a single room with a phone and a small carton of stock. This guide shows you how to start properly: what to sell, where to buy it, how much it costs, how to price it, and how to find customers who pay and return.

## Why Hair Is a Strong Business in Nigeria

The demand for hair in Nigeria is constant. Hair is not a luxury that people buy once a year. For many women, it is part of looking presentable for work and social life, and it is replaced regularly. A wig bought in January may be replaced by June. Bundles bought for a wedding may lead to an order for a birthday hairstyle a month later. That cycle creates repeat sales, which is the foundation of any good retail business.

Hair also suits online selling. Customers are used to buying wigs on Instagram and WhatsApp, checking videos of the hair, asking about length and texture, and paying by transfer before delivery. You do not need a large shop to start. A well-organised Instagram page, a WhatsApp Business catalogue, and a reliable delivery rider can carry your first hundred sales. The product is light enough to post across Nigeria, and it does not spoil if NEPA takes light, which is a real advantage over food or frozen goods.

The market is competitive, so you cannot succeed by simply posting "wigs available" and waiting. You succeed by choosing a clear niche, selling hair you have tested yourself, pricing honestly, and serving customers so well that they recommend you. This guide focuses on those four things.

## Choose Your Niche Before You Buy Stock

The biggest mistake beginners make is trying to sell everything: wigs, bundles, braids, closures, frontals, attachments, and hair accessories, all at once. A small budget spread across twenty products buys a little of everything and enough of nothing. Choose one niche first, master it, and expand with profit.

Human hair wigs are the most profitable niche for many vendors. A good human hair wig sells for ₦45,000 to ₦250,000 depending on length, density, and origin. Customers pay more because the hair lasts longer, can be styled with heat, and looks natural. The capital required is higher, but the profit per unit is higher too, and customers who buy human hair wigs tend to return for maintenance products and a second wig.

Hair bundles are the second main niche. Bundles are wefts of human hair sold in packs, usually three or four bundles for a full head, priced from ₦35,000 to ₦150,000 per set depending on length and quality. Many customers prefer bundles because a stylist can sew them in exactly the way they want. If you have relationships with hairstylists and salons, bundles can move quickly through those channels.

Synthetic wigs are the third niche, and the best entry point on a small budget. A synthetic wig sells for ₦8,000 to ₦35,000, which suits students, young workers, and customers buying their first wig. The profit per unit is smaller, but the volume is higher, and synthetic wigs let you build a large customer list. Many vendors start with synthetic wigs, learn the market, and add human hair later.

Braiding hair and attachments are the fourth niche. Products such as attachment, expression, and crochet hair sell for ₦1,500 to ₦6,000 per pack, move in large volumes, and are bought weekly by salons and home braiders. The margin per pack is small, but a vendor who supplies ten salons regularly can earn a steady income from volume alone.

You can also specialise by customer rather than by product. Some vendors serve only brides and wedding guests. Others serve only students. Others serve only corporate women who want a natural, office-friendly look. A clear customer focus helps you choose the right products, the right prices, and the right words in your adverts.

## How Much Money Do You Need to Start?

With ₦50,000 to ₦100,000, you can start with synthetic wigs or braiding hair. That budget buys 10 to 20 synthetic wigs at ₦4,000 to ₦7,000 each from a wholesaler, or a carton of attachments, plus packaging and a small advertising budget. Sell each synthetic wig for ₦10,000 to ₦18,000, and you can turn ₦70,000 of stock into ₦150,000 or more in sales if you choose styles customers actually want.

With ₦200,000 to ₦400,000, you can start a human hair business properly. This buys 5 to 10 good human hair wigs or several sets of bundles, plus a wig stand, packaging, and an advertising budget of ₦20,000 to ₦30,000. At this level, you can serve customers who want quality and are willing to pay for it, and your profit per sale is enough to grow the business month by month.

With ₦500,000 to ₦1,000,000, you can stock a full range, open a small shop or a space inside a salon, and begin supplying smaller vendors at wholesale prices. Rent is the biggest cost at this level. A small shop in a busy Lagos area can cost ₦300,000 to ₦800,000 a year, so do not rush into a shop before your online sales prove the demand. Many successful vendors sold online for a year before taking a shop, and they chose the location based on where their customers already lived.

Keep business money separate from personal money from day one. Open a separate account or wallet for the business, record every purchase and sale in a notebook or spreadsheet, and pay yourself a fixed weekly amount. A vendor who spends sales money on personal needs without recording it will think the business is failing when it is actually growing, or think it is growing when it is actually losing money.

## Where to Source Hair in Nigeria and Abroad

Your supplier decides your quality, your price, and your reputation. In Nigeria, the main wholesale markets for hair are in Lagos, Onitsha, and Kano. In Lagos, Balogun Market, Idumota, and Trade Fair Complex have large hair sections where you can compare prices and quality in one visit. In Onitsha, the main market supplies retailers across the South East. In Abuja, Wuse Market and the shops around Banex Plaza are common starting points for new vendors.

When you visit a market, do not buy from the first stall. Visit at least five sellers, ask for the same length and grade, and compare. Touch the hair, check the weft, smell it, and ask whether it is human hair, a blend, or synthetic. A good human hair bundle should feel soft, have minimal shedding when you run your fingers through it, and return to its texture after washing. Test a small quantity first, wash it, style it, and wear it or give it to a friend to wear for two weeks. Only place a large order after the test passes.

Many vendors import directly from China, Vietnam, India, or Cambodia, where much of the world's human hair is processed. Direct import can lower your cost, but it adds shipping, customs, and risk. A shipment can be delayed, or the hair can arrive in a lower grade than ordered. For a beginner, buying from a trusted Nigerian wholesaler or an importer with a physical shop you can visit is safer. Consider direct import only after you know exactly what sells and how much you sell each month.

Online suppliers on Instagram and WhatsApp can be genuine, but this is also where many new vendors lose money to fraud. Never pay in full to a supplier you have not verified. Ask for a video call showing the stock, ask for the shop address, and if possible, send someone you trust to the shop. A genuine supplier will welcome verification and will have a physical location, a business name, and other customers you can check with. A fraudster will offer an unbelievably low price, rush you to pay, and disappear.



## How to Price Hair for Profit

Pricing hair is different from pricing a small item, because the cost per unit is high and the customer compares prices carefully. Start from your true cost: the price you paid the supplier, plus shipping or transport, plus packaging, plus a share of your advertising cost. Then add your margin. For human hair wigs, a margin of 25 to 40 percent is normal. If you buy a wig for ₦60,000 and sell it for ₦85,000, your gross profit is ₦25,000 before other costs. For synthetic wigs, margins of 40 to 60 percent are normal. For bundles, margins of 30 to 45 percent are common.

Offer clear price tiers. A vendor might offer synthetic wigs from ₦10,000, mid-range human hair blends from ₦35,000, and premium human hair wigs from ₦70,000 upwards. Tiers let a student with ₦15,000 and a bride with ₦150,000 both find something in your shop, without either feeling the shop is not for them.

Be transparent about what the price includes. Does it include a free wig cap, a small bottle of hair serum, or free delivery within your area? Say so clearly. A customer who sees "₦85,000, includes wig cap and free delivery in Lekki" can compare that fairly with a competitor selling the same wig for ₦80,000 with no extras and a ₦3,000 delivery fee.

Avoid price wars. There will always be a seller offering a similar wig for less, and often that wig is a lower grade or a fake. Compete on quality, honesty, and service. Offer a loyalty discount for repeat customers, a small gift with a first purchase, or a free revamp service for wigs bought from you. These build loyalty without destroying your margin, and they give customers a reason to return to you rather than to the cheapest advert they see.

## How to Sell on Instagram, TikTok, and WhatsApp

Your Instagram page is your shop window. Post clear photos and videos of the hair, on a model or a wig stand, in good light. Show the hair from the front, the back, and the side. Show it straight, curled, and in a ponytail if the wig allows it. State the length, the texture, the price, and how to order in every post. A post that says "22-inch bone straight human hair wig, ₦95,000, free delivery in Lagos, WhatsApp 0803 123 4567 to order" will sell more than a beautiful photo with no price and no instructions.

Video sells hair better than photos. A short video of you brushing the wig, running your fingers through it, or showing how it moves when the model turns her head gives customers confidence that the hair is real and full. Use Instagram Reels and TikTok for these videos. A thirty-second video, filmed on your phone in daylight, can bring more enquiries than ten still photos. Post at least four or five times a week, and mix product posts with useful content: how to wash a wig, how to store it, how to choose a length for your face shape, and the difference between a closure and a frontal.

WhatsApp is where the sale closes. Use your Status daily to post new stock, customer reviews, and delivery updates. Create a broadcast list of customers who have bought from you or asked about your products, and send a useful update once or twice a week. A message that says "New stock: 20-inch body wave wigs just arrived, from ₦75,000" is welcome. Three messages a day is spam, and customers will mute you.

Run small adverts when you are ready. A budget of ₦5,000 to ₦10,000 on Instagram can test whether an advert brings enquiries. Target women aged 20 to 45 in Lagos, Abuja, or Port Harcourt, with interests in beauty, fashion, and hairstyles. Send advert traffic to WhatsApp, and track how many enquiries turn into sales. An advert that brings fifty messages and zero sales is not working, no matter how many likes it gets.

## Delivery, Returns, and Customer Service

Delivery is part of the product. A wig ordered in Lekki from a seller in Ikeja should arrive the same day or the next day, in good condition, exactly as described. Partner with a reliable dispatch rider and agree on prices in advance. Within Lagos, delivery typically costs ₦1,500 to ₦4,000 depending on distance. For Abuja, expect ₦1,500 to ₦3,500. For interstate delivery, use a trusted courier and share the tracking number immediately.

Package every order carefully. Place the wig on a wig stand or fold it neatly in a satin bag, put it in a sturdy box or bag, and include a wig cap, a thank-you card, and care instructions. A customer who opens a well-packaged order feels they bought from a professional, and they are more likely to post a photo and tag you, which is free advertising.

Have a clear policy on returns and exchanges, and state it before the customer pays. Most hair vendors do not accept returns on wigs that have been worn, cut, or altered, for hygiene reasons. That is reasonable, but you should accept a return or exchange if you sent the wrong length, the wrong colour, or a damaged product. Say this clearly on WhatsApp and Instagram, so there is no argument later.

Answer messages quickly. A customer who messages five vendors at the same time will buy from the one who replies first with a clear price and a confident answer. Aim to reply within an hour during business hours, and use a WhatsApp auto-reply when you are away. If you are travelling or out of stock on a popular length, say so on your Status, so customers do not think you have disappeared.

Follow up after delivery. A short message two days later, asking "Hello Ada, I hope the wig fits well. Please let me know if you need help with styling", shows care and opens the door to a second sale. It also gives you a testimonial you can post, with the customer's permission, which is the most persuasive advert you can have.



## Your First 30 Days: A Simple Plan

In your first week, choose your niche, find two or three suppliers, compare prices, and buy a small test quantity. Set up your WhatsApp Business account and Instagram page, take good photos and videos of every product, and write down your prices and delivery costs.

In your second week, start selling to people you know. Tell friends, family, colleagues, and your hairstylist what you sell. Post on WhatsApp Status daily, offer a small launch discount or a free wig cap with the first twenty orders, and ask every happy customer for a photo or testimonial.

In your third week, expand beyond your circle. Post in local buy-and-sell groups with permission, collaborate with a stylist or a micro-influencer, and run a small Instagram advert to test the response. Track every enquiry, every sale, and every naira spent.

In your fourth week, review the numbers. Calculate your profit, identify your best-selling length and style, and reorder before it runs out. Ask three customers what they liked and what could be better, and act on it. Then repeat the cycle, a little bigger each month.

## Conclusion

Starting a hair business in Nigeria does not require a large shop or a loan. It requires a clear niche, hair you have tested yourself, honest pricing, and the discipline to serve customers well and reinvest your profit. Start with a product line you understand, buy a small quantity, test it, and grow from what sells. Build your presence on Instagram and WhatsApp, show the hair in video, deliver quickly and carefully, work with stylists, and follow up after every sale. Do that consistently for six months, track your numbers, and you will have a business that pays you, grows with you, and can one day become a brand customers ask for by name. Your first wig is the hardest sale. After that, the hair speaks for itself.
