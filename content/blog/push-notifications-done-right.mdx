Push notifications are one of the most powerful tools in mobile app engagement — and one of the most misused. A well-timed, relevant push notification brings users back to your app, drives sales, and builds habit. A poorly executed notification annoys users and pushes them toward the uninstall button.

The statistics are stark. Apps that use push notifications effectively see 3-10x higher engagement rates than those that do not. But 60% of users say they have uninstalled an app because of annoying notifications. This guide explains how to use push notifications strategically so your Nigerian app engages users without driving them away.

## Why Push Notifications Matter

Push notifications re-engage users who have not opened your app recently. The average app loses 80% of its users within the first 30 days. Push notifications are the most effective tool for bringing users back.

They drive time-sensitive actions like flash sales, limited-time offers, and booking reminders. They provide value through order confirmations, delivery updates, and appointment reminders. They build habit by encouraging daily use of your app. And they personalize the user experience by delivering relevant content based on user behaviour.

## Types of Push Notifications

Transactional notifications are triggered by user actions. They include order confirmations, delivery updates, payment receipts, password reset confirmations, and account updates. These notifications are expected and valued by users. They should always be sent.

Engagement notifications encourage users to return to your app. Examples include "You have new messages," "Your wishlist item is back in stock," "New content you might like," and "You haven't completed your profile." These should be personalized and limited in frequency.

Promotional notifications advertise offers, sales, and new features. Examples include "50% off your next order," "New features are here," "Refer a friend and earn credit," and "Limited time offer." These must be used sparingly to avoid fatigue.

Re-engagement notifications target users who have not opened your app in a while. Examples include "We miss you — here is something new," "Come back and complete your order," and "Your account will be deactivated if not used." Use these as a last resort before losing a user.

## Best Practices

Personalization dramatically improves notification performance. Use the user's name, reference their past behaviour, and send content relevant to their interests. A notification that says "John, your favourite brand just dropped a new collection" performs much better than "New products available."

Timing matters. Send notifications when users are most likely to engage. For Nigerian audiences, mornings (7-9 AM), lunch breaks (12-2 PM), and evenings (6-9 PM) are typically effective. Avoid sending notifications late at night or very early in the morning. Let users set their own notification preferences and quiet hours.

Frequency is critical. Too many notifications cause fatigue and uninstalls. Start conservatively — 1-3 notifications per week for most apps. Track unsubscribe and opt-out rates. If opt-out rates exceed 5%, you are sending too many. Let users choose their notification frequency in settings.

Provide clear value. Every notification should answer "What is in it for the user?" If a notification does not provide immediate value, do not send it. Users should never wonder why they received a notification.

Segment your audience. Not all users should receive the same notifications. Segment based on user behaviour — active users get different notifications than inactive users. Segment based on preferences — users who opted into promotions get promotional notifications. Segment based on purchase history — repeat customers get loyalty notifications. Segment based on location — Lagos users get local event notifications.

Use rich media when appropriate. Images, emojis, and action buttons increase engagement. A notification with an image of the product generates more clicks than a text-only notification. Action buttons like "View Order" or "Shop Now" let users take immediate action.

## What Not to Do

Do not send notifications without user permission. iOS and Android both require explicit user opt-in for push notifications. Never send the request at app launch — wait until the user has experienced value from your app. The best time to ask is after the user completes a positive action.

Do not send generic broadcasts to all users. Sending the same notification to everyone ignores their individual interests and behaviour. It reduces relevance and increases annoyance.

Do not send too many promotional notifications. Promotional notifications are the most likely to cause uninstalls. Limit promotional pushes to 1-2 per month. Rely on transactional and engagement notifications for regular communication.

Do not send notifications for every small action. Not every app activity warrants a notification. Be selective. If the user can check it when they open the app, it probably does not need a notification.

Do not ignore notification analytics. Track open rates, conversion rates, opt-out rates, and uninstall correlation. Use data to refine your notification strategy continuously.

## Measuring Notification Performance

Track delivery rate — the percentage of notifications successfully delivered to users. Delivery failures indicate technical issues or users who have uninstalled. Track open rate — the percentage of delivered notifications that users open. Industry average is 20-30%. Track conversion rate — the percentage of opened notifications that lead to desired actions. Track opt-out rate — the percentage of users who disable notifications after receiving them. Any rate above 5% requires attention. Track uninstall correlation — do users who receive many notifications uninstall at higher rates?
