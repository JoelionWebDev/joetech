WhatsApp is where Nigerian business happens. Customers enquire on WhatsApp, negotiate on WhatsApp, pay after a WhatsApp confirmation, and send delivery addresses on WhatsApp. For many small businesses in Lagos, Abuja, and Port Harcourt, WhatsApp is the shop, the till, and the customer service desk all in one app.

Broadcast lists are the most powerful — and the most misused — feature in that shop. A broadcast list lets you send one message to many people at once, with each person receiving it as a private message from you. Done well, it brings sales every week. Done badly, it gets your number reported as spam, blocked by customers, and even banned by WhatsApp. The difference is not the tool. It is how you use it.

This guide shows you how to run WhatsApp broadcast lists properly in Nigeria: how to build a list with consent, how to segment it, what to send, how often to send, and how to stay on the right side of WhatsApp's rules and your customers' patience.


## Step 1: Build Your List With Consent — Never Buy Numbers

A good broadcast list is built slowly, one consenting customer at a time. A bad list is bought, scraped, or copied from a random group, and it will damage your business faster than it helps it.

Here is how to build a list properly:

- **Ask every buyer to save your number and to join your updates.** After a successful order or delivery, send a short message: "Thank you for your order. If you would like updates on new arrivals and offers, please save this number and reply YES, and I will add you to my broadcast list." Only add people who reply YES, or who clearly agree in person.
- **Ask at the point of sale.** In your shop, put a small sign near the till: "Want updates on new stock and offers? Save our number and send YES on WhatsApp." For online sellers, add the same line to your order confirmation message and your delivery note.
- **Use your WhatsApp status and social media.** Post on your status: "I send new arrivals and offers by broadcast twice a week. Save this number and send YES if you want to receive them." People who opt in are the people who will actually read your messages.
- **Collect numbers in person, with permission.** At a market stall, a fair, or an event, ask customers directly: "Can I add you to my WhatsApp updates?" Write the number down only if they say yes. A notebook full of numbers from people who never agreed is not a list — it is a spam risk.
- **Never buy a list.** Lists sold online are full of dead numbers, wrong numbers, and people who have never heard of you. Messaging them is spam, they will report you, and WhatsApp can restrict or ban your number. A bought list can kill a good WhatsApp Business account in days.

Aim for quality over quantity. A list of 100 customers who saved your number, agreed to receive updates, and actually buy from you is worth more than a list of 2,000 strangers who will report you. Start with your existing buyers, grow by a few people a week, and your list will be both effective and safe.

## Step 2: Segment Your List — One Message Does Not Fit Everyone

Sending the same message to everyone is the fastest way to make people ignore you. A customer who buys baby clothes does not need to hear about men's shoes. A customer in Abuja does not need a Lekki-only delivery offer. A customer who bought last week does not need a "we miss you" message.

Segmentation means dividing your list into smaller groups based on what people buy, where they live, and how they behave. WhatsApp broadcast lists are limited to 256 contacts per list, so you can create several lists for different segments. Use clear names you will remember:

- **New customers:** People who bought once in the last 30 days. Send them a welcome, a thank-you, and a gentle second-purchase offer.
- **Regular customers:** People who buy at least once a month. Send them new arrivals first, loyalty rewards, and early access to offers.
- **VIP or high-value customers:** People who spend the most. Send them exclusive previews, birthday messages, and personal offers. They should feel special, not spammed.
- **By product:** Hair buyers, food buyers, fashion buyers, skincare buyers. Send each group only the products they care about.
- **By location:** Lekki, Ikeja, Surulere, Abuja, Port Harcourt. Send location-specific delivery offers and event invitations only to the people in that area.
- **By status:** People who enquired but have not bought yet. Send them helpful information, testimonials, and a first-order offer — not a hard sell every day.
- **Dormant customers:** People who have not bought in 60 or 90 days. Send them a short, warm re-engagement message once, not a daily barrage.

You do not need a complicated system to segment. A simple notebook or Google Sheet with columns for name, phone number, product interest, location, last purchase date, and total spent is enough. Update it when a customer buys. When you want to send a message, filter by the segment that matches, and send only to that list. The extra five minutes of preparation will double your results, because the message will feel relevant instead of random.

## Step 3: Set Up WhatsApp Business Properly

If you are selling on WhatsApp, use WhatsApp Business, not the regular WhatsApp app. It is free, it is made for small businesses, and it gives you tools that make broadcast lists work better.

Set up these features on day one:

- **Business profile:** Add your business name, address, category, opening hours, email, and website. This builds trust and helps customers find your details without asking.
- **Catalogue:** Add your products with photos, prices, and short descriptions. When you send a broadcast about a new product, link to the catalogue item so customers can see the price and details without a long chat.
- **Greeting message:** An automatic message for new customers who message you first: "Hello, thank you for contacting us. How can we help you today?" This makes a good first impression even when you are busy.
- **Away message:** An automatic reply for messages received outside business hours: "Thank you for your message. We are currently closed and will reply by 9am tomorrow." This manages expectations and stops customers from thinking you are ignoring them.
- **Quick replies:** Save short answers to questions you hear often — prices, delivery fees, opening hours, payment details. Type a short code and the full answer appears. This saves hours every week.
- **Labels:** Use WhatsApp Business labels to organise chats: New Customer, Regular, VIP, Pending Payment, Delivered, Enquiry Only. Labels are the simplest form of segmentation inside WhatsApp itself, and they help you find the right people when you want to build a broadcast list.

Keep your business number separate from your personal number if you can. A dedicated business number, even on a second SIM, keeps your personal chats private, makes it easier to hand the phone to staff, and protects your business if you change your personal number. If you use a dual-SIM phone, this is easy to set up.

## Step 4: How Often Should You Send? Less Than You Think

Frequency is where most Nigerian sellers go wrong. They send a broadcast every morning, sometimes twice a day, because they think more messages mean more sales. The opposite is true. More messages mean more mutes, more blocks, and more reports.

A safe rhythm for most businesses is **two to three broadcasts per week, to each segment** — not two to three per day. A food vendor with daily lunch menus may send one short menu message each morning to a specific lunch list, because customers expect it and want it. A fashion seller should send two or three messages a week at most. A service business like a salon or a cleaning company should send one or two a week.

Never send the same message twice in one day. Never send a broadcast late at night or very early in the morning, unless it is an urgent delivery update the customer is waiting for. The best times in Nigeria are mid-morning, around 10am to 11am, and early evening, around 5pm to 7pm, when people are checking their phones after work or school. Avoid Sunday mornings for sales messages, when many people are in church or with family.

If you are not sure whether you are sending too much, ask your list. Once a month, send a short message: "I send updates twice a week. Is that okay, or would you prefer once a week? Reply 1 for twice a week, 2 for once a week." The answers will tell you what your customers actually want, and asking shows respect.

## Step 5: Write Messages People Actually Open and Read

A broadcast message is competing with messages from family, friends, colleagues, and other sellers. It must be short, clear, and worth opening. Here is how to write one:

- **Start with the value, not with your business name.** "New Ankara styles just arrived — from ₦18,000" is better than "Good morning, this is XYZ Fashion, we are pleased to inform you..."
- **Keep it short.** Three to five lines is enough for most messages. If it takes more than ten seconds to read, most people will skip it. Put the most important information first: what it is, how much it costs, and how to order.
- **Use one clear photo or short video.** A single good photo of the product, in natural light, with the price in the caption, will sell more than five photos in one message. Do not send a long chain of images — it eats data and annoys people on slow MTN or Airtel connections.
- **Include one call to action.** "Reply YES to order", "Tap here to see the catalogue", "Call or WhatsApp 0803..." — one action, clearly stated. Do not ask the customer to do three things at once.
- **Personalise when you can.** Use the customer's name in a one-to-one follow-up, and use the segment to make the broadcast feel relevant: "For our Lekki customers — free delivery this weekend." Personalisation is what makes a broadcast feel like a message, not spam.
- **Use simple, polite Nigerian English.** Write the way you would speak to a customer in your shop. Avoid shouting in capital letters, avoid excessive emojis, and avoid long formal paragraphs. One or two emojis are fine; ten are not.
- **Always include the price or the price range.** A message without a price generates enquiries from people who cannot afford it, and wastes your time and theirs. A price filters the audience to people who are ready to buy.

Here is a good example for a fashion seller: "Good afternoon. New Ankara gowns just arrived — sizes 8 to 16, from ₦18,000. Free delivery in Lekki this weekend. Reply YES to see photos and order." That message is short, clear, priced, and actionable. Compare it with a bad example: a long paragraph, no price, five blurry photos, and no clear instruction. The first will sell. The second will be ignored.


## Step 7: Stay Within WhatsApp's Rules — And Avoid a Ban

WhatsApp takes spam seriously, and a banned number can be difficult to recover. Follow these rules to stay safe:

- **Only message people who agreed.** This is the most important rule. If someone did not ask to receive your broadcasts, do not send them.
- **Do not send too many messages too quickly.** Sending the same broadcast to hundreds of people in a few minutes can trigger WhatsApp's spam detection. Send to one list, wait a few minutes, then send to the next. Do not use third-party bulk-sending apps or software that promises to send to thousands of numbers — these violate WhatsApp's terms and are a common cause of bans.
- **Do not use a new number for heavy broadcasting immediately.** A brand-new number that sends hundreds of messages on its first day looks like spam. Warm up a new number slowly: chat with a few customers, build a small list, and increase gradually over two to three weeks.
- **Make it easy to leave.** Tell customers how to opt out: "Reply STOP if you no longer want updates, and I will remove you immediately." When someone says STOP, remove them the same day, without argument and without a guilt message. A customer who leaves politely may return later. A customer who is forced to stay will block and report you.
- **Watch your block and report rate.** If several people block you in a week, you are sending too much, or to the wrong people, or with the wrong content. Pause, review your last messages, and adjust. If WhatsApp shows you a warning, take it seriously and reduce your sending immediately.
- **Keep your app updated and use the official app.** Unofficial WhatsApp versions and bulk-sending tools can get your number banned permanently. Use the official WhatsApp Business app from the Play Store or App Store.
- **Respect data and light realities.** Many customers are on limited data bundles or are managing phone battery during a power outage. Keep photos compressed, keep videos short, and avoid sending large files in a broadcast. A message that costs a customer data without giving value will be resented.



## A 7-Day Plan to Fix Your Broadcast Lists

- **Day 1:** Export or list your current broadcast contacts. Remove anyone who never agreed to receive messages, and anyone who asked to leave.
- **Day 2:** Set up or clean up your WhatsApp Business profile, catalogue, greeting message, away message, and labels.
- **Day 3:** Segment your remaining contacts into three or four lists by product, location, or buying behaviour. Ask your best customers to save your number if they have not already.
- **Day 4:** Write three broadcast templates: a new arrival, an offer, and a useful tip. Keep each one to three to five lines, with a price and a call to action.
- **Day 5:** Send your first segmented broadcast to one list only. Watch the replies, the questions, and the orders for 24 hours.
- **Day 6:** Send a different message to a second segment. Compare the response. Which segment replied more? Which message worked better?
- **Day 7:** Review the week. How many people replied? How many ordered? How many asked to leave? Adjust your frequency, your content, and your segments based on what you learned, and set a simple weekly schedule for the next month.

After thirty days, you should see more replies, more orders, and fewer blocks. After ninety days, your broadcast list should be one of your most reliable sales channels — not because you shout the loudest, but because you message the right people, with the right message, at the right time.

## Final Word

WhatsApp broadcast lists are not a way to shout at strangers. They are a way to stay in touch with customers who already trust you, and to tell them — briefly, politely, and usefully — when you have something they will want. Build the list with consent, segment it with care, send less often than you think you should, and always include a price and a clear next step.

Do that consistently, and your broadcasts will do what they are meant to do: bring customers back, week after week, without annoying them, without getting your number reported, and without turning your best marketing tool into a spam problem.
