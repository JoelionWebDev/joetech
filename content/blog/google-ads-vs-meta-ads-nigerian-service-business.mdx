When a service business in Nigeria decides to run paid advertising, the first question is usually which platform to start with: Google Ads or Meta Ads, meaning Facebook and Instagram ads. Both can bring customers. They simply find customers in opposite ways, and that difference decides which one deserves your first budget.

Google Ads meet people who are already looking. Meta Ads meet people who are not looking yet, but might be interested. Neither is better in general. The right first choice depends on how your customers behave when they need a service like yours. For background on how paid campaigns are put together, see our post on [AI-assisted paid advertising](/blog/ai-paid-advertising-google-social). This post is about the decision itself: which platform first, and why.

## How Google Ads Work: Catching People Who Are Already Looking

Google Ads appear when someone searches. A person types what they need into Google, and ads related to that search appear at the top of the results. The visitor has already expressed intent. They are not browsing for fun. They have a problem, and they are looking for someone to solve it.

For a service business, that intent is valuable. A person searching for a web designer in Lekki, a plumber in Ikeja, or a cleaning service in Lagos is close to making contact. Your ad does not have to convince them they need the service. It has to convince them to choose you over the other options they can see.

That also means Google Ads reward clarity. Your ad needs to match what the person searched for, and the page it leads to needs to answer their need quickly, with a clear way to contact you. If someone searches for a specific service and lands on a vague homepage, they will leave and click the next result. The platform gives you people with intent. Your website still has to convert them.

## How Meta Ads Work: Finding People Before They Search

Meta Ads work the other way round. They appear in feeds on Facebook and Instagram, among posts from friends, creators and pages a person follows. The viewer was not searching for your service. They were scrolling, and your ad interrupted them.

That interruption can still work, because Meta lets you show ads to people based on their interests, location, behaviour and demographics. A fashion brand, a restaurant, a fitness coach or a beauty service can reach people who are likely to care, even if those people were not thinking about it a moment ago. A striking image, a short video, or a clear offer can stop the scroll and start a conversation.

But the job is harder in one respect. You must first create the desire, then capture it. Your creative, meaning your image or video and your words, has to do the persuading that Google's search intent does for free. That is why Meta Ads live or die on the quality of the creative, while Google Ads live or die on matching the search and the landing page.

## Side by Side: What Actually Differs

Here is how the two compare on the points that matter to a service business:

- **Customer mindset.** Google visitors arrive with a need already in mind. Meta viewers arrive without one, and your ad must create it.
- **Speed of enquiry.** Google enquiries often come faster, because the visitor was already looking. Meta enquiries may take more touches, such as seeing the ad, visiting your page, and returning later.
- **What you need ready.** Google needs a strong service page or landing page that matches the search, plus a fast way to contact you. Meta needs strong creative, a clear offer, and a page or WhatsApp flow that keeps the conversation going.
- **Targeting.** Google targets by what people search for. Meta targets by who people are, where they are, and what they are interested in.
- **Learning what works.** Both platforms need time to show which ads bring real enquiries, not just clicks. Judge them by enquiries and jobs won, not by likes or clicks alone.
- **Budget control.** Both let you set limits on what you spend, and both reward patience. Starting small, watching which ads bring genuine enquiries, and then shifting spend towards the winners beats launching large on day one.

## Which Should a Service Business Run First?

There is no single answer, but there is a practical way to decide. Ask yourself one question: when someone needs your service, do they search for it?

If the answer is yes, start with Google Ads. Services people actively search for include web design, plumbing, electrical work, legal services, cleaning, repairs, tutoring and medical or dental services. The customer already knows they need help, so meeting them at the moment of search is the most direct route to an enquiry.

If the answer is no, or not usually, start with Meta Ads. Services people discover rather than search for include fashion, beauty, food, events, fitness, decor and lifestyle services. Customers may not search for these until something catches their eye, so showing up in their feed, with appealing creative, is the better first move.

If your service sits in between, consider this: run the platform that matches your strongest asset first. If you have a clear, convincing service page and customers already search for what you do, Google first. If you have striking photos or videos and an offer people react to on sight, Meta first. Many businesses eventually run both, using Google to catch ready buyers and Meta to build demand and stay visible, but starting with one, learning it properly, and then adding the second is calmer and easier to manage than launching both at once with no clear read on either.

## What to Have Ready Before You Spend

Whichever platform you choose, advertising sends people somewhere, and that somewhere must be ready:

- **A page that matches the ad.** If your ad promises a service, the page it leads to should be about that service, not your general homepage. Visitors should understand within seconds that they are in the right place.
- **A clear next step.** Tell visitors exactly what to do: send a WhatsApp message, fill a short form, or call a number. One clear action beats several competing buttons.
- **Fast replies.** An enquiry answered hours later is often an enquiry lost. Make sure someone monitors messages and calls during the hours your ads run.
- **A way to track enquiries.** Note where each enquiry came from, even with a simple record. Without that, you cannot tell which platform is actually bringing customers, and you will keep spending on guesswork.
- **Creative that looks like your business.** Use your own photos, your own colours and your own words where possible. Borrowed-looking creative undermines the trust your ad is trying to build.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I run both Google Ads and Meta Ads at the same time?**

Yes, and many businesses do eventually. If your budget is limited, though, start with the platform that matches how your customers behave, learn what brings real enquiries, and add the second platform once the first is working.

**Do Meta Ads work for services people do not usually search for?**

Yes, that is exactly where Meta is strongest. When customers discover services through images, videos and recommendations, a well-targeted Meta ad can create demand that search advertising would miss.

**Do Google Ads work if my website is weak?**

Poorly. Google Ads bring visitors, but your website turns them into enquiries. If your service page is unclear, slow, or missing a simple way to contact you, fix that before spending on ads, or much of the spend will be wasted.

**How long should I test before deciding a platform is not working?**

Give it enough time and enough enquiries to judge fairly, rather than deciding after a day or two. Watch enquiries and jobs won, not just clicks and likes, and adjust the ad, the creative or the landing page before concluding the platform itself is the problem.

## Start With the Platform Your Customers Already Use

Google Ads and Meta Ads are not rivals fighting for the same job. Google catches people who are already looking. Meta finds people who might want what you offer. For a service business, the right first choice is the one that matches how your customers behave when they need you. Get that decision right, have your page and your replies ready, and judge the result by enquiries, not by noise.
