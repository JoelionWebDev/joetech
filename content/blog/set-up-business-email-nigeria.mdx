A customer is comparing two businesses in Lagos. One replies from a free personal email address. The other replies from an address that carries its own business name. Before either has said a word about price or quality, the customer has already formed an opinion about which one is more serious.

That is the quiet power of a professional business email. It does not change what you sell, but it changes how people judge you before they buy. In Nigeria, where customers are rightly careful about who they send money to, that first impression can decide whether a conversation continues or ends.

## Why a free email address costs you business

A free email address works, and for a brand-new hustle it is a reasonable place to start. But as soon as you ask customers to trust you with their money, it starts working against you. A free address tells the customer nothing about your business. It could belong to anyone, and it can be created in two minutes by anyone. A business address tied to your own domain tells them you have invested in your business and you plan to be around.

Free accounts are also easy targets for impersonation. Anyone can create an address that looks similar to yours and message your customers. A business email on your own domain is much harder to copy convincingly, because the domain itself belongs to you.

## Start with your domain name

Your business email address will end with your domain name, so this is the first decision to get right. If you already have a website, use the same domain for your email. Your website and your email should match, because customers check both and a mismatch looks careless.

If you do not yet own a domain, choose one now. Keep it short, easy to spell, and as close to your business name as possible. Avoid numbers, hyphens, and clever spellings that people will get wrong when you say the address out loud. Before you settle on a name, read our guide on [how to choose a domain name for your Nigerian business](/blog/choose-domain-name-nigerian-business), which covers the common mistakes people make when picking one.

Once you own the domain, you control it. Your email addresses will keep working even if you change your website host or your email provider later, because the domain is yours. Your email can be ready before your website.

## Choose an email service that fits your business

A domain name on its own does not receive email. You need an email hosting service that connects to your domain and gives you inboxes, sending, and storage. The right one depends on how your business works.

Google Workspace is the most widely used option for small businesses. It gives you Gmail with your own domain, plus shared calendars and cloud storage. If your team already uses Gmail every day, this is the easiest move, because nothing about the way you read and send email changes.

Microsoft 365 is a strong choice if your business already uses Word, Excel, or Outlook. It gives you Outlook with your own domain, plus the Office apps and cloud storage. Zoho Mail is a popular lower-cost option that many Nigerian small businesses use, offering business email on your own domain with the essential features.

Compare options on addresses, storage, spam filtering, and support, and choose based on how your team actually works each day.

## Set up your business email step by step

Once you have chosen a service, the setup follows the same pattern. It takes longer to read about than to do, but doing it in the right order saves you from problems later.

1. Buy or confirm your domain name, and make sure you can log in to where it is registered.
2. Sign up for your chosen email service and enter your domain name when it asks.
3. Create your first email address, usually your main one, such as info@ or hello@ followed by your domain.
4. Add the MX records the service gives you to your domain settings. These tell the internet where your email should be delivered. Add them exactly as shown.
5. Add the security records too: SPF, DKIM, and DMARC. These prove that emails from your domain really come from you, and they stop your emails from landing in spam folders or being rejected.
6. Wait for the records to take effect, which can take a few minutes or up to a day, then send yourself a test email from the new address and reply to it.

If this feels unfamiliar, ask your web developer to help. The security records are the part that matters most, and skipping them is the most common reason a new business email ends up in spam.

## Create the right addresses for your business

One inbox is not enough for most businesses. Customers write for different reasons, and the address they use tells you what they need before you open the message.

- info@ or hello@ for general enquiries from new customers
- sales@ for people asking about prices and placing orders
- support@ for existing customers who need help
- accounts@ for payments, invoices, and receipts

You do not need a separate person for each address. One person can monitor several addresses, or several people can share access to one inbox. The point is that the customer writes to the right place, and the message reaches the right person without being lost in one crowded inbox. Give staff their own named addresses too, and close or redirect an address when someone leaves.

## Write emails that look professional

The address is only half of the impression. Set up a proper email signature with your full name, role, business name, phone number, and website address, and keep it consistent across the team. A customer who needs to call you should not have to search for your number.

Use a clear subject line that says what the email is about. "Your order for Saturday delivery is confirmed" tells the customer everything before they open it, while "Re: Your order" says almost nothing. Reply promptly, and if you cannot answer fully at once, send a short holding reply. "Thank you, I have received your message and I will confirm the price by tomorrow morning" is far better than silence.

## Common mistakes to avoid

Most business email problems come from a few avoidable mistakes. Check your setup against this list before you start using your new address widely:

- Using a personal free email for invoices and payments
- Skipping the SPF, DKIM, and DMARC security records
- Sharing one password across the whole team
- Leaving a former staff member's inbox active and unmonitored
- Sending important emails from a phone without checking the signature
- Forgetting to test that you can receive email, not just send it

If you already made one of these mistakes, it is not too late. Moving to a professional setup is a normal step as a business grows, and your customers will notice the improvement immediately. Announce the change once, clearly, in a short message to your regular customers, so they know your new address and update their contacts.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I keep using Gmail with a business email address?**

Yes. Google Workspace gives you Gmail with your own domain name, so you keep the familiar Gmail interface and get a professional address at the same time.

**How many email addresses do I need?**

Start with the ones your customers actually use: a general address, a sales address, and a support address. Add named addresses for each staff member who deals with customers. You can always add more later, but start with the ones that keep your messages organised.

**What happens to my old email address?**

Keep it for a while. Set up an automatic reply on the old address telling people your new business address, and check it for a few weeks until the messages slow down. Do not delete it suddenly, because some customers will take time to update their contacts.

**Do I need a website before I can have a business email?**

No. You only need a domain name. You can register a domain, set up your business email on it, and build the website later. Many businesses do it in this order, because email is needed from the very first day of trading.

Set it up properly, and it pays for itself in credibility from the very first email.
