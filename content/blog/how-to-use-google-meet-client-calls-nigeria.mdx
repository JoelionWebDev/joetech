A client in Abuja wants to discuss a website for their hotel. A customer in Port Harcourt wants to see fabric samples before placing a large order. A company in Lagos wants to interview you for a branding project. In each case, the meeting happens on a video call, and the client forms an opinion about your business within the first minute. If your video is dark, your audio is unclear, or you join ten minutes late, the client starts to doubt you before you have said a word about your work.

Google Meet is one of the simplest tools for professional video calls, and it is free. It runs in a browser, works on phones and computers, and needs no installation for guests. A client only has to click a link to join. For a Nigerian small business, it is a practical way to meet clients across the country, present your work, close deals, and build relationships without spending money and hours on transport through Lagos traffic.

This guide shows you how to use Google Meet professionally for client calls in Nigeria. You will learn how to set up and schedule meetings, how to prepare your space and your equipment, how to handle slow data and power problems, how to present your screen and your work, and how to follow up after the call so the client remembers you. Whether you are a web developer in Ikeja, a fashion designer in Surulere, a consultant in Abuja, or a caterer in Port Harcourt, these steps will help you look and sound like a business worth hiring.

## Why Google Meet Works Well for Nigerian Businesses

Google Meet has several advantages for a small business in Nigeria. The first is that it is free and easy to join. You need a Google account to start a meeting, but your client does not need one to join from a computer. They click the link you send, type their name, and they are in. There is no app to download, no account to create, and no password to remember. For clients who are not comfortable with technology, that simplicity matters.

The second advantage is that it works on almost any device. A client can join from an Android phone, an iPhone, a laptop, or a desktop computer. That matters in Nigeria, where many clients will join from a phone, often while they are on the move or at another office. Google Meet adapts to the device and the connection, and it will keep working, with lower video quality if necessary, when the network is weak.

## Step 1: Set Up Google Meet and Schedule Your First Call

Setting up Google Meet takes a few minutes. You need a Google account, which is free. If you already use Gmail, you already have one. On a computer, go to the Google Meet website and sign in. On a phone, download the Google Meet app from the Play Store or the App Store and sign in with the same account. Use your business Google account if you have one, so that the name clients see is your business name rather than a personal nickname.

To start a meeting immediately, click or tap the option to create a new meeting, and Google Meet will give you a link. You can copy that link and send it to your client by WhatsApp or email. To schedule a meeting for later, use Google Calendar. Create a new event, add the client's email address, and turn on the option to add a Google Meet link. The client will receive an invitation with the date, the time, and the link, and the meeting will appear in both of your calendars. This is the professional way to do it, because it gives the client a reminder and a record.

Always confirm the time clearly. Nigeria is on West Africa Time, and most of your clients will be in the same time zone, but if you ever meet a client abroad, state the time zone in your message. Write the date and time in full, for example: "Thursday, 20 November at 11am WAT." Send the link at least a day before the meeting, and send a short reminder on WhatsApp an hour before it starts. A message such as "Good morning, just a reminder that our call is at 11am today. Here is the link" reduces no-shows and shows the client you are organised.

## Step 2: Prepare Your Space, Light and Sound

Your environment is part of your professional image. Before an important client call, choose a quiet place where you will not be interrupted. A small office, a quiet room at home, or a corner of your shop can all work, as long as the background is tidy and the noise is low. Tell family members or staff that you are on a call and should not be disturbed. Put your phone on silent, close unnecessary windows and doors, and keep pets and children out of the room for the duration of the call.

Lighting makes the biggest difference to how you look on camera. Sit facing a window or a lamp, so the light falls on your face. Never sit with a bright window behind you, because the camera will darken your face and the client will see only your silhouette. If your room is dark, place a lamp in front of you, or use a small ring light, which costs little and makes a big difference. Test how you look before the call by joining the meeting a few minutes early and checking your own video.

Sound matters just as much. The microphone built into a laptop or a phone is usable, but a simple pair of earphones with a microphone will make your voice much clearer and reduce background noise. Test your microphone before the call by speaking a few sentences and listening to yourself. If you are in a noisy area, keep your microphone muted when you are not speaking, and unmute only when it is your turn to talk. Also check your internet connection and close apps that use data in the background, such as app updates or video streaming, so your call gets as much bandwidth as possible.

## Step 3: Join the Call Like a Professional

First impressions on a video call are formed fast, so join the meeting properly. Join two or three minutes early. This gives you time to check your camera and microphone, fix any problem, and be calm and ready when the client arrives. Joining late, especially for a first meeting, tells the client that their time does not matter to you.

When you join, your camera should be on, unless you have agreed otherwise. A client who sees your face trusts you more than a client who sees a blank screen or your initials. Look at the camera when you speak, not at your own image on the screen, because looking at the camera feels like eye contact to the person on the other side. Smile when you greet the client, say their name, and thank them for their time. A warm, confident greeting sets the tone for the whole meeting.

Dress as you would for an in-person meeting with that client. You do not need a suit, but you should look neat and appropriate for your business. A clean shirt or a smart top is enough for most calls. Avoid busy patterns that can look strange on camera, and avoid wearing sunglasses or a cap indoors. Keep a glass of water nearby, and keep a pen and paper within reach so you can write down important points, dates and numbers during the call.

## Step 4: Run the Meeting with a Clear Plan

A professional call has a plan. Before the meeting, write down three things: the purpose of the call, the points you need to cover, and the outcome you want. For a sales call, the outcome might be a signed agreement or a date for a proposal. For a project update, the outcome might be approval to proceed to the next stage. Keep this plan in front of you during the call, and use it to keep the conversation on track.

Start by confirming the agenda with the client. Say something like, "Thank you for your time. I suggest we discuss your requirements first, then I will show you a sample of our work, and then we can talk about pricing and next steps. Does that sound good?" This takes thirty seconds and makes the client feel in control. It also stops the meeting from wandering into unrelated topics.

Listen more than you speak, especially at the start. Ask the client about their needs, their timeline, and their budget, and let them talk. Take notes as they speak, and repeat key points back to them to show you understood. When it is your turn to present, be clear and specific. If you are showing a website, a design, or a document, share your screen and walk the client through it slowly, explaining what they are seeing. Do not rush, and pause after each point to ask whether they have questions.

## Step 5: Share Your Screen and Present Your Work

Screen sharing is one of the most useful features of Google Meet, because it lets you show a client your work instead of only describing it. You can share a website, a design, a price list, a presentation, or a document, and the client can see it at the same time as you. To share your screen, click the present button in Google Meet and choose whether to share your whole screen, one window, or one browser tab. Sharing a single tab or window is usually best, because it keeps your other tabs and notifications private.

Before you share, prepare what you will show. Close any tabs or documents that contain private information, such as other clients' details, your personal messages, or your bank app. Open the page or file you want to show, and check that it looks correct. If you are showing a website, make sure it is the live version and that it loads quickly. If you are showing a design, open it at full size so the client can see the details. Tell the client what you are about to show before you share, so they know what to expect.

When you are sharing, speak slowly and explain what the client is seeing. Move your cursor to point at important details, and pause to let the client take in each screen. Ask for their reaction as you go, rather than showing everything and asking for feedback at the end. If the client wants a copy of what you showed, offer to send it by email or WhatsApp after the call. When you finish presenting, stop sharing your screen so the client sees your face again. This returns the conversation to a personal level and signals that the presentation part is over.

## Step 6: Handle Slow Data, Power Cuts and Other Problems

Every Nigerian business owner knows that technology can fail at the worst moment. Data can slow down, the network can drop, and power can go off in the middle of a call. A professional does not panic when this happens. They prepare for it, and they handle it calmly.

Start by reducing your data use before the call. Google Meet lets you lower the video quality in the settings, which uses less data and keeps the call stable on a weak connection. If your network is very slow, turn off your camera and continue with audio only. A clear voice call is better than a frozen video call, and most clients will understand. You can say, "My network is a bit slow today, so I will switch off my video to keep the audio clear." That is honest and professional.

Prepare a backup plan. Keep your phone charged and have enough mobile data available, so that if your Wi-Fi or your office connection fails, you can switch to your phone's hotspot or join from the phone itself. Keep a power bank charged for your phone and your laptop, so a power cut does not end the call. If your laptop battery is low, charge it fully before the meeting, or use your phone instead.

If the call drops, do not panic. Wait a few seconds and rejoin using the same link. If you cannot rejoin, send the client a quick WhatsApp message explaining the problem and suggesting a new time, or offering to continue by phone. A short message such as "Good afternoon, my network dropped. I am rejoining now, or we can continue by phone if that is easier" shows the client you are in control. Clients in Nigeria are used to network problems, and they will forgive a dropped call if you handle it calmly and communicate quickly. They will not forgive silence.

## Step 7: Follow Up After the Call

The call is not the end of the work. What you do in the hours after the call often decides whether you win the client. Within a few hours, send a follow-up message by email or WhatsApp. Thank the client for their time, summarise what was agreed, and attach anything you promised during the call, such as a proposal, a price list, or a sample of your work. Keep the message short and clear, and include the next step and the date it will happen.

Keep a record of every client call. Write down the client's name, the date, what was discussed, what was agreed, and what happens next. You can keep this in a notebook, a spreadsheet, or a simple document. This record is useful when you follow up, when you send a proposal, and when the client calls you a month later and you need to remember what you discussed. A business that remembers its clients wins more work than one that forgets them.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

The first mistake is treating a video call like a casual chat. A client call is a business meeting, even when it happens from your living room. Prepare for it, dress for it, and give it your full attention. The second mistake is joining from a noisy or messy environment. A client who hears a generator, a television, or children shouting in the background will wonder whether your business is organised enough to handle their work. Find a quiet space, even if it is a small one.

The third mistake is reading from a script or looking at your phone during the call. Clients notice when your eyes keep moving away from the camera, and it makes them feel ignored. Keep your notes on paper beside your keyboard, not on your phone. The fourth mistake is talking too much about yourself and too little about the client's needs. A call is a conversation, not a lecture. Ask questions, listen to the answers, and respond to what the client actually said.

The fifth mistake is having no plan for what happens after the call. A good call that ends with no follow-up and no proposal is wasted effort. Always end with a clear next step, and always follow through on it. The sixth mistake is ignoring the technology. Joining a call without testing your camera, your microphone and your connection is like arriving at a client's office without knowing the address. Test everything a few minutes before every call, and you will rarely be caught out.

## Start Using Google Meet This Week

You can make Google Meet part of your business this week. Set up your account, schedule one client call, and prepare for it properly. Choose a quiet space, check your light and sound, write down your plan, and test your connection. After the call, send a prompt follow-up and record what was agreed. The first call will feel unfamiliar. By the fifth, it will feel natural.

Video calls will not replace face-to-face meetings entirely, and some clients will always prefer to meet in person. But for many Nigerian businesses, Google Meet is the fastest way to reach clients in other cities, present your work professionally, and win jobs without the cost and time of travel. Learn to use it well, handle problems calmly, and follow up promptly, and your clients will see a business that is organised, reliable, and worth hiring.
