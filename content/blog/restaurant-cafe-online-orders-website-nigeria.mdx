Food delivery platforms are useful for discovery. A hungry customer can find your restaurant, browse your menu, and order without ever visiting your website. The trade-off is that every order through a platform carries a commission, and the customer relationship belongs largely to the platform, not to you.

A restaurant or cafe website that takes direct orders changes that balance. It does not need to replace platforms overnight. It needs to give regular customers, and new visitors who already know your name, a simple way to order directly, without commission on each order. This post explains how to build that direct ordering path: a clear menu, a short checkout, delivery and pickup options, and follow-up that brings customers back.

For the wider picture of selling online in Nigeria, including how websites compare with marketplaces and social shops, see our guide on [ecommerce in Nigeria](/blog/ecommerce-nigeria-online-store). The rest of this post focuses on the specific job of taking food orders directly.

## A Menu That Helps People Decide Quickly

A hungry customer decides fast. If the menu is hard to read, slow to load, or full of items without prices, the customer will leave and order elsewhere. The menu page should therefore be built for quick decisions, not for decoration.

Group items the way customers think: starters, mains, sides, drinks, and desserts, or breakfast, lunch, and dinner where that fits your business. Each item needs a clear name, a short description, a price, and a photo where possible. Photos matter in food ordering, because customers often choose with their eyes. Use real photos of your own dishes, in good light, rather than generic images that set the wrong expectation.

Mark the practical details customers ask about: portion information where useful, options such as spice level or extras, and clear labels for items that are unavailable. An unavailable item that can still be added to a basket creates frustration and a failed order. A menu that is accurate builds trust, and trust is what makes a first-time direct order happen.

## A Checkout That Does Not Lose Hungry Customers

Checkout is where direct ordering succeeds or fails. A customer who has chosen their food should be able to confirm the order in a small number of steps: review items, add delivery or pickup details, add a phone number, and confirm. Every extra step is a chance to abandon the order.

Avoid forcing account creation before ordering. Let first-time customers order as guests, with an optional account offer after the order is placed. Keep the form short: name, phone number, delivery address for delivery orders, and an optional note for the kitchen. Payment options should match what your customers actually use, and the total, including delivery fees where they apply, should be visible before confirmation. Surprise charges at the last step are a common reason for abandoned orders.

After confirmation, tell the customer what happens next: that the order has been received, the expected preparation and delivery or pickup time, and how they will be updated. A confirmation message on screen plus a WhatsApp or SMS update makes the order feel real and reduces anxious follow-up calls to the restaurant.

## Delivery and Pickup, Explained Honestly

Delivery and pickup need different information, and the website should handle both clearly. For delivery, state the areas you serve, delivery hours, delivery fees where they apply, and how long delivery usually takes. If delivery is handled by your own riders or a partner service, the customer does not need the internal details, only a reliable time estimate and a way to track or ask about the order.

For pickup, state the pickup location, pickup hours, and how the customer will know the order is ready. A simple pickup flow, where the customer shows an order reference or name at the counter, avoids queues and confusion during busy periods.

Be honest about limits. If certain items travel poorly, say so or exclude them from delivery. If delivery takes longer during peak hours, say so rather than promising a time you cannot meet. Customers forgive honest timing far more readily than a missed promise, and honest timing is what turns a first direct order into a second one.

## What Changes Without Commission

Ordering directly changes the economics of each order in a simple way: the restaurant keeps the full order value, apart from its own payment and delivery costs, rather than sharing a portion with a platform on every sale. Over time, that difference adds up, especially for regular customers who order often.

It also changes the customer relationship. When orders come through your own website, you learn who your regular customers are, what they order, and how often they return. That knowledge supports simple, useful follow-up: a message when a favourite item returns, a reminder about a new menu addition, or a thank-you offer for repeat customers. None of this requires complicated software. It requires a record of orders and permission to contact customers who have agreed to hear from you.



## Encouraging Repeat Orders

A first direct order is only the beginning. Repeat orders come from a good first experience plus a gentle reason to return. Make reordering easy: a clear order history for returning customers, a simple way to reorder a previous order, and saved delivery details where the customer agrees to it.

Small, honest incentives help. A free drink with a repeat order, a discount on a slow weekday, or early access to a new menu item can all work, provided the terms are clear. Avoid complicated loyalty rules that customers cannot understand at a glance. The best repeat incentive is one the customer understands in a single sentence.

Communication should be sparing and useful. A short WhatsApp message about a new dish, a holiday menu, or a limited offer is welcome when it is occasional and relevant. Frequent, generic messages are not. Treat each message as a reason to protect the customer's attention, and they will stay open to the next one.

## Simple Checks Each Week

A direct ordering website needs regular attention, but not constant redesign. Each week, check that the menu is accurate: prices, availability, and photos all current. Test an order yourself on a phone, from menu to confirmation, and fix anything that slows you down. Read customer messages and note repeated questions, then answer those questions directly on the menu or checkout pages.

Also check where orders stop. If many customers view the menu but few start checkout, the menu clarity or prices may need work. If many start checkout but few confirm, the form, payment options, or total cost presentation is likely the problem. Fix one stage at a time and watch whether completion improves. These small weekly checks keep direct ordering reliable, which is what earns customer trust over time.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do we still need delivery platforms if we take direct orders?**

Not necessarily, and many restaurants keep both. Platforms help new customers discover you, while your website serves regular customers and anyone who already knows your name. The goal is a healthier mix, with a growing share of direct orders over time.

**What if we cannot offer online payment yet?**

Offer a clear order request flow with payment on delivery or pickup, where that fits your business. Let customers submit the order, receive confirmation of the total and timing, and pay by the stated method at handover. State the payment options plainly before confirmation so there are no surprises.

**How do customers find our direct ordering website?**

Put the website link everywhere customers already meet you: your social profiles, your packaging, your receipts, and your in-store signage. A short note such as order directly on our website, placed where regular customers will see it, does more than a single launch announcement.

**Is a direct ordering website only for large restaurants?**

No. Small restaurants and cafes often benefit most, because regular customers form a large share of their trade. A simple menu, a short checkout, and clear pickup or delivery information is enough to start. Complexity can come later, once direct orders are already flowing.

## Make Direct Ordering the Easy Option

Customers will order directly when it is as easy as ordering through a platform, and when the menu, total cost, and timing are clear. Build that path first: a readable menu with real photos, a short checkout, honest delivery and pickup information, and prompt confirmation.

If your restaurant or cafe already has regular customers, start there. Tell them about direct ordering, make the first direct order smooth, and give them a reason to return. The commission saved on each order is only part of the reward. The customer relationship you build is the larger one.
