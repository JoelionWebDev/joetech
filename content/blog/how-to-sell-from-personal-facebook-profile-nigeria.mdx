If you have a Facebook profile in Nigeria, you already have a shop. Your friends, your family, your old classmates, your church members, your colleagues and your neighbours are all there, scrolling every day. Many of them would happily buy from you — food, clothes, hair, shoes, cakes, electronics, services — if they knew what you sell, trusted you, and found it easy to order. The problem is not a lack of customers. The problem is how most people sell on their personal profile: ten product posts a day, no prices, blurry photos, "DM for price", and a timeline that looks like a market stall shouting at everyone who passes.

That approach does not work. People do not open Facebook to be sold to. They open it to catch up with friends, laugh at a video, and see what is happening in their world. If your profile feels like a spammy advert board, they will unfollow you, mute you, or hide your posts — and then they will never see the product they actually wanted.

There is a better way. You can sell from your personal profile in a way that feels natural, helpful and trustworthy, turning friends into customers and customers into repeat buyers. This guide shows you how to set up your profile, what to post and how often, and how to price, take orders and grow without annoying the people you are trying to sell to.

## Why Your Personal Profile Works for Selling in Nigeria

Facebook is still one of the most used platforms in Nigeria. Millions of Nigerians check it daily, including people who are not on Instagram or TikTok. Your personal profile has something a business Page does not: trust. People know you. They went to school with you. They worship with you. They have seen your face, your family and your life. When you recommend a product, it carries the weight of a personal recommendation, not an advert from a stranger.

Your profile also reaches people for free. A post can be seen by friends, shared into groups, and commented on by people you know. A business Page often needs paid adverts to reach anyone. For a small business starting out, that free reach is gold.

But trust is fragile. Your friends trust you as a person. If you use that trust badly — by spamming them, by selling poor products, or by disappearing after they pay — you lose both the sale and the friendship. The rule for selling from a personal profile is simple: protect the relationship first, and the sales will follow.

## Step 1: Set Up Your Profile Like a Seller, Not a Stranger

Before you post a single product, make your profile look like a real person who runs a real business. Customers need to know who you are, what you sell, and how to reach you, in less than ten seconds.

Start with your profile photo and cover photo. Use a clear photo of your face as your profile picture — not a logo, not a cartoon, not a blurry photo from five years ago. People buy from people they can see. Your cover photo can show your business: your products, your shop, or a simple banner with your business name and what you sell. If you sell cakes in Surulere, your cover could be a photo of your best cake with the words "Fresh cakes in Surulere — order on WhatsApp".

Next, fix your bio. You have a short space, so use it well. Write what you sell, where you are, and how to order. For example: "I sell quality wigs and hair in Lagos. Wholesale and retail. Order on WhatsApp: 0803 123 4567. Delivery nationwide." Add your location — Lekki, Ikeja, Wuse, GRA Port Harcourt — because customers want to know where you are before they ask about delivery.

Check your privacy settings. Your product posts should be set to Public, so that friends of friends can see and share them. Your personal posts — family photos, opinions, private moments — can stay Friends Only. That separation matters. You want your products to travel, but your private life does not need to.

Finally, clean up your timeline. If your last 20 posts are arguments or complaints, a new customer will not trust you with their money. Remove or hide posts that make you look unreliable or rude.

## Step 2: Decide What You Sell and Who You Sell To

Do not try to sell everything to everybody. A profile that sells wigs on Monday, phones on Tuesday, food on Wednesday, land on Thursday and clothes on Friday looks like a person who sells whatever they can find, not a business that can be trusted. Pick one main thing, and be known for it.

Choose your main product based on three things: what you can supply reliably, what you know well, and what your friends already ask you about. If people already ask you where you buy your shoes, sell shoes. If your jollof rice is famous in your estate, sell food. If you are good at fixing phones, sell repairs and accessories.

Then define your customer in one sentence. "Working women in Lagos aged 25 to 45 who want quality wigs at fair prices." "Parents in Abuja who want birthday cakes delivered on time." "Young men in Port Harcourt who want original sneakers." That one sentence will guide everything you post. If a post does not serve that customer, do not post it.

Write a simple price list and keep it updated. You do not need to publish every price on every post, but you should have prices ready the moment somebody asks. A seller who replies "DM for price" to every question loses customers who do not want to start a private chat just to learn a price. Put the price in the post, or in the first comment, and watch how many more people order.

## Step 3: Post Like a Person, Not a Billboard

This is the heart of selling from a personal profile without looking spammy. The rule is balance: for every selling post, post something else that is useful, interesting or human. A good ratio is one selling post for every three or four other posts. That means if you post twice a day, only one of those posts should be a direct product post. The rest can be tips, stories, questions, or a glimpse behind the scenes.

Here are the types of posts that sell without feeling like adverts.

**Show the product in real life.** A photo of a dress on a hanger is an advert. A photo of you wearing the dress, or a happy customer wearing it, is a story. Show your food being cooked, your cakes being decorated, your shoes being packed, or your hair being installed. Real photos in real settings outsell studio photos, because customers can imagine the product in their own life.

**Teach something useful.** If you sell hair, post a tip on keeping a wig looking new. If you sell food, share a storage tip. Teaching builds authority — customers buy from the person who taught them.

**Share customer stories.** With permission, post a delivered order, a happy customer's message, or a video of a customer opening their package. Testimonials are proof from a real person, not a claim from you.

**Ask questions.** "Which colour should I stock next — red or blue?" Questions get comments, comments bring reach, and reach brings customers. They also tell you what to stock before you spend money.

**Post about your life, in moderation.** A photo from church or a birthday reminds friends you are a person, not a shop. Keep it genuine and occasional.

## Step 4: Write Product Posts That People Actually Read

When you do post a product, write it well. A good product post has five parts, and it should fit in a few short lines.

First, a clear photo or video. Use daylight, a clean background, and the real product. Show the size, the colour, and the details. A video of 15 to 30 seconds — you holding the product, turning it around, showing how it works — will outsell a single photo, because customers can see it is real.

Second, a short description. What is it, who is it for, and why is it good? "Original human hair wig, 22 inches, natural colour, soft and tangle-free. Perfect for everyday wear or a special occasion."

Third, the price, in naira, clearly stated. "₦45,000." If delivery is extra, say so: "Delivery within Lagos ₦2,000." Customers who know the full price will order. Customers who have to ask will often leave.

Fourth, how to order. "Send a WhatsApp message to 0803 123 4567 with the word WIG, and we will confirm your order." Give one clear action. Do not give three phone numbers, two Instagram handles and an email address. One action, one number.

Fifth, a simple call to action, not a shout. "Limited stock — 6 pieces left." "Order before Friday for weekend delivery." That is enough. Avoid writing in all capital letters, using ten exclamation marks, or saying "HURRY, BEFORE IT FINISHES" on every post. Urgency works when it is true and occasional. When it is on every post, it becomes noise.

Post at the right time: usually 7 a.m. to 9 a.m., 12 noon to 2 p.m., and 7 p.m. to 10 p.m. in Nigeria. Check which of your posts get the most reactions, and post products at those times.

## Step 5: Handle Orders, Payments and Delivery Professionally

A sale does not end when a customer comments "I want it". It ends when they have paid, received the product, and are happy. Handle every step like a business, even though you are selling from a personal profile.

When a customer comments or sends a message, reply quickly and politely. Confirm the product, the price, the delivery fee and the total in one message. "Thank you. The wig is ₦45,000, delivery to Ikeja is ₦2,500, so the total is ₦47,500. Please send payment to confirm your order." That single message prevents almost every misunderstanding.

Collect payment before dispatch for new customers and for all interstate orders. This is standard in Nigeria, and it protects you from cooking, packing or posting goods for a customer who never pays. Accept transfers, Opay, Moniepoint and Paystack links, and confirm when you have seen the money. Send a short message: "Payment received, thank you. Your order will be dispatched today." That message builds trust instantly.

Deliver on time, and communicate if there is a delay. If the rider is late, message the customer before they message you. If a product is out of stock, say so honestly and offer an alternative or a refund. A customer who gets an honest message will forgive a delay. A customer who is ignored will tell their friends.

After delivery, follow up once: "Hello, I hope you love the wig. Thank you for buying from me." That single message often earns a repeat order or a referral.

## Step 6: Use Groups and Shares Without Spamming Them

Facebook Groups are one of the best places to sell in Nigeria — and one of the easiest places to get banned from for spam. The difference is whether you follow the group's rules and add value, or whether you dump adverts into every group you can find.

Join groups where your customers already are: buy-and-sell groups in your city, estate groups, women's groups, church groups, alumni groups, and groups for your product type. Read the group's rules before you post. Many groups allow selling only on certain days, or only with prices included, or only in a specific format. Follow the rules exactly.

Post in groups sparingly — once or twice a week per group, at most — and make the post useful, not just a product photo. A post that says "Fresh lunch packs available today in Lekki, ₦3,500, delivery by 1 p.m." is useful. The same product photo posted ten times a day in ten groups is spam, and the admins will remove you.

Encourage shares, but do not beg for them. A post that says "Please share, please share, please share" looks desperate. A post that is genuinely useful, funny or beautiful will be shared on its own. And when a friend shares your post, thank them publicly. That thank-you is itself a post that shows you are grateful and trustworthy.

Never add people to a group or a broadcast list without their permission. Never tag 50 people in a product post. Never send a sales message to somebody who has never talked to you, just because they are your Facebook friend. Those are the behaviours that make people hide your posts, and they are the reason Facebook's spam filters exist.

## Step 7: Grow Your Audience and Your Reputation

Selling from a personal profile is a long game. The more trusted you are, the more you sell. Grow your audience slowly and honestly.

Add friends who are real people in your area or your target market. Accept friend requests from people who look genuine. Do not buy followers or add thousands of strangers. A profile with 500 real friends who know you will sell more than a profile with 5,000 strangers who ignore you.

Be consistent. Post every day, or at least four or five days a week. One good post a day for three months builds more trust than 20 posts in one weekend and silence for a month.

Ask for reviews. After a happy customer receives their order, ask them to leave a recommendation on your profile or to post about their experience and tag you. Save those recommendations and share them occasionally. A profile with visible recommendations from real people is a profile new customers will trust.

Handle problems with grace in public and speed in private. If a customer complains on your timeline, reply politely — "I am sorry, please send me a message so I can fix it" — then fix it fast. People judge you by how you handle the complaint.

## Common Mistakes That Make You Look Spammy

- **Posting products ten times a day.** Your friends will mute you. One product post a day is plenty.
- **"DM for price" on every post.** It wastes the customer's time and looks like you are hiding the price.
- **Blurry, stolen or fake photos.** Use your own photos of your own products. A photo from Google will be recognised, and it destroys trust.
- **Tagging everyone you know.** Tag only people who asked to be tagged, or who are in the photo.
- **Arguing in the comments.** If somebody says your price is too high, reply politely once and move on. An argument on your timeline will be seen by every potential customer.
- **Disappearing after payment.** Reply to messages, even when you are busy. A short "I will reply in 30 minutes" is better than silence.
- **Selling a product you do not have.** Never take payment for stock you do not hold. If it is out of stock, say so.

## Your First Month Selling From Your Profile

Week 1: Fix your profile photo, cover photo and bio. Write your price list. Clean up your timeline. Tell your friends, in one honest post, what you sell and how to order.

Week 2: Post one product a day, at the best time for your audience, with a clear photo, price and order instruction. Post three non-selling posts — a tip, a behind-the-scenes photo, and a question — in between.

Week 3: Join two or three groups where your customers are, read their rules, and post your best product once in each group. Ask your first three happy customers for a recommendation or a testimonial.

Week 4: Look at your posts. Which ones got the most comments, shares and orders? Do more of those. Which ones were ignored? Do less of those. Set a simple goal for next month: more orders, more repeat customers, or more recommendations.
