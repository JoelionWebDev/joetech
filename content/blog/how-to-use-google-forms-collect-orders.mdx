If you sell on WhatsApp or Instagram, you know how messy orders can get. A customer sends a message at 9 in the morning, another sends a voice note at noon, a third sends a screenshot of a product with no size or quantity, and by evening you are scrolling through dozens of chats trying to remember who ordered what, who has paid, and who is still waiting for a reply. Orders slip through the cracks, deliveries go wrong, and customers get frustrated. There is a free, simple fix for this: a Google Form. With one form, customers give you every detail you need in an organised way, and every order lands in a single spreadsheet you can check at a glance. This guide shows you how to set it up, step by step, even if you have never used Google Forms before.

## Why a Google Form Works So Well for Orders

The biggest advantage of an order form is that it forces the customer to give you complete information. On WhatsApp, a customer might say "I want that dress" without saying the size, the colour, or their delivery address — and then you spend the whole day going back and forth with questions. A form asks for all of it up front: name, phone number, product, size, quantity, delivery address, and payment method. The customer fills it in once, and you get everything in one place.

The second advantage is organisation. Every response goes into a Google Sheet automatically, in the order it arrives. You can see at a glance who ordered, what they ordered, and whether they have paid. You can sort the sheet by date, search for a customer's name, and mark orders as completed. Compare that with searching through weeks of WhatsApp chats, and the difference is obvious.

The third advantage is that it is free and it works everywhere. Google Forms costs nothing, it works on any phone or computer, and your customers do not need to install anything or create an account to fill it in. You only need a Google account to create the form. For a small business that is not ready to pay for a website with an online store, a Google Form is the best free stepping stone: it gives you a proper ordering system today, with no cost and no technical skill required.

## Creating Your First Order Form

Start by opening Google Forms. You can go to forms.google.com in your browser, or open Google Drive and click "New", then "More", then "Google Forms". You will need to be signed into a Google account — if you do not have one, creating one is free and takes a few minutes. Use a business email address if you have one, so the form and the orders belong to your business, not your personal account.

Google Forms will open a blank form with a title and a first question. Give the form a clear title that tells customers exactly what it is, for example "Order Form — Adaeze Fashion House" or "Cake Order Form". Under the title, write a short description: what the customer should do, how long delivery takes, and how payment works. For example: "Fill in this form to place your order. We will confirm your order on WhatsApp within a few hours. Delivery within Lagos takes 1 to 2 days. Payment confirms your order."

Your first question is already there, waiting for a title. Click on it and type your first question. Google Forms offers different question types: short answer for names and phone numbers, paragraph for addresses, multiple choice for choosing one option, checkboxes for choosing several options, and dropdown for long lists. Choose the type that fits each question. For most order forms, short answer and multiple choice will cover nearly everything.

Add new questions by clicking the plus sign on the side toolbar. You can drag questions to reorder them, duplicate a question if you need a similar one, and delete questions you do not need. At the top of the form you can also add a header image — your business logo or a photo of your products — which makes the form look more professional and more trustworthy. Keep the design simple and clean: a clear title, your logo, and questions that are easy to read on a phone screen, since most of your customers will fill the form in on their phones.

## The Questions Every Order Form Should Ask

A good order form asks for everything you need to fulfil the order — and nothing you do not need. Every extra question is a reason for a customer to give up halfway, so keep the form as short as you can while still getting the essentials.

Start with the customer's name and phone number. Make both required, so the form cannot be submitted without them — you can set this by turning on the "Required" switch at the bottom of each question. Ask for a WhatsApp number specifically if that is how you will contact them, since some customers have a different number for WhatsApp than for calls.

Next, ask what they want to order. The best way to do this depends on your business. If you sell a small number of products, use a multiple choice or dropdown question listing each product with its price — for example, "Small cake — Ankara fabric (3 yards)", or "Jollof rice pack". If you sell many products, add a question for the product name as a short answer, and let customers type what they want. Add follow-up questions for size, colour or flavour, quantity, and any special instructions. A paragraph question labelled "Anything else we should know?" catches the details you did not think to ask for.

Then ask about delivery. A multiple choice question works well: "Pickup" or "Delivery". If they choose delivery, you need their full address — a paragraph question gives them room to write it properly. You can also ask for their area or landmark, which helps your dispatch rider find them. If you deliver to specific areas, a dropdown of those areas makes it easy to charge the right delivery fee.

Finally, ask how they want to pay. A multiple choice question with your payment options — bank transfer, cash on delivery, or payment on pickup — sets the expectation clearly. In the description or in a final section, write your bank account details and your payment instructions, so customers know exactly what to do. You can also add a question asking them to confirm they have read your delivery and payment terms.

## Making Your Form Look Professional

A form that looks professional gets more orders than one that looks thrown together. Google Forms lets you customise the colours and the header image. Click the paint palette icon at the top to choose a theme colour that matches your business — the colour of your logo, for example — and upload a header image with your business name or your best product photo. The image should be wide, clear, and not too busy, since the title sits on top of it.

Write your questions in clear, simple language. Instead of "Input your residential address for logistics purposes", write "Your delivery address". Instead of "Select preferred remuneration modality", write "How would you like to pay?" Your customers are busy, and plain language respects their time.

Use the section feature to break a long form into steps. Google Forms lets you add sections — for example, "Your Details", "Your Order", and "Delivery and Payment" — with a heading and description for each. Sections make a form feel shorter and easier to complete, because the customer sees one small group of questions at a time instead of one long list.

Before you share the form, fill it in yourself as a test. Check that every question makes sense, that required questions are marked, and that the confirmation message at the end tells the customer what happens next. Google Forms shows a default message after submission; change it to something useful, like "Thank you! We have received your order and will confirm it on WhatsApp within a few hours."

## Sharing Your Form With Customers

Once your form is ready, you need to get it in front of customers. Click the "Send" button at the top right of Google Forms. You will see a link — copy it. This is the link you will share everywhere. Before you share it, click the link settings and make sure the form is set so that anyone with the link can fill it in. You can also shorten the link with a link shortener, which makes it neater to share on WhatsApp and Instagram.

Put the link in all the places your customers already find you. Add it to your WhatsApp Business catalogue or your business profile, pin it in your WhatsApp group or broadcast list, add it to your Instagram bio, and include it in your Facebook page. When a customer messages you asking to order, reply with the link and a short message: "Please fill in this form so we can process your order quickly." Over time, customers will learn to use the form first, and your chat will be free for real conversations instead of order-taking.

You can also print the link as a QR code and display it in your shop, on your packaging, or on a flyer. Google Forms does not generate a QR code itself, but free QR code generators online can turn your link into one in seconds. A customer in your shop can scan the code and place an order without you writing anything down.

## Managing Orders Once They Start Coming In

Every response to your form is stored automatically. To see your orders, click the "Responses" tab at the top of the form. Google Forms shows you a summary of the responses and lets you see each one individually. For serious order management, connect the form to a Google Sheet: in the Responses tab, click the green spreadsheet icon and choose "Create a new spreadsheet". From then on, every order appears as a new row in the sheet, with the time it was submitted and the answer to each question in its own column.

The spreadsheet is where your order system really comes alive. Add extra columns at the end for your own use: "Payment received", "Order packed", "Delivered". Mark each order as you work through it, and you will always know which orders are waiting, which are paid, and which are done. You can also use the sheet's filter to show only unpaid orders, or sort by date to see today's orders first. If you have staff, share the sheet with them so everyone works from the same list instead of asking you what to do next.

Turn on email notifications so you never miss an order. In the Responses tab, click the three dots and choose "Get email notifications for new responses". Google will send you an email each time someone submits the form, so you can confirm orders quickly even when you are away from your shop. Quick confirmation matters: a customer who hears back within an hour is far more likely to pay and far less likely to order from someone else.

Finally, protect your customers' information. The spreadsheet contains names, phone numbers and addresses — treat it like a physical order book that you would not leave open on a counter. Share it only with staff who need it, and do not publish the sheet or its link anywhere public.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

The most common mistake is asking too many questions. A form with twenty questions feels like an exam, and many customers will abandon it halfway. Ask only what you need to fulfil the order. You can always follow up on WhatsApp for small details, but you cannot follow up with a customer who never submitted the form.

The second mistake is not testing the form before sharing it. A broken link, a question that does not make sense, or a form that asks for information you forgot to include will cost you orders. Fill it in yourself, ask a friend or family member to fill it in, and fix any confusion before you share it widely.

The third mistake is forgetting to check the responses. A form only helps if you actually look at the orders. Check your responses at least twice a day — once in the morning and once in the evening — and turn on email notifications so nothing sits unnoticed. A customer who fills in a form and hears nothing for two days will assume you are not serious.

The fourth mistake is using the same form forever without updating it. When you add new products, change your prices, or stop offering delivery to an area, update the form the same day. An order form with old prices creates arguments and refunds that could have been avoided with a two-minute edit.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Google Forms really free?

Yes. Google Forms is completely free to use with any Google account. You can create as many forms as you like, collect as many responses as you like, and connect your forms to Google Sheets at no cost.

### Do my customers need a Google account to fill in my form?

No. By default, anyone with the link can fill in a Google Form without signing in. If you turn on the setting that collects email addresses, customers may be asked to sign in — so leave that setting off unless you specifically need it.

### Can I collect payment through Google Forms?

Google Forms does not process payments itself. The usual approach for a small business is to include your bank account details in the form and ask the customer to transfer payment, then confirm on WhatsApp. You can add a question asking for the payment reference or a screenshot, though screenshots cannot be uploaded unless you turn on the file upload option.

### Can customers upload a photo with their order?

Yes. Google Forms has a file upload question type, which lets customers attach a photo — for example, a design they want, or a screenshot of a product. Note that file uploads require the customer to be signed into a Google account, which may put some people off, so use it only when you really need it.

### How do I know when someone places an order?

Turn on email notifications in the Responses tab, and Google will email you each time a new response comes in. You can also keep the linked Google Sheet open and check it during the day.

### Can I edit my form after I have shared the link?

Yes, and the link stays the same. You can add or remove questions, change prices, and update your delivery information at any time, and customers who open the link will see the updated version.

### What if a customer makes a mistake on the form?

You can allow customers to edit their responses after submitting — there is a setting for this in the form's settings. Alternatively, they can message you on WhatsApp and you can correct the order in your spreadsheet.

### Can I use Google Forms for bookings and enquiries too?

Yes. The same approach works for bookings, enquiries, and customer feedback. You can create a separate form for each purpose — one for orders, one for bookings, one for feedback — and manage them all from your Google Drive.

## What to Do Next

Create your first order form today. Go to forms.google.com, give it a clear title with your business name, and add the essential questions: customer name, WhatsApp number, product, quantity, delivery or pickup, delivery address, and payment method. Keep it short — ten questions or fewer is a good target. Add your bank details and a clear confirmation message, test it by filling it in yourself, then connect it to a Google Sheet and turn on email notifications.

Then share the link with your next customer instead of taking their order in chat, and see how much easier it feels. Within a week, you will have a neat list of orders, fewer lost details, and faster replies to customers.
