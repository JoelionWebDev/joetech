You have an app idea. You have identified a development agency or freelancer. Now you have scheduled your first meeting — the discovery call. This meeting sets the foundation for your entire project. A productive discovery call leads to an accurate proposal, realistic timeline, and successful partnership. An unproductive one leads to misunderstandings, scope creep, and disappointment.

This guide explains what happens in an app discovery call, what the developer needs from you, and what you should learn from them before signing a contract.

## What Is a Discovery Call?

A discovery call is an initial meeting between you (the client) and the development team. It typically lasts 30-60 minutes. The purpose is not to get a quote — it is to understand your idea well enough that the developer can prepare an accurate proposal.

The developer will ask questions about your idea, your target audience, your goals, and your constraints. You will ask questions about their process, their experience, and how they work. By the end of the call, both sides should have enough information to decide whether to proceed.

## What the Developer Needs to Know

Come prepared to answer these questions. The more specific your answers, the more accurate your proposal will be.

What problem does your app solve? Describe the problem in concrete terms. "People in Lagos struggle to find reliable plumbers" is better than "I want to connect people with service providers." The developer needs to understand the user's pain point to design an effective solution.

Who is your target user? Be specific about demographics, behaviours, and technical proficiency. An app for Lagos teenagers will be designed differently from an app for elderly users in rural areas. An app for tech-savvy users can use complex interactions. An app for first-time smartphone users needs to be simple and guided.

What are the core features? List the essential features your app must have to solve the problem. Distinguish between must-have and nice-to-have. Most founders list every feature they can imagine. The developer needs to know what is truly essential for the launch version.

What platforms do you need? iOS only, Android only, or both? If both, are you open to cross-platform development? Each choice affects cost and timeline.

Do you have a timeline? When do you need to launch? Be realistic. If you need an app in 4 weeks, you are limited to a very simple MVP. If you have 6 months, you can build something more comprehensive.

What is your budget? Many founders are reluctant to share their budget, but it helps the developer design a solution that fits your constraints. If your budget is ₦2,000,000, the developer can propose a realistic scope for that amount. If you do not share your budget, the developer may propose a ₦8,000,000 solution that you cannot afford.

## Questions You Should Ask the Developer

The discovery call is also your opportunity to evaluate the developer. Ask these questions to determine if they are the right partner.

What is your development process? A professional developer should have a clear, structured process covering discovery, design, development, testing, and deployment. Vague answers suggest they do not have a reliable process.

Have you built similar apps? Experience in your industry or with similar features reduces risk. Ask for examples of apps they have built that are similar to yours. Ask what worked well and what they would do differently.

Who will work on my project? Will you work directly with a senior developer, or will a project manager be your primary contact? Will junior developers be assigned to your project? Ask about the team's experience levels.

How do you handle changes during development? Scope changes are inevitable. A good developer has a structured change request process. A red flag is a developer who says "no changes allowed" or "changes are free" — neither is realistic.

What is your typical timeline and budget range for similar projects? This helps you calibrate your expectations. If the developer's estimate is significantly different from other quotes you have received, ask why.

How do you communicate and how often? Weekly updates? Daily standups? A Slack channel? Communication frequency affects how informed you feel during the project.

What happens after launch? Do you offer maintenance and support? What is the response time for urgent issues? Post-launch support is essential.

Can you provide references? A reputable developer will happily connect you with past clients. Contact those references and ask about their experience.

## What You Should Leave the Call With

At the end of a productive discovery call, you should understand the developer's proposed approach, the estimated timeline range, the estimated budget range, the next steps (proposal preparation, additional discovery if needed), what you need to provide (content, brand assets, existing research), and the developer's availability to start.

## Common Discovery Call Mistakes

Being too vague prevents the developer from preparing an accurate proposal. Provide as much detail as you can, even if you are uncertain about some aspects.

Focusing only on price prevents you from evaluating whether the developer is the right partner. A cheap developer who builds the wrong app is more expensive than a more expensive developer who builds the right app.

Not asking about post-launch support leaves you with an app that has no one to maintain it. Ask about maintenance before signing the contract.

Expecting a fixed price quote during the call. A discovery call is for exploration. Accurate quotes require time to analyze requirements and prepare a proposal. Do not expect an instant price.

Not sharing your budget leads to proposals that do not match your financial reality. Be transparent about your budget constraints so the developer can design a feasible solution.

## How to Prepare

Before your discovery call, write down your app concept in one paragraph, list the core features, identify your target users, research competitors, define your success criteria, set your budget range, and prepare your questions for the developer.

Investing 30 minutes in preparation will make your discovery call significantly more productive.
