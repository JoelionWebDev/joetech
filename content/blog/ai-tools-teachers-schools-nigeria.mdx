Artificial intelligence will not replace a good teacher. It cannot know your students, manage a noisy classroom, or notice that a child who was lively last term has gone quiet. But it can take over some of the repetitive writing and organising that keeps teachers working long after the school bell. This guide shows you where to start, using free or low-cost tools and everyday school tasks.

## Start With the Problem, Not the Tool

Before downloading anything, pick one task that drains your time every week. Lesson planning is a good first choice for most teachers. Marking support is another. Parent communication is a third. Choose one, get comfortable with it, then add another.

You also do not need a powerful laptop. Most of the tools in this guide work in a phone browser, which matters when the school computer is shared or slow.

## Lesson Planning: From Blank Page to First Draft

A blank lesson note is the hardest part of planning. AI tools can turn your topic into a structured first draft in a minute or two, and you then shape it into a real lesson.

Here is a practical way to do it:

1. **Tell the tool the facts.** Subject, class level, topic, how long the lesson runs, and what you want students to be able to do by the end.
2. **Ask for a plan with clear parts.** Introduction, main teaching points, a class activity, and a short way to check understanding.
3. **Edit it like a professional.** Change examples to things your students know. Replace anything that does not fit your syllabus or your class.
4. **Save what works.** Keep your best prompts and your best edited plans so next term is faster.

One important rule: never submit an AI draft as your lesson note without reading and editing it. The tool does not know your scheme of work, your students' level, or the mistakes they commonly make.

## Marking Help: Faster Feedback, Not Automatic Grades

Marking is where teachers spend the most unpaid hours, and it is also where AI needs the most care. An AI tool should never decide a student's final grade for you. It can, however, help you give better feedback faster.

Useful ways to use AI for marking include:

- **Drafting feedback comments.** Give the tool a few examples of the comments you write, then ask it to suggest similar comments for common situations, such as good effort with weak structure. You choose which comment fits each student.
- **Building rubrics.** Ask the tool to draft a simple marking guide for an assignment: what earns full marks, what earns half, and what to look for.
- **Spotting patterns.** If you paste in a set of short answers with names removed, the tool can summarise the common mistakes the class made, so your next lesson can target them.
- **Setting practice questions.** Ask for questions at different levels of difficulty on a topic, then pick the best ones and adjust them.

## Parent Communication: Clear Messages in Less Time

Many teachers delay writing to parents because each message takes time and care. AI can help you draft messages that are clear, polite and professional, whether it is a note about homework, a meeting invitation, or a gentle concern about a child's progress.

A simple workflow:

1. Write down the key facts in plain words: what happened, what you need, and what happens next.
2. Ask the tool to turn it into a short, respectful message suitable for WhatsApp or a school letter.
3. Read it back and adjust the tone. It should sound like you, not like a robot.
4. Send it yourself, and follow up in person for sensitive matters.

Sensitive issues should always involve a real conversation, not just a message. AI can draft the words; it cannot read a parent's reaction or show the empathy the moment needs.

## Quizzes, Worksheets and Revision Materials

Once you are comfortable with lesson planning, the same tools can generate practice materials quickly: short quizzes with answers, worksheets at two difficulty levels, one-page revision summaries in simple language, and short stories that explain difficult ideas. Always check facts first. AI tools sometimes invent details that sound correct but are wrong, so verify anything you will teach from against your textbook.

## Running a School: Where AI Helps Administrators Too

School owners and head teachers can use the same tools for administration: drafting announcements and newsletters, writing job adverts, summarising meeting notes into action points, and planning the school calendar. These tasks are all writing-heavy and repetitive, which is exactly where AI is strongest.

## Free and Low-Cost Tools Worth Trying

You do not need a paid subscription to start. These widely available tools all have free versions that cover the tasks in this guide:

- **ChatGPT** (free version): lesson drafts, feedback comments, parent messages, quizzes.
- **Google Gemini** (free version): lesson planning, summaries, and works well alongside Google Docs.
- **Microsoft Copilot** (free version): drafting and summarising, useful if your school already uses Microsoft tools.
- **Canva** (free version): neat worksheets, posters and slides, with AI help for layouts and text.
- **Google Docs voice typing** (free): speak your lesson notes instead of typing them.

Start with one. Use it for two weeks on a single task before deciding whether to add another.

## Keeping Students Safe: Rules Every School Needs

AI in school raises real questions about safety and privacy, and a school should set simple rules before tools spread on their own:

- **Never enter students' full names, photos or personal details into a public AI tool.** Remove names before pasting any student work.
- **Check everything before it reaches a child.** AI can produce wrong facts or unsuitable content. The teacher is the filter.
- **Teach students honesty.** Show older students how to use AI for learning and revision, not for copying homework.
- **Keep records offline.** Attendance, grades and fees belong in the school's own records, not in a chatbot conversation.
- **Talk to parents.** Let parents know the school uses AI tools to help teachers prepare, and that no child's personal information is shared.

## A Simple First-Week Plan

- **Day 1:** Pick one tool and one task, such as drafting next week's lesson notes.
- **Day 2:** Write three specific prompts for your real lessons and save the best results.
- **Day 3:** Edit one AI draft into a full lesson note and teach from it.
- **Day 4:** Try drafting feedback comments or a parent message.
- **Day 5:** Review the week. What saved time? What needed too much fixing? Keep what worked.

## FAQ

### Is it cheating for a teacher to use AI to write lesson notes?

No, as long as you read, edit and take responsibility for what you teach. Using a tool to draft a plan is no different from using a textbook or a colleague's advice. The teaching, the examples and the judgement are still yours.

### Do these tools work on a phone?

Yes. The main AI chat tools and Canva all work in a phone browser or app. A phone is enough for drafting lessons, messages and quizzes.

### Will AI replace teachers?

No. AI can draft, summarise and organise, but it cannot manage a classroom, understand a child's struggles, inspire a student or build trust with parents. The teachers who benefit most are the ones who use AI to spend more time teaching and less time on paperwork.

### What if our school has poor internet?

Use AI when you have a connection, and save your results offline. Draft several lessons or worksheets in one session, save them as documents on your phone, and use them through the week without needing to be online again.
