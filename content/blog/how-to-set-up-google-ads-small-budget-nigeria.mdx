Google Ads can feel like a luxury for big companies, but a small business in Lagos, Abuja or Port Harcourt can start with a modest daily budget and still reach people who are actively searching for exactly what it sells. The difference between wasting money and getting customers is not the size of the budget. It is the structure: the right keywords, the right location settings, a clear landing page and a habit of checking the numbers every week. This guide walks you through the setup, step by step, using Nigerian examples and naira figures. Whether you sell hair, run a laundry service or offer catering, the process is the same. You need a plan, a small test budget and the discipline to pause what does not work.


## How Much Should You Budget?

Many Nigerian small businesses start with ₦30,000 to ₦60,000 for a first test month, roughly ₦1,000 to ₦2,000 per day. Others start with ₦75,000 to ₦150,000 per month if the service is high value, such as real estate, event planning or corporate training. Do not start with ₦500 per day and expect clear results. At that level you may get only a handful of clicks, and you will not learn enough to judge whether the campaign works. Pick a test budget you can afford while you learn, then scale what works. Low-value, high-volume services such as laundry and food delivery can start with ₦1,500 to ₦3,000 per day. Mid-value services such as fashion, beauty, home cleaning and phone repairs can start with ₦2,000 to ₦4,000 per day. High-value services such as wedding planning, real estate and corporate catering can start with ₦3,000 to ₦5,000 per day. Your budget should also match your capacity. If you can only handle five new customers a week, do not spend money to attract fifty.

## Step 1: Get Your Business Ready Before You Spend a Kobo

Before you create an account, sort out the basics. Google Ads will send people somewhere, and that somewhere must be ready to turn a visitor into a customer. First, decide exactly what you want people to do. Pick one action per campaign: send a WhatsApp message, call this number, fill the booking form, or buy this product. Do not send people to a homepage and hope they find their way. If your ad says "Affordable Wedding Catering in Lekki", the landing page should show your packages, sample menus, a price range, photos of past events and a clear WhatsApp button. Second, make sure your WhatsApp and phone lines work. Many Nigerian customers prefer WhatsApp to a website form. Add a WhatsApp button that is visible without scrolling, test it yourself, and check that someone replies quickly. If you reply after six hours, the customer has already found someone else. Third, prepare a simple price range. "Catering from ₦8,500 per guest" is more useful than "Affordable catering". If your price is hidden, you will pay for clicks from people who cannot afford you. Fourth, check your website on a phone. Most clicks in Nigeria come from mobile phones, so check that it loads fast, that the text is readable and that your phone number is clickable.

## Step 2: Create Your Google Ads Account

Creating the account is free. You only pay when someone clicks your ad, or when your ad is shown, depending on the campaign type. For this guide, we focus on Search campaigns, where you pay per click. Go to ads.google.com and sign in with a Google account you control. Use a business email if you have one. Avoid using a personal account that someone else manages, because you will need access to billing and reports later. Google will try to guide you through a simplified setup, which often creates a broad campaign that spends money quickly. Look for the option to switch to Expert Mode. It gives you full control over keywords, locations, bidding and ad settings. It looks more complex at first, but it saves you money. When Google asks you to create your first campaign, look for the option to create an account without a campaign, or to skip campaign creation. You want to set up billing, locations and conversion tracking properly before you launch anything. Set your billing country to Nigeria and your currency to naira. Make sure the card you add has enough funds and that your bank allows online payments. Some Nigerian banks block international online transactions by default, so check with your bank if a payment fails. Fill in your business name exactly as customers know it and add your website address.

## Step 3: Set Up Conversion Tracking

Conversion tracking tells Google what a valuable action looks like, so it can show your ads to more people likely to take that action. Without it, you are flying blind. For most Nigerian small businesses, the most important conversions are a WhatsApp message started from your website, a phone call from your ad or website, a form submission such as a booking or quote request, and a purchase if you sell online. Google Ads will give you a small piece of code, called the Google tag, to add to your website. If you use WordPress, you can add it through a plugin or your theme settings. If a developer manages your site, send them the tag and ask them to install it on every page. Next, create a conversion action for WhatsApp clicks. In Google Ads, go to Tools, then Conversions, then create a new conversion. Choose the website option, name it clearly, such as "WhatsApp Click", and set the value to the average value of an enquiry if you know it. Also create a conversion action for phone calls. Even a simple setup that counts calls lasting more than 30 seconds is better than no tracking at all. Test the tracking before you launch. Submit a test form, click the WhatsApp button and make a test call if possible. Do not launch and discover a week later that no conversions were recorded.

## Step 4: Do Keyword Research the Nigerian Way

Keywords are the words people type into Google. Your job is to find the words that your real customers use, not the words you use inside your business. Start with what customers actually say. Write down ten phrases a customer might type. Think like a customer in Lagos or Abuja, not like a business owner. A customer does not search for "integrated laundry solutions". They search for "laundry near me", "dry cleaner in Surulere" or "wash and iron in Lekki". A caterer should think about "wedding caterer Lekki" and "small chops for party Lagos". A fashion seller should think about "ankara styles for wedding" and "ready to wear dresses Lagos". A real estate agent should think about "2 bedroom flat for rent Lekki". Use Google Keyword Planner, which is free inside Google Ads. Go to Tools, then Keyword Planner, then Discover New Keywords. Enter your seed phrases and set the location to Nigeria, or to Lagos if that is your market. The tool will show related keywords, average monthly searches and a suggested bid range. Pay attention to intent. A keyword with many searches but low intent, such as "wedding ideas", is less valuable than one with fewer searches but high intent, such as "wedding caterer prices Lekki". The second person is ready to buy. For your first campaign, start with 20 to 40 strong keywords, grouped by theme. For a catering business, one group might be wedding catering, another corporate catering, and another small chops.

## Step 5: Choose the Right Match Types and Negative Keywords

Match types control how closely a search must match your keyword before your ad shows. Choosing the wrong match type is one of the fastest ways to waste a small budget. For a small budget, start with phrase match and exact match. Phrase match shows your ad when the search contains your keyword phrase in the same order, with extra words before or after. Exact match shows your ad when the search means the same thing as your keyword. If your keyword is "wedding caterer Lekki" on phrase match, your ad can show for "best wedding caterer Lekki" or "wedding caterer Lekki prices". That is useful and controlled. Be careful with broad match at the start. It can show your ad for searches only loosely related to your keyword, bringing irrelevant clicks that waste money. Use phrase and exact match until you understand what your customers search for. Negative keywords tell Google when NOT to show your ad. If you sell wedding dresses, add "free", "DIY", "how to sew" and "second hand" as negative keywords unless you offer those things. If you are a caterer in Lekki, add locations you do not serve, such as "Ibadan" or "Enugu". Check your Search Terms report after the first week and add every irrelevant search as a negative keyword. This one habit can save you thousands of naira every month.

## Step 6: Set Your Location, Language and Schedule

Location targeting is where many Nigerian advertisers lose money. If you serve only Lagos, do not show ads to the whole country. In your campaign settings, choose "Enter another location" and type Lagos, or a specific area such as Lekki, Ikeja or Surulere. Choose "People in or regularly in your targeted locations". Avoid the default that includes people interested in your location but not physically there. If you deliver nationwide, you can target Nigeria, but consider starting with Lagos and Abuja, where your delivery is fastest, then expand after you see results. Set your language to English. Most searches in Nigeria are in English. Set your ad schedule to your working hours. If you reply to WhatsApp messages between 8am and 8pm, do not run ads at 2am when nobody will answer. Start with 7am to 9pm, then adjust based on when you actually get enquiries. If you run an online store that takes orders automatically, you can run ads all day, but check whether late-night clicks convert or just cost you money. Most traffic will come from mobile, so start with all devices and reduce the bid on any device that spends money without bringing enquiries.

## Step 7: Choose a Bidding Strategy

Bidding is how Google decides how much to pay for a click. For a small budget, keep it simple at first. Start with manual CPC or Maximise Clicks. Manual CPC lets you set the maximum you are willing to pay per click. Maximise Clicks lets Google get as many clicks as possible within your budget. Both are good for learning. Manual CPC gives you more control. Maximise Clicks is easier to manage. If you start with manual CPC, set a maximum cost per click that makes sense for your business. If a customer is worth ₦50,000 to you, paying ₦200 to ₦500 per click can be reasonable. If a customer is worth ₦5,000, paying ₦500 per click is too much. Work backwards from the value of a customer. After you have at least 20 to 30 conversions, you can try Maximise Conversions or Target CPA, which is cost per acquisition. Smart bidding uses Google's data to find people more likely to convert, but it needs data to work well, so do not switch too early. Give your campaign at least two weeks of steady data before you change strategy. Do not chase the top position at any cost: positions two and three often bring cheaper clicks and similar results.

## Step 8: Write Ads That People Actually Click

Your ad is a small shop window. You have very little space, so every word must earn its place. Put the keyword in the headline. If someone searches for "wedding caterer Lekki", your headline should include those words. Google bolds the matching words, and the searcher sees immediately that your ad is relevant. Write at least three headlines and two descriptions for each ad, and let Google test different combinations. Example headlines for a caterer could be "Wedding Caterer in Lekki", "Tasty Food for Your Big Day", "Catering from ₦8,500 per Guest" and "Book Your Date Today". Include a clear benefit and a call to action. "Fresh, hot food delivered on time" is a benefit. "Chat on WhatsApp now" is a call to action. Avoid vague claims like "best service". Be specific. Mention your location, your price range, your delivery time or your experience. Use ad assets to take up more space. Add sitelink assets that link to Menus, Prices, Gallery or Book Now. Add call assets with your phone number, and location assets if you have a Google Business Profile. Add callout assets for short benefits, such as "Free delivery in Lekki" or "Same-day service". Write for a Nigerian customer: mention the area you serve and WhatsApp if that is how people contact you.

## Step 9: Build a Landing Page That Converts

The ad gets the click. The landing page gets the customer. A weak landing page wastes good ads. Match the page to the ad. If the ad promises catering prices, the page must show catering prices. If the ad promises same-day delivery, the page must explain how same-day delivery works. A mismatch makes people leave immediately, and Google notices that. Keep the page focused on one offer. Do not send ad traffic to a page with ten different services. Send it to a page about one service. Remove distractions. Keep the headline, the main benefit, social proof and the contact button visible. If you have testimonials from past customers in Lagos or Abuja, add two or three with names and locations. Real names and real places build trust. Make contact easy and obvious. Your phone number and WhatsApp button should be visible at the top and bottom of the page. Do not make people scroll to find how to reach you. If you use a form, keep it short. Name, phone number and a short message are enough. Every extra field reduces the number of people who complete it. Make the page fast: a page that takes more than three seconds to load on a phone will lose visitors, so compress your images and avoid heavy videos.

## Step 10: Launch, Then Check Daily for the First Week

Launching is not the end. It is the start of learning. Set your daily budget, make sure your ads are approved, and launch. Check the campaign after a few hours to confirm it is getting impressions. If it gets no impressions, check your location settings, your keywords and your bids. A bid that is too low can mean your ads rarely show. Check your Search Terms report every day at first. It shows the actual searches that triggered your ads. You will find good keywords you had not thought of, and you will find irrelevant searches to block. Add the good ones as keywords, and add the bad ones as negative keywords. Do this daily for the first week, then weekly. Pause keywords that spend without converting. If a keyword has spent ₦5,000 and brought no enquiries, pause it or reduce its bid. If a keyword brings enquiries at a good cost, keep it and consider increasing its budget. Do not keep a keyword running just because it gets clicks. Clicks are not customers.



## A Simple Weekly Routine and When to Scale

You do not need to live inside Google Ads. Spend 30 minutes each week checking spend, conversions and cost per conversion, reviewing your Search Terms report for new negative keywords, and testing one new ad variation. Increase your budget only when your cost per conversion has been profitable for at least two weeks. Raise it by 20 to 30 percent at a time, watch the numbers for a week, and step back if the cost per conversion rises sharply. Do not increase your budget because you feel impatient. Increase it because the numbers say the campaign is profitable.


## Final Thoughts

Google Ads on a small budget in Nigeria is not about tricks or shortcuts. It is about focus: a clear offer, a tight set of keywords, a location you can serve, a landing page that makes contact easy, and a weekly habit of checking what works. Start with one campaign, one service and one location. Spend a small test budget for two to three weeks. Learn from the Search Terms report. Pause what wastes money. Keep what brings enquiries. Then scale slowly. You do not need to outspend the big companies. You need to out-serve the customers in your area, and Google Ads can help you reach them at the exact moment they are looking for you. Start small, stay consistent, and let the numbers guide your next step.
