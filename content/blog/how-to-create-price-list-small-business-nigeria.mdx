A customer asks, "How much is this dress?" You hesitate. You quote ₦12,000. The next day, another customer asks for the same dress, and you quote ₦15,000 because you forgot the first price. A third customer asks on WhatsApp, and you take ten minutes to reply because you need to check your supplier's price. This confusion is common in Nigerian small businesses, and it costs sales.

A price list solves this problem. It is a simple document that shows your products or services, their prices, and what each price includes. When you have a price list, you answer faster, you charge consistently, your staff charges the same price when you are away, and customers trust you more because your prices look organised and fair.

Many small business owners in Nigeria avoid price lists because they fear customers will bargain less, or because prices change often, or because they do not know how to calculate a good price. This guide will show you how to create a price list that sells. You will learn how to calculate prices that cover your costs and leave a profit, how to organise the list, how to present it on WhatsApp, Instagram, and in your shop, and how to update it when costs change.

## Why a Price List Helps You Sell More

A price list does more than list numbers. It builds trust. When a customer sees a clear price, they feel the seller is honest. When prices are hidden or change from person to person, customers suspect they are being cheated. In a market where trust is hard to earn, a clear price list is a strong advantage.

A price list also saves time. If you sell 20 products, and each customer asks for five prices, you will spend your whole day answering price questions. With a price list on WhatsApp or printed in your shop, customers can see prices themselves and ask only about the items they truly want. You can then spend your time closing sales, not repeating prices.

A price list helps you sell more per customer. When a customer sees your full range in one place, they may notice an item they did not plan to buy. A customer who came for a ₦10,000 handbag may see a matching purse for ₦4,000 and add it to the order. A customer who ordered jollof rice for ₦3,500 may see a small chops pack for ₦2,000 and add it. This is called upselling, and a good price list makes it happen naturally.

A price list also protects your profit. When you calculate prices properly and write them down, you are less likely to sell at a loss in a rush or under pressure from a hard bargainer. You have a reference to check. Your staff has the same reference. That consistency protects your business.

Finally, a price list makes your business look professional. A tailor with a printed price list for styles, a food vendor with a clear menu and prices, a salon with a service list, or a phone accessories seller with a WhatsApp catalogue all look more serious than a seller who says, "Just ask me." Customers are more likely to recommend a professional-looking business to others.

## Step 1: List Every Product and Service You Sell

Start by writing down everything you sell. Do not rely on memory. Open a notebook or a sheet on your phone and list each product or service, one per line. If you sell clothes, list each type: Ankara gowns, chiffon tops, jeans, corporate shirts, children's wear. If you sell food, list each dish and each size: small, medium, large. If you offer services, list each service: hair braiding, wig installation, manicure, pedicure, makeup.

For each item, write a short, clear description. Customers need to know what they are paying for. Instead of "Dress – ₦12,000," write "Ankara midi gown, knee length, sizes 8–16 – ₦12,000." Instead of "Hair – ₦20,000," write "Knotless braids, medium length, with attachment included – ₦20,000." Clear descriptions reduce questions and reduce complaints after the sale.

Group similar items together. Put all dresses in one group, all tops in another, all children's wear in another. Put all food packs in one group, drinks in another, extras in another. Grouping helps customers find what they want quickly. It also helps you see which groups earn the most money and which groups need more products.

If you have many products, start with your 20 best sellers. You do not need to list 200 items on your first price list. List the items customers ask for most, and add the rest later. A short, accurate price list is better than a long, outdated one with wrong prices.

Remove items you no longer sell or cannot supply reliably. An outdated price list creates disappointment. If a customer orders an item from your list and you say, "We do not have that one again," they will lose trust. Review your list every month and remove what is gone.

## Step 2: Calculate Your True Cost for Each Item

This is the most important step. Many Nigerian small businesses fail because they do not know their true cost. They know the supplier price, but they forget transport, packaging, data, and other costs. Then they set a selling price that looks like a profit but is actually a loss.

For each item, add up all the costs involved in getting it to the customer. Start with the purchase cost or production cost. If you buy a handbag for ₦7,000, that is your starting cost. If you make a dress, add the cost of fabric, thread, zip, lining, and your tailor's fee or your own time.

Next, add transport and delivery costs. If you pay ₦2,000 for transport to buy ten handbags, add ₦200 to each bag. If you pay a rider ₦1,500 to deliver an order, decide whether delivery is included in your price or charged separately. Be clear about this in your price list.

Next, add packaging costs. A good bag, box, or wrapper may cost ₦200 to ₦500 per item. Customers notice packaging, and it protects your product, so include it in your cost.

Next, add a share of other monthly costs, such as rent, data, and electricity. If your monthly costs are ₦60,000 and you sell 200 items, add ₦300 to each item.

Now you have your true cost. For the handbag example: purchase ₦7,000, transport ₦200, packaging ₦300, other costs ₦300. True cost is ₦7,800. If you sell it for ₦8,000, your profit is only ₦200. That is too low. You need a selling price that leaves a real profit after all costs.

Do this calculation for your top ten products first. You will be surprised by how many items you have been selling at a very small profit, or even at a loss. Knowing the truth is the first step to fixing it.

## Step 3: Choose a Profit Margin That Works in Nigeria

Once you know your true cost, you need to add a profit margin. A margin is the percentage you add to your cost to make your selling price. Different businesses need different margins.

For clothes, shoes, and fashion items, a margin of 40% to 70% is common, because these items sell more slowly and you may need to offer discounts. If a dress costs you ₦9,000 in total, a 50% margin gives a selling price of ₦13,500. You can round it to ₦14,000. That leaves room for a small discount and still protects your profit.

For services such as hair, makeup, cleaning, or repairs, your main cost is your time and skill. Price your time fairly. If a service takes three hours and uses ₦5,000 of materials, and you want to earn at least ₦2,000 per hour for your skill, your price should be at least ₦11,000. Do not price your skill at zero. Your time is a real cost.

Use round numbers that are easy to pay. In Nigeria, prices like ₦2,500, ₦5,000, ₦7,500, ₦10,000, and ₦15,000 are easier for customers to remember and pay by transfer than prices like ₦2,370 or ₦13,480. Round your calculated price to the nearest sensible number. If your calculation gives ₦13,500, use ₦14,000. If it gives ₦2,100, use ₦2,000 or ₦2,500, depending on your market.

## Step 4: Organise Your Price List So Customers Can Read It Fast

A price list that is hard to read will not sell. Customers will skip it and ask you directly, which defeats the purpose. Make it clear, simple, and easy to scan.

Use clear headings for each group. For a fashion business, headings could be "Dresses," "Tops," "Trousers," "Children's Wear," and "Accessories." For a food business, headings could be "Rice Dishes," "Swallow and Soup," "Small Chops," "Drinks," and "Extras." For a salon, headings could be "Hair," "Nails," "Makeup," and "Extras."

Under each heading, list items in a simple format: item name, short description, and price. Keep descriptions to one line. Use the same format for every item, so customers learn how to read the list quickly.

Add a note about what the price includes. For example, "All prices include packaging. Delivery is charged separately, from ₦1,000 within Lekki." For a service, add "Price includes materials. Home service attracts an extra ₦2,000." These notes prevent arguments later. Customers appreciate knowing the full cost before they order.

Add your business name, phone number, WhatsApp number, location, and opening hours at the top or bottom of the list. A price list without contact details is a missed opportunity. A customer who receives your price list from a friend should be able to contact you without asking for your number.

Use large, readable text. If you print the list, use a font size customers can read easily. If you share it on WhatsApp as an image, make sure the text is not too small or blurry. Test it by sending it to yourself and reading it on your phone. If you cannot read it easily, your customers cannot either.

## Step 5: Choose the Right Format for Your Customers

Your price list can live in several places, and you should use the format your customers already use.

For WhatsApp, use the WhatsApp Business catalogue. Add each product with a clear photo, a short description, and the price. Customers can browse the catalogue without messaging you first. Also keep a simple price list as a PDF or an image you can send quickly when a customer asks, "Send your price list." Save it in your phone so you can send it in seconds, not minutes.

For Instagram, use Highlights. Create a Highlight called "Price List" and save your price list images there. Also add prices to your post captions when you post a product. Many customers will not ask if the price is already in the caption. If you do not want to show prices publicly, add "DM for price" only if you have a good reason, because many customers will scroll past a post with no price.

Keep one master price list that you update, and then copy it into the other formats. If you update WhatsApp but forget Instagram, customers will see different prices and lose trust. Update all formats on the same day.

## How to Handle Price Changes and Bargaining

Prices in Nigeria change often. Fuel prices rise, the naira changes value, suppliers increase their prices, and transport costs go up. Your price list must change with these costs, or your profit will disappear.

Review your price list every month. Check your supplier prices, your transport costs, and your other costs. If a cost has risen by more than 10%, consider raising your selling price. Do not wait six months and then raise prices by 50% at once. Customers will be shocked. Small, regular updates are easier for them to accept.

When you raise a price, tell your regular customers in advance if you can. A short message such as, "Good morning, ma. From next week, our lunch pack will be ₦4,000 instead of ₦3,500, because the cost of rice and chicken has increased. Thank you for your understanding," is respectful and honest. Most customers will accept a fair increase if they understand the reason.

A price list does not stop bargaining, but it reduces it. When a customer sees a written price, they are less likely to offer half of it. When they do bargain, you can point to the list and say, "That is our price, ma. It is the same for everyone." This is fair and firm. You can still offer a small discount for bulk orders or loyal customers, but the list gives you a strong starting point.

For bulk orders, add a note to your price list, such as "Buy 5, get 5% off. Buy 10, get 10% off." This encourages larger orders and gives customers a clear discount rule, instead of a long negotiation. Make sure the discount still leaves you a profit. Calculate it before you print it.

Do not change your price for each customer. If one customer pays ₦10,000 and another pays ₦15,000 for the same item on the same day, word will spread, and customers will feel cheated. Charge the same price to everyone, except for a clear, published discount such as a bulk discount or a loyalty discount. Fairness builds a reputation that brings more customers than secret discounts ever will.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid When Creating a Price List

Avoid these mistakes, and your price list will work much better.

The first mistake is guessing prices without calculating costs. If you set a price because it "sounds good" or because a friend uses it, you may be selling at a loss. Always calculate your true cost first, even if it takes an hour. That hour can save your business.

The third mistake is forgetting delivery and extra charges. If delivery is not included, say so clearly. If a service has an extra charge for home service, say so. Hidden charges cause arguments and bad reviews. Clear charges build trust.

## A Simple Example Price List

Here is a short example for a small fashion business in Lagos, to show you the format. You can adapt it to your own business.

Dresses: Ankara midi gown, sizes 8–16 – ₦14,000. Chiffon maxi gown, sizes 8–14 – ₦18,000. Corporate shirt dress, sizes 8–16 – ₦12,000. Children's Ankara dress, ages 3–8 – ₦7,000.

Tops: Ankara peplum top, sizes 8–16 – ₦8,000. Chiffon blouse, sizes 8–16 – ₦9,000. Corporate shirt, sizes 8–16 – ₦7,500.

Accessories: Handbag, medium, leather – ₦12,000. Scarf, silk – ₦3,500. Earrings, gold-plated – ₦2,500.

Note: All prices include packaging. Delivery within Lekki is ₦1,500. Delivery to other parts of Lagos is from ₦2,000. Payment by bank transfer or cash. Thank you for shopping with us.

This example is short, clear, and easy to read. A customer can find a dress, see the price, and know the delivery cost in less than a minute. That is the goal for your own list.

## Your 30-Day Plan to Build a Price List That Sells

In week one, list your products and calculate your true cost for your top ten items. Write down the purchase cost, transport, packaging, and other costs for each one. This is the hardest part, but it is the most valuable.

In week two, choose your profit margins and set your selling prices. Round them to sensible numbers. Check two or three competitors to make sure your prices are fair for your area. Write your first price list in a notebook or a sheet, with clear headings and short descriptions.

In week three, create the formats your customers use. Add your products to your WhatsApp Business catalogue. Create a simple PDF or image price list you can send quickly. Print a copy for your shop if you have one. Add a Price List Highlight to Instagram if you use it.

In week four, share the list and test it. Send it to ten regular customers and ask if it is clear. Watch how many customers ask for prices after you share it. If they still ask many questions, your list may need clearer descriptions. If they order faster, your list is working. Update any prices that are wrong, and set a monthly date to review the list again.

A good price list is not a luxury for big businesses. It is a basic tool for any small business that wants to sell with confidence, charge fairly, and grow. Create yours this week, calculate your costs honestly, set prices that protect your profit, and share the list where your customers already are. Your customers will thank you for the clarity, your staff will thank you for the consistency, and your business will thank you for the profit.
