A customer visits your website at 9pm. She likes your product, but she has one question about delivery to her area. There is no one to ask, so she closes the tab and buys from a seller on Instagram who replies within minutes. Your website did nothing wrong. It simply had no way for her to ask.

This happens to Nigerian small businesses every day. A website that only lists information is a brochure. A website with live chat is a shop with someone at the counter. Adding live chat is simpler than most business owners expect, and this guide explains how to do it well.

## What live chat is, and what it is not

Live chat is a small chat window, usually a button in the bottom corner of a website, that lets a visitor send a message and get a reply without leaving the page. When someone is available, the conversation happens in real time. When nobody is available, the visitor can leave a message and the business replies later by email or WhatsApp.

Live chat is not the same as a chatbot, though the two often work together. A chatbot answers automatically with pre-written replies, which is useful for common questions like opening hours or prices. Live chat means a real person is answering. Many tools combine both: the chatbot handles simple questions instantly, and a person takes over when the question needs a human answer. If you are considering automated replies too, read our guide on [how to add an AI chatbot to your website](/blog/add-ai-chatbot-to-website), which explains where automation helps and where it gets in the way.

## Why live chat helps a small business website

The main benefit is speed. A visitor with a question is a visitor who is close to deciding. If they have to find your email address, write a formal email, and wait a day for a reply, most of them will not bother. Live chat removes that friction. The question is asked and answered in the same minute, while the visitor is still looking at your product.

Live chat also helps you learn what your customers actually worry about. After a few weeks of chats, you will see the same questions again and again: delivery areas, payment methods, sizes, prices, and timing. Those repeated questions show you exactly what your website should explain better and what your product pages are missing.

## Step one: decide who will answer the chats

Before choosing any tool, decide who will answer. A live chat window that nobody answers is worse than no chat at all, because it promises a quick reply and then breaks that promise.

For most small businesses, the answer is the owner or one staff member, during stated hours. You do not need a full support team. You need one person who checks the chats during working hours and replies within a few minutes. Put your chat hours on the website, for example "We reply within minutes, 8am to 6pm, Monday to Saturday." That sets an honest expectation.

If you cannot answer during certain hours, set the chat to show an offline message instead: "We are away right now. Leave your message and your WhatsApp number, and we will reply in the morning." Never leave the chat button promising instant replies when nobody is there.

## Step two: choose a live chat tool

There are many live chat tools, and most of them work the same basic way: you create an account, you get a small piece of code, and you paste it into your website. The chat window then appears on every page. When choosing, check these points:

- **A free plan that is genuinely usable.** Several tools offer a free plan for one or two users. For a small business starting out, that is usually enough.
- **A mobile app.** You will answer most chats from your phone, so the tool must have a reliable app that notifies you when a new message arrives.
- **Offline messages.** The tool should collect messages when you are away, and let you reply to them later.
- **WhatsApp handover.** Some tools let you move a chat to WhatsApp with one tap, which suits Nigerian customers who prefer to continue there.
- **Simple setup.** You should be able to add the chat to your site without rebuilding anything. Most tools need only a short code snippet pasted once.

## Step three: add the chat to your website

Adding live chat is usually a ten-minute job for a developer, and it follows the same steps on most platforms:

1. Create an account with your chosen live chat tool and set up your business name, welcome message, and working hours
2. Copy the small code snippet the tool gives you
3. Paste the code into your website, usually in the site settings, footer, or header section, so it appears on every page
4. Open your website on your phone and on a computer, and test the chat by sending a message to yourself
5. Check that the notification reaches your phone, and that you can reply from the app

If your website was built with WordPress, many chat tools also offer a plugin that does the same job without touching any code. Either way, test it yourself as a visitor would, on a phone with mobile data, not only on office Wi-Fi.

## How to answer chats so visitors become customers

The tool is only half the job. How you answer decides whether the visitor buys. A few habits make a large difference:

- **Reply quickly.** Aim for under five minutes during your stated hours. A fast first reply matters more than a perfect answer.
- **Answer the exact question asked** before offering anything else, and greet the visitor by name if they give it.
- **Keep answers short.** Long paragraphs are hard to read in a chat window. Give the answer in one or two sentences, then offer the next step.
- **Always end with a next step.** "Would you like me to confirm delivery to your area?" or "Shall I send you the payment details on WhatsApp?" A chat that ends with a question keeps the conversation moving toward a sale.
- **Save answers to common questions.** After a week, you will know the questions that repeat. Write short, friendly replies for them so you can answer in seconds.
- **Know when to move to WhatsApp or a call.** If the conversation needs photos, voice notes, or payment details, offer to continue on WhatsApp.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does live chat slow down my website?**

A good live chat tool adds very little weight to a page, but a heavy one can slow things down, especially on slow mobile connections. Choose a lightweight tool, test your site speed on a phone after adding it, and remove it if the delay is noticeable.

**What if I miss a chat while I am busy?**

The visitor should be able to leave a message, and you should reply as soon as you can. Most tools send missed chats to your email as well. Set a rule for yourself: every missed chat gets a reply the same day, even if it is just an apology and an answer.

**Should I use live chat or just a WhatsApp button?**

Both, if you can manage it. Some visitors prefer to chat on the spot, others prefer WhatsApp. Offering both meets each visitor where they are most comfortable.

**Is live chat worth it for a very small business?**

Yes, if you can answer within a few minutes during the day. Even a handful of chats a week can produce orders you would otherwise have lost. If you cannot answer at all during the day, start with a simple contact form and WhatsApp button, and add live chat when you have someone to answer it.

## Make your website answer back

A website that cannot answer questions is a missed sale waiting to happen. Live chat turns your website from a static page into a conversation, and conversations are where Nigerian customers decide to trust you and buy from you.

Start small: choose one simple tool, set honest hours, and answer every chat yourself for a few weeks. You will quickly see how many visitors were one quick answer away from becoming customers. That is the real value of live chat, and it costs far less than the orders it saves.
