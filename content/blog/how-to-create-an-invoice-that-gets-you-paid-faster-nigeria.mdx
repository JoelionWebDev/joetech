Every Nigerian small business owner knows the feeling. You deliver the goods or finish the job, the client is happy, and then the payment drags on. The client says "send me your account number" — as if you never gave it to them — and then goes quiet again.

In many cases, the problem is not that the client does not want to pay. The problem is that they were never given a clear, professional invoice. A WhatsApp message saying "please pay 250k" is not an invoice. An invoice fixes all of that: it turns a casual request into a clear record of what was delivered, how much is owed, and when payment is expected.

This guide covers what to put on an invoice, how to number it, payment terms and due dates, bank details, VAT, and following up on late payment.

## What an Invoice Is, and Why It Matters

An invoice is a document you send to a customer after you have delivered goods or completed a service. It is not a receipt, which confirms payment already made, or a quotation, which comes before the work starts.

An invoice matters for three practical reasons. First, it creates a record. If a client later disputes the amount or claims they never received the goods, your invoice is your evidence. Second, it speeds up payment inside companies. Their finance department needs a document with your business name, the amount, and your bank details before they can raise a payment.

Third, an invoice helps you track your own money. When you number your invoices and record which ones have been paid, you can see at a glance who owes you and how much. A neat invoice also builds trust, and organised businesses get paid first.

## What to Put on Every Invoice

A good invoice does not need to be complicated, but it does need to be complete. Your business name and contact details go at the top: business name, phone number, email address, . If your business is registered with the Corporate Affairs Commission, you can add your RC number. It adds credibility, especially with companies.

The word "INVOICE" should appear clearly at the top, so nobody mistakes it for a quotation or a receipt. Below it, add your invoice number and the date the invoice was issued. Next, add the client's details: the name of the person or company you are billing, their phone number or email address. For a company, name the contact person, so it reaches the right desk.

Then comes the most important part: a clear description of what you delivered. List each item or service on its own line, with the quantity, the unit price, and the line total. For example: "Branded T-shirts — 50 units at ₦4,000 = ₦200,000". Be specific. Vague descriptions such as "services rendered" cause problems

Below the items, show the subtotal, any discount you agreed, any tax, and the total amount due in naira, written clearly and in bold. Add your payment details in full: your bank name, your account name, and your account number. Put it on the invoice itself. Finally, add your payment terms and due date, and a short note thanking the client for their business.

## Invoice Numbering: Keep It Simple and Consistent

Every invoice needs a unique number. Numbering lets you and your client refer to a specific invoice without confusion and track what is paid. The easiest system is a sequence that starts at 001 and goes up: INV-001, INV-002, INV-003, and so on. You can add the year, for example INV-2026-001.

What matters is that you never repeat a number, and you never skip a number without a reason. If you cancel one, note it rather than reusing the number. Avoid starting your numbering at a high number to look bigger than you are. Starting at INV-500 to look bigger only raises questions. Start at 001 and let the numbers grow naturally.

Keep a simple record of your invoices, even if it is just a notebook or a spreadsheet. For each invoice, note the number, the date, the client, the amount, the due date, and whether it has been paid. This small habit saves you from discovering, months later, that a client never paid and you have no record of the invoice. Many owners lose money this way.

## Setting Payment Terms and Due Dates

Payment terms are the rules you set for when and how you expect to be paid, and they belong on the invoice itself. The most common terms for Nigerian small businesses are payment on delivery, payment within 7 days, payment within 14 days, or payment within 30 days.

For goods, payment on delivery or before delivery is normal. For services, especially work done for companies, 14 days is a fair and common term. Thirty days is common for larger clients, but a slow payer can stretch it to 60 or 90 days.

Whatever term you choose, write it clearly on the invoice: "Payment due within 14 days of the invoice date" and then state the actual due date, for example "Due date: 29 November 2026". A due date gives the client a day to work towards and you a day to follow up from. You can also state a late fee, such as 2% per month after the due date. For new clients, consider asking for a deposit before you start work — often 50% upfront, with the balance due on delivery. Agree it in writing and show it on the invoice.

## Bank Details, Transfers, and Other Ways to Get Paid

In Nigeria, bank transfer remains the most common way for businesses to pay each other, so your invoice must make a transfer easy. Print your bank name, account name, and account number clearly, and double-check the digits before you send the invoice. Confirm that the account name on the invoice matches the name on the bank account exactly.

If you run a registered business, use a business account where possible. Corporate clients often prefer, and sometimes require, payment into a business account. A business account is worth opening as you grow.

Beyond bank transfer, you can offer other options on your invoice. Customers may prefer a POS card payment in person, or a payment link online. Platforms such as Paystack and Flutterwave let you generate a payment link for card or transfer payments. Put any payment link on the invoice too. And never share your bank PIN, your one-time password, or your card details with anyone — a real client will never ask for them.

## What About VAT and Tax on Your Invoice?

Many small business owners in Nigeria are unsure whether to add VAT — Value Added Tax — to their invoices. VAT is charged at 7.5%, but not every small business must charge it. If your business is registered for VAT with the Federal Inland Revenue Service, you should charge VAT on your invoices and show it as a separate line, for example "VAT (7.5%): ₦15,000". If you are not VAT-registered, do not add a VAT line.

If you are not VAT-registered, the invoice simply shows the agreed price as the total. What matters is that you agree the price with the client in advance, and that the invoice matches the agreement. Some corporate clients need a VAT invoice for their own tax records.

Withholding tax is another term you may hear from corporate clients. When a company pays you for a service, they may deduct a percentage — commonly 5% for individuals and 10% for companies — and remit it to the tax authority. They should give you a withholding tax credit note as proof. Your invoice still shows the full amount, and the client pays you the amount after the deduction. If you are unsure, speak to an accountant before adding tax lines. Keep copies of every invoice for your accountant.

## Make Your Invoice Look Professional

An invoice does not need a designer to look professional, but it does need to be neat and easy to read. Use a clean layout: your business name and logo at the top, "INVOICE" in bold, and clear sections. Use one font throughout, and make sure the numbers line up in a simple table. A messy invoice suggests sloppy work.

If you have a business logo, put it on the invoice. If you do not have one yet, your business name in bold letters is enough. Use your brand colours lightly — a coloured header or a coloured total line is plenty. 

Save your invoice as a PDF before you send it, not as an editable Word document or a screenshot. A PDF cannot be edited by accident and is easy to file. If you are sending the invoice on WhatsApp, send the PDF file itself rather than a photo of it. Send the invoice promptly — the same day you deliver the goods or finish the job. Same-day invoicing gets you paid faster.

## How to Follow Up When Payment Is Late

Even with a perfect invoice, some clients will pay late. Following up is not begging — it is a normal part of doing business. Follow up early, politely, and in writing. Start with a gentle reminder a few days before the due date: "Hello, this is a friendly reminder that invoice INV-023 for ₦150,000 is due on Friday, 29 November." It often prompts payment before the deadline.

If the due date passes without payment, send a firmer reminder the next day. Keep it polite but clear: state the invoice number, the amount, the due date, and ask when you can expect payment. Attach the invoice again, so the client does not have to search for it. Invoices often get buried in a busy inbox.

If a week passes with no payment and no response, call the client. A call often reveals the real reason for the delay. Once you know the reason, agree on a new date together, and get that new date in writing. For clients who keep delaying, set a limit. After two or three follow-ups with no result, send a final written notice stating that the account is overdue and that you will pause further work or deliveries until the outstanding amount is settled. Keep every message, call note, and email as a record.

## Common Invoice Mistakes That Delay Payment

The first mistake is sending an invoice with missing information — no account number, no due date, or no description of what was delivered. The second mistake is sending the invoice to the wrong person. If you send it to the manager who ordered the work but not to the accounts person who pays, your invoice may sit unread for weeks. Ask who handles payments and send it to them too.

The third mistake is making the amount unclear. Adding charges the client never agreed to, or changing the price after the work is done, leads to disputes. Agree the price before you start, and make sure the invoice matches the agreement exactly. The fourth mistake is forgetting to follow up. Most clients pay the businesses that remind them, so a polite follow-up is part of your job.

The fifth mistake is having no system at all — amounts discussed only in voice notes, and no record of who has paid. Put everything in writing, keep a record of every invoice, and check your list of unpaid invoices at least once a week. Check it weekly.

## Templates and Tools to Create Invoices Quickly

You do not need expensive software. A simple template in Word or Google Docs, with your business name and a table for items, is enough to start. Create the template once, save it, and reuse it for every client, changing only the client details, the items, and the amounts. Export each invoice as a PDF before sending. A spreadsheet can also work well, especially if you want the totals to calculate themselves. Let the spreadsheet add the totals up.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What should I include on an invoice in Nigeria?

Your business name and contact details, the word "INVOICE", a unique invoice number, the date, the client's name and contact, each item with quantity and price, the total amount due, your bank name, account name and account number, your payment terms, and the due date.

### How long should I give a client to pay an invoice?

For goods, payment on delivery is normal. For services, 14 days is a fair and common term for small businesses in Nigeria. Large corporate clients may ask for 30 days. Always write the exact due date on the invoice.

### Do I need to add VAT to my invoices?

Only if your business is registered for VAT with the Federal Inland Revenue Service. VAT is charged at 7.5%. If you are not VAT-registered, do not add a VAT line — simply show the agreed price as the total. Ask for a withholding tax credit note if a client deducts tax.

### What is the best way to send an invoice to a client?

Send it as a PDF, by email or WhatsApp, to the person who handles payments as well as your main contact. Send it on the same day you deliver the goods or finish the work.

### What should I do if a client keeps saying "send me your account number"?

Put your full bank details — bank name, account name, and account number — on the invoice itself, and re-attach the invoice when you follow up. Send the details with the invoice if they still ask.

### Can I charge a late payment fee in Nigeria?

Yes, you can state a late payment fee in your payment terms, such as 2% per month after the due date, as long as the client agreed to those terms or they were clearly stated on the invoice before the work began.

First, create your invoice template today. Set up a simple template with your business name, logo if you have one, a table for items and prices, your bank details, and space for payment terms and a due date. Save it, export a sample as a PDF, and check that it reads clearly on a phone screen.

Second, send a proper invoice for every unpaid job from the last few weeks. Use the numbering system in this guide, starting from your next number, and include a clear due date on every invoice. Send any overdue ones today.

Third, check your unpaid invoices every week. Every Monday morning, open your invoice record, see who owes you, and send a polite reminder to anyone whose payment is due that week or already overdue. Businesses that follow up get paid. Businesses that wait, wait longer.
