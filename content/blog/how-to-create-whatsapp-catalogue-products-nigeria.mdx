If you sell products in Nigeria, your customers are already on WhatsApp. They use it to chat with family, follow up on orders and ask questions before they buy. Yet many small business owners still run their sales entirely through scattered chats. A customer asks "what do you have?" and you scroll through your gallery, send ten random photos, type prices one by one, and hope they do not get bored and move on to another seller. That is slow, stressful and it loses sales every single day.

A WhatsApp catalogue changes this completely. It is a built-in shop window inside the WhatsApp Business app. You list your products with photos, prices and short descriptions, and customers can browse everything in one place, tap on what they like, and send you a message about that exact item. No website needed, no extra app to download, and no cost to set up. Whether you sell fashion in Lekki, foodstuffs in Onitsha, hair in Abuja, phone accessories in Ikeja Computer Village or handmade soaps in Port Harcourt, a good catalogue makes you look professional and makes buying from you easy. This guide walks you through setting it up properly and using it to sell more.

## What a WhatsApp Catalogue Is and Why It Sells

A WhatsApp catalogue is a list of your products and services displayed inside your WhatsApp Business profile. When a customer opens your profile or taps the shopping bag icon in your chat, they see your items arranged like a small online shop, each with a photo, a name and a price. They can tap any item to see more photos and a description, and then tap "Message Business" to ask about it or place an order.

The reason it sells so well in Nigeria is simple. Customers do not want to download another app or create an account on a website just to see your prices. WhatsApp is already on their phone, they already trust it, and they already use it every day. A catalogue meets them where they are. It also answers the most common questions before they are asked: what do you sell, how much does it cost, and is it available? When those answers are visible, customers make decisions faster and you spend less time repeating the same information in chat after chat.

A catalogue also builds trust. A seller with a neat catalogue, clear prices and good photos looks like a serious business. A seller who only sends random pictures in chat looks disorganised, even if their products are excellent. In a market where customers worry about scams and fake sellers, looking organised is a real competitive advantage. Your catalogue is often the first impression a new customer gets, so it is worth doing well.

## Step 1: Switch to WhatsApp Business and Set Up Your Profile

You cannot create a catalogue with regular WhatsApp. You need the WhatsApp Business app, which is free and available on the Google Play Store and the Apple App Store. If you are still using regular WhatsApp for your business, download WhatsApp Business and move your number to it. Your chats and contacts carry over, and your customers will not notice any disruption. Keep your personal number separate from your business number if you can. It protects your privacy and stops business messages from mixing with family chats at midnight.

Once the app is installed, set up your business profile properly. Tap the three dots, go to Settings, then Business Tools, then Business Profile. Add your business name exactly as customers know it. Add your business category, for example clothing store, grocery store or beauty salon. Add a short description that says what you sell and who it is for. A fashion seller might write "Affordable ready-to-wear outfits and shoes for men and women in Lagos. Nationwide delivery available." Add your address or the area you operate in, your business hours, and your email if you have one. Add your website or Instagram link if you have one.

Choose a clear profile photo, ideally your logo or a clean photo of your best product. This photo appears next to every message you send, so it should look professional. Avoid blurry photos or pictures with too much text. When your profile is complete, customers who find you through a group or a referral can see at a glance that you are a real business. This step takes about fifteen minutes and it is the foundation everything else is built on.

## Step 2: Take Product Photos That Make People Want to Buy

Photos are the most important part of your catalogue. Customers cannot touch your products, so the photo has to do all the selling. You do not need a professional camera or a studio. A good smartphone, natural daylight and a clean background are enough. Take your photos near a window or outside in the morning light. Avoid using your phone flash, which creates harsh shadows and washes out colours.

Use a plain background for every product. A white bedsheet, a plain wall or a clean table works well. The background should never distract from the product. If you sell clothes, hang them neatly or lay them flat and smooth out the creases. If you sell food items, arrange them in a clean bowl or container. If you sell shoes or bags, photograph them from the front and from the side so customers can see the shape. Take at least three photos of each important product: one main shot, one close-up showing the quality or texture, and one showing the product in use or being worn.

Keep your photos consistent. Use the same background, the same lighting and the same style for all your products. Consistency makes your catalogue look like a real shop instead of a random collection of pictures. Before uploading a photo, check it on your own phone at full size. Is it sharp? Are the colours true to life? Can you see the details? If the photo is blurry, dark or misleading, retake it. A photo that makes a ₦25,000 dress look like a ₦5,000 dress will cost you sales, and a photo that makes a product look better than it is will cost you customers when the real item arrives.

## Step 3: Add Your Products With Clear Names, Prices and Descriptions

Now add your products to the catalogue. In the WhatsApp Business app, go to Business Tools, then Catalogue, then tap "Add New Item". You can add up to ten photos per item. Upload your best photo first, because that is the one customers see in the list. Add more photos to show different angles, colours or sizes.

Write a clear product name. Use the words customers actually search for. "Red Ankara Gown – Size 12" is far better than "Beautiful Gown". "5kg Bag of Foreign Rice" is better than "Rice". "Human Hair Wig, 18 Inches, Bone Straight" is better than "Fine Wig". Good names help customers find what they want quickly and they also help when someone searches for your product.

Add the price in naira, and make sure it is current. Nothing frustrates a customer more than seeing one price in the catalogue and being told a different price in the chat. If your prices change because of the dollar rate or supplier costs, update the catalogue the same day. If a product has different prices for different sizes, mention it in the description, for example "Small ₦8,000, Medium ₦9,000, Large ₦10,000". If you offer a discount, show the original price crossed out in your description and write the promo price clearly.

Write a short description for each product, two or three sentences that answer the questions a buyer would ask. What is it made of? What sizes or colours are available? How long does delivery take? For example: "Pure cotton Ankara gown, fully lined. Available in sizes 8 to 16. Ready to wear, no sewing needed. Delivery within Lagos takes 24 hours." Add a product code if you have many similar items, such as "Code: AG-014", so customers can refer to the exact item when they message you.

## Step 4: Organise Your Catalogue Into Collections

When your catalogue grows beyond ten or fifteen items, customers start to struggle. They scroll endlessly and give up before finding what they want. Collections solve this by grouping similar products together. WhatsApp Business lets you create collections and assign products to them, so customers can browse by category instead of scrolling through everything.

Think about how your customers shop. A fashion seller might create collections called "Gowns", "Two-Piece Sets", "Shoes" and "Bags". A foodstuff seller might use "Rice and Grains", "Oils", "Spices" and "Foodstuff Combos". A beauty seller might use "Wigs", "Hair Care", "Makeup" and "Skin Care". A phone accessories seller might use "Chargers", "Phone Cases", "Earphones" and "Power Banks". Keep the number of collections small, five or six at most, and give each one a name your customers will understand instantly.

Put your best sellers and newest arrivals in a collection of their own, such as "New Arrivals" or "Best Sellers", so they are easy to find. You can also create a collection for items on promotion, like "December Deals" or "Clearance", which draws attention during festive seasons when Nigerians are buying gifts. Review your collections every month. Remove products you no longer stock, add new ones, and move items between collections when it makes sense. A tidy catalogue with working collections tells customers you are active and organised.

## Step 5: Promote Your Catalogue Everywhere Customers Find You

A catalogue only sells if people know it exists. Share it everywhere your customers already find you. The easiest way is to share the catalogue link. In the app, go to your catalogue, tap the share icon, and you will get a link you can post on your WhatsApp status, your Instagram bio, your Facebook page and your business flyers. Add a short message with it, such as "Browse all our products and prices here, no need to ask. Tap to shop."

Post your catalogue link on your WhatsApp status at least twice a week. Do not just post the link alone. Post a photo of a popular product with the price and add "Full catalogue in my profile, tap to browse". When you add new products, announce them on your status the same day. Customers who see new arrivals regularly will keep coming back to check what is new, and that repeat attention turns into sales.

Add the catalogue link to your Instagram bio and mention it in your posts. When someone comments "Price?" on your Instagram post, reply with the price and add "Full price list in our WhatsApp catalogue, link in bio". This moves interested people from Instagram to WhatsApp, where the sale actually happens. You can also print a short link or QR code on your flyers, business cards and packaging, so customers who meet you at a market or an event can find your full range later.

Also use your catalogue inside chats. When a customer asks what you have, do not send ten separate photos. Send the catalogue link or send the specific item from the catalogue using the attachment option. This is faster for you and clearer for the customer, because they see the price and description attached to the product instead of a photo with no context.

## Step 6: Turn Catalogue Browsers Into Paying Customers

Getting people to browse is only half the job. You also need to convert them into buyers, and that happens in the chat after they tap on a product. Reply quickly. In Nigeria, a customer who messages three sellers about the same item will buy from the one who replies first. Aim to reply within fifteen minutes during your business hours. If you are busy, set up a greeting message in the Business Tools section that acknowledges them immediately, for example "Hello, thanks for messaging us. We will reply shortly. Meanwhile, you can browse our full catalogue here."

When a customer asks about a product, confirm the details before taking payment. Restate the item, the size or colour, the price and the delivery fee. For example: "Yes, the red Ankara gown in size 12 is available at ₦25,000. Delivery to Ikeja is ₦2,500, so your total is ₦27,500." This prevents misunderstandings and builds trust. Ask for their delivery address and confirm the delivery time. If the item is out of stock, say so immediately and suggest a similar item from your catalogue. Never take money for an item you do not have.

For payments, keep it simple and safe. Most small sellers in Nigeria collect payment by bank transfer before delivery, and that works well when you are a known seller. Always confirm the payment before you dispatch the product, and send the customer a receipt or a confirmation message. Some sellers also use payment links from Nigerian payment providers so customers can pay with a card. Whichever method you use, explain it clearly, confirm every payment, and keep a record of each order: customer name, product, amount paid, delivery address and date. That record is your protection if there is ever a dispute.

Follow up after delivery. A short message like "Your order has been delivered. Please let us know when you receive it, and we would love your feedback" turns a one-time buyer into a repeat customer. Happy customers will recommend you to friends, and a catalogue full of clear products makes it easy for them to share your link with others.

## Common Mistakes That Kill Catalogue Sales

The first mistake is listing products without prices. Many sellers hide prices because they fear competitors or because they want customers to ask. In practice, hidden prices drive customers away. People assume the price is too high or that the seller is not serious. Always show your price. Customers who can afford it will message you. Those who cannot would not have bought anyway.

The second mistake is using old or wrong photos. If the photo shows a blue bag but you send a black one, the customer will feel cheated. Always use photos of the exact item you are selling, and update the catalogue when your stock changes. Hide or delete items that are out of stock instead of leaving them visible. A customer who tries to buy an item that is not available will lose confidence in you.

The third mistake is letting the catalogue go stale. A catalogue that has not been updated in three months tells customers the business may be closed or inactive. Add new products regularly, even if it is just two or three items a week. Remove old items, update prices when they change, and refresh your best seller photos from time to time.

The fourth mistake is a catalogue with too many similar items and no organisation. Fifty photos of almost identical gowns with no collections is overwhelming. Group similar items, keep your best photos, and remove duplicates. Quality matters more than quantity. Twenty well-presented products will sell better than a hundred messy ones.

## Start Building Your Catalogue Today

You can set up a basic WhatsApp catalogue in one afternoon. Start by installing WhatsApp Business and completing your profile. Then choose your ten best-selling products, take good photos of each one, and add them with clear names, prices and descriptions. Group them into two or three collections and share the catalogue link on your WhatsApp status and your social media pages.

After that, build the habit. Add new products the day they arrive. Update prices the day they change. Hide items that sell out and bring them back when you restock. Reply to catalogue messages quickly and confirm every order clearly. Over time, your catalogue becomes more than a list of products. It becomes your shop window, your price list and your sales assistant, working for you twenty-four hours a day, even while you sleep. In a country where customers shop on their phones at all hours, that is an advantage no serious seller in Nigeria should be without.
