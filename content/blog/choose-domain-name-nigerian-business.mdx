Your domain name is the address customers type to find you online. It appears on your business cards, your WhatsApp messages, your adverts, and your email address. A good domain name is easy to remember, easy to say, and easy to spell. A bad one confuses customers, gets mistyped, and quietly costs you enquiries for years.

Choosing one deserves more thought than a quick decision during website setup. This guide walks through the principles that work, the common mistakes to avoid, and the checks to run before you register.

## Start with your business name, not a clever idea

The simplest domain names are usually the best. Start with your business name, or a short version of it, before you try to be clever.

If your business is called "Lagos Fresh Bakes," then lagosfreshbakes is a natural starting point. If your business name is long, look for the shortest recognisable version. Customers should be able to hear the name once and guess the domain, or see the domain once and remember the business.

Avoid names that need explaining. If you have to spell your domain letter by letter every time you mention it, or explain a joke or a pun behind it, it is working against you. A domain name should be boring in the best sense: clear, obvious, and forgettable in the way a good address is forgettable, because it simply works.

## Keep it short, simple, and easy to spell

Aim for a domain that is short and easy to type on a phone. Long domains get mistyped, especially when someone is typing quickly or hearing the name spoken aloud.

Practical rules that help:

- Keep it to two or three words at most, and fewer if possible.
- Avoid hyphens if you can. A hyphen is easy to forget, and you will spend time explaining "with a dash between the words" every time.
- Avoid numbers unless they are part of your actual brand name. A number can be confused with a word, such as "4" versus "for," and customers will guess wrong.
- Avoid unusual spellings. If your business is "Kraft," do not register "craft" or "krafte" hoping customers will work it out. Use the spelling customers already know.
- Say it out loud. If it sounds awkward, or if a listener would need to ask how it is spelt, shorten it or simplify it.

Test your shortlist with real people. Tell a friend the name over the phone and ask them to type it. If they get it right without help, you are on the right track. If they hesitate, keep looking.

## Choose the right extension

The extension is the ending of your domain, such as .com, .ng, or .com.ng. It matters less than the name itself, but it still sends a signal.

A .com address is the most familiar worldwide, and many customers will type .com by habit even if you tell them otherwise. If your business serves customers beyond Nigeria, or you plan to grow internationally, .com is a strong default.

A .ng or .com.ng address signals clearly that you are a Nigerian business, and it can be a good choice if your customers are mainly in Nigeria. It also opens up names that are already taken as .com, because the .ng version may still be free. The trade-off is that some customers outside Nigeria may be less familiar with it, so make sure your marketing always shows the full address.

Other extensions, such as .org, .net, or newer options, are best avoided for a regular business unless you have a specific reason. A .org address suggests a non-profit organisation, and an unusual extension can look less trustworthy to customers who do not recognise it.

## Check availability, and check for clashes

Before you fall in love with a name, check three things:

1. Is the domain available to register? Use a reputable domain registrar to search. If your first choice is taken, do not immediately add hyphens or strange spellings to force it. Try a shorter version, a different extension, or a slightly different name.
2. Does another business already use a very similar name? Search for the name on Google and on social media. If another business in your industry uses almost the same name, customers will confuse the two, and you may face disputes later.
3. Does the name mean something unintended? Read it as one word, and check how it looks without spaces. A combination of two words can accidentally form an embarrassing or confusing phrase when joined together. Reading it aloud helps here too.

If your preferred name is taken by someone who is not actively using it, be cautious about paying a large amount to buy it. For most small businesses, a slightly different, available name is a better investment than an expensive purchase. Your marketing and your service will build the brand, not the domain alone.

## Register it in your own name

This is the most important practical point in this guide, and the one most often overlooked.

Your domain name should be registered in your name or your business name, with an email address you control. If a developer, a friend, or an agency registers it for you, make sure the registration is transferred to you, or at minimum that you have the login details and the account is in your name.

Why does this matter? Because the domain is your online address. If someone else controls it and you fall out with them, or they disappear, you can lose your website address, your business email, and years of customer recognition in one move. Recovering a domain registered to someone else is slow, stressful, and sometimes impossible.

When you register, also note the renewal date. Domains are rented, not owned forever. Set a reminder before the renewal date, keep your contact email up to date with the registrar, and renew on time. Letting a domain expire, even briefly, can take your website and email offline, and someone else may register the name before you notice.

## A simple shortlist process

Use this process to choose with confidence:

1. Write down your business name and two or three short versions of it.
2. Add a keyword only if it helps, such as your city or your service, and only if it keeps the name short.
3. Check each option for availability, similar businesses, and awkward meanings.
4. Say each option out loud and ask a friend to type it from hearing it once.
5. Pick the shortest, clearest option that passes all the checks, and register it in your own name.

Do not spend months searching for a perfect name. A clear, available name that you register today is far better than a perfect name you never settle on.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I choose .com or .ng?

Choose .com if you want the most familiar address, especially if you serve customers outside Nigeria. Choose .ng or .com.ng if your customers are mainly in Nigeria and you want to signal that clearly. Either can work well, as long as you use the full address consistently in your marketing.

### What if my business name is already taken as a domain?

Try a shorter version of the name, a different extension, or add a clear keyword such as your city or service, as long as the result stays short and easy to spell. Avoid hyphens, numbers, and unusual spellings just to force the original name.

### How long should a domain name be?

There is no strict rule, but shorter is better. Aim for a name that is easy to say, easy to spell, and easy to type on a phone. If it takes more than a few seconds to explain, it is probably too long or too complicated.

### Do I own my domain name forever once I register it?

No. A domain is registered for a set period, usually one year or more, and must be renewed before it expires. Keep your contact details up to date with your registrar and set a reminder for the renewal date.

## The bottom line

A good domain name is short, clear, easy to spell, and registered in your own name. It will not make your business successful on its own, but a bad one will quietly make everything harder, from adverts to word of mouth.

Take an hour, run through the checks in this guide, and choose a name you can live with for years. Then build a website worthy of it.
