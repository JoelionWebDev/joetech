Read the "About Us" page of five businesses in your industry. Chances are they all sound the same.

"We are committed to delivering exceptional solutions." "Our team is passionate about providing world-class service." "Customer satisfaction is our top priority."

These sentences are not wrong — they are just generic. They could appear on any website in any industry. And that is the problem.

When you sound like everyone else, you give people no reason to choose you.

## What Brand Voice Is (and Is Not)

Brand voice is not your logo, your colour palette, or your font choice. It is the personality and emotion infused into your words. It is how you sound when you write an email, a website headline, a social media post, or a service description.

A strong brand voice makes people feel like they are talking to a person, not reading a brochure.

## The Four Dimensions of Brand Voice

To define your voice, decide where you fall on each of these spectrums:

### 1. Formal vs. Casual

Does your industry require formality? A law firm might lean formal. A creative agency can be casual. Most businesses sit somewhere in the middle — professional but approachable.

### 2. Serious vs. Playful

Are you a trusted authority solving serious problems? Or are you the fun, energetic option? A cybersecurity company needs seriousness. A children's party planner can be playful.

### 3. Respectful vs. Provocative

Do you challenge your audience or reassure them? Some brands thrive on provocative takes that stir debate. Others succeed by being safe and supportive.

### 4. Detailed vs. Concise

Some audiences want every detail. Others want the short version. A B2B software company needs thorough explanations. A fast-food chain needs quick, punchy copy.

## How to Define Your Brand Voice in Three Steps

### Step 1: Describe Your Brand as a Person

If your business was a person, how would they talk? What words would they use? What would they never say?

Write down three adjectives that describe your brand's personality. For us at Joetech, it is: Clear. Helpful. Direct.

Every piece of copy we write gets filtered through those three adjectives. Does this sound clear? Is it actually helpful? Is it direct, or are we being vague?

### Step 2: Write Down What You Will Never Say

Defining your voice is as much about boundaries as it is about expression. List the words, phrases, or tones you will avoid.

Maybe you avoid jargon. Maybe you never use exclamation marks. Maybe you refuse to say "synergy" or "leverage" or "circle back." These boundaries keep your voice consistent.

### Step 3: Create a Voice Reference Document

Write a one-page guide your team can use. Include your three personality adjectives, your "do say" and "don't say" lists, and a few before-and-after examples.

Here is an example:

- **Before:** "We provide comprehensive digital solutions to optimise your business processes."
- **After:** "We build websites and marketing that actually bring in clients."

The second version is clearer, more specific, and sounds like a person wrote it.

## Consistency Is What Makes It Stick

Defining your voice is step one. Using it consistently is what builds recognition. Every email, every blog post, every social media caption, every page on your site should sound like the same person wrote it.

That does not mean every piece of content reads identically — a social post can be shorter and punchier than a service page — but the underlying personality should be recognisable across all of them.

## Your Brand Voice Check

Look at your website's homepage and your last three social media posts. Ask:

- Do they sound like the same business?
- Would I say these words out loud to a customer?
- Would anyone notice if you replaced your brand name with a competitor's?

If the answer to any of those is uncomfortable, you have a voice problem worth fixing.
