Every business that sells to real customers will face refunds and returns. A dress does not fit, a phone accessory stops working after two days, a food order arrives late, or a customer simply changes their mind. It does not matter how good your products are — sooner or later, someone will ask for their money back. How you handle that moment decides whether you lose one sale or lose a customer for good, and whether that customer tells their friends a good story about you or a bad one.

Many Nigerian small business owners dread refund requests. The money has already been spent, the profit is already counted, and giving it back feels like a loss. Some sellers argue, some ignore the customer's messages, and some refund angrily and block the customer. All three responses cost more than the refund itself, because an unhappy customer in Lagos tells their family group chat, their colleagues, and their followers on WhatsApp status. A calm, fair refund, on the other hand, often creates a customer who trusts you more than before. This guide shows you how to build a clear returns policy, respond to refund requests without panic, and protect your profit at the same time.

## Write Your Refund and Return Policy Before You Need It

The single most important step in handling refunds is writing your policy before a customer asks for one. A policy written in the middle of a dispute will always sound defensive, because you are writing it to win an argument. A policy written calmly, when nothing is wrong, is fair to both sides and easy to follow. Write it down in plain language, in a few short paragraphs, and keep it where customers can see it — in your WhatsApp catalogue, in an Instagram highlight, and on your website.

A good policy answers four questions. First, how many days does a customer have to return a product? Many Nigerian sellers allow three to seven days for most products, which gives the customer time to check the item without leaving the window open forever. Second, what condition must the product be in? Usually it must be unused, with tags attached, in its original packaging, and with the receipt or proof of purchase. Third, what will the customer get — a refund, a replacement, or store credit? And fourth, who pays for the return delivery? Answer all four questions clearly, and most disputes never start.

Be honest about what you cannot do. If you sell custom-made items, such as a tailored outfit, a personalised gift, or a cake made to order, say plainly that custom orders cannot be returned unless the item is faulty or wrong, because the product was made for one person and cannot be resold. If you sell food, say that perishable goods cannot be returned once delivered, but that you will replace a wrong or spoiled order. Customers accept clear limits when they know them before they buy. They only argue when a limit appears after they have already paid.

## Stay Calm and Listen First When a Customer Complains

When a refund request arrives, the first few minutes matter more than the policy. The customer is often frustrated, and their first message may be angry. Your job in that moment is not to defend yourself. It is to listen, acknowledge the problem, and show the customer that you take it seriously. A reply such as, "I am sorry this happened, please tell me what is wrong so I can help you," costs nothing and changes the whole conversation. An argument, on the other hand, makes a small problem much bigger.

Ask for the facts before you decide anything. What exactly is wrong with the product? When did the customer receive it? Can they send a photo or a short video of the fault? Photos protect both sides: they show you whether the product is genuinely faulty, and they give you evidence if you need to claim from your supplier. Stay polite even if the customer is not. Do not accuse the customer of damaging the product, and do not demand that they prove their honesty. Most refund requests are genuine, and treating an honest customer like a fraudster is the fastest way to lose them.

Once you understand the problem, say clearly what will happen next and when. For example, "Please send the item back by dispatch, and once I confirm it has arrived I will send your refund within 24 hours." A customer who knows the next step and the timing will wait patiently. A customer who gets no answer, or a vague answer such as "let me check", will message you every hour, post about it on their status, and tell their friends. Speed and clarity are the cheapest forms of customer service you can offer.

## Decide Fairly: Refund, Replace, or Give Store Credit

Not every problem needs a full refund, and the right answer depends on what went wrong. If the product is faulty, damaged, or not what the customer ordered, the fair response is a replacement or a full refund, and you should cover the cost of returning the item, because the mistake was yours. If the product is fine but the customer ordered the wrong size or colour, a replacement in the right size is usually fair, and it is reasonable for the customer to pay the delivery cost of the exchange. If the customer simply changed their mind, store credit or an exchange is a fair middle ground that keeps the money in your business while still treating the customer well.

Store credit works best when the customer likes your products and will shop with you again. Offer it warmly, not as a way of avoiding a refund. Say, "I can give you store credit for the full amount, which you can use any time, or a refund if you prefer." Giving the customer the choice shows respect, and many customers will choose the credit themselves, especially if they already have their eye on another item. But if the product was faulty, do not push store credit on a customer who wants their money back — that will feel like a trick, and it will damage trust.

Keep a simple record of every refund, replacement and exchange: the date, the customer, the product, the reason, and what you gave them. Over a few months, this record shows you patterns. If the same product keeps coming back faulty, the problem is your supplier or your quality check, and you need to fix it at the source. If most returns are wrong sizes, you need better size guides or clearer photos in your listings. Refunds are not just a cost — they are free feedback about what is wrong in your business.

## Protect Yourself From Fraud Without Punishing Honest Customers

A small number of refund requests are not honest. A customer may wear a dress to an event and return it, claim a product is faulty when it is not, or send back a different, damaged item. This happens, and you should protect yourself — but you should do it with systems, not with suspicion towards every customer. The best protection is inspection: check every returned product carefully before you send a refund, and confirm it is the same item, in the expected condition, with all parts included. If you sell electronics or accessories, test them on arrival before you refund.

Photograph your products before dispatch, especially high-value items. A quick photo of the item, its packaging, and the delivery label takes seconds and settles most arguments about what was sent. Keep the customer's order details — the size, the colour, the quantity — in your records, so you can check a return against what was actually ordered. For higher-value orders, use a delivery method with proof of delivery, and ask the rider to confirm the handover. These small steps cost very little and protect you when a dispute arises.

When you suspect a return is not genuine, stay calm and stick to your policy. Say, "I have checked the item, and it does not match the condition described in our returns policy, so I cannot refund it, but I can offer you a repair or a discount on your next order." You do not need to accuse the customer of dishonesty, and you do not need to argue. A polite, policy-based refusal is professional and final. And remember that refusing one dishonest refund is good business, but treating every customer as a suspect is bad business — the honest majority will feel it and shop elsewhere.

## Use Clear Photos, Descriptions and Size Guides to Prevent Returns

The cheapest refund is the one that never happens. Most returns in Nigerian small businesses are not caused by faulty products. They are caused by wrong expectations: the colour looked different on the phone screen, the size was smaller than the customer imagined, or the product was not what the description suggested. You can prevent many of these returns before the sale by showing your products clearly and honestly.

Take good photos in natural light, from several angles, and show the product being used or worn where possible. A dress photographed on a hanger tells the customer far less than the same dress worn by a model or shown next to a common object for scale. Write descriptions that include measurements, not just sizes — a "large" means different things to different sellers, but a chest measurement in inches is exact. For shoes, state the sizes you have and whether they run small or large. For food and cosmetics, list the ingredients or contents plainly.

Answer questions honestly before the sale, even if the honest answer costs you that sale. If a customer asks whether a fabric is thick, and it is not, say so. If a customer asks whether a phone accessory works with their model, and you are not sure, check before you answer. A customer who buys with correct information rarely returns a product. A customer who was misled will return it and will not come back. Honest listings are not just good service — they are a practical way to cut your refund rate.

## Handle Refunds on WhatsApp, Instagram and in Your Shop

Most Nigerian small businesses sell across several channels, and refund requests can arrive anywhere — a WhatsApp message, an Instagram DM, a comment on a post, or a customer standing in your shop. The policy should be the same on every channel, but the way you handle it needs care. On WhatsApp and Instagram, reply promptly and move the conversation to a private chat if it starts in a public comment. Never argue with a customer in public, because every other person reading the exchange is judging your business, not the customer.

In your shop, handle a return with the same calm you would use online. Invite the customer to show you the problem, check the item, and offer the solution from your policy. A customer who is treated well in person often becomes a regular, because they have seen with their own eyes that you stand behind your products. Do not make a scene, do not raise your voice, and do not make the customer feel small for asking. Other customers in the shop are watching, and they will remember how you treated someone with a problem.

Keep the same records across all channels, and train anyone who works with you to follow the same policy. If you have a staff member who handles your WhatsApp, show them the policy, give them examples of good replies, and agree what they can decide on their own and what they should bring to you. A staff member who argues with a customer or promises a refund you cannot give will create problems that are hard to undo. Consistency across channels and across staff is what turns a policy from words on a page into trust.

## Turn a Refund Into a Customer Who Comes Back

A refund feels like the end of a sale, but it can be the start of a loyal relationship. Customers remember how a business treats them when something goes wrong far more than they remember a smooth purchase. A seller who refunds quickly, apologises sincerely, and follows up afterwards earns a level of trust that no advertisement can buy. That customer will return, and they will tell others that your business is safe to buy from.

After a refund or replacement, follow up once. A short message such as, "I hope the replacement is working well for you. Please let me know if there is anything else I can help with," shows the customer that you care about the outcome, not just about closing the complaint. Do not overdo it — one follow-up is enough — and do not use it to push a new sale. The point is to leave the customer feeling respected.

You can also learn from every refund. Once a month, look at your refund record and ask what it is telling you. Are customers returning a particular product? Is a supplier sending you faulty stock? Are your photos misleading? Fix the cause, and your refund rate will fall. And when you fix something because of a customer's complaint, tell your customers — a post saying, "We have improved our packaging after your feedback," shows that you listen. A business that handles refunds well is a business that customers trust, and trust is what keeps a small business growing in a crowded market.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How many days should I allow for returns?**

Three to seven days is a common window for most products in Nigeria. It gives the customer time to check the item without leaving the window open so long that used products come back. State the number of days clearly in your policy, and apply it consistently.

**Must I always give a full refund when a customer asks?**

No. If the product is faulty or wrong, a refund or replacement is fair. If the customer ordered the wrong size or changed their mind, an exchange or store credit is a fair alternative. The key is to apply your written policy fairly, not to decide differently for each customer.

**Who should pay for return delivery?**

If the mistake was yours — a faulty product or a wrong item — you should pay. If the customer changed their mind or ordered the wrong size, it is fair for them to pay. Write this rule in your policy so it is clear before anyone buys.

**What should I do if a customer threatens to post about me online?**

Stay calm, follow your policy, and do not let the threat change your decision. If your policy is fair and you have followed it, a public post will show other people that you handled the matter professionally. Responding angrily or giving in to threats both damage your business more than the post itself.

**How can I reduce the number of refunds I get?**

Use clear photos, honest descriptions, exact measurements and size guides, and answer questions truthfully before the sale. Check products before dispatch, and package them well so they arrive in good condition. Most returns come from wrong expectations or damage in transit, and both are preventable.
