A customer comes to your WhatsApp to buy one item for ₦12,000. You reply, you take payment, you arrange delivery, and the sale is done. Now imagine the same customer spends ₦22,000 instead, because you offered them a sensible combination of products they actually needed together. You did not spend more on adverts. You did not find a new customer. You simply sold more to the customer you already had.

That is the power of bundling. Bundling means selling two or more products together as one package at a single price, usually lower than buying each item separately. It is one of the simplest ways for a Nigerian small business to increase sales, clear slow-moving stock and make life easier for customers who do not want to choose between five separate items.

You see bundling everywhere, even if you do not notice it. A skincare seller offers a face wash, toner and moisturiser as a complete glow set. A food vendor sells a party pack of rice, chicken and drinks for a family celebration. A phone accessories seller offers a phone case, screen guard and charger as a starter kit. A fashion store sells a dress with a matching bag and scarf. In each case, the customer gets a complete solution, and the seller gets a bigger order.

This guide explains how to bundle products for your own business in Nigeria: what to combine, how to price a bundle so you still make profit, how to present it clearly, and how to promote it on WhatsApp, Instagram and your website without confusing your customers.

## What Bundling Is, and Why It Works in Nigeria

Bundling works because it solves three problems at once. First, it helps customers decide faster. Many customers in Nigeria browse for a long time because they are unsure which product goes with which. A ready-made bundle removes that doubt. Instead of asking "Which cream should I use after this soap?", the customer sees a complete set and buys it.

Second, it raises the average amount each customer spends. If your average order is ₦15,000, and you can move it to ₦25,000 with a bundle, your revenue grows even if the number of customers stays the same. For a small business that cannot afford to spend heavily on adverts every week, that matters a lot.

Third, it helps you move products that sell slowly on their own. A popular product can carry a slower one. If your best-selling body lotion always sells out, but your body scrub sits on the shelf, put them together. Customers who came for the lotion will try the scrub, and some of them will come back to buy the scrub on its own next time.

## The Main Types of Bundles

You do not need a complicated system. Most Nigerian small businesses use one of four simple bundle types.

**1. The starter kit.** This is a bundle for a new customer who needs everything to begin. A hair vendor might offer a starter kit with a wig, a wig cap, a brush and a small bottle of hair serum. A makeup seller might offer a beginner kit with foundation, powder, lipstick and a brush set. A baby shop might offer a newborn starter pack with clothes, diapers, wipes and a blanket. Starter kits work well because the customer does not need to know what to buy — you have already chosen for them.

**2. The complete set.** This is for customers who already use one product and need the rest of the routine. A skincare brand can sell a complete set: cleanser, toner, serum and moisturiser. A kitchenware seller can sell a complete cooking set: pots, pans and utensils. The logic is simple: the products are meant to be used together, so selling them together makes sense.

**3. Buy more, save more.** This is a bundle of the same product in a larger quantity. For example, buy two bottles of groundnut oil for ₦18,000 instead of ₦10,000 each, or buy three packs of hair extensions and get a discount on the total. This works well for food items, consumables and products people use regularly and will need again soon.

**4. The gift or occasion bundle.** This is a bundle built around an event. A Christmas hamper, a Valentine's gift box, a wedding gift set, a birthday package, or a "back to school" bundle for parents. Occasion bundles sell well in Nigeria because gifting is a big part of our culture, and a ready-made gift saves the buyer time and stress.

## Step 1: Look at What You Sell and What Customers Buy Together

Good bundles start with your own sales records, not with guesswork. Before you create a bundle, look at what your customers already buy together.

Open your WhatsApp chats, your order book or your website orders from the last two or three months. Look for patterns. Do customers who buy a dress often ask for a bag too? Do customers who buy a wig often ask for a wig cap or serum in the same message? Do customers who buy rice often add plantain or chicken? Those patterns are your best bundle ideas.

Make a short list of your best sellers. Write down your top five products by sales. Then, for each one, write down one or two products that naturally go with it. For example:

- Best seller: body lotion. Goes with: body wash, body scrub.
- Best seller: Ankara dress. Goes with: head tie, matching bag.
- Best seller: phone. Goes with: phone case, screen guard, charger.
- Best seller: rice (5kg). Goes with: vegetable oil, seasoning cubes, tomatoes.

Do not force a bundle. If two products do not make sense together, customers will notice. A customer will not buy a shoe and a cooking pot together just because you put them in the same package. The products in a bundle should feel like they belong together, and the customer should be able to explain in one sentence why they are together.

## Step 2: Design Bundles Your Customers Actually Want

Once you have your list, design three to five bundles to start with. Fewer, clearer bundles sell better than ten confusing ones.

Give each bundle a clear name. A name helps the customer understand what it is and who it is for. "Glow Starter Kit" is clearer than "Bundle A". "Family Weekend Pack" is clearer than "Pack 2". "Complete Bridal Set" tells a bride exactly what she is getting. Use simple names in plain English, or in a mix of English and Pidgin if that fits your brand voice, but keep the name short enough to fit on a flyer.

Decide exactly what is in each bundle. List every item, including sizes, colours and quantities. For example, a skincare bundle might be: 1 face wash (200ml), 1 toner (150ml), 1 moisturiser (100ml). A food bundle might be: 5kg rice, 2 litres groundnut oil, 1 pack seasoning cubes, 1kg chicken. Be specific. Vague bundles create questions, and questions slow down sales.

Keep the number of items in a bundle small. Two to four items is ideal for most small businesses. A bundle with eight items looks impressive, but it is hard to price, hard to photograph, and hard for the customer to understand. If you have many related products, create two bundles instead of one huge one — for example, a basic set and a premium set.

## Step 3: Price Your Bundle So You Still Make Profit

Pricing is where bundling succeeds or fails. A bundle must be cheaper than buying the items separately, or there is no reason for the customer to buy it. At the same time, it must still leave you a profit after all your costs.

Here is a simple method you can use for every bundle.

First, add up the normal prices of the items in the bundle. For example, if a face wash is ₦8,000, a toner is ₦7,000 and a moisturiser is ₦10,000, the total is ₦25,000.

Second, decide on a bundle discount. Most successful bundles in Nigeria offer 10 to 20 percent off the total. A 10 percent discount is safe and still attractive. A 20 percent discount is strong and works well for a launch or a festive period. Going above 25 percent is risky unless you are clearing stock or the products cost you very little to buy.

Third, work out the bundle price. At 15 percent off ₦25,000, the bundle price is ₦21,250. Round it to a clean number, such as ₦21,000 or ₦21,500, so it is easy to pay by transfer.

Fourth, check your profit. Add up your true cost for the three items — what you paid for them, plus packaging, plus delivery if you offer free delivery, plus payment charges. If your true cost is ₦16,000 and you sell the bundle for ₦21,000, you make ₦5,000. That is good. If your true cost is ₦20,000, your profit is only ₦1,000, and the bundle is not worth it unless it brings you many new customers. If the bundle price is below your true cost, you are losing money on every sale. Raise the price or change what is in the bundle.

Show the savings clearly. Customers need to see the value. On your flyer or caption, write: "Face wash ₦8,000 + Toner ₦7,000 + Moisturiser ₦10,000 = ₦25,000. Bundle price: ₦21,000. You save ₦4,000." When the saving is clear, the decision is easy.

Avoid the temptation to raise individual prices just to make the bundle look cheaper. Customers remember your normal prices, and they will notice. Keep your individual prices honest, and let the bundle discount be real.

## Step 4: Make the Bundle Easy to See and Easy to Buy

A bundle that is hard to understand will not sell, no matter how good the price is. Your job is to make it obvious what is inside, what it costs, and how to order it.

Take a photo of the bundle together, not just separate photos of each item. Lay the items out neatly on a clean surface, in good light, so the customer can see everything at once. A flat-lay photo works well for skincare, food and fashion accessories. A short video of you holding the items and naming each one also works well, especially on WhatsApp Status and Instagram Reels.

Write a short description for each bundle. Use bullet points, not long paragraphs. For example:

**Glow Starter Kit — ₦21,000 (save ₦4,000)**
- Face wash (200ml)
- Toner (150ml)
- Moisturiser (100ml)
- Free delivery within Lagos
- To order, send "GLOW" to our WhatsApp

That is all a customer needs. They can read it in ten seconds and decide.

Create a simple code or name for each bundle so customers can order quickly. "Send GLOW to order" is faster than "Send a message saying you want the face wash, toner and moisturiser bundle." On WhatsApp, speed matters. Prepare a saved reply with the bundle details, the total price including delivery, and your account details, so you can respond in seconds.

If you have a website, create a separate product page for each bundle, with its own photo, price and "Add to Cart" button. Customers should not have to add three separate products to their cart and work out the discount themselves. If you use Paystack or Flutterwave payment links, create one link per bundle so the customer pays one amount in one step.

Update your WhatsApp Business catalogue. Add each bundle as a product with its photo, price and description. Many customers browse the catalogue first before they message you. If the bundle is not in the catalogue, they may never know it exists.

## Step 5: Promote Your Bundles Where Your Customers Already Are

You do not need a big advert budget to sell bundles. Start with the customers you already have.

Send a WhatsApp broadcast to your existing customers. Keep it short and send it once, at a time when people are likely to be on their phones, such as early evening. Include a photo of the bundle, the price, the saving, and how to order. Do not send the same broadcast every day — people will mute you. One clear message, then a reminder a few days later, is enough.

Post on Instagram and Facebook. Use a clear photo or a short video, and write a caption that names the bundle, the price and the saving. Use a call to action: "Send us a DM with the word GLOW to order." Post at a time when your followers are active, and reply to comments quickly. If someone comments "Price?", do not just say "DM". Reply with the price in the comments if you are comfortable doing so, or reply quickly in the DM. A slow reply loses the sale.

Use your WhatsApp Status every day. Status is free, and many Nigerian customers check it daily. Post one bundle per day, with a photo and the price. Add a simple line like "Only 10 sets available this week." Scarcity works when it is honest — if you truly have limited stock, say so.

## Step 6: Test, Listen and Improve Your Bundles

Your first bundles will not be perfect, and that is normal. The best approach is to launch, listen to customers, and improve.

After two weeks, review your bundles. Which one sold the most? Which one got a lot of questions but few orders? Which one did customers ask to change — for example, "Can I swap the toner for a serum?" Those questions are valuable feedback. If many customers want the same swap, consider offering it as an option, or create a second version of the bundle.

Ask customers who bought a bundle a simple question after delivery: "Did you like the set? Is there anything you would change?" Most customers will answer honestly, and their answers will help you improve the next bundle.

Watch your numbers. For each bundle, track how many you sold, how much revenue it brought in, and how much profit you made. A bundle that sells 50 units but makes almost no profit is less useful than a bundle that sells 20 units with good profit. Keep the profitable bundles, improve or drop the ones that do not work.

## Common Bundling Mistakes to Avoid

Many small businesses make the same mistakes with bundling. Avoid these and your bundles will work much better.

Do not bundle products that do not belong together. A random combination confuses customers and makes them doubt the value. Every item in the bundle should have a clear reason to be there.

Do not make the bundle too expensive. If your average customer spends ₦15,000, a bundle priced at ₦80,000 will be out of reach for most of them. Create bundles at different price points — for example, one at ₦15,000, one at ₦25,000 and one at ₦40,000 — so different customers can find one that fits their budget.

Do not hide the individual prices. Customers want to see what they are saving. If you only show the bundle price, they cannot tell whether it is a good deal.

Do not offer too many bundles at once. Three to five clear bundles are enough to start. Ten bundles on one flyer will overwhelm customers and they will buy nothing.

## Bundle Checklist

- [ ] Look at your sales records and find products customers already buy together
- [ ] Choose three to five bundles to start, with two to four items each
- [ ] Give each bundle a clear name and list exactly what is inside
- [ ] Add up the normal prices, apply a 10 to 20 percent discount, and check your profit
- [ ] Show the saving clearly: total value, bundle price, amount saved
- [ ] Photograph the bundle together and write a short bullet-point description
- [ ] Add each bundle to your WhatsApp catalogue and your website
- [ ] Promote bundles to existing customers first, then on Instagram and WhatsApp Status
- [ ] Track sales and profit for each bundle for two weeks, then improve or drop what does not work

Bundling is not a trick. It is a way to make buying easier for your customers and more profitable for your business. Start with what your customers already buy together, price it honestly, present it clearly, and listen to what they tell you. Do that well, and a single customer who came to buy one item will happily leave with a complete set — and come back for the next one.
