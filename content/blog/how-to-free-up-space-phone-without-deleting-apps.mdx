"Storage almost full." Few messages are as annoying as that one. Your phone starts misbehaving — photos will not save, WhatsApp will not download videos, apps refuse to update, and the phone itself becomes slow. The usual advice is to delete apps, but that is rarely the real problem. Most of the time, your storage is eaten up by things you do not need: old photos and videos, app cache, downloaded files you forgot about, and thousands of WhatsApp media files.

The good news is that you can reclaim a surprising amount of space — often several gigabytes — without deleting a single app. This guide shows you how, on both Android and iPhone, with steps you can finish in one sitting.

## First, Find Out What Is Using Your Space

Before you delete anything, find out where your storage is actually going. Guessing wastes time; a quick check tells you exactly what to target.

On Android, go to Settings, then Storage. You will see a breakdown of how much space is used by apps, photos and videos, audio, and system files. Tap each category to see more detail. Many Android phones also have a built-in cleaner — Samsung, Tecno, Infinix and Xiaomi all include one in Settings or in their file manager app — which can suggest files to remove safely.

## Tame WhatsApp — the Biggest Hidden Storage Eater

For most Nigerians, WhatsApp is the single biggest user of phone storage. Every photo, video, voice note and document you receive is saved to your phone — including the videos forwarded in group chats that you never asked for. Over months, this can grow to several gigabytes.

Start by checking how much space WhatsApp is using. On Android, open WhatsApp, go to Settings, then Storage and data, then Manage storage. You will see how much space WhatsApp media takes up, which chats use the most, and a list of large files. On iPhone, go to Settings, then Storage and Data, then Manage Storage for the same view.

From that screen, you can review and delete large files, forwarded files, and media from chats you no longer need — without deleting the chats themselves. Delete the heavy videos first; a single forwarded video can be larger than a hundred photos.

Next, stop the problem from coming back. In the same Storage and data settings, look for "Media auto-download". Turn off auto-download for videos on mobile data, and consider turning it off for photos in large groups too. From now on, media will only download when you tap it — which means your phone only stores what you actually wanted.

Also, turn off the setting that saves every photo and video to your phone's gallery. On Android, this is "Media visibility" in the chat settings; on iPhone, it is "Save to Camera Roll" in Chats settings. Your media stays inside WhatsApp, and your gallery stops filling up with duplicates.

Finally, remember that deleting WhatsApp media from your phone does not delete your chats. But before you clear anything important, make sure your chats are backed up — read our guide on [how to back up your WhatsApp chats on Android and iPhone](/blog/how-to-back-up-whatsapp-chats-android-iphone) so you never lose a customer conversation while cleaning up.

## Clear Cache and Temporary Files Safely

Cache is temporary data that apps store to load faster — images from websites you visited, thumbnails, and small files. Over time it piles up, and clearing it is safe: it does not delete your photos, your chats, your logins or your app settings. It just makes the app rebuild its temporary files next time you open it.

On Android, go to Settings, then Apps, and tap an app to see how much cache it holds. Social media apps, browsers and shopping apps usually hold the most. Tap "Storage", then "Clear cache". Do this for your biggest apps — Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, Chrome and YouTube are usually worth clearing first. Never tap "Clear data" by mistake: that logs you out and deletes the app's saved information, which is not what you want.

Many Android phones have a one-tap option to clear all cached data at once. In Settings, then Storage, look for cached data, or use your phone's built-in cleaner app. This is the fastest way to reclaim space, and you can do it once a month as regular maintenance.

On iPhone, there is no single "clear all cache" button. Instead, you clear cache app by app. For Safari, go to Settings, then Safari, then "Clear History and Website Data". For other apps, check inside the app's own settings — many have a cache or storage option. Also clear your downloads folder: old PDFs, installation files and images you downloaded once and forgot about sit there for months.

## Move Photos and Videos to the Cloud

Photos and videos are usually the biggest category on any phone, and they are also the easiest to move. You do not need to delete your memories — you just need to store them somewhere other than your phone.

Google Photos is the most popular option on Android, and it works on iPhone too. Install Google Photos, sign in with your Google account, and turn on backup. Your photos and videos upload to the cloud over Wi-Fi, and once they are backed up, you can use the "Free up space" option in Google Photos to remove the copies from your phone. Your photos stay safe in the cloud and you can view or download them any time.

On iPhone, iCloud Photos does the same thing. Go to Settings, tap your name, then iCloud, then Photos, and turn on iCloud Photos with "Optimise iPhone Storage". Your phone keeps small versions of your photos and downloads the full version only when you open them, which can save several gigabytes.

If your cloud storage is full, you have choices. Google and Apple both offer paid storage plans at a modest monthly price, and for a business owner whose product photos matter, that is often money well spent. Alternatively, you can move old photos and videos to a laptop, an external drive or a USB stick, then delete them from the phone. Review your videos too: a single minute of video can take up more space than hundreds of photos, so old screen recordings and duplicate clips are prime candidates to move or delete.

## Offload Apps You Rarely Use — Without Losing Them

If you still need more space after clearing cache and media, look at the apps you rarely open. You do not have to delete them permanently. On iPhone, there is a feature built exactly for this: "Offload Unused Apps". Go to Settings, then General, then iPhone Storage, and turn it on. Your iPhone will automatically remove apps you have not used in a while, while keeping their documents and data. The app icon stays on your home screen, and tapping it downloads the app again with your data intact.

On Android, there is no identical feature on every phone, but you can do the same thing manually. Look at your app list sorted by size and by last use. Games you finished, apps you downloaded for one trip, shopping apps you never open — these can go. Uninstalling an app does not delete your account with that service, so you can always reinstall it later and sign back in. A simple rule works well: if you have not opened an app in three months, you probably do not need it taking up space today.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is it safe to clear cache on my phone?

Yes. Clearing cache only removes temporary files that apps use to load faster. It does not delete your photos, messages, contacts or app settings. Your apps may take a second longer to open the first time after clearing, because they rebuild the cache, but nothing important is lost.

### Why is WhatsApp taking up so much storage?

Because WhatsApp saves every photo, video and document you receive to your phone, including media forwarded in group chats. Over time this adds up to gigabytes. Use the Manage Storage screen in WhatsApp to delete large and forwarded files, and turn off auto-download so only the media you tap gets saved.

### Will moving photos to Google Photos or iCloud delete them?

No, as long as the backup has finished before you free up space. The photos remain in your cloud account and you can view or download them any time. Only use the "Free up space" option after the app confirms your photos are backed up.

### How much free space should I keep on my phone?

Aim to keep at least 10 to 15 percent of your total storage free. A phone that is almost completely full runs slowly, cannot install updates, and may stop saving photos or receiving messages. If your phone has 64GB of storage, try to keep at least 6 to 8GB free.

## Conclusion

A "storage full" warning does not mean it is time for a new phone, and it does not mean you have to delete the apps you use every day. In most cases, the space is hiding in plain sight — WhatsApp videos you never watched, cached files, old downloads, and photos that could live safely in the cloud. Work through the steps in this guide once, set your auto-download and backup settings properly, and repeat a quick clean-up once a month. Your phone will run faster, your apps will update without drama, and you will stop losing photos and messages to a full memory.
