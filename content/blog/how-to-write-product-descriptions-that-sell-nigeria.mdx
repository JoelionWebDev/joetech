A customer in Ikeja is scrolling through Instagram at 9pm. She stops on your dress. The photo is good. She taps to read the caption. It says: "Beautiful dress available. DM for price." She keeps scrolling. Ten minutes later she buys a similar dress from another vendor whose caption told her the fabric, the sizes, the price, the delivery fee to Ikeja, and how to order in one tap.

That is the whole game. In Nigeria, your product photo earns the attention, but your description earns the money. Most small sellers write descriptions as an afterthought — a few adjectives, "DM for price", and a prayer. The sellers who sell consistently treat the description as their salesperson: it answers questions, removes doubt, and tells the buyer exactly what to do next.

This guide shows you how to write product descriptions that sell in the Nigerian market, whether you sell on Instagram, WhatsApp, Jumia, Konga, your own website, or a market stall with a phone. No copywriting degree needed. Just a clear structure, honest details, and an understanding of how Nigerian buyers actually decide.

## Why Product Descriptions Matter More in Nigeria

In a physical market in Balogun or Onitsha, a buyer touches the fabric, tries the shoe, and haggles face to face. Online, she cannot touch anything. Your description replaces her hands, her eyes, and the trader's voice. When it is vague, she fills the gaps with suspicion — and suspicion kills sales.

Nigerian buyers also have specific worries that a buyer in London does not. Will this be the same quality as the photo, or a cheap copy? Is the size true to what I wear? How much is delivery to Abuja, really? Will my money be safe if I pay first? A good description answers these worries before they are asked. A bad description leaves them unanswered, and the buyer moves to a seller who bothered.

There is also the "DM for price" problem. Many sellers hide prices hoping to force a conversation. In practice, hiding the price usually forces the buyer to leave. She is comparing five vendors at once on a phone with limited data and limited patience. If your price is hidden and the next seller's price is visible, she buys from the visible one. Transparency is not a weakness in this market — it is a competitive advantage.

Finally, descriptions do real work for search. On Google, Jumia, and your own website, the words in your description decide whether a buyer searching "ankara midi dress Lagos" or "men's leather sandals size 44" ever finds you. Write for the buyer first, but use the words buyers actually type, and search engines will reward you.

## Know Your Buyer Before You Write a Word

You cannot write a description that sells until you know who is reading it. Before writing, answer five questions about your typical buyer:

1. **Who is she?** Age, location, income level, and lifestyle. A description for a university student in Nsukka reads differently from one for a bank manager in Victoria Island.
2. **What problem does this product solve?** Nobody buys a blender; they buy smooth pap and quick smoothies before work. Nobody buys a wig; they buy a good hair day without a salon visit. Name the problem.
3. **What is she afraid of?** Wrong size, fake product, late delivery, paying and not receiving goods. Your description should calm each fear directly.
4. **How does she talk?** If your buyers say "original", "quality", "lasts long" and "not China copy", use their words. Mirror their language and they will feel understood.
5. **Where will she read this?** A WhatsApp status caption must be short. A website product page can be long. An Instagram caption sits in between. Write for the place.

Take ten minutes and write down the answers for your best-selling product. Those answers become the raw material for every description you write afterwards. A seller who knows her buyer writes descriptions that feel personal, and personal descriptions sell.

## The Anatomy of a Description That Sells

Every strong product description, whether it is two sentences or two hundred words, contains the same seven building blocks. Miss one and you leave a hole a buyer can fall through.

**1. A clear headline.** The first line should name the product and its most attractive feature. "Original Leather Sandals — Handmade in Aba, Size 40–46" beats "Nice sandals available." The buyer should know what it is and why it matters in the first five seconds.

**2. The benefit, not just the feature.** Features describe the product; benefits describe the buyer's life. "100% cotton" is a feature. "Soft, breathable cotton that keeps you cool in Lagos heat" is a benefit. "2000mAh battery" is a feature. "Lasts a full day of calls, WhatsApp and music without charging" is a benefit. Give the feature, then translate it into the benefit in the same sentence.

**3. The honest details.** Material, size, colour options, what's included in the box, weight, capacity, and measurements in centimetres. Nigerian buyers have been burned by "one size fits all" claims. Give real numbers: chest 100cm, length 120cm, fits sizes 10–14. Honest details reduce returns and build trust.

**4. The price and how to pay.** State the price plainly. Add what payment methods you accept — bank transfer, POS, pay on delivery where available, Paystack or Flutterwave links. If the price is negotiable, say so. If it is fixed, say "price is fixed" so you do not spend your day haggling with people who never intended to buy.

**5. Delivery information.** This is where most Nigerian descriptions fail. State where you deliver, how long it takes, and what it costs. "Delivery within Lagos: ₦1,500–₦2,500, 24–48 hours. Nationwide: ₦3,000–₦5,000, 2–4 days. Pickup available in Lekki." That one paragraph answers the three most common DMs you receive every day.

**6. Trust signals.** Why should a stranger send you money? Mention your location, how long you have sold this product, your return or exchange policy, and real customer feedback. "Over 300 sold" or "see our highlights for customer reviews" works, as long as it is true. Never invent numbers.

**7. A clear call to action.** Tell the buyer the next step in plain language: "Tap the WhatsApp button to order", "Send 'ORDER + your size' to 0803...", "Click Add to Cart". A buyer who does not know what to do next does nothing.

## Step 1: Start With the Benefit in the First Two Lines

On Instagram and WhatsApp, only the first two lines of your caption show before the buyer must tap "more". Those two lines decide whether she reads the rest. Put your strongest benefit there — not "Available in all sizes" and not "Good morning my lovelies".

Good openers name the buyer and the result:

- "The dress that gets you compliments before you sit down — soft Ankara, true to size."
- "No more NEPA worries: this rechargeable fan runs 8 hours on one charge."
- "Your skin, but better: a foundation that survives Lagos humidity and a full workday."

Then expand. Describe the product in one short paragraph of two to four sentences. Then add the details as bullets, because buyers scan. Walls of text get skipped; bullets get read.

A simple template for the opening paragraph: who it is for + the main benefit + the one feature that proves it. For example: "Made for busy mums in Lagos, this food flask keeps jollof hot from 7am to 2pm, thanks to its double-wall stainless steel interior. No leaks in the school bag, no cold lunch, no complaints."

## Step 2: Write Features as Benefits, One by One

Take every feature of your product and ask "so what?" until you reach the buyer's life. Do this for each feature and your description writes itself.

- **Feature:** 100% cotton. **So what?** It breathes. **Benefit:** "Stays comfortable through a full day in Lagos heat — no sweating, no sticking."
- **Feature:** Adjustable strap. **So what?** It fits many bodies. **Benefit:** "Fits sizes 8 to 16 comfortably, so you can order without guessing."
- **Feature:** Stainless steel. **So what?** It does not rust or break. **Benefit:** "Built to last years, not months — even with daily use and washing."
- **Feature:** Comes with a free case. **So what?** Saves money. **Benefit:** "Free protective case included, so you save ₦5,000 and your phone is safe from day one."

Write three to five of these benefit lines as bullets. Use simple words. Avoid jargon like "ergonomic synergy" — nobody in Surulere searches for that. Use the words your customers use when they ask about the product in your DMs. Those words are your best copy, because they came from buyers.

## Step 3: Be Honest About Size, Colour and Quality

Returns and angry customers in Nigeria usually come from one cause: the product did not match the description. A dress described as "free size" that only fits a size 8, a "red" bag that arrives orange, a "leather" shoe that peels in three weeks — each one costs you a refund, a bad review, and a customer who will never return.

Measure your products and write the measurements down. Chest, waist, length, foot length in centimetres. If you sell shoes, include a size chart: "Size 42 = 27cm foot length." If you sell clothes, state the model's size and height: "Model wears size 12, height 5'7"." That single line prevents dozens of wrong orders.

Photograph the real product in natural light and describe the colour in plain words: "deep navy blue, not black" or "burnt orange, slightly darker than the photo in bright sunlight". If the colour varies slightly between batches, say so. Buyers forgive a small difference they were warned about; they do not forgive a surprise.

On quality, say what it is and what it is not. "Genuine leather" means genuine leather. "High-quality synthetic leather, looks and feels like the real thing" is honest and still sells. "Original" should only be used when you can prove it. Nigerian buyers have sharp eyes for fakes, and one exposed claim destroys trust faster than ten good sales build it.

## Step 4: Price It Clearly and Remove Payment Fear

Your price is not a secret, and hiding it does not make the product more valuable. State it, and state it with confidence. If the price is ₦25,000, write "₦25,000". Do not write "25k" on your website, where a new visitor may not know your shorthand, and do not write "DM for price" anywhere if you want to sell at scale.

If your price is higher than competitors, your description must justify it. Explain what the buyer gets for the extra money: better material, a warranty, free delivery, after-sales support, a longer lifespan. "Costs more, lasts three times longer" is a powerful sentence when it is true.

Payment fear is real in Nigeria because scams are real. Reduce it in your description:

- Offer pay on delivery where you can, even if only within Lagos.
- Mention your physical location or pickup point: "Pickup available at our Lekki Phase 1 store."
- Show your business name, your real phone number, and your social media handle consistently everywhere.
- State your exchange or refund policy in one line: "Exchange within 3 days if unworn and with tags."

Each of these is a brick in the wall of trust. A buyer who trusts you will pay first; a buyer who does not will demand pay on delivery or walk away entirely.

## Step 5: Make Delivery and Ordering Dead Simple

Delivery is the most asked-about topic in every Nigerian seller's DM, and the most common reason a sale dies. The buyer likes the product, likes the price, and then asks "delivery to Enugu how much?" and never hears back for six hours because you were busy. By the time you reply, she has bought from someone else.

Put the delivery information in the description so the question is answered before it is asked. Include the delivery fee by zone, the delivery time, and the pickup option. If you use a dispatch rider within Lagos, say so and name the areas covered. If you send nationwide with a courier, name the courier and the delivery window.

Then make ordering a single step. On WhatsApp, that means a clickable link or a clear instruction: "To order, send your name, size, and delivery address to 0803 123 4567 on WhatsApp." On your website, it means an obvious order button and a checkout that takes under two minutes. Every extra step loses buyers. Count your steps, then remove one.

Use urgency honestly. "Only 3 left in size 12" is effective when it is true, and it is also useful information. "Sale ends Sunday" works when the sale genuinely ends on Sunday. Fake urgency — "only 2 left" on a product you have fifty of — is discovered quickly and costs you credibility. Nigerian buyers talk to each other, and they remember sellers who lied.

## Step 6: Use Social Proof the Nigerian Way

Nigerians buy what other Nigerians have bought and liked. That is why testimonials, reviews, and "sold out" posts work so well here. Your description should carry proof, not just promises.

The simplest form is a customer quote in the buyer's own words: "Mrs Adaeze, Lekki: 'The material is even better than the picture. I have ordered a second one.'" Real names and real locations make it believable. Screenshots of WhatsApp chats, with permission, are even stronger — a buyer trusts another buyer's words more than yours.

If you have a number worth sharing, share it truthfully: "Our bestseller — restocked three times this month" or "Trusted by customers in Lagos, Abuja and Port Harcourt." If you are new, do not invent numbers. Instead, borrow proof honestly: show the product being worn, used, or unboxed in a short video. A thirty-second video of the dress moving, the bag opening, or the blender working does more than a thousand adjectives.

Encourage reviews after delivery. A simple message three days after delivery — "Hope you love it! If you have a moment, send us a quick review or a photo" — builds a library of proof you can reuse in future descriptions. Offer nothing in exchange that would make the review dishonest; a genuine "I love it" is worth more than a paid paragraph.

## Step 7: Write for Search Without Sounding Like a Robot

If you sell on a website, Jumia, Konga, or a marketplace, your description also needs to be found. Buyers type specific phrases into search: "ankara gown Lagos", "men's sandals size 44", "rechargeable fan Nigeria", "skin care for oily skin". Those phrases belong in your description naturally, not stuffed in a list at the bottom.

Use the product's real name and the words buyers use in the first two sentences. "This Ankara midi dress, made in Lagos, is perfect for weddings, church, and owambe parties" contains three search phrases without sounding forced. On a website, use the same words in the product title and in one subheading.

Keep sentences short. Avoid stuffing the same keyword ten times — Google and Jumia both penalise that, and human readers hate it. Write the description for a human first, then check that the main search phrase appears in the title, the first paragraph, and once in a bullet. That is enough.



## Final Word

Good product descriptions are not about fancy words. They are about respect for the buyer: her time, her money, and her right to know what she is buying before she pays. Write the benefit first, the honest details second, the price and delivery plainly, and the next step clearly — and do it in the words your customers already use.

Start with your best-selling product today. Rewrite its description using the template above. Then rewrite your next five. Within a month you will notice the change: fewer "how much?" messages, fewer wrong-size orders, fewer abandoned chats, and more buyers who arrive ready to pay. The photo brought them to your page. Your words will bring their money to your account.
