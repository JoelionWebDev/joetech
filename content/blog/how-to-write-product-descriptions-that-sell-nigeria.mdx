A customer in Lagos opens your WhatsApp catalogue. She sees a photo of a dress, a price, and two words: "Available now." She scrolls past. Another seller shows the same kind of dress, but her description says the fabric, the sizes, the length, how it fits, how to wash it, the delivery fee to Lekki, and that payment is on delivery in Lagos. The customer messages the second seller.

That is the power of a product description in Nigeria. Most customers cannot touch the product before they buy, so your words and photos are the shop. If the words are vague, the customer fills the gap with doubt — and doubt kills sales.

This guide shows you how to write descriptions that answer questions before they are asked, build trust, and make buying easy, whether you sell on WhatsApp, Instagram, Jumia, Konga, or your own website.

## Why descriptions matter more in Nigeria than elsewhere

In a physical market in Onitsha or Balogun, a customer picks up the item, checks the stitching, and haggles face to face. Online, all of that is gone, so the description has to do the work of the trader and the shop assistant.

Three Nigerian realities make descriptions especially important.

First, trust is low. Many shoppers have been disappointed by online orders — wrong size, different colour, poor quality, or an item that never arrived. A detailed, honest description signals that you are a serious seller, not a fly-by-night account. When you state the exact size, material, and condition, you reduce the fear that the buyer will be cheated.

Second, most buying happens on a phone, often on WhatsApp. Shoppers scroll quickly, on MTN or Airtel data, sometimes with a weak network. Your description must be short enough to read on a phone screen, but complete enough to answer the main questions. Long, rambling paragraphs get skipped. Clear, short lines get read.

Third, delivery and payment questions decide the sale. A buyer in Abuja wants to know the delivery fee to Abuja, how long it takes, and whether she can pay on delivery. A buyer in Port Harcourt wants to know if you deliver there at all. If your description does not answer those questions, the buyer must message you — and many will not bother. They will buy from the seller whose description already told them.

A good description therefore does three jobs: it describes the product clearly, it removes doubt, and it tells the buyer exactly how to order.

## The 7 things every description must include

Before you write anything clever, cover the basics. Every product you sell should state these seven facts, in this order if possible:

1. **What it is.** The exact product name, not a vague label. "Ankara midi dress" is better than "fine dress". "Oraimo 20000mAh power bank" is better than "power bank".
2. **Key specifications.** Size, colour, material, weight, capacity, or dimensions — whichever applies. For clothes: sizes available, fabric, length, and fit. For electronics: brand, model, battery, and what is in the box. For food: weight, ingredients, and expiry or shelf life.
3. **What it is good for.** One line on who it suits or when to use it. "Perfect for office, church, and owambe" tells a buyer more than "very nice".
4. **What is included.** Does the price include the charger, the case, the extra strap, or delivery? Say so. Hidden extras cause arguments after payment.
5. **Price and any condition.** State the price clearly. If it is fairly used, say so. If it is new, say so. Never write "DM for price" if you want fast sales — it adds friction, and many buyers assume the price will change depending on who asks.
6. **Delivery details.** Where you deliver, how long it takes, and the fee, or at least the fee range. "Delivery within Lagos: ₦2,000–₦3,500, 24–48 hours. Nationwide: 2–4 days, fee depends on location" is enough to start.
7. **How to order.** "Send a DM with your size and location" or "Tap the WhatsApp link and send 'I want the blue size M'". Give the buyer one clear next step.

If you cover those seven, your description is already better than most Nigerian online sellers. The rest of this guide is about making it persuasive as well as complete.

## Know who you are writing for

A description that tries to please everyone pleases no one. Before you write, picture one buyer.

A seller of baby clothes in Surulere writes for mothers: soft cotton, gentle on skin, easy to wash, sizes from 0 to 24 months. A seller of sneakers in Lekki writes for young men who care about style and authenticity: original, sizes 40–45, limited stock.

Ask three questions about your buyer:

- What problem are they trying to solve? A working mother wants a dress she can wear to the office and to church. A student wants a power bank that lasts through lectures and a long bus ride. A bride wants aso-ebi that arrives on time and matches the colour exactly.
- What are they afraid of? Wrong size, fake product, late delivery, or being scammed. Your description should calm each fear directly.
- What words do they use? Nigerian buyers search and chat in plain English, sometimes with Pidgin. "Original", "durable", "soft", "lasts long", "no fading", "easy to wash" are words they trust. Avoid jargon they will not understand. If you sell a phone, say "battery lasts a full day" as well as "5000mAh". The plain version sells; the technical version supports it.

Write in the second person — "you" — more than "we" or "I". "You will love how soft this feels" speaks to the buyer. "We sell quality products" speaks about you, and the buyer already assumes you think your products are good.

## Lead with the benefit, not the feature

A feature is what a product is. A benefit is what it does for the buyer. Features inform. Benefits sell. The best descriptions give both, benefit first.

Compare these two descriptions for the same bag:

- Feature only: "Leather handbag, 30cm by 25cm, zip closure, two compartments."
- Benefit first: "Carry your laptop, phone, wallet and makeup in one neat bag that still looks smart at work. Made from strong leather with a zip closure and two compartments, so nothing falls out on a busy Lagos bus."

The second version still gives the size and material, but it starts with the buyer's life. She can picture herself using it.

Try this formula for each product: benefit, then feature, then proof.

- Benefit: what changes for the buyer.
- Feature: the specification that makes it true.
- Proof: a detail that makes it believable, such as the material, the brand, a guarantee, or a review.

Examples:

- "Stay cool all day in Lagos heat. This 100 percent cotton shirt breathes well and does not stick to your skin, even in traffic on Third Mainland Bridge."
- "Charge your phone twice without looking for a socket. This 20000mAh power bank charges a phone from zero to full twice, and it comes with a one-year warranty."
- "Cook jollof for a party of 50 without stress. This 25-litre pot spreads heat evenly, so the bottom does not burn while the top is still raw."

Notice that each example uses a Nigerian detail — Lagos heat, traffic, a party of 50. Local details make a description feel written for the buyer, not copied from a foreign website.

## Be specific. Vague words lose sales

"Quality", "nice", "good", "original", and "durable" are the most overused words in Nigerian online selling. They mean almost nothing on their own, because every seller uses them, including sellers of fake products.

Specificity is what makes those words believable. Replace vague claims with numbers, materials, and facts:

- Instead of "quality material", write "100 percent cotton Ankara, 6 yards, no fading after washing".
- Instead of "big size", write "fits a 13-inch laptop, with a padded pocket".
- Instead of "long-lasting battery", write "lasts 8 hours of video playback, charges in 2 hours".
- Instead of "fast delivery", write "delivery in Lagos within 24 hours, Abuja in 2 days".
- Instead of "many colours", list them: "Available in black, navy, wine, and emerald green".

Specificity also protects you. If you write "waterproof" and the bag is only water-resistant, a buyer will return it and complain. If you write "water-resistant — handles light rain, not heavy downpour", you set the right expectation and avoid a refund. Honest descriptions reduce returns, and returns are expensive in Nigeria, where the buyer often pays the return delivery fee and blames you for it.

Be honest about flaws, too. If a fairly used phone has a small scratch on the back, say so and show it in a photo. Buyers who accept the flaw before paying rarely complain after. Buyers who discover it after paying will demand a refund and may post a negative review.

## Use a simple structure that works on WhatsApp

Most Nigerian sales start on WhatsApp, not on a website. Your description must work in a WhatsApp catalogue item, a status post, and a chat message. That means short lines, clear breaks, and no long paragraphs.

Here is a structure you can copy for almost any product:

**Product name and one-line benefit**
Ankara midi dress — flattering, comfortable, and ready for office or owambe.

**Key details**
- Fabric: 100 percent cotton Ankara
- Sizes: S, M, L, XL (UK 8–16)
- Length: midi, below the knee
- Colours: blue, green, wine
- Care: machine wash cold, do not bleach

**Price and delivery**
- Price: ₦35,000
- Lagos delivery: ₦2,000–₦3,500, 24–48 hours
- Nationwide: 2–4 days, fee by location
- Payment: transfer or pay on delivery in Lagos

**How to order**
Send a WhatsApp message with your size and location, and we will confirm availability before you pay.

That structure fits on a phone screen, answers the main questions, and ends with a clear action. Adapt the headings to your product, but keep the order: benefit, details, price and delivery, how to order.

For Instagram, put the benefit and price in the first two lines, before the caption is cut off: "Ankara midi dress, ₦35,000 — soft cotton, sizes S to XL, delivery in Lagos in 24 hours".

For Jumia or Konga, follow the marketplace format, but add the same details in the description box. Marketplaces rank products partly on how complete the listing is, and buyers compare listings side by side. A complete listing wins over a bare one, even at a slightly higher price.

## Write for search, add proof, and answer objections

Buyers find products by searching, so include the words they type naturally in the first two sentences: "Ankara midi dress in Lagos — 100 percent cotton, sizes S to XL" beats "Beautiful dress for beautiful ladies". Use specific Instagram hashtags such as #AnkaraDress and #LagosFashion, and name image files descriptively on your website. Add proof with one short real customer quote, true numbers you can defend, and a clear guarantee: "7-day exchange if the size is wrong, as long as the item is unworn with tags." Use clear photos in natural light from several angles, plus a 10 to 15 second video where fit matters. Answer objections before they are asked: name the brand and source for originality, give a size chart in centimetres, state care instructions, give honest delivery ranges, and say clearly whether pay on delivery applies and where. Read your WhatsApp chats each week, note the questions customers ask most, and add the answers to the description.

## Three worked examples you can adapt

**Example 1: A dress on WhatsApp**

Ankara midi dress — soft, flattering, and ready for work or owambe. Price: ₦35,000.

- Fabric: 100 percent cotton Ankara, no fading
- Sizes: S, M, L, XL (UK 8–16)
- Length: midi, below the knee
- Colours: blue, green, wine
- Care: machine wash cold

Delivery: Lagos ₦2,000–₦3,500 in 24–48 hours. Nationwide 2–4 days. Pay on delivery available in Lagos. To order, send your size and location on WhatsApp.

**Example 2: A power bank on Instagram**

Never run out of battery in Lagos traffic again. Oraimo 20000mAh power bank — charges your phone twice from zero to full. Price: ₦28,500.

- Capacity: 20000mAh, charges two phones at once
- Fast charging, with cable in the box
- Warranty: 1 year
- Delivery: Lagos 24 hours, nationwide 2–4 days

DM "POWER BANK" to order. Limited stock — sizes and colours sell out fast on weekends.

**Example 3: A food product on a website**

Pure wild honey, 500ml — raw, unfiltered, sourced from beekeepers in Kogi State. Price: ₦12,000.

- Weight: 500ml glass jar
- No added sugar, no preservatives
- Best before: 12 months from production, printed on the jar
- Use: tea, pap, baking, or a spoon daily

Delivery: Lagos ₦2,500 in 48 hours, Abuja ₦3,000 in 3 days. We pack each jar with bubble wrap to prevent breakage. Order online and pay by transfer, card, or Paystack.

Notice that each example is short, specific, and ends with how to order. Copy the structure, change the details to your product, and you have a working description.

## Mistakes that cost Nigerian sellers sales

**Mistake 1: "DM for price."** It slows the sale, invites haggling from people who were never going to buy, and makes buyers suspect the price changes by customer. State the price, except for custom orders where the price genuinely depends on the specification.

**Mistake 2: Copying another seller's description word for word.** Customers notice, Google notices, and you lose the chance to describe your own product honestly. Use another description as inspiration, then rewrite it in your own words with your own details.

**Mistake 3: Using only foreign descriptions.** A description copied from Amazon US may mention sizes, seasons, or shipping that do not apply in Nigeria. Rewrite it for Lagos, Abuja, or your market, with naira prices, local delivery, and local payment methods.

**Mistake 4: Hiding the delivery fee.** Buyers hate surprise fees at checkout. State the fee or the range early. If the fee depends on location, say so and give an example for Lagos and Abuja.

**Mistake 5: Writing in all caps or with too many emojis.** "ORIGINAL!!! BEST QUALITY!!! 🔥🔥🔥" looks desperate and is hard to read. Use normal sentence case, one or two emojis at most, and let the details do the persuading.

**Mistake 6: Never updating the description.** Prices change, sizes sell out, and delivery times shift during fuel scarcity or festive periods. An outdated description causes arguments. Update the price, stock, and delivery line the moment they change, especially before December, when demand and delivery times both rise sharply.

**Mistake 7: No call to action.** A buyer who finishes reading should know the next step. "Send a DM now", "Tap the WhatsApp link", "Add to cart" — pick one, and make it obvious.

## A simple weekly routine to improve your descriptions

You do not need to rewrite every listing in one day. Improve them gradually.

1. **Monday:** Pick your five best-selling products. Check each description against the seven essentials above. Fill in what is missing.
2. **Wednesday:** Read your WhatsApp chats from the past week. List the five questions customers asked most. Add the answers to the relevant descriptions.
3. **Friday:** Check your prices, stock, and delivery times. Update any listing that is out of date. Remove products you no longer stock, so buyers do not order what you cannot supply.
4. **Monthly:** Look at which products sell fastest and which sit unsold. Read the fast sellers' descriptions — what do they do well? Apply the same structure to slow sellers, and consider whether the slow product needs better photos, a clearer size guide, or a price review.

Keep a simple template in your notes app with the headings from this guide, and copy it for every new product.

## Final word: describe like you are there with the buyer

Imagine your buyer is standing in front of you in your shop. What would you tell her? You would show her the fabric, tell her the size, explain how to wash it, and tell her the price and when she can collect it. Your product description is that conversation, written down.

Write clearly, be specific, be honest, and always tell the buyer how to order. Do that for every product, and your descriptions will become your best salesperson — working on WhatsApp at midnight and on your website while you sleep.

Start with your best seller today. Rewrite its description using this guide, post it, and watch the questions fall and the orders rise.
