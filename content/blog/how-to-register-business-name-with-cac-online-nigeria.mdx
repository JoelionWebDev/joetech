A registered business name changes how people see your business. It lets you open a corporate bank account, issue proper receipts and invoices, apply for grants and loans, bid for contracts, and protect your brand name so nobody else can use it. In Nigeria, business name registration is handled by the Corporate Affairs Commission, known as the CAC, and the whole process can now be done online through the CAC post-incorporation portal.

Many small business owners in Lagos, Abuja, Enugu, and Port Harcourt delay registration because the process sounds complicated. It is not. If you follow the steps in order, have your documents ready, and avoid the common mistakes, you can complete it yourself without paying an agent. This guide explains the entire process in plain language.

## First, Understand What You Are Registering

In Nigeria, there are two main ways to formalise a small business with the CAC. The first is a business name, registered under Part B of the Companies and Allied Matters Act. This is for sole proprietorships and partnerships — one person, or a small group of partners, trading under a name like "Adaeze Fashion House" or "Chukwu Logistics Services". It is the cheapest and simplest option, and it is what most small businesses need.

The second is a limited liability company, registered under Part A, with names ending in "Limited" or "Ltd". A company is a separate legal entity from its owners, which protects your personal assets if the business runs into debt. It costs more and involves more paperwork, including directors, a share capital, and annual filings. If you are just starting out as a fashion designer, a food vendor, a digital marketer, or a trader, a business name is usually enough. You can upgrade to a limited company later as the business grows.

This guide covers business name registration. Before you start, decide on your business name and your line of business. The CAC will ask what your business does, and your answer must match your name. A name like "Bright Star Catering Services" should be in catering, not in phone repairs.

## Step 1: Search for Your Business Name

Everything starts with a name search on the CAC portal. Go to the CAC website and use the public search to check whether your chosen name is already taken. Type the exact name you want and search. If an identical or very similar name already exists, you will need to choose something else. Do not skip this step — submitting a name that is already registered wastes your time and your reservation fee.

When choosing a name, follow the CAC rules. Your name must not be identical or confusingly similar to an existing registered name. It must not contain words that suggest government connection, like "National", "Federal", or "State", unless you have special permission. It must not contain restricted words like "Bank", "Insurance", or "University" unless you are actually licensed in that field. Avoid names that are offensive or misleading.

Pick three name options in order of preference before you log in. Your first choice might be rejected or taken, and having backups ready saves you from starting over. For example, if "Golden Touch Beauty Salon" is taken, your second choice could be "Golden Touch Beauty Studio" and your third "Golden Touch Glamour House". Make each option distinct enough that a rejection of one does not automatically affect the others.

## Step 2: Create an Account on the CAC Portal

To register online, you need an account on the CAC post-incorporation portal. Visit the CAC website and click on the portal link. You will create an account with your email address and a password. Use an email address you check regularly, because approval messages, queries, and your certificate will all come to that email. A Gmail address works fine.

After creating the account, log in and find the business name registration section. The portal will ask for your personal details as the proprietor: your full name, date of birth, phone number, residential address, and means of identification. Your name here must match your ID exactly. If your national ID says "Igono Joel", do not write "Joel Igono" on the form. Mismatches between the form and your ID are one of the most common reasons applications get queried.

You will also need a passport photograph. Take a clear, recent photo against a plain background. It does not need to be a studio photo, but it must be clear and show your face properly. A phone photo in good light against a white wall is acceptable.

## Step 3: Reserve Your Business Name

Name reservation is the formal step where the CAC checks your chosen name and holds it for you while you complete the registration. On the portal, fill in your preferred name, your alternative names, and the nature of your business. Describe your business activity clearly: "retail sale of female clothing and accessories" is better than just "trading". A clear description helps the approving officer understand your business and reduces queries.

Pay the reservation fee online through the portal. The fee for business name reservation is currently around ₦500, though CAC fees change from time to time, so confirm the exact amount shown on the portal before paying. Payment is made by card or bank transfer through the portal's payment gateway. Keep your payment receipt and the transaction reference, and take a screenshot as backup.

After payment, your reservation goes for approval. This usually takes one to three working days, though it can take longer during busy periods. You will receive an email when it is approved, along with an availability code, often called an AV code. This code is important — you will need it for the next stage. Keep it safe. The reservation is typically valid for 60 days, so do not delay the next step for too long.

If your name is rejected, the CAC will state a reason. Common reasons are similarity to an existing name or a restricted word. Read the reason carefully, adjust your name, and resubmit with your next preferred option. You may need to pay the reservation fee again for a fresh application, so getting the name search right the first time saves money.

## Step 4: Complete the Registration Application

Once your name is reserved, return to the portal and start the business name registration, using your availability code. You will fill in the full application form. The details include the proprietor's information, the business address, the nature of business, and the date the business started trading.

Your business address can be your shop address, your office, or your home address if you run the business from home. It must be a real address in Nigeria where CAC correspondence can reach you. Use an address you can access easily, because some CAC communications still arrive by post.

You will upload documents at this stage. The main ones are a valid means of identification for the proprietor, such as a National Identity Number slip or card, an international passport, a driver's licence, or a voter's card. You will also upload your passport photograph and your signature, which you can scan or photograph clearly on white paper. Make sure all uploads are clear and readable. Blurry documents are a common cause of delays.

If the business has partners, each partner's details and ID must be added. Partners should agree in advance on how profits and responsibilities are shared, and it is wise to write this down in a simple partnership agreement, even if the CAC does not require you to upload one.

## Step 5: Pay the Registration Fee and Submit

After completing the form, the portal will show the registration fee. The business name registration fee is currently around ₦10,000, but confirm the exact figure on the portal, because CAC fees are reviewed periodically. Pay through the portal's payment gateway and keep the receipt.

Review every detail on your application before you submit. Check the spelling of your business name, your own name, your address, and your date of birth. A spelling mistake on your certificate is expensive and slow to correct. Once you are sure everything is correct, submit the application.

After submission, your application goes to a CAC officer for review. This stage usually takes a few working days, but it can stretch to one or two weeks if the officer raises a query. Check your email and your portal dashboard regularly. If a query comes in, respond quickly and provide exactly what is asked for. A fast response can save you days of waiting.

## Step 6: Download Your Certificate

When your application is approved, the CAC will issue your business name certificate. You can download it from the portal as a PDF. This certificate is your proof of registration. It shows your business name, your registration number, and the date of registration. Print a copy and keep the digital copy safe as well.

Your registration number, which starts with "BN" followed by digits, is what banks, government agencies, and corporate clients will ask for. Keep it where you can find it easily — in your phone notes, for example — because you will use it often.

With the certificate, you can now open a corporate bank account in your business name. Most Nigerian banks will ask for the certificate, your ID, a utility bill, and passport photographs. You can also register for a Tax Identification Number, known as a TIN, with the Federal Inland Revenue Service, which you will need for tax filings and for dealing with corporate clients who deduct withholding tax.

## Common Mistakes That Delay Registration

The first mistake is choosing a name without searching first. A name that is already taken, or too close to a registered company, will be rejected, and you lose the reservation fee and the time. Always search the CAC public register before you pay anything.

The second mistake is inconsistent personal details. Your name, date of birth, and address must match across your form, your ID, and your other documents. Even a small difference, like "Street" in one place and "St" in another, is usually fine, but a different spelling of your name is not.

The third mistake is uploading poor-quality documents. A dark, blurry photo of your ID or a signature written on lined paper will be queried. Take your time, use good light, and check each upload before submitting.

The fourth mistake is using an email address you do not check. Approval notices, queries, and certificates all arrive by email. If you registered with an old email you rarely open, you may miss a query for weeks. Use your main email and check your spam folder too.

The fifth mistake is paying an agent more than necessary. There are many agents who offer to register your business name for ₦50,000 or more. The official CAC fees are a small fraction of that. If you can follow this guide, you can do it yourself. If you do hire help, agree the fee in advance, ask for receipts for every CAC payment, and insist on receiving your login details and your availability code.

## What Registration Does Not Do

Registering a business name does not give you a trademark. If you want exclusive rights to a logo or brand name across Nigeria, trademark registration is a separate process with the Trademarks Registry. Business name registration stops another business from registering the same name with the CAC, but it does not stop someone using a similar name on Instagram or on a shop sign.

Registration also does not automatically make you tax compliant. You still need a TIN, and you still need to file your taxes. For a small business, this usually means registering with the Federal Inland Revenue Service or your state tax authority, keeping records of your income and expenses, and filing annually. The process is simpler than most people fear, and it protects you from penalties later.

Finally, registration does not replace the licences your specific business needs. A food business may need health and safety approvals. A pharmacy needs a pharmacist's licence. A school needs state education approval. Check the requirements for your industry separately.

## How Long Does Registration Take, and What Does It Cost in Total?

A realistic timeline for business name registration is one to three weeks from start to finish, if everything goes smoothly. Name search and reservation take one to three working days. Preparing your documents takes a day. The registration review takes a few working days to two weeks, depending on how busy the CAC is and whether your application is queried. If you are queried, add a few more days for your response and the re-review.

The total official cost is modest. Name reservation is around ₦500, and business name registration is around ₦10,000, so the official fees come to roughly ₦10,500, plus small bank or payment gateway charges. CAC fees are reviewed from time to time, so always confirm the figure shown on the portal before you pay. Be wary of anyone who quotes you ₦50,000 or ₦80,000 for a simple business name registration — that is mostly their service fee, not the CAC fee, and you can do the work yourself with this guide.

Budget a little extra for the practical items around registration: printing your certificate, taking passport photographs, and transport to the bank for your corporate account. These small costs add up to a few thousand naira at most. Compared with the contracts, grants, and corporate clients that registration unlocks, it is one of the cheapest investments you will ever make in your business.

One more practical tip: start the process when you have a quiet week, not when you are under pressure to submit documents for a contract the next day. Registration rewards patience. Rushing leads to mistakes, mistakes lead to queries, and queries lead to delays. Give yourself three weeks, follow the steps in order, and you will get there without stress.

## After Registration: Put It to Work

Once you have your certificate, use it. Update your invoices, receipts, and letterhead with your registered business name and registration number. Add it to your WhatsApp Business profile and your Instagram bio. Open that corporate bank account so your business money is separate from your personal money — this one step makes your finances far easier to manage and looks professional to clients.

Tell your customers. A short WhatsApp broadcast announcing that your business is now officially registered builds confidence, especially with corporate clients who need your CAC documents before they can pay you. Many government agencies and large companies will not engage an unregistered vendor at all, so your certificate opens doors that were closed before.

Keep your registration details safe and keep your business records from day one. File your receipts, track your income, and note your expenses. In a year, when you apply for a loan or a grant, these records will matter as much as the certificate itself.

Registering your business name with the CAC is one of the highest-value steps a Nigerian small business can take. It costs little, it can be done online in a few days, and it turns a hustle into a recognised business. Start with the name search today, and work through the steps one by one. In a few weeks, you will hold a certificate that makes your business official.
