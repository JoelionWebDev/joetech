If you run a small business in Nigeria, your phone is your shop. Customers call it, send WhatsApp messages to it, and pay into accounts linked to it. But when that phone number is also your personal number, the lines blur fast. Customers call at 11 pm on a Sunday. Your business messages mix with family chats. A customer who saves your personal number can see your WhatsApp status, your profile photo, and when you were last online. And if you ever lose the phone or change your number, your business loses its customers with it.

A second phone number for your business solves all of this. It separates work from personal life, it makes your business look professional, and it protects your privacy. It costs far less than most people think, and there are several ways to get one in Nigeria, from a second SIM card to a virtual number that lives entirely in an app. This guide explains each option, what it costs, how to set it up, and how to use it properly with WhatsApp Business.

## Why Your Business Needs Its Own Number

The first reason is professionalism. A number that appears on your flyers, your Instagram bio, your Google Business Profile and your invoices should belong to the business, not to you personally. When a corporate client in Lagos or Abuja sees a dedicated business number, they take you more seriously. When that number changes because you changed your personal line, every flyer, every post, and every saved contact becomes wrong.

The second reason is privacy. With a separate business number, customers cannot see your personal WhatsApp status or your personal profile photo, and they cannot call you at midnight on your private line. You control what they see. You can set business hours, an away message, and a professional greeting, and you can switch off work at the end of the day without switching off your personal phone.

The third reason is organisation. With a business number on WhatsApp Business, your chats, your catalogue, your labels, and your automated replies all live in one place, separate from your family and friends. You can label customers as "New", "Paid", "Pending" or "VIP", and you can find an old order in seconds instead of scrolling through months of personal chats.

The fourth reason is continuity. If you hire staff, you can hand them the business phone or the business WhatsApp account, and the number stays with the business. If your personal phone is stolen, your business keeps running. If you ever sell the business, the number goes with it. A business built on a personal number is a business that can disappear overnight.

The fifth reason is trust. Customers are more likely to pay a business that has a dedicated number, a proper WhatsApp Business profile, and a consistent presence. It signals that you are serious, that you will answer, and that you will still be there next week. In a market where scams are common, that signal matters.

## Option 1: A Second SIM Card — the Simplest Route

The easiest way to get a second number in Nigeria is to buy a second SIM card. Walk into any MTN, Airtel, Glo or 9mobile shop with your National Identity Number, your NIN, and a valid means of identification, and you can buy a new SIM for a few hundred naira. You will need to link the SIM to your NIN, which is a legal requirement in Nigeria. If you already have a NIN, the process takes a few minutes. If you do not have one, visit a NIMC enrolment centre first — enrolment is free.

Once you have the SIM, put it in a dual SIM phone. Most Android phones sold in Nigeria support two SIM cards, and many business owners already carry one. If your phone is single SIM, you have two choices: buy a cheap second phone for the business, or use a dual SIM adapter. A basic Android phone for business use can cost as little as ₦50,000 to ₦100,000, and it is worth it if your business depends on the phone. A second phone also means your business keeps working if your personal phone dies or is stolen.

Choose your network carefully. MTN has the widest coverage in Nigeria, which matters if your customers are spread across the country. Airtel is strong in many cities and often has competitive data prices. Glo offers generous data bundles. 9mobile has loyal users in some areas. The best choice is the network with the strongest signal where you live and work, because a business number that customers cannot reach is useless. Ask neighbours and other business owners which network works best in your area.

Register the SIM in your business name where possible, and keep the SIM pack and the phone number written down somewhere safe. If you ever lose the SIM, you will need your NIN and identification to retrieve the number, and the process is much faster if you have the details handy.

## Option 2: eSIM — a Second Number Without a Second SIM

If your phone supports eSIM, you can add a second number without a physical SIM card at all. An eSIM is a digital SIM built into the phone. Many newer iPhones and Samsung phones support it, and MTN, Airtel and 9mobile all offer eSIM in Nigeria.

To get an eSIM, visit a service centre of your chosen network with your NIN and identification, and ask for an eSIM for a new number. The staff will give you a QR code, which you scan with your phone to activate the line. The process usually takes a few minutes, and the eSIM costs little or nothing beyond the price of the number itself.

The advantage of eSIM is that your phone can hold two active numbers — one physical SIM and one eSIM — without a second device. You can set one number for personal calls and data, and the other for business.

The disadvantage is that not every phone supports eSIM, and if your phone is damaged or you switch to a phone without eSIM, moving the number is more work than moving a physical SIM. Check whether your phone supports eSIM before you go to the service centre. On an iPhone, go to Settings, then Mobile Data, and look for the option to add an eSIM. On a Samsung, go to Settings, then Connections, then SIM Manager.

## Option 3: A Virtual Number — a Business Number in an App

A virtual number is a phone number that lives in an app or a cloud service rather than on a SIM card. You do not need a second phone, and you can receive calls and messages on your existing device through the app. Virtual numbers are popular with businesses that want a professional number without carrying two phones.

There are several ways to get a virtual number. Some international services, such as Google Voice, offer free virtual numbers, but they are generally not available with Nigerian numbers, and they are not designed for Nigerian businesses. Other services sell virtual Nigerian numbers, or international numbers that forward to your phone. Prices vary widely, from a few thousand naira a month to much more, depending on the provider and the features.

Before you pay for a virtual number, check three things. First, can it receive calls and SMS reliably on Nigerian networks? Some virtual numbers work well for calls but cannot receive the SMS verification codes that banks, WhatsApp, and Paystack send. That makes them useless for a business that depends on those services. Second, who owns the number? If you stop paying, or the provider closes, you may lose the number and all the customers who saved it. Third, is the provider reputable? Read reviews from Nigerian users, and avoid any service that asks for payment through a personal account or promises a number that sounds too cheap to be true.

For most small businesses in Nigeria, a physical second SIM is more reliable and cheaper than a virtual number. Virtual numbers make sense if you need an international number — for example, a UK or US number for customers abroad — or if you run a call centre and need several numbers on one system. For a one-person business, a second SIM is usually the better choice.

## Option 4: WhatsApp Business on Your Existing Number

If you cannot get a second number right away, the next best step is to move your business to WhatsApp Business on your existing number. WhatsApp Business is a free app from Meta, designed for small businesses, and it works alongside the regular WhatsApp app. You cannot use the same number on both apps at the same time, so you will need to choose: either use your personal number for WhatsApp Business, or keep your personal WhatsApp and use a second number for WhatsApp Business. The second option is better, which is why this guide starts with getting a second number.

Here is how to set up WhatsApp Business:

1. Download WhatsApp Business from the Google Play Store or the Apple App Store. It is free.
2. Open the app, agree to the terms, and enter your business number. WhatsApp will send a verification code by SMS or call. Enter the code to verify the number.
3. Set up your business profile. Tap Settings, then your business name, and fill in your business name, category, description, address, business hours, email, and website. Add a clear logo or a photo of your products as your profile picture.
4. Create your catalogue. Tap Catalogue in the business tools, add your products or services with photos, prices, and descriptions. Customers can browse your catalogue inside WhatsApp and order directly from it.
5. Set up automated messages. In Business Tools, create a greeting message that welcomes new customers, and an away message that tells customers when you will reply. Set your business hours so the away message sends automatically outside those hours.
6. Use labels to organise your chats. Create labels such as New Customer, Order Placed, Paid, Pending Payment, and Delivered. Label every chat so you can find orders quickly.
7. Set up quick replies. These are shortcuts for messages you send often, such as your price list, your delivery fees, or your bank details. Instead of typing the same message twenty times a day, you type a short code and the full message appears.

WhatsApp Business also gives you simple statistics: how many messages you sent, delivered, read, and received. These numbers help you see which days are busy and whether your messages are being read.

## How to Use Your Business Number Properly

Getting a second number is only half the job. Using it well is what builds trust and brings sales.

First, put the number everywhere. Add it to your Instagram bio, your Facebook page, your Google Business Profile, your flyers, your business cards, your invoices, and your email signature. Use the same number consistently. If you change it, update every place it appears, and tell your regular customers.

Second, set business hours and stick to them. Decide when you are available — for example, 8 am to 6 pm, Monday to Saturday — and put those hours in your WhatsApp Business profile. Outside those hours, your away message should say when you will reply. Customers respect a business that is clear about its hours, and you protect your evenings and your Sundays.

Third, reply quickly during business hours. In Nigeria, the business that replies first often wins the sale. Aim to reply to every message within an hour during business hours, and within a few minutes if you can. If you cannot reply properly, send a short message saying you have seen their message and will reply shortly. Silence is the fastest way to lose a customer to a competitor.

Fourth, keep your business chats professional. Use a polite greeting, confirm orders clearly, and send a receipt or confirmation after payment. Do not send personal messages, jokes, or chain messages from your business number. Do not add customers to groups without their permission. Do not broadcast your menu to people who never asked for it — they will block you, and a blocked business number is hard to recover.

Fifth, back up your chats. In WhatsApp Business, go to Settings, then Chats, then Chat Backup, and turn on automatic backup to Google Drive. Your chats contain your orders, your customer contacts, and your payment confirmations. If you lose your phone, a backup is the difference between starting again from zero and picking up where you left off.

Sixth, protect the number. Turn on two-step verification in WhatsApp, using a PIN you will remember. Never share the verification code WhatsApp sends you with anyone, even someone who claims to be from WhatsApp or MTN. Scammers in Nigeria regularly try to steal business WhatsApp accounts by tricking owners into sharing their codes. If you lose access to the number, contact your network provider with your NIN and identification to retrieve the SIM.

## Costs: What a Second Number Really Costs

A second SIM card costs a few hundred naira to buy, plus the cost of linking it to your NIN, which is free at a service centre. A monthly data and call bundle for the business line costs ₦2,000 to ₦10,000 depending on how much you use it. If you need a second phone, a basic Android phone costs ₦50,000 to ₦100,000, and you can buy a good used phone for less.

An eSIM costs little or nothing beyond the price of the number, but you need a phone that supports it. A virtual number costs from a few thousand naira a month upwards, depending on the provider, and you should be cautious about the cheapest options.

WhatsApp Business is free. The real cost of a business number is not the SIM — it is the discipline to use it properly, to reply on time, and to keep it separate from your personal life. That discipline costs nothing, and it is what turns a second number into a real business asset.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

The first mistake is giving customers your personal number "just this once". Every time you do, you blur the line you worked to create. Use the business number for all business contact, every time, without exception.

The second mistake is using the business number for personal chats. Your business number is a business asset. Do not use it to chat with friends, join personal groups, or share personal status updates. Customers who see your personal life on your business profile will take you less seriously.

The third mistake is never setting an away message. Customers who message you at night and hear nothing until morning will assume you are not serious. An away message costs you nothing and keeps them informed.

The fourth mistake is buying a number from an unregistered vendor or a roadside seller. Always buy from an official service centre and link the SIM to your own NIN. A SIM registered in someone else's name can be blocked or retrieved by that person, and you will lose your business number.

The fifth mistake is letting the number lapse. If you do not recharge a prepaid SIM for a long time, the network may recycle the number and give it to someone else. Recharge regularly, even with a small amount, and use the line often enough to keep it active.
