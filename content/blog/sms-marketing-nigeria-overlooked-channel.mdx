## Why SMS Still Works in Nigeria

### Open rates other channels dream about

SMS open rates consistently sit around 95 percent or higher worldwide, with most messages read within minutes of arrival. Compare that to email open rates that often struggle past 20 percent, and organic social media reach throttled by algorithms to single or low double digits. When your message must be seen rather than merely available, text is the surest path to human eyes.

### It reaches every phone, not just smartphones

Your market includes customers using basic feature phones in areas with patchy internet. WhatsApp needs data and a smartphone. Email needs an account your customer actually checks. Instagram needs the app and a decent connection at the right hour. SMS works on every phone ever made, offline included. For businesses selling across Lagos mainland traffic zones, markets in Onitsha, farms around Kaduna, or estates where connectivity fluctuates, this universality is not a nice-to-have; it is the whole point.

### It cuts through the noise

Nigerians receive dozens of WhatsApp broadcasts, endless social content, and promotional emails daily, but personal text messages still carry a sense of importance. A well-crafted SMS feels like a notification, not like content to scroll past. Psychologically, texts occupy the same mental space as messages from family; that borrowed intimacy is exactly why disciplined businesses protect it and careless ones destroy it with spam.

### It costs almost nothing relative to return

We will break down exact numbers shortly, but preview the economics: reaching one thousand customers typically costs less than lunch for two. If even ten of those thousand walk in and buy, you have made multiples of your spend. Few marketing channels offer that ratio with such predictability.

## Understanding DND Numbers (The Make-or-Break Detail)

Here is where many Nigerian businesses get burned, so read this section carefully before spending money.

Millions of Nigerian phone numbers sit on the Do-Not-Disturb registry, a free service subscribers use to block promotional messages. DND exists because Nigerians were drowning in unsolicited texts and calls, and regulators responded decisively. Respecting it is both legally wise and strategically smart.

### What DND means for your campaigns

1. **Transactional SMS** — order confirmations, OTPs, appointment reminders, delivery updates, receipts — reach all numbers, including DND-active ones. These are considered service messages the subscriber implicitly wants.
2. **Promotional SMS** — sales blasts, offers, marketing announcements — may not deliver to DND-active numbers through standard routes. Some providers offer partial or filtered routes; results vary and honesty varies with them.

### The practical playbook

Structure messages around genuinely useful, service-related information wherever honestly possible. An order update with your promotion appended travels further than a naked advertisement. Before committing money to any provider, ask specifically: "what percentage of my messages will deliver to DND numbers?" and request recent delivery reports as proof. Providers answer that question very differently once they realize you know to ask.

And always maintain healthy channel diversity. SMS is powerful, but no business should depend entirely on any single channel whose rules it does not control. We design systems where SMS, WhatsApp, and email each cover the others' blind spots.

One more cost consideration: message length matters. Standard SMS carries roughly 160 characters; exceed it and providers bill multiple units per recipient. Tight writing is not just good copywriting here, it is direct cost control.

## Seven SMS Campaigns That Consistently Work

Theory is cheap; these seven formats pay bills across industries:

### 1. Order and delivery updates

The trust-builder. Confirm orders instantly, share dispatch updates, and notify on delivery completion. Customers who feel informed reorder with confidence, complain less, and rate you professionally. Even businesses without formal systems can semi-automate this with templates and a spreadsheet initially.

### 2. Appointment and booking reminders

Salons, clinics, gyms, driving schools, repair services, lesson teachers — anyone who books time — cut no-shows dramatically with a reminder twenty-four hours before plus another the morning of. Every recovered slot is pure recovered revenue that was already sold. One salon owner we worked with recovered enough weekly appointments from reminders alone to pay for her entire yearly messaging budget many times over.

### 3. Flash sales and limited offers

"50% off all fabrics, today only. Reply STOP to opt out." Short deadlines create action because urgency plus familiarity plus ease equals response. Keep these rare enough that each lands with impact — perhaps once or twice monthly at most — so your brand never becomes background noise.

### 4. Abandoned order recovery

If someone starts buying but stalls before payment, a gentle nudge an hour later recovers surprising revenue: "You left something behind. Your order is reserved for 24 hours." Pair it with a small incentive if margins allow, or simply remind without discount first; often presence alone completes the sale.

### 5. Loyalty and re-engagement messages

"We miss you. Here is 10% off your next visit this week." Reaching a past customer costs a fraction of acquiring a new one, and SMS is the fastest bridge back to lapsed buyers who already trust you. Segment these carefully: someone absent three months needs different warmth than someone absent two years.

### 6. Payment confirmations and receipts

Professionalism compounds quietly. Instant receipts make a small business feel established, reduce payment disputes to near zero, and signal systems-mindedness that customers tell friends about. This single automation elevates perception more than most redesigns.

### 7. Event and webinar reminders

Selling tickets or hosting training? A message the day before plus one an hour before measurably multiplies attendance compared to email alone. Webinar hosts routinely see attendance jump thirty percent or more when SMS joins the reminder stack.

## Writing SMS That Gets Results

You have roughly 160 characters per segment, so every word must earn its place. Follow these principles:

1. **Lead with value or urgency**, never with company history. Nobody cares that you are "a leading provider of excellence" — they care about the offer.
2. **Identify yourself immediately.** Start with your brand name so recipients know who is messaging within one second.
3. **One message, one action.** A single clear link outperforms a paragraph of options every time.
4. **Use trackable links.** Short links you control let you measure exactly which messages drive visits and sales, turning guesswork into data.
5. **Always include opt-out language.** It is professional, builds trust, keeps you compliant, and paradoxically increases response because recipients feel respected.
6. **Send at humane hours.** Between 9am and 7pm is safe territory. Nobody loves a promotional text at 5:30am, and early morning blasts generate complaints faster than sales.
7. **Personalize where honest.** Using a saved name ("Chidi, your order ships today") lifts response meaningfully over generic blasts — but only when the name is spelled correctly. Wrong-name personalization is worse than none.

### Templates you can adapt today

**Order confirmation:**
> "Joetech: Thank you Chidi! Order #1042 confirmed. We will text you when it ships. Questions? Reply here or call 08167099131."

**Flash sale:**
> "Joetech: FLASH SALE — 40% off all website packages until Friday midnight. Only 10 slots. Claim yours: [link] Reply STOP to opt out."

**Appointment reminder:**
> "Reminder: your appointment with Bright Salon is tomorrow at 2pm, Adeniyi Jones. Need to reschedule? Call 08167099131. See you soon!"

Notice what each does: identifies sender instantly, delivers one clear value, provides one action, offers a help path, respects the reader.

## Building Your SMS List the Right Way

Do not buy phone number lists. Beyond being largely ineffective — purchased audiences feel no connection to you — messaging people who never asked to hear from you torches your sender reputation, generates complaints, and wastes money. Instead, build consent-first:

1. **Collect numbers at every touchpoint**: checkout counters, order forms, delivery confirmations, events, packaging inserts. Train every team member to ask naturally.
2. **Offer a reason to join**: early access to sales, birthday discounts, useful reminders, members-only offers. People trade their number willingly for genuine value.
3. **Say clearly what they will get and how often.** Informed subscribers stay; surprised subscribers leave and complain.
4. **Record consent properly.** Keep a simple log of who joined, when, and through which channel. As you grow, this discipline becomes legally protective and operationally invaluable.
5. **Make opting out effortless.** Honor STOP replies immediately. Counterintuitively, easy exits improve list quality because remaining subscribers genuinely want your messages.

A list of five hundred willing customers beats ten thousand reluctant ones every single time — in revenue, in reputation, and in peace of mind.

## Measuring What Matters

Treat SMS like any serious channel by tracking four numbers monthly:

1. **Delivery rate** — what percentage actually arrived. Below 95 percent means list hygiene or routing problems worth investigating.
2. **Opt-out rate per campaign** — under 1 percent is healthy; spikes mean you messaged too often, too irrelevantly, or at wrong hours.
3. **Click-through rate on links** — reveals message quality and offer appeal. Strong campaigns frequently achieve 10–25 percent depending on audience warmth.
4. **Conversion and revenue per campaign** — the only number that ultimately matters. Tag links properly so sales trace back to specific messages.

Review monthly, kill weak formats, scale winners, and test one variable at a time — timing, wording, or offer — so you always know what caused change.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is bulk SMS still effective in the WhatsApp era?

Yes, and the reasons are complementary rather than competing. WhatsApp excels at rich conversation, catalogs, and community; SMS wins on universality, urgency, and reliability across all devices and networks. Businesses coordinating both report better overall reach than those relying on either alone. The strongest systems use each for what it uniquely delivers.

### How often should I text my customers?

For promotional messages, once or twice a month is the ceiling for most businesses. Transactional messages — receipts, reminders, updates — are welcome whenever genuinely useful because they serve the customer's interest. The eternal test: if YOU would find the message annoying receiving it, so will they. Send accordingly.

### Can small businesses automate SMS affordably?

Absolutely. Entry-level tools connect simple triggers like booking confirmations for modest monthly fees, and costs scale gradually with volume. The smarter investment question is not tool price but whether flows are designed correctly — sequence, timing, and wording — because a poorly designed automation simply sends bad messages faster. Design first; automate second.

### What is the biggest mistake businesses make with SMS?

Treating it as a megaphone instead of a relationship. The businesses winning with SMS in Nigeria send fewer, better, more respectful messages than their competitors — and their customers reward that restraint with attention, trust, and repeat purchases.
