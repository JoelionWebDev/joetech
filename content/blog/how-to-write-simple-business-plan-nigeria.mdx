Starting a business in Nigeria without a plan is like driving from Lagos to Abuja without knowing the route or how much fuel you need. You might get there, but you will waste money and time along the way, and you may break down in the middle of nowhere.

A business plan need not be a thick document. For a small business, five to eight pages is enough to answer the basic questions: what are you selling, who will buy it, what will it cost, what will you charge, and how will you make a profit?

## Why You Need a Business Plan

Many small business owners in Nigeria skip the plan and go straight to buying goods or renting a shop. Three months later, the money is finished, sales are slow, and they cannot explain what went wrong. A plan prevents most of that.

First, a plan forces you to think before you spend. When you write down your costs, you discover the real amount you need: many people budget ₦500,000 for a business that actually needs ₦1,200,000, and they run out of cash before it starts earning.

Second, a plan helps you get money: banks, microfinance banks, cooperatives, and grant programmes all ask for one, and a clear, honest plan shows you are serious.

Third, a plan gives you something to measure against. After three months, compare your real sales with what you planned: if you planned ₦300,000 a month and you are selling ₦120,000, you know whether the problem is pricing, location, or marketing.

A good plan also keeps you focused, so you build one thing well instead of chasing every opportunity.

## What a Simple Business Plan Contains

A simple plan has seven parts. Short sentences and honest numbers are better than long paragraphs.

1. Executive summary: a short summary of the whole plan.
2. Business description: what your business does and why it will work.
3. Market research: who your customers are and who your competitors are.
4. Products and services: what you sell, and your prices.
5. Marketing plan: how customers will find and buy from you.
6. Operations plan: how the business runs day to day.
7. Financial plan: your costs, your prices, and your break-even point.

Write the executive summary last, even though it appears first: it is much easier to summarise a plan after you have written the rest of it.

## Step 1: Describe Your Business Clearly

Write your business name, what you sell, where you operate, and who you serve, and be specific. "I sell food" is too vague; "I run a lunch delivery service for office workers in Lekki Phase 1, selling rice, swallow, and soups from Monday to Friday" is a real business description.

Answer these questions in this section:

- What problem does your business solve? For example, office workers in Lekki need affordable lunch delivered quickly during a short break.
- Who exactly is your customer? Age, location, income level, and habits matter: a customer who earns ₦150,000 a month spends differently from one who earns ₦800,000.
- What makes you different? Cheaper food, faster delivery, better quality, or an area nobody else covers.
- What is your business structure? Most small businesses start as sole proprietorships and register a business name with the Corporate Affairs Commission, which builds trust with customers and banks.

Also state your mission in one sentence, such as: "To provide clean, affordable lunch to busy office workers in Lekki within 30 minutes of ordering."

## Step 2: Research Your Market Honestly

Market research sounds like big-company work, but for a small business it simply means finding out whether people will pay for what you want to sell.

Start by counting your potential customers. If you want to open a salon in Surulere, walk around the area, count the existing salons, sit nearby for an hour and count how many customers enter, and ask people what they pay and what they complain about. That is real research, and it costs nothing.

Talk to at least 20 potential customers before you spend money. Ask them:

- What do you currently buy, and how much do you pay?
- What frustrates you about the current options?
- Would you buy from a new business, and what would make you switch?
- How often would you buy, weekly or monthly?

Write down what they say. If most people say your price is too high, you have learned something cheap before spending your capital.

Also study your competitors. List three to five businesses doing something similar and note their prices, strengths, and weaknesses. Perhaps the most popular food vendor in your area has great food but slow delivery: that gap is your opportunity. Perhaps the cheapest tailor uses poor fabric: yours is better quality at a slightly higher price.

If five shops on your street sell the same Ankara fabric at the same price, opening a sixth identical shop is not a plan. Find your angle: better designs, faster delivery, or payment in installments.

## Step 3: Define Your Products, Services, and Prices

List everything you will sell, with the price of each item and what it costs you to provide it. Many Nigerian small businesses make their biggest mistake here: they set prices by copying others or by guessing, without knowing their own costs.

Use a simple table. For a food business, it might look like this:

- Jollof rice with chicken: sells for ₦3,500, costs ₦2,100 to make, profit ₦1,400 per plate.
- Fried rice with turkey: sells for ₦4,000, costs ₦2,400 to make, profit ₦1,600 per plate.
- Small chops pack: sells for ₦2,500, costs ₦1,500 to make, profit ₦1,000 per pack.

Your price must cover three things: the direct cost of the product, your running costs like rent and transport, and your profit. If your price only covers the direct cost, you will work hard and still lose money when rent arrives.

Test your prices: start with one that gives you a healthy margin, then watch how customers respond. It is easier to reduce a price later than to increase it once customers are used to a low one.

## Step 4: Write Your Marketing Plan

A good product that nobody knows about will not sell. Your marketing plan explains how customers will hear about you and why they will choose you.

Start with your cheapest channels. For most Nigerian small businesses, these work best:

- WhatsApp: your status, broadcast lists, and groups. Post your products daily, reply quickly, and use WhatsApp Business for a catalogue, an automatic greeting, and quick replies.
- Instagram and Facebook: post clear photos and short videos of your products, your work, and happy customers. People buy what they can see.
- Google Business Profile: if customers come to a physical location, a free Google profile helps people in your area find you when they search. Add your address, phone number, opening hours, and photos.
- Referrals: reward customers who bring new customers. A simple offer, such as a 10 percent discount on the next purchase for every referral, can bring steady growth.
- Local presence: flyers in your estate, a signboard, and partnerships with nearby businesses, such as a laundry service partnering with a hotel.

Set a small monthly marketing budget and spend it consistently: ₦20,000 a month, every month, beats ₦200,000 spent once and forgotten.

## Step 5: Plan Your Daily Operations

The operations section explains how the business runs from morning to night. Lenders read it to see whether you have thought about the practical details.

Cover these points:

- Location: where will you operate, and why there? A shop in a busy market has high rent but high foot traffic; a home-based business has low rent but needs stronger online marketing. Write down your rent.
- Suppliers: where will you buy your materials, and at what price? List two or three suppliers for your most important items, so one supplier failing does not stop your business.
- Equipment: what do you need to start, and what does each item cost? A POS machine, a freezer, a generator, a sewing machine, or a laptop: list each one with its price.
- Staff: will you work alone or hire help? If you hire, state the role, the salary, and the hours. A sales assistant in Lagos typically earns between ₦60,000 and ₦120,000 a month.
- Daily routine: what time do you open, when do you buy stock, and when do you deliver? A clear routine prevents wasted hours and missed orders.
- Delivery: how will products reach customers: a dispatch rider, a logistics company, or yourself? Delivery in Lagos costs between ₦1,500 and ₦5,000 depending on distance, so decide whether the customer pays or you include it in your price.

Think about what can go wrong and write down your backup plan: a generator if power fails, a second supplier if prices rise.

## Step 6: Build Your Financial Plan

This is the most important part of your plan, and the maths is simple: you only need to get five numbers right.

### Startup costs

List everything you must pay for before your first sale. For example, a small fashion retail business might need:

- Business name registration: ₦25,000
- Shop rent for one year in a decent area: ₦600,000
- Initial stock: ₦800,000
- Shelves, racks, and fittings: ₦150,000
- POS machine and signage: ₦80,000
- Opening marketing: ₦50,000
- Total startup cost: ₦1,705,000

List every item, and add a 10 percent cushion for unexpected costs, because there are always unexpected costs.

### Monthly running costs

List what you pay every month, whether you sell or not:

- Rent divided by 12 months
- Salaries
- Electricity and generator fuel
- Internet and data
- Transport and delivery
- Marketing
- Bank charges and POS charges

Suppose your monthly running costs total ₦350,000. Before you make any profit, your business must earn ₦350,000 above the cost of goods every month, just to survive.

### Sales forecast

Estimate how much you will sell each month for the first year, and be realistic, not hopeful. Twenty plates a day at ₦3,500 is ₦70,000 a day, but will you really sell 20 plates every day in your first month? Probably not. Start with a modest number, such as 10 plates a day, and grow it as your customer base grows.

### Break-even point

Your break-even point is the level of sales where your income exactly covers all your costs: below it you lose money, above it you make a profit. If your monthly running costs are ₦350,000 and your average profit per sale is ₦1,400, you need 250 sales a month to break even, about 12 a day.

### Cash flow

Cash flow is the money coming in and going out each month. A business can be profitable on paper and still fail because cash arrives late, so keep one month of running costs in reserve and limit credit sales: many Nigerian small businesses die from giving too much credit to customers who never pay.

## Step 7: Funding Options in Nigeria

Once you know how much you need, decide where the money will come from.

Personal savings remain the most common source. If you have saved ₦500,000 and your plan needs ₦1,200,000, you have a gap of ₦700,000 to fill.

Family and friends can help, but put the agreement in writing: the amount, whether it is a loan or an investment, and when it will be repaid. Money has ended many family relationships because the terms were never clear.

Cooperative societies and ajo or esusu contributions are a traditional way to raise capital: regular contributions build discipline, and the lump sum can fund your startup costs without interest.

Microfinance banks lend to small businesses with simpler requirements than commercial banks, but interest rates are higher, so borrow only what you need and only if your plan shows you can repay from profit.

The Bank of Industry and the Lagos State Employment Trust Fund run loan and grant programmes for small businesses. They require a business plan, a registered business, and sometimes a guarantor, so read their requirements carefully and apply with a complete plan.

Grants from NGOs and government programmes exist, but they are competitive. A clear plan, a registered business, and evidence of sales give you an advantage. Never pay anyone who promises to secure a grant for you for a fee.

Whichever source you choose, never borrow more than your plan shows you can repay: a loan your sales cannot service will kill a good business faster than no loan at all.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

The most common mistake is writing a plan full of copied figures that do not match reality. If you copy a sample plan from the internet, the numbers will not fit your market, your rent, or your prices, so use samples as a guide but put in your own figures.

Another mistake is ignoring competitors. Saying "I have no competitors" is never true: if nobody else sells what you sell, it may mean there is no demand, or that customers solve the problem differently.

Finally, do not write the plan and forget it. Review it every three months, update your figures with real sales, and adjust your prices when costs change.

## A One-Page Business Plan Example

If the full plan feels heavy, start with this one-page version and expand it later.

- Business name and what you sell: Ada's Kitchen, lunch delivery for office workers in Ikeja.
- Customers: office workers aged 25 to 45, earning ₦200,000 to ₦600,000 a month, who need lunch before 1 pm.
- Problem solved: workers waste lunch breaks searching for food.
- Products and prices: jollof rice with chicken ₦3,500; fried rice with turkey ₦4,000; small chops ₦2,500.
- Monthly running costs: ₦380,000, including ingredients, rider, fuel, and data.
- Monthly sales target: 600 plates, about ₦2,100,000 in revenue, roughly ₦840,000 gross profit.
- Marketing: WhatsApp status daily, Instagram three times a week, flyers in five office buildings, 10 percent referral discount.
- Funding needed: ₦1,000,000, from ₦600,000 personal savings and a ₦400,000 cooperative loan.
- Goal for year one: 40 regular weekly customers and a second rider by month nine.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long should a small business plan be in Nigeria?

Five to eight pages is enough for most small businesses, and a full plan of up to ten pages is fine if you need a bank loan. What matters is honest numbers and clear thinking, not length.

### Do I need a business plan if I am funding the business myself?

Yes. Your own money deserves the same care as a bank's money: a plan protects your savings by showing the real cost before you spend, and it gives you targets to measure against.

### How much does it cost to register a business name with the CAC?

Business name registration with the Corporate Affairs Commission typically costs under ₦30,000, including the name reservation fee, depending on the current charges. Limited liability company registration costs more, so check the CAC portal for the current fees before you budget.

### Can I write a business plan without hiring a consultant?

Yes. Most small business plans are best written by the owner, because you know your market, your costs, and your customers better than any consultant. Fill in your own figures and ask a trusted friend or mentor to review it.

### How often should I update my business plan?

Review it every three months and update it fully once a year. Update it immediately if something major changes, such as a big price increase from suppliers, a new competitor, or a new product line.

## What to Do Next

1. Open a blank document and fill in the one-page plan above with your own business idea, your real prices, and your real costs. Do not copy the example figures; use yours.
2. Talk to 20 potential customers this week. Ask what they pay now, what frustrates them, and whether they would buy from you, then adjust your plan based on what you learn.
3. Register your business name with the Corporate Affairs Commission and open a separate bank account for the business, so business money and personal money never mix. Then review your plan once a month for the first year and update your figures with real sales.
