Shoes and bags are things Nigerians buy all year round. A woman needs a handbag for work, a sling bag for owambes, and a tote for church. A man needs loafers for the office, sneakers for weekends, and sandals for home. Students need affordable sneakers, and brides need heels that match their aso ebi. That constant, everyday demand is what makes shoes and bags one of the most reliable products to sell online in Nigeria.

This guide shows you how to start and grow an online shoes and bags business — from choosing your niche and finding suppliers, to pricing honestly, photographing your stock, setting up WhatsApp and Instagram, taking payments safely, and delivering to customers across the country.

## Why Shoes and Bags Sell So Well Online

Shoes and bags are repeat purchases. A customer who buys a handbag today will come back for another one in two months, or for a pair of heels for a wedding next month. That repeat buying is what makes this business grow faster than many other online businesses.

The margins are attractive when you buy well. A pair of sneakers you buy for ₦25,000 can sell for ₦40,000 to ₦50,000. A handbag that costs ₦18,000 can sell for ₦30,000 to ₦35,000. But those margins only exist if you buy quality stock, price honestly, and deliver exactly what you advertised. One pair of shoes that falls apart in two weeks, or a bag whose zip breaks on the first day, can destroy a reputation you spent a year building.

## Step 1: Decide What Shoes and Bags You Will Sell

Do not try to sell everything. A vendor who stocks men's shoes, women's heels, sneakers, sandals, handbags, sling bags, tote bags, backpacks and wallets all at once will tie up all her money in stock that moves slowly. Start narrow and expand as you learn what your customers actually buy.

Here are the main lanes in the Nigerian shoes and bags market:

- **Women's handbags:** The most popular lane. Tote bags, sling bags, shoulder bags and clutches, from affordable everyday bags to luxury-inspired designs. Prices in Lagos typically run from ₦15,000 for a simple everyday bag to ₦80,000 or more for a premium design.
- **Sneakers:** Very popular with young people, students and professionals who want comfort. Prices run from ₦25,000 for affordable brands to ₦120,000 or more for premium designs.
- **Men's corporate shoes:** Loafers, Oxfords and brogues for office workers and grooms. Prices run from ₦30,000 to ₦90,000.
- **Women's heels and sandals:** Block heels, stilettos, mules and sandals for parties, weddings and church. Prices run from ₦18,000 to ₦65,000.
- **Kids' shoes:** School shoes, sneakers and sandals for children. A steady lane, because children outgrow shoes quickly and parents buy often.
- **Luxury-inspired bags and shoes:** Higher-end designs for customers who want a premium look. These sell from ₦60,000 to ₦250,000 and carry high margins, but they also carry high risk if the quality does not match the price.

Choose based on three things: how much capital you have, who your customers are, and what you know. If your audience is students and young professionals in Yaba and Akoka, sneakers and affordable bags will move fastest. If your audience is working professionals and brides in Lekki and Victoria Island, corporate shoes, heels and premium bags will sell better.

## Step 2: Find Reliable Suppliers

Your supplier decides your quality, your price and your reputation. Buy badly once, and the complaints will follow you for months. Never buy a large quantity from a supplier you have not tested.

### Where shoe and bag vendors source stock

- **Balogun market, Lagos:** One of the biggest sources for shoes and bags in West Africa. You can check the stitching, test the zips, try the soles, and negotiate face to face. Many successful online vendors started here, buying a few pairs at a time.
- **Onitsha main market:** Another major source, especially for shoes and bags at wholesale prices. Worth a trip if you are based in the East or you buy in large quantities.
- **Trade Fair Complex, Lagos:** A well-known source for imported shoes and bags, with many wholesalers selling in bulk.
- **Direct importers and agents:** Many established vendors import directly from China, Turkey and Dubai through agents. The prices are lower, but you need to factor in shipping, customs and the risk of a supplier who sends different stock from the sample. Only go this route after you have tested the supplier with a small order.

### How to test a supplier

Order a small quantity first — two or three pairs or bags. Check the stitching: is it neat and strong, or loose and uneven? Test the zips and clasps: do they open and close smoothly? Check the soles: are they well glued, or already peeling?

Build relationships with two or three good suppliers rather than depending on one. If one supplier runs out of a popular design, the other may have it. And always keep records of what you bought, from whom, and at what price.

## Step 3: Price Your Shoes and Bags Honestly

Pricing is where many new vendors fail. They price by copying a big vendor on Instagram, or by guessing, and then discover they are selling at a loss after transport and delivery costs.

### Work out your true cost

Your cost is not just the price you paid the supplier. Add transport to the market, packaging, delivery, data and your time. A pair of sneakers that costs ₦25,000 at the market might cost you ₦29,000 by the time it is photographed, packaged and ready to send.

Use this formula:

**Selling price = supplier cost + transport + packaging + delivery allowance + your profit**

Aim for at least 30 to 40 percent gross margin. If your total cost is ₦29,000, your price should be ₦40,000 to ₦45,000. Customers will pay for shoes that are comfortable, well made, and exactly as advertised. They will not come back to a vendor whose shoes fell apart in two weeks.

### Publish a clear price list

Publish prices for your main products and pin them in your WhatsApp Business catalogue and Instagram highlights. Be specific: name the product, the sizes available, the colours and the price. For example:

- Women's tote bag, black and brown — ₦28,000
- Men's loafers, sizes 41–45 — ₦45,000
- Sneakers, sizes 38–44 — ₦42,000
- Block heels, sizes 37–42 — ₦25,000
- Sling bag with chain strap — ₦22,000

State the sizes you have in stock, and update the list when a size sells out. Nothing frustrates a customer more than ordering a size 42 and being told after payment that it is out of stock. And never advertise a price you cannot honour — a customer who sees ₦30,000 on your page and is told ₦45,000 in the chat will leave and tell her friends.

## Step 4: Set Up Your Online Shopfront

Your customers will find you on Instagram, TikTok and WhatsApp. Each one has a job to do, and you need all three working together.

### Instagram

Your Instagram page is your shop window. Use a business account with a clear name, for example "Shoes and Bags by Ada — Lagos." Your bio should say what you sell, where you are based, and how to order: "Quality shoes and bags in Lagos. Nationwide delivery. Order via WhatsApp. Link below." Put your WhatsApp link in your bio so a customer can tap once and start chatting.

Your feed should be full of your actual stock. Post every new design, worn by a model or photographed neatly, with the sizes, colours and price in the caption. Post videos of the shoes being worn and the bags being opened and carried.

### TikTok

TikTok is where many Nigerian shoe and bag vendors find new customers today. Short videos of new arrivals, styling ideas, unboxings and "what's in my bag" clips go viral easily. You do not need expensive equipment — a phone, good light and a clean background are enough. Post at least three times a week and use sounds that are trending in Nigeria.

### WhatsApp Business

Download WhatsApp Business and set up a business profile with your shop name, area, opening hours and a clear profile photo. Your catalogue is your price list: add every shoe and bag with a good photo, the exact name, the sizes, the colours, the price and a short description. "Men's loafers, brown leather, sizes 41–45, ₦45,000." Set up quick replies for the questions you answer every day: "/prices" sends your price list, "/sizes" sends your available sizes, "/delivery" sends your delivery areas and fees, "/payment" sends your account details.

## Step 5: Photograph Your Shoes and Bags Properly

Shoes and bags are visual products. Nobody can touch your stock from a photo, so the photo and video have to do all the selling. Good content will bring you orders; bad content will quietly kill your business.

Photograph in daylight, near a window, against a clean background. Photograph shoes from several angles: the front, the side, the back, and the sole. Photograph bags open and closed, so customers can see the inside, the compartments and the lining. Show close-ups of the stitching, the zips, the straps and the buckles.

Always show the real product. Never use another vendor's photos, and never post a photo of a premium leather bag when you are selling a synthetic one. The customer will open the package, see the difference, and demand a refund — or worse, post about it. Honest photos build trust, and trust is what brings repeat customers.

Take a short video of every new design you stock. A 20-second clip of the shoes being worn and walked in, or the bag being carried and opened, is worth more than ten still photos. Post these videos on your status, your feed and TikTok on the same day the stock arrives.

## Step 6: Take Orders and Payments Safely

Orders will come from Instagram DMs, TikTok comments, WhatsApp chats, status replies and referrals. You need one simple order process that works every time.

Ask every customer for the same details: name, the product they want, the size and colour, the delivery address with a landmark, and their phone number. For shoes, always confirm the size twice — a wrong size is the most common reason for returns in this business. Confirm the order back to them in one message: "Confirmed: men's loafers, brown, size 43, ₦45,000 + delivery to Ikeja (₦2,500) = ₦47,500. Please send payment to confirm your order."

### Payment before dispatch

For new customers, collect full payment before you send the shoes or bag. This is standard in the Nigerian online fashion business. Accept transfers to your bank account, and consider a Moniepoint or Opay account for fast confirmation alerts. Always confirm when you have seen the money: "Payment received, thank you! Your order will be dispatched today."

Be very careful with payment scams. Common tricks include fake transfer alerts and screenshots of payments that never arrived. Check your own account balance before dispatching anything — never trust a screenshot alone.

## Step 7: Deliver Shoes and Bags Safely Across Nigeria

Shoes and bags are sturdy, but they still need good packaging. A crushed shoe box or a scratched bag will disappoint a customer who paid good money.

Pack shoes in their original box if you have it, or wrap each pair in tissue and place them in a sturdy box. Stuff the toes with tissue so the shoes keep their shape in transit.

For deliveries within Lagos, Abuja or Port Harcourt, use a dispatch rider you trust. Agree the fee with the customer before you dispatch, and add it to the total. A typical short delivery within Lekki might cost ₦2,000 to ₦3,500. For deliveries outside your city, use a trusted logistics company: GIG Logistics, DHL, or a reliable inter-state bus service for customers who prefer it. Always share the tracking details with the customer and follow up until the package arrives.

Tell the customer the delivery fee upfront, before they pay. Nothing annoys a customer more than a surprise delivery charge after payment.

### Exchanges and returns

Have a clear exchange policy and state it before the customer pays. A fair policy is: exchange within 3 days if the size is wrong and the item is unworn, with the customer paying the return delivery. State this in your catalogue and your chats. A clear policy prevents arguments, and customers trust vendors who are upfront about it.

## Step 8: Market Your Shoes and Bags Every Day

You do not need paid adverts to start. You need consistency and visibility in the places your customers already are.

Post on your WhatsApp Status at least once a day: a new design, a restock announcement, a customer wearing your shoes, or a short video of a new arrival. The best times are the morning, around 7 a.m. to 8 a.m., and the evening, when people are scrolling after work.

On Instagram, post a Reel at least three times a week. Styling videos, unboxings, and "how to style this bag" clips perform very well. Use stories for polls and questions: "Loafers or sneakers for this weekend?" Customers love to vote, and every vote pushes your page to more people.

Plan your stock around the calendar. Stock heavily before December, before wedding season, and before Sallah and Easter. Announce your new arrivals early, so customers can plan their purchases with you instead of buying from someone else.

## Step 9: Keep Customers Coming Back

Getting an order is hard. Keeping a customer is cheaper and more profitable. A customer who buys shoes and bags for every season is worth more than 20 one-time buyers.

After delivery, send a short follow-up message: "Hello Ada, hope you love the loafers. Thank you for shopping with us!" That single message makes people feel valued. If there was a problem, they will tell you privately instead of posting about it.

Keep a simple record of your customers: their names, their shoe sizes, what they bought, their preferred colours and styles, and when they last ordered. Before December and wedding season, send a friendly message to past customers: "Hello! New designs just arrived in your size. Would you like me to send you photos?"

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Buying a large quantity from an untested supplier.** Test with two or three pairs first, always.
- **Advertising premium leather and delivering synthetic.** Your reputation will not survive it.
- **Dispatching before confirming payment in your own account.** Never trust a screenshot alone.
- **No price list, or no sizes listed.** Customers who have to ask for every detail will buy from a vendor who shows them.
- **Poor photos and videos.** Dark, blurry photos of shoes and bags will kill your sales.
- **No records.** If you do not write down what you sold and what you spent, you will not know whether you are making money.
- **Ignoring customer messages.** Fashion customers ask many questions before they buy. Slow replies send them to another vendor.

## How Much Can You Realistically Make?

It depends on your capital, your niche and your consistency, but here is a realistic example. If you sell 40 items a month at an average price of ₦35,000, that is ₦1,400,000 in monthly sales. If your average cost per item is ₦22,000, your gross profit is ₦13,000 per item, or ₦520,000 a month before transport, delivery, data and packaging costs.

## Your First Month Action Plan

Do not wait for everything to be perfect. Start small and start this month.

1. **Week 1:** Choose your niche. Visit Balogun or your nearest market and test two or three suppliers with small purchases.
2. **Week 2:** Set up Instagram, TikTok and WhatsApp Business. Photograph and film your first stock in daylight.
3. **Week 3:** Post daily on your status and at least three Reels. Send your price list to 30 contacts personally.
4. **Week 4:** Take your first paid orders. Confirm payment in your account before dispatching. Follow up with every customer.

If you sell only ten items in your first month, that is a win. You have proof that people will pay for your shoes and bags. Build from there, one happy customer at a time.

Shoes and bags are a business built on trust and good taste. Source honestly, price fairly, show the real product, confirm payment before dispatch, and deliver what you promised. Do that every week, and the orders will come — from your phone to doorsteps across Nigeria, one stylish step at a time.
