When a new customer finds your Instagram page, they make a decision in seconds. They look at your bio, they glance at your last few posts, and then they tap your Highlights. Those small circles under your bio are often the first place a stranger goes to answer their real questions: what do you sell, how much does it cost, do you deliver to my area, and can I trust you? If your Highlights are empty, messy or out of date, that customer will leave without messaging you. If they are clear, organised and full of proof, the same customer will tap your WhatsApp link and place an order.

Instagram Stories disappear after 24 hours, but Highlights save the best Stories on your profile permanently, until you remove them. That makes them the most valuable free shop window a Nigerian small business has on Instagram. A well-organised set of Highlights works like a shop assistant who never sleeps: it shows your products, states your prices, explains delivery, shares customer reviews, and tells people exactly how to order. This guide explains which Highlights every small business needs, how to organise them, and how to keep them fresh so they keep selling for you.

## Start With the Highlights Every Customer Looks For

You do not need twenty Highlights. You need a small set that answers the questions every buyer asks. Start with five essential ones: Products, Prices, Reviews, Delivery, and How to Order. Products shows what you sell, in clear photos and short videos. Prices states what things cost, so customers do not have to message you just to ask. Reviews shares screenshots of happy customers and photos of your products in use. Delivery explains where you deliver, how long it takes, and what it costs. How to Order gives the exact steps: send a message on WhatsApp, confirm your size or flavour, pay, and receive your order.

Give each Highlight a short, clear name that a stranger can understand in one second. "Dresses", "Prices", "Reviews", "Delivery" and "Order Here" are better than clever names that nobody understands. A customer in a hurry will tap "Prices" before they tap something vague. Use a simple cover image for each Highlight, ideally in your brand colours, with a small icon or a single word on it. You can create these covers with a free design app on your phone in under an hour, and they make your profile look professional at a glance. Keep the covers consistent, so your profile looks like a proper shop, not a random collection of Stories.

Order your Highlights by importance, with the most useful ones first. Put your products and prices on the left, where the eye lands first, and put less urgent Highlights, such as behind-the-scenes clips, further to the right. Remember that a new visitor sees only the first few circles without scrolling, so the first three or four Highlights do most of the work. Check your own profile from a stranger's point of view: if you knew nothing about this business, would these Highlights tell you what is for sale, what it costs, and how to buy it? If the answer is no, rearrange them until it is yes.

## Showcase Your Products Like a Shop Window

Your product Highlights are your catalogue, and they should be organised the way a customer thinks, not the way your stockroom is organised. If you sell fashion, create separate Highlights for dresses, tops, trousers, or for different collections, such as a new arrivals Highlight and a sale Highlight. If you sell food, create Highlights for cakes, small chops, and party packages. If you sell hair, create Highlights for different lengths, styles or colours. A customer looking for a specific item should be able to find it in one tap, without scrolling through dozens of unrelated Stories.

Each product Story inside a Highlight should show the product clearly, state the price, and list the sizes, colours or options available. A photo alone is not enough. Add text on the Story itself, in large readable letters, with the price and the key details. Use a short video where it helps: a dress being worn, a bag being opened, a cream being applied, or a cake being cut. Show the product from more than one angle, and show it in use by a real person where you can. Nigerian customers want to see what they are actually buying, and a product shown in real life is far more convincing than a product on a plain background.

Keep your product Highlights up to date, because an out-of-date catalogue loses sales. When a product sells out, remove its Story from the Highlight, or add a clear "Sold Out" label, so customers do not message you about something you no longer have. This is especially important during busy periods such as December, when stock moves fast and customers are shopping for Christmas, or during owambe season, when a popular aso-ebi style can sell out in a day. A customer who messages you about a sold-out item and waits hours for a reply may buy from another vendor in the meantime. Regular tidying also keeps your page looking alive and well managed, which matters to a first-time buyer who is deciding whether to trust you with their money. Set aside ten minutes every Sunday evening to check your Highlights, remove what is gone, and add what is new, so Monday morning starts with an accurate shop window. When you get new stock, add it to the right Highlight on the same day, and announce it in your daily Stories. When a price changes, update the Highlight immediately. A customer who orders at an old price from your Highlight and is then told a different price on WhatsApp will feel misled, even if the change was honest. Your Highlights are a promise, so keep them accurate.

## Use Reviews and Delivery Highlights to Build Trust

Many Nigerian customers are nervous about buying from a seller they found online, and your Reviews Highlight is where you calm that fear. Save every good message a customer sends you, every photo of a customer wearing or using your product, and every delivery confirmation, and add the best ones to your Reviews Highlight. Real reviews from real people, with first names and locations where the customer agrees, do more to convince a new buyer than anything you can say about your own products. A short video of a happy customer talking about their order is even better, because it is hard to fake.

Ask for reviews in a natural way. After a customer receives their order, send a short message asking how they liked it, and whether they would mind if you shared their feedback on your page. Most happy customers are glad to help, especially if you make it easy by asking a simple question. Share the review quickly, while the purchase is still fresh, and thank the customer by name. Do not invent reviews, and do not use reviews from another business. Customers talk to each other, and a fake review that is discovered will damage your reputation far more than having fewer reviews.

Your Delivery Highlight should answer every delivery question before it is asked. State which areas you deliver to, such as Lagos, Abuja, Port Harcourt and nationwide delivery by dispatch or courier, how long delivery takes to each area, what the delivery fee is, and whether customers can pick up from your location. Show photos of parcels being packed, handed to a dispatch rider, and delivered. If you offer free delivery above a certain amount, say so clearly, with the exact amount and the areas it applies to. If delivery to a particular area takes longer, say so honestly. Customers would rather know the truth upfront than be disappointed later, and a clear Delivery Highlight saves you from answering the same questions twenty times a day on WhatsApp.

## Make Ordering Obvious With a How to Order Highlight

Even a customer who loves your product and trusts your reviews will not buy if they do not know what to do next. Your How to Order Highlight should give the exact steps, in order, in plain language. Step one is usually to send a WhatsApp message or a direct message with the product name or a screenshot. Step two is to confirm the size, colour, flavour or quantity. Step three is payment, with your payment options stated clearly. Step four is delivery, with the time and the fee confirmed. Use simple numbered Stories, one step per Story, so a customer can follow along without getting confused.

Include your payment details in a way that is easy to use. Show your bank name, account name and account number as plain text that can be read clearly, or explain that you will send a payment link on WhatsApp. State whether you accept bank transfer, card payment, or cash on delivery, and be honest about any deposit you require before you start work, especially for custom orders such as cakes, aso-ebi or tailored clothes. If you require a deposit, state the amount or percentage clearly in the Highlight, so there are no arguments later. Add a Story that shows what happens after payment: you confirm the order, you pack it, you send it with a dispatch rider, and the customer receives it.

Link your How to Order Highlight to your daily Stories. When you post a product in your Stories, remind viewers to check the How to Order Highlight, or add a link sticker that takes them straight to WhatsApp. Keep the Highlight short, no more than five or six Stories, so a customer can read it in under a minute. Test it yourself: ask a friend who has never bought from you to read the Highlight and tell you whether they could place an order without asking you a single question. If they cannot, add the missing step.

## Keep Your Highlights Fresh and Use Them in Your Daily Stories

Highlights are not a one-time job. A profile with Highlights from last year, showing products you no longer sell at prices you no longer charge, is worse than a profile with no Highlights at all, because it misleads customers. Set a simple routine: once a week, open each Highlight and remove anything that is sold out, out of date, or no longer true. Once a month, review the covers, the names and the order, and tidy anything that looks messy. When you launch a new product, add it to the right Highlight the same day. When you change a price or a delivery area, update the Highlight before you announce it anywhere else.

Use your daily Stories to send people to your Highlights. When you post a new product, say "Full price list in our Prices Highlight" or "See more styles in our Dresses Highlight." A short mention like this costs you nothing, yet it guides a warm viewer to the exact place where they can compare options, check the price, and decide to buy without waiting for your reply. Over time, this habit also trains your audience to use your Highlights as a reference, which means fewer repeated questions in your inbox and faster decisions from customers who already have the information they need. When a customer asks a question you have already answered in a Highlight, reply politely and point them to it. Over time, your followers will learn that your Highlights hold the answers, and they will check them before messaging you, which saves you time and helps them buy faster. You can also use your Highlights to run simple promotions: a Sale Highlight for discounted items, a New In Highlight for the latest stock, or a festive Highlight for Christmas, Eid or Valentine's Day, which you can archive when the season ends.

Watch which Highlights get the most taps. Instagram shows you how many people viewed each Story inside a Highlight, so you can see which ones your visitors care about most. If your Reviews Highlight gets far more views than your Products Highlight, your customers need reassurance before they buy, and you should add more reviews. If your Delivery Highlight is the most viewed, delivery is the main thing stopping people from ordering, and you should make your delivery terms clearer and more attractive. Let the numbers guide you, and keep improving the Highlights that do the most work.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How many Instagram Highlights should a small business have?**

Five to eight Highlights is a good number for most small businesses. Fewer than three leaves too many questions unanswered, and more than ten makes your profile look cluttered. Start with Products, Prices, Reviews, Delivery and How to Order, then add one or two more for your best-selling categories.

**What should I put in my Instagram Highlights if I am just starting out?**

Start with what you have. Show your first products with prices, explain how to order and how delivery works, and add every review you receive, even if it is only one or two at first. Your Highlights will grow with your business, and customers respect a new business that is honest and organised.

**How do I create a Highlight cover on my phone?**

Use a free design app to make a square image in your brand colours, add a simple icon or one word, such as Prices or Reviews, and save it to your phone. Then open your Highlight, tap edit, and choose that image as the cover. Keep all your covers in the same style so your profile looks neat.

**Do Highlights help me get more sales, or are they just for decoration?**

They help you sell. Highlights answer the questions that stop people from buying, show proof that other customers trust you, and give clear ordering steps, all without you having to be online. Many customers will check your Highlights before they ever send you a message.

**How often should I update my Instagram Highlights?**

Check them every week for sold-out products and old prices, and do a full review once a month. Update a Highlight immediately whenever a price, a delivery area or a product range changes, so customers always see the correct information.
