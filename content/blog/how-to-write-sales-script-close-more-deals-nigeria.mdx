Two salespeople sell the same product in Lagos. The first one calls a prospect and says, "Hello, we sell good products, please buy from us." The prospect says "I will think about it" and never calls back. The second one calls and says, "Hello Mrs. Adeyemi, I noticed your salon is growing fast. Many salons in Lekki lose customers when their chairs are uncomfortable. Can I ask what kind of chairs you use now?" Mrs. Adeyemi talks for five minutes, the salesperson listens, and by the end of the call she has agreed to a meeting. The difference is not the product. It is the script.

A sales script is a written guide for your sales conversations. It tells you what to say when you open a call, which questions to ask, how to present your offer, how to answer objections, and how to ask for the sale. It is not a speech to memorise word for word. It is a map that keeps you on track when a conversation goes in an unexpected direction.

This guide explains how to write a sales script that closes more deals. You will learn what a good script contains, how to open a conversation, how to ask questions that reveal what the customer really wants, how to present your offer, how to handle the objections every Nigerian seller hears, and how to close and follow up without being pushy.

## What a Sales Script Is and Why It Works

A sales script is a simple document that plans out your sales conversation in advance. It covers the opening, the questions you will ask, the key points about your product, the answers to common objections, and the close. It can be one page for a simple product or several pages for a complex service. The point is that you have thought about the conversation before it happens, instead of making it up on the spot.

A script works because selling is a conversation with a structure. Every successful sale follows a similar path: you get the customer's attention, you learn what they need, you show how your product solves their problem, you answer their doubts, and you ask for the order.

A script also makes your results consistent. Without one, you might sell well on a good day and badly on a tired day. With a script, your team can sell the same way every day, and a new staff member can learn to sell in days instead of months. In a small business where the owner cannot take every call, a script lets a staff member handle enquiries without losing sales.

## How to Know Your Customer Before You Write

A script written for everyone sells to no one. Before you write a single line, decide who your ideal customer is. Are they small business owners in Lagos, parents buying school supplies in Ibadan, or brides planning a wedding in Abuja? Each group has different problems, different budgets, and different words they use to describe what they want. Your script must speak their language.

Write down the three biggest problems your customer faces that your product solves. A dispatch rider in Port Harcourt worries about fuel costs and late deliveries. A boutique owner in Lekki worries about slow sales and customers who window-shop but never buy. If you cannot name your customer's problems, you cannot write a script that speaks to them.

Next, write down the three most common objections you hear. In Nigeria, they are almost always the same: "It is too expensive", "I will think about it", "I need to ask my husband or my partner", and "I already have someone who supplies me". Your script must have a ready answer for each one, because an objection you have not prepared for will stop you in your tracks.

Finally, gather the proof that your product works. That could be a testimonial from a happy customer, a before-and-after photo, or a simple story about a client who got results. Proof turns a claim into something believable, and believable claims close deals.

## How to Write the Opening of Your Script

The opening is the first thirty seconds of the conversation, and it decides whether the customer keeps talking or ends the call. A good opening is short, friendly, and focused on the customer, not on you. Start with a greeting, your name, and your business, then give the customer a reason to keep listening. For example: "Good afternoon, my name is Tunde from Joetech. I am calling because we help small businesses in Lekki get more customers through their website. Do you have two minutes?"

Never open with a long speech about your company. The customer does not care about your history yet. They care about whether this conversation will help them. A good opening mentions a problem they might have or a result they might want, then asks for permission to continue.

For WhatsApp and in-person selling, the opening is slightly different. On WhatsApp, your first message should be short and personal, not a broadcast that looks like spam. For example: "Hello Mrs. Bello, thank you for asking about our Ankara fabrics. Are you looking for something for yourself or for an event?" In person, a warm greeting and a simple question work best: "Good morning, welcome. What are you looking for today?"

Write two or three different openings for different situations: one for a cold call, one for a WhatsApp enquiry, and one for a walk-in customer. Practise each one until it sounds natural. An opening that sounds rehearsed will put the customer off.

## How to Ask Questions That Reveal What the Customer Wants

Questions are the heart of a good sales script, because they tell you what the customer actually needs. Most sellers ask too few questions and make too many assumptions. They assume the customer wants the cheapest option, when the customer really wants the fastest delivery. They assume the customer wants a big package, when the customer really wants a small trial first. Questions remove those guesses.

Use open questions that cannot be answered with a simple yes or no. Instead of "Do you need a website?", ask "What is the biggest problem your business faces online right now?" Instead of "Do you like this dress?", ask "What kind of event are you buying this dress for?" Open questions get the customer talking, and when the customer talks, they tell you exactly how to sell to them.

Listen to the answers and take notes. If the customer says they lost three clients last month because they could not deliver on time, that is the problem your product must solve. Repeat their words back to them: "So the main issue is late delivery, is that right?" That simple repetition shows you were listening.

Plan five or six key questions in your script and write them in order. Start with easy questions that build comfort, then move to deeper questions about problems and budgets. For example: "How long have you been in business?", "What is working well for you right now?", "What is the one thing you would change if you could?", "What have you tried before?" By the time you have asked these, you will know exactly what to offer.

## How to Present Your Offer So It Sells

Once you understand the customer's problem, present your offer as the solution to that problem. Do not list features. List benefits. A feature is what your product does. A benefit is what it does for the customer. "This phone has a 5,000mAh battery" is a feature. "This phone will last you a full day of calls, even when NEPA takes light" is a benefit. Nigerian customers buy benefits, not features.

Connect each benefit to something the customer already told you. If Mrs. Adeyemi said her salon loses customers because the chairs are uncomfortable, say: "These chairs have thick padding, so your customers can sit comfortably for two hours while their hair is done. That means they will come back, and they will tell their friends." That sentence sells, because it answers the problem she described in her own words.

Keep your presentation short. Pick the two or three benefits that matter most to this customer and say them clearly. A long presentation bores people and gives them time to think of reasons to say no. A short, focused presentation keeps their attention and leads naturally to the close.

Use simple, honest language and avoid exaggeration. Do not say your product is "the best in Nigeria" or that it "never fails". Customers have heard those claims before and they do not believe them. Instead, give a specific fact or a real example: "Our last twenty customers in Ikeja all received their orders within 48 hours."

## How to Handle Objections Without Arguing

An objection is not a rejection. It is a request for more information, and it usually means the customer is interested but not yet convinced. The most common objection in Nigeria is price: "It is too expensive." Never argue with that. Instead, agree first, then explain the value. Say: "I understand, ₦80,000 is a lot of money. Let me show you what it includes, and why our customers find it worth it." Then break the price down: the materials, the labour, the delivery, and the guarantee.

The second common objection is "I will think about it." That usually means the customer is unsure, or they want to compare prices. Ask a gentle question: "That is fine. May I ask what you would like to think about, so I can give you the information you need?" That question often reveals the real concern, such as a fear about quality or a need to check with a partner.

The third common objection is "I already have a supplier." Reply with respect, not with an attack on the competitor. Say: "That is good, it means you understand the value of this service. Many of our customers also had a supplier before they switched. Would it be alright if I showed you one difference that made them change?" That response keeps the conversation open without insulting anyone.

The fourth common objection is "I need to ask my husband, my wife, or my partner." Do not push past that person. Instead, offer to help: "Of course. Would it help if I sent you a short summary you can share with them, with the price and what is included? I can also answer any questions they have." That offer makes you helpful instead of pushy, and it keeps the sale alive.

## How to Close the Sale and Follow Up

Closing is simply asking for the order, and it is the step most sellers skip. They present the product, answer questions, and then wait, hoping the customer will volunteer to buy. Customers rarely do. You must ask. A good close is direct and easy to answer: "Would you like to go ahead with the blue one, or the black one?" That question assumes the sale and asks the customer to choose, which is easier than asking "Do you want to buy?"

Another good close is the summary close: "So, to confirm, you will get the three-bedroom cleaning package, delivery on Saturday, for ₦45,000. Shall I book you in?" That summary reminds the customer of everything they agreed to, and the question at the end is easy to say yes to. If the customer says yes, stop selling at once. Take the order, confirm the payment details, and thank them.

If the customer says no, stay polite and ask one question: "Thank you. May I ask what stopped you today, so I can improve?" The answer will teach you more than any training course. Write it down.

Follow up the next day with every customer who did not buy, and with every customer who did. For the ones who did not buy, a short message is enough: "Hello, thank you for your time yesterday. The offer is still open until Friday, and I am happy to answer any questions." For the ones who did buy, confirm the delivery and ask if they are happy.

## Common Mistakes Sellers Make with Scripts

The first mistake is reading the script word for word like a robot. Customers can tell when you are reading, and it makes them feel like a number instead of a person. Learn the structure of your script, not the exact words, so you can speak naturally and adapt to each customer. The second mistake is a script that is all about you. If your script starts with your company history and lists your features, rewrite it. A good script starts with the customer's problem and stays focused on the customer.

The third mistake is having no answer for objections. If your script ends at the presentation and has no plan for "It is too expensive" or "I will think about it", you will freeze when those words come. Write an answer for every common objection and practise saying it until it feels natural. The fourth mistake is never asking for the sale. A conversation without a close is just a chat. End every sales conversation with a clear question that asks the customer to decide.

The fifth mistake is using one script for every customer. A script for a corporate client in Victoria Island should sound different from a script for a market trader in Onitsha. Adjust your language, your examples, and your prices to fit the person in front of you.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need a sales script if I sell face to face?

Yes. A script helps in person just as much as on the phone. It reminds you which questions to ask, what to say when the customer objects, and how to close. You do not need to read it in front of the customer. Learn the structure, keep a copy nearby for reference, and let it guide the conversation. Even experienced sellers use a script, because it keeps them consistent.

### How long should a sales script be?

For a simple product, one page is enough. For a service with several options, two or three pages is fine. The test is not the length but whether it covers the opening, the key questions, the presentation, the objections, and the close. If any of those parts is missing, the script is too short. If it takes more than five minutes to read aloud, it is too long.

### Will a script make me sound like a robot?

Only if you read it word for word. A good script is a guide, not a speech. Learn the flow of the conversation, understand why each question is there, and then speak in your own words. Practise with a friend or a colleague until the words feel natural. Customers respond to a confident, natural conversation, not to a perfect recital.

### How do I write a script for WhatsApp selling?

Keep it short and conversational. Your first message should greet the customer by name and ask one question. Then send short messages, one idea at a time, instead of a long block of text. Use voice notes where they help, because a voice feels more personal than text. Save your best answers as quick replies on WhatsApp Business, so you can respond fast without typing the same message twenty times a day.

### How often should I update my sales script?

Review it every month, or sooner if you notice a pattern. If three customers in a week raise the same objection, add a better answer for it. If a question never gets a useful answer, replace it. If a new product or price comes in, update the script the same day. A script that is never updated slowly stops working, because the market moves on without it.

## What to Do Next

1. Write down who your ideal customer is and the three biggest problems your product solves for them.
2. List the three most common objections you hear, and write a calm, honest answer for each one.
3. Draft your opening, your five key questions, your presentation, and your close on one page.
4. Add one piece of proof, such as a testimonial or a customer story, to your presentation.
5. Practise the script out loud with a friend until it sounds natural, not rehearsed.
6. Use the script for one week with real customers, and note where they get confused or lose interest.
7. Change the weak parts of the script, and test the new version for another week.
8. Review and update your script every month, so it keeps up with your prices, your products, and your customers.
