Perfume is one of the most forgiving businesses a Nigerian can start. The product is small, light, and easy to store. It does not spoil quickly, it does not need a large shop, and customers buy it again and again. A bottle that lasts a customer six weeks brings that customer back six weeks later, and if they loved the scent, they bring a friend. That repeat buying is why perfume sellers in Lagos, Abuja, Port Harcourt, and Onitsha build steady incomes from what looks, from the outside, like a simple side hustle. This guide explains how to start properly: what to sell, where to buy it, how much it costs, how to price it, and how to find customers without wasting money.

## Why Perfume Works as a Small Business in Nigeria

Nigerians love to smell good. Perfume is part of daily life for students, office workers, bankers, pastors, traders, and party-goers. It is also a popular gift for birthdays, weddings, and Christmas, which means demand spikes at predictable times of the year. Unlike food, perfume does not expire in a day if NEPA takes light. Unlike clothes, one size fits everybody. And unlike many products, a customer can fall in love with a scent from a single spray, which makes selling on WhatsApp and Instagram surprisingly effective.

The business also scales in stages. You can start with ₦50,000 worth of stock from your bedroom, sell to friends and colleagues, and grow into a shop, a distributor, or your own brand. Many successful perfume sellers in Nigeria started exactly that way, with a small carton of oil perfumes or a dozen bottles bought from a wholesaler, and reinvested every naira of profit until the business could pay rent. The key is to start small, learn what your customers actually want, and grow with your own money rather than a loan you cannot repay.

## Decide What Kind of Perfume Business You Want

Before you spend a kobo, decide what you will sell. There are four main paths, and each one suits a different budget and customer.

Oil perfumes, often called perfume oils, are the cheapest entry point. You buy concentrated oil in bulk, usually in 3ml, 6ml, or 12ml bottles, and sell them for ₦2,500 to ₦15,000 depending on the brand and size. Popular names include designer-inspired oils and Arabian oils. The margins are good, the bottles are small, and customers who cannot afford a ₦40,000 designer bottle can still buy a beautiful scent for ₦5,000. This is the best starting point for most beginners.

Designer and luxury perfumes are the second path. These are branded bottles such as Dior, Chanel, Armani, and Tom Ford, sold for ₦35,000 to ₦250,000 or more. The profit per bottle is high, but the capital required is high too, and customers are more careful because fake designer perfumes flood the Nigerian market. You should only sell designer perfumes if you can prove your source is genuine, because one fake bottle can destroy your reputation permanently.

Body sprays, mists, and deodorants are the third path. They sell for ₦3,000 to ₦12,000, move quickly, and suit students and young buyers. The profit per unit is smaller, but the volume is higher, and they are excellent for building a large customer list that you can later sell higher-value perfumes to.

Your own perfume brand is the fourth path, and it is the most ambitious. You create a name, design a bottle and label, and have a manufacturer fill it for you, or you blend oils yourself. This takes more capital, often ₦500,000 and above, and more work, but it gives you the highest margins and a business you truly own. Most people should start with one of the first three paths and move to their own brand only after they understand their customers.

You can also combine paths. Many sellers start with oil perfumes, add body sprays for volume, and keep a small range of designer bottles for customers who ask for them. The rule is simple: do not stock everything at once. Pick one main line, master it, and add the next line with profit from the first.

## How Much Money Do You Need to Start?

The honest answer depends on your path. With ₦50,000 to ₦100,000, you can start a small oil perfume business. That budget buys roughly 30 to 60 bottles of oil perfume at wholesale prices of ₦1,500 to ₦3,000 each, plus a few thousand naira for packaging, a WhatsApp Business setup, and delivery within your area. Sell each bottle for ₦4,000 to ₦8,000, and you can turn ₦70,000 of stock into ₦150,000 or more in sales if you choose popular scents.

With ₦150,000 to ₦300,000, you can start a stronger retail business. This buys a wider range, perhaps 80 to 120 bottles across oils, sprays, and a few designer bottles, plus proper packaging, a small display table, and a modest advertising budget of ₦20,000 to ₦30,000 for Instagram and WhatsApp promotion. At this level, you can serve walk-in customers as well as online buyers.

With ₦500,000 to ₦1,000,000, you can open a small shop or a kiosk in a busy area, stock a full range, and begin wholesale supply to smaller sellers. Rent in a busy Lagos market area can cost ₦300,000 to ₦800,000 a year depending on location, so shop rent is usually the biggest single cost. If you cannot afford a shop yet, do not force it. Selling online from home with good delivery is cheaper, and many perfume sellers earn more online than a badly located shop earns in a month.

Keep a separate record of every naira you spend and earn. Use a notebook or a simple spreadsheet. Write down the cost of each bottle, the selling price, delivery costs, and advertising spend. Many beginners think they are making profit when they are actually spending it, because they mix business money with personal money. Open a separate bank account or a separate wallet for the business, even if it is just a second account on the same bank app, and pay yourself a fixed amount rather than dipping into sales whenever you need cash.

## Where to Source Perfume in Nigeria

Your supplier decides your profit and your reputation, so choose carefully. The main sourcing options are local wholesale markets, importers, and online suppliers. In Lagos, popular wholesale areas include Trade Fair Complex, Balogun Market, and Idumota, where you can compare prices from several sellers in one day. In Onitsha, the main market has large perfume wholesalers who supply retailers across the South East. In Kano, Kantin Kwari and Sabon Gari markets serve northern buyers. In Abuja, Wuse Market and the shops around Banex Plaza are common starting points.

When you visit a market, do not buy from the first stall. Walk around, ask prices from at least five sellers, and compare the same product. Check the packaging, the seal, the batch code, and the smell. A genuine oil should smell rich and last on your skin for hours. A fake one smells sharp, fades in minutes, and sometimes leaves a rash. Buy a small quantity first, test it yourself for a week, and only then place a large order. A supplier who refuses a small test order is telling you something.

Some sellers import directly from Dubai, Turkey, or the United Kingdom, where many Arabian and designer oils originate. Direct import can lower your cost per bottle, but it adds shipping, clearing, and the risk of a shipment getting stuck. For a beginner, buying from a trusted Nigerian wholesaler is safer and faster. You can consider direct import after you know exactly which products sell and in what quantity.

Online, there are legitimate wholesale suppliers on Instagram and WhatsApp, but there are also many fraudsters. Never pay in full before receiving goods from a supplier you have not met, especially if they only accept transfers and refuse video calls or a physical address. Ask for a video call showing the stock, ask for their shop address, and if possible, send someone you trust to verify. A supplier who is genuine will welcome verification. A fraudster will rush you, offer an unbelievably low price, and disappear after payment.



## How to Price Your Perfumes for Profit

Pricing is where many beginners lose money. The rule is simple: your selling price must cover the cost of the product, the cost of getting it to the customer, and leave a profit that is worth your time. A common beginner mistake is to copy a competitor's price without knowing their cost. Their supplier may be cheaper, or they may be selling fakes. Price from your own costs, not from someone else's advert.

Start with the cost of the bottle, add delivery or transport costs, add packaging costs, and add a share of your advertising costs. Then add your profit margin. For oil perfumes, a margin of 40 to 60 percent is normal. If you buy a bottle for ₦3,000 and sell it for ₦5,500, your gross profit is ₦2,500 before delivery and packaging. For designer perfumes, margins are often 20 to 35 percent, because the cost is higher and customers compare prices more carefully. For body sprays, margins of 30 to 50 percent are normal.

Offer clear price tiers so customers can choose. A small business might offer a budget range of ₦3,000 to ₦7,000, a mid-range of ₦8,000 to ₦20,000, and a premium range of ₦25,000 and above. Tiers help a customer who came with ₦5,000 to find something without embarrassment, and help a customer with ₦50,000 to find something special without feeling the shop is too cheap for them.

Avoid the temptation to slash prices to win customers. A perfume sold too cheaply looks fake, and a customer who buys only because of a low price will leave for the next seller who is cheaper. Compete on scent quality, honesty, packaging, and service. Offer a small free sample with a purchase, a loyalty card that gives a free small bottle after five purchases, or free delivery within your area for orders above ₦20,000. These build loyalty without destroying your margin.

Accept the payment methods your customers actually use. Bank transfer is the most common, followed by Paystack or Flutterwave payment links for customers who prefer to pay with a card. Cash on delivery is popular but risky, because some customers order and then refuse the delivery. If you offer pay on delivery, confirm the order by phone first, and consider a small commitment fee of ₦1,000 to ₦2,000 for large orders. Keep a clear record of every payment, and send a receipt on WhatsApp immediately after payment is confirmed.


## How to Sell on WhatsApp and Instagram

Most Nigerian perfume sales start on Instagram and close on WhatsApp. Your Instagram page is your shop window. Post clear photos and short videos of your products, show the bottle, name the scent, state the price, and say how to order. A post that says "Oud Royale oil perfume, 12ml, ₦8,500, long-lasting, perfect for evenings. Send a DM or WhatsApp 0803 123 4567 to order" will sell more than a beautiful photo with no price and no instructions. Customers do not want to ask for the price three times before they can decide.

Post consistently, at least four or five times a week. Mix product posts with useful content: how to make perfume last longer, how to choose a scent for a wedding, the difference between oil perfume and spray, and short videos of you testing a new scent. Useful content builds trust, and trust sells perfume. Use Instagram Reels to show the bottle, spray it, and describe the scent in plain words. A thirty-second video of a real person describing a scent honestly is more persuasive than a polished advert.

On WhatsApp, use your Status every day. Post new stock, customer reviews, delivery updates, and short videos. WhatsApp Status is seen by people who already know you, and they are the most likely to buy. Create a broadcast list of customers who have bought from you or asked about your products, and send them a useful update once or twice a week, not every day. A broadcast that says "New stock: three long-lasting Arabian oils just arrived, from ₦5,000" is welcome. A broadcast sent three times a day is spam, and customers will mute you.

Run small, targeted adverts when you are ready. A budget of ₦5,000 to ₦10,000 on Instagram can test whether an advert brings enquiries. Target Lagos or Abuja, people aged 20 to 45, with interests in fashion, beauty, and fragrances. Send advert traffic to WhatsApp, not to a page with no clear next step. Track how many enquiries each advert brings, and how many turn into sales. An advert that brings fifty enquiries and zero sales is not working, no matter how many likes it gets.


## Delivery, Returns, and Customer Service

Delivery is part of the product in Nigeria. A customer in Lekki who orders from a seller in Ikeja expects the bottle to arrive the same day or the next day, intact and as described. Partner with a reliable dispatch rider or a logistics company, and agree on prices in advance for the areas you serve. Within Lagos, delivery typically costs ₦1,500 to ₦4,000 depending on distance. Within Abuja, expect ₦1,500 to ₦3,500. For interstate delivery, use a trusted courier and share the tracking number with the customer immediately.

Package every order as if it were a gift. Wrap the bottle, seal the bag, and include a receipt and a thank-you note. Before you send it, check the bottle, the size, and the scent against the order. A wrong bottle sent to a customer costs you the delivery fee twice, the customer's trust, and often a bad review. A correct order sent quickly earns you a repeat customer.

Have a clear returns policy and state it before the customer pays. Because perfume is a personal product, most sellers do not accept returns on opened bottles, for hygiene reasons. That is reasonable, but you should accept a return or exchange if you sent the wrong product, a damaged bottle, or a fake. Say this clearly on WhatsApp and on your Instagram page, so there is no argument later. A customer who knows your policy in advance will respect it. A customer who discovers it after a problem will feel cheated.

Answer messages quickly. In this business, speed is service. A customer who messages five sellers at the same time will buy from the one who replies first with a clear price and a confident answer. Aim to reply within an hour during business hours, and set a WhatsApp auto-reply for times when you are unavailable. If you are going to be away, tell your customers in advance on your Status, so they do not think you have disappeared with their money.



## Conclusion

Starting a perfume business in Nigeria does not require a large shop, a loan, or a degree in chemistry. It requires a good product, an honest supplier, clear pricing, and the discipline to serve customers well and reinvest profit. Start with a product line you understand, buy a small quantity, test it, and grow from what sells. Build your presence on WhatsApp and Instagram, let customers smell before they buy, deliver quickly and carefully, and follow up after every sale. Do that consistently for six months, track your numbers, and you will have a business that pays you, grows with you, and can one day become a brand of its own. Your first bottle is the hardest sale. After that, the scent sells itself.
