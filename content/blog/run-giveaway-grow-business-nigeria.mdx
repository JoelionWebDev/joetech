A business in Lagos announces a giveaway: a brand-new phone for one lucky follower. The post gets thousands of likes, hundreds of comments, and a flood of new followers. A week later, the winner is announced, and within days most of the new followers are gone. The business spent money on a prize and gained almost nothing that lasts. This happens often, and it is not bad luck. It is a giveaway designed for attention instead of growth.

A good giveaway does more than attract a crowd. It attracts the right crowd, gives them a reason to stay, and turns some of them into customers. That takes planning. The prize, the rules, the timing, and the follow-up all matter more than the excitement of the announcement itself.

## Choose a prize your real customers want

The prize decides who enters. A generic prize, such as cash or a phone, attracts everyone, including people who will never buy from you. A prize from your own business attracts people who are already interested in what you sell, which is exactly the audience you want.

A skincare brand should give away its own products, not a phone. A restaurant should give away a meal for two, not cash. A fashion designer should give away an outfit from the new collection, not a gadget. When the prize is your product, every entrant is a potential customer, and every winner becomes a person who has tried your product and can talk about it.

Think about the value of the prize too. It should be generous enough to excite people, but not so large that it eats your profit or attracts only prize hunters. If the prize is a product you already sell, its cost to you is lower than its selling price, which makes it an efficient prize.

Also decide how many winners there will be. One big winner creates excitement, but several smaller winners spread the joy and give more people a reason to stay after the draw. Three small prizes often bring better long-term results than one large one, because three winners tell three circles of friends about your business.

## Set rules that are simple, fair, and useful

Giveaway rules should do three jobs: they should be easy to follow, they should be fair, and they should help your business. Rules that fail any of these will cause problems.

Keep the entry steps to three at most. A common pattern is: follow the page, like the post, and tag two friends who would like the prize. Each step has a purpose. Following brings them onto your page, liking shows engagement, and tagging brings new people who are likely to be interested because their friend thought of them.

Be careful with rules that ask too much. Asking people to follow ten pages, share to their story, comment five times, and tag ten friends will annoy genuine customers and attract only people who enter every giveaway they see. Simple rules respect your audience, and a respected audience stays longer.

Write the rules in plain language and put them where everyone can see them: in the caption of the post and, if the giveaway runs for more than a few days, in a pinned comment or a story highlight. Include the closing date, how the winner will be chosen, and when the winner will be announced. If the winner must collect the prize in person or pay for delivery, say so upfront. Surprises after the draw create complaints and damage trust.

Choose the winner fairly and openly. A random draw from the comments, done on a live video or recorded and shared, is the most trusted method. Avoid picking a winner who is your friend, your staff member, or someone who did not follow the rules, because people notice, and they will not enter your next giveaway.

## Plan the timing and the announcement

Timing matters more than most businesses realise. A giveaway that runs for too long loses energy, because people forget about it. A giveaway that runs for too short a time does not reach enough people. For most small businesses, five to seven days is a good length: long enough for the post to spread, short enough to keep the excitement alive.

Announce the giveaway when your audience is most active. For many Nigerian businesses, that is in the evening, when people are home from work and scrolling on their phones, or on a weekend morning. Avoid launching late at night, when the post will be buried by morning.

Do not rely on a single post. Remind people once or twice during the week with a story or a short update, such as a reminder of the closing date or a look at the prize being prepared. Each reminder brings the giveaway back to the top of people's feeds without looking desperate. After the closing date, announce the winner quickly, ideally within a day. A slow announcement makes people suspect the draw was not fair, and the excitement fades.

## Turn entrants into customers, not just followers

The giveaway ends when the winner is announced, but the growth work starts the day after. Most businesses do nothing at this point, and that is why their new followers drift away. A simple follow-up plan makes the difference.

First, welcome the new followers. Post a short message thanking everyone who entered, and tell them what is coming next: a new product, a useful tip, or a small offer for everyone who took part. People who feel welcomed are more likely to stay than people who are ignored after the draw.

Second, give the non-winners a reason to buy. A small, time-limited offer for all entrants, such as a discount on the prize product for one week, turns the giveaway into sales. The offer should be genuine and easy to use, and it should be sent where your customers actually are, such as your WhatsApp broadcast list or your Instagram story. Do not make the offer complicated, and do not extend it endlessly, or people will learn to wait for the next one.

Third, keep posting useful content. The new followers came for a prize, but they will stay for value. Post the kind of content that helped your business before the giveaway: product tips, behind-the-scenes looks, customer stories, and answers to common questions. If your page goes quiet after the giveaway, people will unfollow within weeks.

Fourth, ask the winner to share their experience. If the winner is happy to post a photo or a short video with the prize, that is the best promotion your giveaway can produce, because it comes from a real customer rather than from you. Always ask permission first, and never pressure a winner who prefers to stay private.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much should I spend on a giveaway prize?**

Spend enough to excite your real customers, but not so much that it hurts your business. For most small businesses, a prize worth one of your regular products or a small bundle is right. Avoid spending on a prize that has nothing to do with your business.

**Should I ask people to tag friends to enter?**

Yes, tagging brings people who are likely to be interested. Keep it to two friends. Asking people to tag ten friends looks desperate, annoys their friends, and often breaks the rules of the platform.

**What if the winner does not respond?**

Say this in your rules before you launch: if the winner does not respond within a stated period, such as 48 hours, you will draw another winner. Announce the redraw openly, the same way you announced the first draw.

**How often should I run a giveaway?**

For most small businesses, once every two or three months is enough. Tie each giveaway to something real, such as a new product, a business anniversary, or a festive season.

## Make your giveaway the start, not the whole plan

A giveaway is not a growth strategy on its own. It is a spark. The prize brings people in, the fair rules earn their trust, and the follow-up turns them into customers. Choose a prize from your own business, keep the rules simple and honest, announce the winner quickly, and then give every entrant a reason to stay. Do that, and your next giveaway will start from a bigger, warmer audience than the last one, because the people you gained will still be there.
