When someone in Lagos searches Google for "plumber near me", "best cake shop in Ikeja", or "wedding photographer Lekki", they are not browsing for fun. They are ready to buy. Google Ads puts your business at the top of those search results, in front of people who are already looking for what you sell.

For a Nigerian small business, that is powerful. Unlike a flyer or a radio advert, which reaches many people who do not need you, a Google advert reaches only people who typed the words that describe your business. You pay only when someone clicks your advert, and you can start with a small daily budget — as little as ₦1,000 to ₦2,000 a day — and stop at any time.

Many small business owners in Nigeria have heard of Google Ads but have never tried it, because it looks technical, or because they have heard stories of people spending money with no results. This guide explains it in plain language: how it works, how to set up your first campaign, how much to spend, and how to make sure your money brings real customers, not just clicks.

## How Google Ads Actually Works

Google Ads is an auction. When someone searches for something, Google looks at all the adverts that match that search and decides which ones to show. The decision is based on two things: how much you are willing to pay for a click, and how good your advert and your website are for that search.

You do not pay to appear. You pay only when someone clicks your advert. That click is called a pay-per-click, and the amount you pay is the cost per click. In Nigeria, the cost per click varies widely. A search like "buy shoes Lagos" may cost ₦50 to ₦150 per click, because many sellers compete for it. A more specific search like "bridal makeup artist Surulere" may cost less, because fewer businesses compete for it, yet the person searching is more likely to book.

You control your spending with a daily budget. If you set a budget of ₦2,000 a day, Google will show your adverts until that budget is used up, then stop for the day. Your monthly spend is roughly your daily budget multiplied by 30, so ₦2,000 a day is about ₦60,000 a month. You can raise or lower the budget at any time, and you can pause the campaign whenever you want. There is no contract and no minimum spend beyond your daily budget.

Your advert leads to a landing page — the page on your website where the customer arrives after clicking. This page matters as much as the advert. If your advert promises "affordable wedding cakes in Lekki" but the customer lands on a homepage with no cakes and no prices, they will leave in seconds, and you will have paid for a wasted click. A good landing page matches the advert, shows the product clearly, gives the price or a price range, and makes it easy to contact you on WhatsApp or by phone.

## Before You Spend One Naira: Get These Right

A Google Ads campaign will fail if the foundations are wrong, no matter how good your keywords are. Do these four things first.

First, decide exactly what you want the advert to achieve. "More sales" is too vague. A clear goal sounds like this: "Get 20 WhatsApp enquiries a week from people in Lagos looking for a cleaning service." Or: "Sell 15 pairs of shoes a week through my website." A clear goal tells you which keywords to choose, what to write in the advert, and whether the campaign is working.

Second, make sure your website or WhatsApp page is ready. Your page should load quickly on a phone, because most Nigerians search on their phones. It should show what you sell, your prices or a clear way to ask for a price, your location, and a big WhatsApp or call button. If your website is slow or confusing, fix that before you advertise. Paying to send people to a broken page is the fastest way to waste money.

Third, decide who your customer is and where they are. A bakery in Ikeja should show adverts to people in Ikeja, Surulere, and nearby areas, not to people in Abuja who cannot collect a cake. A business that ships nationwide can target the whole country, but a local service should target its city or a few local government areas. Google lets you choose locations precisely, and local targeting saves you from paying for clicks that can never become customers.

Fourth, set up conversion tracking. This sounds technical, but it is simply a way to know which clicks became customers. Google gives you a small piece of code to add to your website, or you can count WhatsApp messages that came from your advert by using a different WhatsApp link or a simple question: "How did you hear about us?" Without tracking, you will know you spent ₦60,000, but you will not know whether it brought you five customers or fifty.

## Choose Keywords That Bring Buyers, Not Browsers

Keywords are the words people type into Google. Your campaign lives or dies on the keywords you choose, so choose them like a customer, not like a business owner.

Start with buying words. Someone who searches "buy Ankara dress Lagos" or "emergency plumber Lekki price" is ready to spend money. Someone who searches "how to sew Ankara" or "what is plumbing" is looking for information, not a seller. Your advert should appear for the first group, not the second. Write down ten phrases a ready customer would type, including the product, the location, and a buying word like buy, price, near me, best, or hire.

Use specific phrases, not single words. A single word like "shoes" is expensive, because every shoe seller in Nigeria competes for it, and the searcher could want anything — pictures, prices, history, or a shop in another state. A phrase like "men's leather shoes Lagos price" is cheaper, has less competition, and brings a searcher who knows what they want. Google calls these long-tail keywords, and for a small budget they are the best place to start.

Add negative keywords. These are words you do not want your advert to show for. If you sell new phones, add "free", "repair", "used", and "jobs" as negative keywords, so your advert does not appear when someone searches "free phone repair jobs". Negative keywords stop you paying for clicks from people who were never going to buy from you, and every small business should review them weekly.

Think about what your customers actually call things. A customer in Lagos may search "cake plug Lagos" or "where to buy cake in Ikeja", not "artisanal patisserie services". Use the words your customers use on WhatsApp and in your shop, not the formal words in a textbook. Ask your last ten customers what they searched for, or what they would search for, and you will get better keywords than any tool can give you.

Start with ten to fifteen keywords in your first campaign. That is enough to learn what works without spreading your small budget too thin. After two weeks, keep the keywords that bring enquiries, pause the ones that bring clicks but no customers, and add new ones based on the actual search terms Google shows you in your campaign report.

## Write Adverts People Actually Click

Your advert is small — a headline, a description, and a link — but it must do a big job in a few seconds. A good advert does three things: it matches the search, it gives a reason to click, and it tells the customer what to do next.

Match the search. If someone searches "wedding cakes Ikeja price", your headline should mention wedding cakes and Ikeja, not just your business name. Google rewards adverts that match the search, and customers click adverts that look like an answer to their question. Use your main keyword in the headline whenever you can.

Give a reason to click. A price, a discount, a free delivery offer, a guarantee, or a clear benefit all work. "Wedding Cakes from ₦35,000 — Free Delivery in Ikeja" is more clickable than "Best Cakes in Lagos". Be honest: if you promise free delivery, offer it. A misleading advert brings clicks that never become customers, and you pay for every one of them.

Tell them what to do next. "Order on WhatsApp", "Call now", "Book a free consultation", or "Shop today" gives the customer a clear next step. A customer who does not know what to do will do nothing.

Write at least three versions of each advert and let Google test them. One version can lead with price, another with quality, and a third with speed or location. After two weeks, check which version brings the most enquiries at the lowest cost, and pause the weakest one. Good advertisers test constantly; they do not write one advert and leave it for a year.



## How Much Should You Spend, and How Do You Pay?

The most common question Nigerian small business owners ask about Google Ads is how much it costs. The honest answer is that it depends on your business, your keywords, and your location — but you can start small and grow.

A sensible starting budget is ₦1,500 to ₦3,000 a day, which is roughly ₦45,000 to ₦90,000 a month. Run your first campaign for at least two weeks at this level before you judge it. Google needs time to learn which searches bring you customers, and you need enough clicks to see a pattern. Spending ₦2,000 for two days and stopping because there were no sales tells you nothing.

Work out what a customer is worth to you before you set a budget. If a new customer spends an average of ₦20,000 with you and earns you ₦6,000 in profit, then paying ₦500 to get that customer through Google Ads is a good deal. If you pay ₦3,000 per customer and your profit per customer is only ₦2,000, the campaign is losing money, and you need cheaper keywords or a better landing page. This calculation — your cost to acquire a customer versus the profit that customer brings — is the most important number in your campaign.

Paying for Google Ads from Nigeria is straightforward. You can pay with a naira debit card, a dollar card, or a bank transfer, depending on the payment settings in your Google Ads account. Set your account currency carefully when you create it, because changing it later is difficult. Many Nigerian advertisers use a naira card linked to their business account. Keep your card funded, because if a payment fails, Google pauses your adverts until you pay, and you lose momentum.

## Track Your Results and Improve Every Week

Google Ads gives you more data than any other form of advertising in Nigeria, but data only helps if you look at it. Set aside 30 minutes every week to review your campaign.

Look at your cost per click first. If a keyword costs ₦400 per click and brings no enquiries, pause it. If another keyword costs ₦80 per click and brings enquiries, give it more of your budget. This simple habit alone can double the results of a small campaign in a month.

Look at your search terms report. This shows the exact words people typed before they clicked your advert. You will find surprises: searches you never expected that bring good customers, and searches that waste your money. Add the good surprises as new keywords, and add the wasteful ones as negative keywords.

Look at your click-through rate, which is the percentage of people who saw your advert and clicked it. A rate below 2 percent usually means your advert does not match the search or does not give a good reason to click. Rewrite the headline, add a price or an offer, and test again.

Most importantly, count real enquiries and sales, not just clicks. A campaign that brings 200 clicks and no sales has failed. A campaign that brings 40 clicks and 10 WhatsApp enquiries has succeeded. Ask every new customer how they found you, and write it down. That simple record, matched against your Google Ads spend, tells you the truth about whether the campaign is working.

Improve one thing at a time. If you change your keywords, your advert, your budget, and your landing page all in the same week, you will never know which change helped. Change one thing, wait a week, check the result, then change the next thing. Patient, steady improvement beats frantic changes every time.

## Mistakes That Waste Money on Google Ads

Most Google Ads waste in Nigeria comes from a few avoidable mistakes. Avoid these and your small budget will go much further.

The first is targeting the whole country when you serve one city. A tailor in Surulere who targets all of Nigeria pays for clicks from Kano, Enugu, and Port Harcourt — people who will never visit the shop. Target your city and the areas around it, unless you genuinely ship nationwide.

The second is sending clicks to a homepage instead of a relevant page. If your advert is about wedding cakes, the click should land on your wedding cake page, with pictures, prices, and a WhatsApp button. Every extra step a customer must take loses you sales.

The third is bidding on your competitors' brand names without a plan. It can work, but it is usually expensive, and the customers who click are often just comparing prices. For a small budget, your own product keywords are a better investment.

The fourth is forgetting to check the campaign. Google Ads is not a machine you switch on and forget. Keywords change in price, competitors enter the market, and adverts go stale. A campaign nobody checks for three months will quietly waste money. A weekly 30-minute review is enough to keep it healthy.

The fifth is giving up too early. The first week of a campaign is often the worst, because Google is still learning and your adverts are untested. Many Nigerian business owners stop after three days and conclude that Google Ads does not work. Give it at least two weeks, fix the obvious problems, and then judge it on real enquiries, not on the first few days.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does Google Ads cost in Nigeria?

There is no fixed price. You set a daily budget, and you pay only when someone clicks your advert. Most small businesses start with ₦1,500 to ₦3,000 a day. The cost per click depends on your keywords and competition, and can range from under ₦50 to several hundred naira.

### Do I need a website to run Google Ads?

You need somewhere for customers to land after they click. That can be a website, a landing page, or a WhatsApp Business page, but a proper website with clear products, prices, and contact details converts far better than a bare social media profile.

### How long does it take to get customers from Google Ads?

You can get clicks within hours of launching a campaign, but real results usually take one to two weeks, while Google learns and you improve your keywords and adverts. Be patient, review weekly, and judge the campaign on enquiries and sales, not on the first day.

### Is Google Ads better than Instagram adverts?

They serve different purposes. Google Ads reaches people who are already searching for what you sell, so it is strong for immediate sales. Instagram adverts reach people who may not know they need you yet, so they are strong for building awareness. Many Nigerian businesses use both: Google for buyers, Instagram for brand building.

### Can I run Google Ads myself, or do I need an agency?

You can run a simple campaign yourself if you follow the steps in this guide and review it weekly. An agency can help with larger budgets and complex campaigns, but for a small business starting with ₦45,000 to ₦90,000 a month, doing it yourself keeps costs low and teaches you what your customers search for.

## What to Do Next

1. Write down one clear goal for your first campaign, your top ten buying keywords, and the landing page each advert will lead to.
2. Set a starting budget of ₦1,500 to ₦3,000 a day, launch the campaign, and let it run for two full weeks before you judge it.
3. Review your campaign every week: pause expensive keywords that bring no enquiries, add the good search terms you discover, and count real WhatsApp messages and sales, not just clicks.
