Most small business owners in Nigeria guess what their customers think. They guess why a regular customer stopped coming, why a new product is not selling, or whether their prices are too high. Guessing is expensive. A short survey replaces guessing with answers, and Google Forms makes that survey free, quick to build, and easy to share on WhatsApp, which is where most Nigerian customers already are.

A customer survey is simply a short list of questions you send to people who have bought from you. It asks what they liked, what frustrated them, what they want next, and whether they would recommend you to others. The answers show you what to fix, what to keep doing, and what new product or service to offer. A salon can learn which style customers want next. A restaurant can learn which dish to keep and which to remove. A shop can learn whether delivery is too slow or prices are unclear.

This guide explains how to create a customer survey with Google Forms in Nigeria, step by step. You will learn how to plan the questions, build the form on your phone or laptop, share it in a way that gets replies, read the results, and turn those results into real changes in your business.

## Decide What You Want to Learn First

Do not open Google Forms and start typing questions at random. Start with a decision: what is the one thing you need to learn this month? A survey that tries to learn everything learns nothing, because customers abandon long surveys halfway. A survey with one clear purpose gets finished.

Write your goal in one sentence. For example: "I want to know why customers who buy once do not return." Or: "I want to know which new flavour my bakery customers want." Or: "I want to know whether my delivery service in Lekki is fast enough." That sentence guides every question you write. If a question does not help answer it, remove it.

Think about who should answer. Your best respondents are recent customers, not strangers. A person who bought from you last week remembers the experience clearly. A person who bought six months ago may not. Aim your survey at customers from the past month where possible. If you run a WhatsApp group for customers, that group is a good place to start. If you keep a list of customer phone numbers from orders, that list is also useful, provided those customers agreed to receive messages from you.

Decide how you will use the answers before you send the survey. If customers say delivery is slow, are you ready to change your delivery partner or add a rider? If they say prices are high, are you ready to review your costs or explain your value better? A survey that leads to no change teaches customers that their views do not matter, and they will ignore your next survey. Only ask about things you are willing to act on.

## Create Your Google Account and Open Google Forms

Google Forms is free with a Google account. Most Nigerians already have a Gmail address on their phone. If you do not, create one first. Use a business email where possible, such as your shop Gmail, rather than a personal email you share with family. Keep the password safe and turn on two-step verification if you can, because the survey responses and your customer contacts will sit in that account.

Open your browser and go to forms.google.com, or open the Google Drive app and choose to create a new form. On a laptop, the page is easier to use because you can see more of the form at once. On a phone, it still works well, and many business owners build their first form on a phone while sitting in the shop. Use whichever device you are comfortable with, and make sure you have a stable connection. Data costs matter, but a Google Form uses very little data to build and to fill in, which is one reason it works well in Nigeria.

Sign in, and you will see a blank form and some templates. The templates include customer feedback, event registration and order forms. A template can be a helpful starting point, but do not use one without editing it. Templates often ask questions you do not need, and they use wording that may not fit your business. Start from a blank form if you want full control, or start from a template and delete every question that does not serve your goal.

Give your form a clear title and a short description. The title should name your business and the purpose: "Tonia Cakes Customer Feedback" or "De Light Salon: Tell Us How We Did". The description should tell customers how long the survey takes and what you will do with the answers: "This survey takes two minutes. Your answers help us serve you better." That single sentence increases replies, because customers know the cost in time and the benefit of answering.

## Write Short, Clear Questions Customers Will Answer

Good questions are short, plain and easy to answer on a phone. Most of your customers will fill the form on a mobile phone, often while doing something else. If a question is long or confusing, they will skip it or close the form. Aim for eight to twelve questions at most, and make most of them quick to answer.

Use a mix of question types. Multiple choice questions are fastest: "How did you hear about us? WhatsApp, Instagram, Google, A friend, Walked past the shop." Rating questions are also quick: "How would you rate our service today? 1 to 5 stars." Use a rating scale where you want to track change over time. If your average rating rises from 3.5 to 4.2 after you fix a problem, you have proof that the fix worked.

Use open questions sparingly, but include at least one. An open question lets customers write in their own words: "What one thing should we improve?" or "What product would you like us to stock?" Open answers take longer to read, but they often contain the most useful insight. A single honest sentence from a customer can reveal a problem you did not know existed.

Avoid leading questions. "How much did you love our amazing service?" pushes the customer toward a positive answer and gives you false comfort. "How would you rate our service today?" is neutral and honest. Avoid asking two things in one question: "Was our food tasty and our delivery fast?" A customer may love the food but hate the delivery, and they will not know how to answer. Split it into two questions.

Write in plain English, and keep the language your customers use. If most of your customers speak Pidgin at your shop, you can use simple, friendly English with a warm tone. Avoid formal or technical words. "How easy was it to order?" is better than "Please assess the efficacy of our ordering process." Test each question by reading it aloud. If it sounds awkward when spoken, rewrite it.

Make only the most important questions required. If every question is required, customers who do not want to answer one question will abandon the whole survey. Make your rating and your main multiple choice questions required, and leave open questions optional. That balance gives you complete data on the key points without forcing customers to write when they do not want to.

## Set Up the Form Settings Before You Share It

Before you share the form, check the settings. In Google Forms, open the settings tab and review the options. Decide whether to collect email addresses. Collecting emails helps you follow up with a customer who reported a problem, but it also makes some customers less honest, because they know you can identify them. For a general satisfaction survey, let customers answer anonymously. For a survey about a specific order or complaint, ask for a name and phone number so you can fix it.

Turn on the option to show a confirmation message after submission. Write a warm thank-you: "Thank you for your feedback. We read every response and use it to serve you better." That message is the last thing the customer sees, so make it kind. You can also add a link back to your WhatsApp or your shop page in the confirmation, so a happy customer can order again right away.

Check how the form looks on a phone. Use the preview button and open it on your own phone. Scroll through it as a customer would. Are the questions easy to read? Are the buttons big enough to tap? Is any text cut off? Fix anything that looks cramped or confusing. A form that is hard to use on a phone will lose replies in Nigeria, where most customers are on mobile data and small screens.

If your customers have limited data, keep the form light. Do not add large images or videos to the form itself. A small logo at the top is fine, but a heavy form loads slowly and costs data. Customers on a tight data budget will abandon a slow form. Plain text loads fast and works everywhere, including on older phones and weaker networks.

Test the form yourself before you share it. Fill it in as a customer, submit it, and check that the response appears in your responses tab. Ask a staff member or a friend to test it too. That test catches mistakes such as a wrong question, a missing option or a setting that blocks submissions. It takes five minutes and saves you the embarrassment of sharing a broken form with hundreds of customers.

## Share the Survey on WhatsApp and Other Channels

A good form with no replies is useless. Sharing is where most surveys fail. The best channel for a Nigerian small business is almost always WhatsApp, because that is where your customers already talk to you. Share the form link in a short, personal message, not a long broadcast that reads like an advert.

Write the message as if you were talking to one customer. For example: "Good afternoon, this is Ada from Ada Hair Studio. Thank you for coming in last week. Please help us serve you better by answering this short survey. It takes two minutes. Here is the link." That message is polite, specific and short. It tells the customer who you are, why you are asking, and how long it will take.

Send the survey to recent customers first, by personal message where you can. A personal message gets more replies than a broadcast to a large group, because the customer feels seen. If you have a customer WhatsApp group, you can also share it there, but add a personal note and ask group members to reply. Do not send the survey every day. One message, plus one gentle reminder after three or four days, is enough. More than that feels like spam.

You can also share the link in other places. Add it to your WhatsApp Status with a short caption. Share it on your Instagram Stories with a link sticker. Print a QR code for the form and place it at your till or on your tables, so walk-in customers can scan it while they wait. Google Forms gives you a link you can shorten or turn into a QR code with a free generator. A printed QR code works well in salons, restaurants and clinics, where customers wait and have time on their hands.

Offer a small, honest incentive if you need more replies, but keep it simple. A small discount on the next visit or entry into a draw for a free product can help. State the incentive clearly and honour it. Do not promise a large reward you cannot afford. A survey that promises a free phone and delivers nothing destroys trust. A survey that promises 10 percent off the next visit and delivers it builds loyalty.

## Read the Results and Turn Answers Into Action

Responses arrive in the Responses tab of your form. Google Forms shows a summary with charts for each question. Start there. Look at the average rating, the most common answers, and any question where the answers spread widely. A question where half the customers are happy and half are unhappy points to an inconsistent service, perhaps one branch or one staff member performing differently from the rest.

Read the open answers one by one. They take time, but they are where the real detail lives. Group them into themes: delivery, price, quality, staff attitude, waiting time, cleanliness. Count how many customers mention each theme. A theme mentioned by one customer may be a personal preference. A theme mentioned by twenty customers is a problem to fix.

Do not ignore negative answers. It is tempting to focus on the praise and skip the criticism, but criticism is the most valuable part of a survey. If customers say your shop is hard to find, add a clearer signboard or a landmark to your directions. If they say your WhatsApp replies are slow, set a rule that messages are answered within one hour during opening times. If they say a product is too expensive, check your costs and compare your price with competitors before you decide whether to adjust it or to explain your quality better.

Share the results with your staff. Show them the praise as well as the complaints. Staff who see that customers appreciate their work feel motivated. Staff who see a specific complaint understand what to change. Keep the discussion practical: pick the top three issues from the survey, agree what will change, and set a date to check whether the change worked.

Close the loop with customers. After you make a change, tell the customers who asked for it. A short WhatsApp message works: "Thank you for your feedback. We now deliver to Ajah on Saturdays, as many of you requested." That message shows customers that their time was not wasted. It also encourages them to answer your next survey, because they know you listen.

Repeat the survey regularly, but not too often. Once every three months is a good rhythm for most small businesses. That gives you time to act on the last survey before you ask again. If you survey every week, customers will tire of it. If you survey once a year, you will miss changes in what they want. A quarterly survey keeps you close to your customers without annoying them.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Google Forms free for business surveys in Nigeria?
Yes. Google Forms is free with a Google account. You can create a survey, share it on WhatsApp, and view the results without paying.

### How many questions should a customer survey have?
Keep it to eight to twelve questions, and aim for two minutes to finish. Short surveys get far more replies than long ones.

### Should I ask customers for their names?
For general feedback, let customers answer anonymously so they are honest. Ask for a name and phone number only when you need to follow up on a specific order.
