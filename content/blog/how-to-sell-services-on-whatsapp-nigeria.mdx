Ask a Nigerian business owner where their customers come from and you will hear the same answer again and again: WhatsApp. The plumber in Ikeja gets his jobs on WhatsApp. The makeup artist in Lekki books her brides on WhatsApp. The web designer, the tutor, the caterer, the cleaner and the consultant all close their sales in a chat window.

This makes sense. WhatsApp is already on almost every phone in Nigeria. People check it dozens of times a day. It is where they talk to family, where they receive church and school messages, and where they ask friends for recommendations. When a customer wants a service, the easiest thing in the world is to open WhatsApp and message the person directly. No forms. No waiting. No friction.

Yet many service providers use WhatsApp badly. Their profile has no photo and no description. Their status is empty for weeks. They reply late, or with one-word answers. They quote prices in a way that scares people off. They lose customers who were ready to buy, simply because the chat did not feel professional.

This guide will show you how to sell your services on WhatsApp in Nigeria, properly. You will learn how to set up a professional WhatsApp Business profile, how to attract the right people to your number, how to post status updates that bring in enquiries, how to handle price questions, how to close a sale inside a chat, and how to turn one happy customer into many more.

## Step 1: Switch to WhatsApp Business

If you sell services, you should be using WhatsApp Business, not the regular WhatsApp app. It is free, it is made for businesses, and it gives you tools the regular app does not have.

**Download WhatsApp Business from the Play Store or App Store.** It is a separate app. You can use your existing business number, and your chats and contacts will move across. If you use one phone for personal and business life, WhatsApp Business lets you keep the two separate, which is worth doing. Customers should not have to scroll past your family photos to find your price list.

**Set up your business profile completely.** Tap the business settings and fill in every field: your business name, your category, your description, your address, your email, your website, and your opening hours. This profile is your shop window. A customer who opens your number for the first time should immediately understand what you do.

**Write a description that sells.** Your description has limited space, so use it well. Say what you do, who you do it for, and where you work. Compare "We do catering" with "Lagos catering for weddings, birthdays and corporate events — from ₦5,000 per guest. DM to book." The second one tells the customer everything they need to know in one line.

**Use a clear profile photo.** Your logo, or a good photo of you at work, is best. Avoid blurry photos, cartoons, or pictures that have nothing to do with your business. Customers trust a face or a brand they can recognise.

**Set your opening hours honestly.** If you work Monday to Saturday, 8am to 6pm, say so. Customers who message at 11pm will understand why you reply in the morning.

**Create a catalogue.** WhatsApp Business lets you build a catalogue of your services, each with a photo, a name, a price and a short description. A cleaning company can list "2-bedroom flat cleaning — ₦25,000", "3-bedroom flat cleaning — ₦35,000" and "Office cleaning — from ₦50,000". A tutor can list subjects and hourly rates. A customer can browse your catalogue without asking you to type the same prices for the hundredth time. Update the catalogue whenever your prices change.

## Step 2: Get the Right People to Your Number

A WhatsApp shop with no visitors makes no sales. You need a steady flow of the right people finding your number. Here is how to build that flow.

**Put your number everywhere it belongs.** On your Instagram bio, your Facebook page, your Google Business Profile, your website, your flyers, your business card, and your email signature. Use a WhatsApp link, the kind that opens a chat directly, so people do not have to save your number first. A link like wa.me/2348012345678 removes a step, and every step you remove brings you more enquiries.

**Use your status to stay visible.** Your WhatsApp status is free advertising to everyone who has your number saved. Post every day. Show finished work, show work in progress, share a customer review, announce a discount, or teach something small about your service. A hair stylist can post a thirty-second video of a finished hairstyle. A cleaner can post before-and-after photos of a living room. Consistency matters more than perfection. A status posted every day keeps you in people's minds, so that when they need your service, you are the first person they think of.

**Ask happy customers to save your number and share it.** A customer who saves your number sees your status. A customer who shares your number with a friend gives you a warm introduction that no advert can buy. After a good job, say: "Thank you so much. Please save my number so you can see my work updates, and feel free to share it with anyone who needs this service."

**Join groups carefully, and add value before you sell.** WhatsApp groups for estates, churches, alumni associations and professional networks can bring customers, but only if you behave well in them. Answer questions. Help people. Share useful tips. A web designer who answers a question about websites in a business owners' group will get enquiries. A designer who drops adverts in ten groups every morning will get muted or removed. One helpful answer is worth more than ten adverts.

**Collect numbers properly.** At events, in your shop, or after a job, ask people if you can add them to your broadcast list for updates. Always ask. Adding strangers to marketing lists without permission is rude, it is against WhatsApp's rules, and it gets numbers reported and blocked.

## Step 3: Use Labels, Quick Replies and Away Messages

WhatsApp Business has three tools that save hours every week. Use all three.

**Labels organise your customers.** Create labels such as "New Enquiry", "Quoted", "Paid Deposit", "Job Done", "Repeat Customer" and "VIP". When a chat comes in, label it. At the end of the day you can open the "Quoted" label and follow up with everyone who received a price but has not replied. Follow-up is where most sales are won, and labels make follow-up easy instead of chaotic.

**Quick replies handle your most common messages.** If you type the same answers every day, save them as quick replies. Your price list, your delivery areas, your opening hours, your account details, your booking process. A quick reply turns a two-minute typing job into a two-second tap. To set one up, go to Business Tools, then Quick Replies, and write the message once. You can then send it in any chat with a short slash command.

**Away messages and greeting messages set expectations.** A greeting message welcomes new customers automatically: "Hello, thank you for contacting [your business]. We offer [your services] in Lagos. How can we help you today?" An away message handles the hours you are not available: "Thank you for your message. Our working hours are 8am to 6pm, Monday to Saturday. We will reply first thing in the morning." These small touches make a one-person business feel organised and professional.

## Step 4: How to Handle the First Message

The first message from a customer sets the tone for the whole sale. Most service providers lose sales here by being slow, vague, or unfriendly.

**Reply fast.** Speed is the biggest advantage you have on WhatsApp. A customer who messages three service providers will usually buy from the one who replies first with a helpful answer. If you cannot reply fully at once, send a short acknowledgement: "Hello, thank you for your message. Let me check my schedule and I will reply within 30 minutes." Then keep that promise.

**Greet them warmly and ask what they need.** "Good afternoon, thank you for contacting us. What service do you need, and when do you need it?" This is better than "Yes?" or "How much?" A warm greeting costs nothing and it changes the whole conversation.

**Ask the right questions before quoting.** A customer who says "how much is a website?" has not given you enough information to answer. Ask what kind of website, how many pages, whether they need online payments, and when they need it. A caterer should ask the number of guests, the date, the venue and the menu. Good questions show you are professional, and they stop you from quoting the wrong price.

**Send your price clearly and confidently.** When you quote, state the price, what it includes, and what the next step is. For example: "A 5-page business website is ₦350,000. That includes the design, mobile-friendly layout, contact form, and basic Google setup. A deposit of ₦175,000 (50%) starts the work, and the balance is due on delivery. I can start on Monday. Shall I send you the account details?" Notice what this message does: it gives the price, it justifies the price, it states the deposit, and it asks for the sale. Many sellers quote the price and then go silent, waiting for the customer to decide. The customer usually decides to check someone else.

**Use voice notes wisely.** Voice notes are personal and fast, and many Nigerian customers prefer them. Use them for warmth and explanation. But always follow a voice note with the key details in text: the price, the date, and the account number. Text can be searched and referred back to. A voice note cannot.

## Step 5: Closing the Sale Inside the Chat

A customer who has asked for your price is interested. Your job now is to make saying yes easy.

**Handle the price objection calmly.** When a customer says "that is too expensive", do not panic and do not slash your price. Ask a question: "What budget did you have in mind?" or explain the value: "The price covers the materials, the labour, and a one-year guarantee. We also deliver and install." Sometimes the customer genuinely cannot afford you, and that is fine. Sometimes they just need to understand what they are paying for. And sometimes they are testing you to see if the price will drop. Hold your price unless you have a real reason to adjust it, such as reducing what is included.

**Offer options, not discounts.** Instead of cutting your price, offer a smaller package. "The full package is ₦150,000. If your budget is ₦100,000, I can do the basic package, which covers the same core service without the extra features." Options keep the sale alive without destroying your margin.

**Send proof.** A photo of a similar job you did, a short video, a screenshot of a good review, or a before-and-after picture. Proof answers the customer's unspoken question: "Can this person actually do the job?" One strong photo can close a sale faster than ten messages.

**Make payment easy.** Send your account details or a payment link the moment the customer agrees. Paystack and Flutterwave links work well, and so do direct transfers to your business account or your Opay and Moniepoint wallets. The longer the gap between agreement and payment, the more likely the customer is to change their mind. Strike while the interest is hot.

**Confirm everything in writing.** Once the customer pays a deposit, send a summary message: the service, the price, the deposit received, the balance, and the delivery date. Ask them to confirm. This protects both sides and it looks professional.

**Follow up with people who went quiet.** A customer who received a quote and stopped replying has not necessarily said no. They may have been busy, or they may have forgotten. Send one polite follow-up after two days: "Good afternoon, just checking in. Did you get the quote I sent? I am happy to answer any questions." Send one more after a week. Then stop. Two follow-ups are professional. Ten are harassment.

## Step 6: After the Sale — Reviews, Referrals and Repeat Business

The sale is not the end. On WhatsApp, the sale is the beginning of the next sale.

**Ask for a review while the customer is happy.** Right after delivery, when the customer is most pleased, ask them to send a short message about their experience. With their permission, screenshot it and post it on your status. Real reviews from real customers are the most powerful marketing you have.

**Ask for a referral directly.** "Thank you for your patronage. If you know anyone who needs this service, I would be grateful if you shared my number with them." Most happy customers are glad to help. They just need to be asked.

**Keep in touch without spamming.** A short message once in a while keeps you in the customer's mind: a festive greeting, a new service announcement, or a special offer for old customers. Once or twice a month is enough. Daily broadcasts will get you blocked.

**Use broadcast lists, not groups, for marketing.** A broadcast list sends your message to each person privately. A group forces strangers to see each other's numbers. For marketing, broadcast lists are better, more private, and more professional. Remember to only add people who agreed to receive your updates.

**Build a VIP list.** Your repeat customers are your best customers. Keep a label for them, and treat them well. A small discount on their third order, a priority booking, or a thank-you message on their birthday. These gestures cost almost nothing and they create loyalty that adverts cannot buy.

## Mistakes That Kill Sales on WhatsApp

**Replying late or not at all.** A reply that comes six hours later often comes too late. The customer has already found someone else.

**No profile, no catalogue, no information.** A blank profile tells the customer nothing. Fill everything in.

**Quoting prices without explaining value.** A bare number with no context invites the customer to compare you with the cheapest option. Always say what the price includes.

**Arguing with customers.** If a customer is rude or unreasonable, stay calm and polite. If the job is not right for you, decline gracefully. A public argument or a rude message can be screenshotted and shared, and it will damage your business far more than one lost sale.

**Mixing business and personal chats.** Customers do not need to see your personal status updates, and your family does not need to see your customer chats. Keep them separate.

**Sending too many broadcasts.** If every message from you is an advert, people will mute you or block you. Mix useful content with offers, and keep the frequency low.

**Forgetting to collect a deposit.** Never start work on a promise. Collect a deposit before you begin, and confirm the payment in your own account before you buy materials or book a date.
