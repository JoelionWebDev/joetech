Open TikTok in Nigeria today and you will see more than dance videos. You will see a woman in Onitsha selling hair and showing how each wig looks on a real head. You will see a food vendor in Lekki filming his jollof rice as it steams, with customers in the comments asking for his location. You will see a tailor in Yaba showing a dress from fabric to finished outfit in thirty seconds. These are not big companies with advertising budgets. They are small business owners with a phone, a product, and a willingness to show up on camera — and TikTok is rewarding them with something that used to cost real money: attention.

TikTok works so well for small businesses in Nigeria because it does not care how big you are. The algorithm shows your video to a small group of strangers first, and if they watch it to the end, like it, or comment, it shows it to a bigger group, and then a bigger one. A first-time seller with zero followers can wake up to thousands of views on a single video — free reach that is almost impossible to buy anywhere else.

This guide is a practical plan for using TikTok to promote your small business in Nigeria. You will learn how to set up your account, what to post, how to film with just your phone, how to use trending sounds and hashtags, when to post, and — most importantly — how to turn viewers into customers who message you on WhatsApp and pay by bank transfer. You do not need a camera or a studio. You only need your phone, your product, and consistency.

## Set Up Your TikTok Business Presence Properly

Before you post a single video, set up your account the right way. Start by switching to a TikTok Business Account — it is free, and it gives you access to analytics, the numbers that tell you which videos people watch, who your audience is, and when they are online. It also gives you access to the commercial music library, which matters because business accounts cannot use every trending sound.

Choose a username matching your business name as closely as possible. If your business is called Ada's Cakes in Lagos, a username like @adascakeslagos is far better than @queen_ada_2026. Use the same name and picture across TikTok, Instagram, and WhatsApp Business, so customers recognise you everywhere. Your bio is your shop signboard, and space is tight, so use it well. Say what you sell, where you are, and what to do next: "Handmade wigs in Lagos. Nationwide delivery. DM or WhatsApp to order." Add your WhatsApp link so one tap takes a viewer to your chat, and set your profile picture to your logo or a clear photo of your product.

## Know Who You Are Talking To

The biggest mistake small businesses make on TikTok is trying to talk to everybody. When you try to talk to everybody, you end up talking to nobody. Before you plan your content, decide who your ideal customer is. Are you selling affordable clothes to university students in Lagos? Premium skincare to working women in Lekki and Abuja? Bulk foodstuffs to caterers and restaurant owners? Each of these customers watches TikTok differently and buys for different reasons. Your videos should speak directly to one of them.

A university student worries about price and style, and wants to see how an outfit looks on a real person. A busy working mother cares about delivery time and ordering on WhatsApp in two minutes. A caterer buying in bulk wants proof your goods are fresh and that you can supply the same quality every week. Watch your competitors, too. Search TikTok for your product — "wigs Lagos", "snacks Abuja", "furniture Nigeria" — and read the comments on the most popular videos. Those comments are free market research. If ten people under a competitor's video ask about delivery to Ibadan, that is a video you can make tomorrow.

## Video Ideas That Actually Sell

You do not need to dance or be funny to sell on TikTok. You need videos that show your product clearly and make people want it. The simplest format is the before-and-after: a tailor shows the fabric, then the finished dress; a cleaner shows a dirty sofa, then the same sofa after cleaning; a baker shows raw ingredients, then the finished cake. People love seeing a transformation.

Another powerful format is the process video. Film yourself making, packing, or preparing your product — a seller packing an order, adding a small free gift, and sealing the box builds trust, because it shows a real person cares about the order. Customer reviews are powerful too: when a customer sends you a voice note or a video saying they loved your product, ask their permission and post it. A happy customer's words carry more weight than anything you can say about yourself. Educational videos also work — if you sell skincare, explain how to use a product; if you sell hair, show how to maintain a wig so it lasts. Mix these types of videos. A page that is only "buy this, buy this, buy this" gets boring fast.

## How to Film Good Videos With Just Your Phone

You do not need an expensive camera — the phone in your pocket is enough. What matters is light, sound, and stability. Film near a window during the day, or outside in the shade, because natural light makes your product look real and attractive. Avoid filming under a yellow bulb at night, which dulls everything and changes the colour of your product. If you sell clothes, food, or cosmetics, correct colours matter: a customer who orders a red dress and receives something orange will be unhappy.

Hold your phone steady — a shaky video is hard to watch, and viewers will scroll past it in one second. Lean your phone against a wall, a stack of books, or a cheap phone tripod. Film vertically and fill the frame with your product. Sound matters too: record in a quiet place, turn off the television, and speak clearly and close to the phone. Use simple English or Pidgin if that is how your customers talk. Keep your videos short, between fifteen and sixty seconds, and start with the most interesting part in the first two seconds. Use TikTok's built-in editing tools to add text on the screen showing the price, your location, or your WhatsApp number, and add captions for people who watch without sound.

## Use Trending Sounds and Hashtags the Smart Way

Trending sounds are one of the fastest ways to reach new people, because TikTok shows users more videos that use sounds they already like. When you hear the same song or sound on several videos in a row, it is trending, and using it gives your video a small boost. But business accounts can only use sounds from TikTok's commercial music library, which is smaller than the full library — check it regularly, because it is updated.

The key is to use a trending sound without letting it swallow your message. The sound should support your video, not replace it. If a funny sound is trending, use it over a video of you packing orders, with text on the screen explaining your product. What you should not do is post a video that is only the trending sound with no product, no message, and no call to action. That video might get views, but it will not get you customers. Hashtags help TikTok understand what your video is about. Mix broad ones like #SmallBusinessNigeria with specific ones like #WigsInLagos, add your location to at least one hashtag, and use three to five hashtags per video. Also follow trends in your niche — the people watching those videos are already interested in what you sell.

## When to Post and How Often

Consistency beats perfection on TikTok. One good video every day is far better than ten videos in one day followed by two weeks of silence. The algorithm rewards accounts that show up regularly, and your followers will forget you quickly if you go quiet. Aim for one video a day when starting out, and two or three a day if you can manage it. Once you get into a routine — film in the morning, edit during a quiet moment, post in the evening — it becomes part of your business day, like opening your shop.

For most Nigerian audiences, the busiest times are early morning, between 7am and 9am; lunchtime, between 12pm and 2pm; and evening, between 7pm and 10pm. Weekends are also strong, especially Saturday mornings and Sunday evenings. These are starting points — after a few weeks, check your analytics to see the exact hours your followers are most active, and post in those windows. Do not post and disappear. Stay on the app for fifteen to thirty minutes after posting and reply to comments quickly: TikTok notices early engagement and pushes the video to more people. Treat every comment as a customer walking into your shop.

## Turn Views Into WhatsApp Orders

Views are not sales. A video with fifty thousand views and zero orders is a failure for a business, while a video with five hundred views and ten orders is a success. Every video should tell the viewer exactly what to do next. Say it out loud, and write it as text on the screen: "Send us a DM to order", "WhatsApp number in bio", or "Click the link in our bio to order". Most viewers need to be told, clearly and simply.

WhatsApp is where most Nigerian TikTok sales actually happen. A customer sees your product on TikTok, taps your bio, and lands on your WhatsApp — so your WhatsApp Business profile must be ready before your videos go viral. Set up a catalogue with your products, prices, and clear photos, and write a short welcome message answering the questions every customer asks: your price, your location, your delivery fee, and how long delivery takes. When a customer messages you, reply fast — one who waits four hours has often already bought from someone else. Make paying easy. Most Nigerian customers will pay by bank transfer, so keep your account details ready to copy and paste, and confirm every payment quickly with a receipt and a thank-you message. Track what works: keep a simple record of which videos brought the most WhatsApp messages and orders, and do more of what works.

## TikTok Shop Basics for Nigerian Sellers

TikTok Shop lets customers buy without leaving the app, and it is gradually becoming available to more sellers. If you have access to it in Nigeria, set it up: a customer sees your product, taps, and buys, without switching to WhatsApp or a website. Register as a seller, provide your business details, and list your products with clear photos, honest descriptions, and correct prices. A product with blurry photos and a one-line description will not sell, even if the price is good.

If TikTok Shop is not available to you yet, or your product is a service, WhatsApp remains your shop — and that is completely fine. Many successful Nigerian sellers do all their selling through WhatsApp. What matters is the path from video to payment: make it as short and clear as you can. And be honest. Do not use another seller's photos, do not promise delivery in one day when it takes three, and do not advertise one price in your video and charge a different price on WhatsApp. A single dishonest video can damage your reputation for months.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

The first mistake is buying followers. Bought followers are fake accounts that never watch, comment, or buy. TikTok's algorithm notices when your videos get no engagement, and it stops showing them to real people. A small account with five hundred real followers who watch and buy is worth far more than one with fifty thousand fake followers.

The second mistake is copying other sellers' videos exactly. Customers notice, and TikTok notices too. Take inspiration from what works, but make it your own. Your accent, your shop, your customers, and your story are what make your videos different. The third mistake is posting only adverts. If every video is "buy this, we sell that", people will get bored and stop watching. Mix selling with useful tips, funny moments, behind-the-scenes clips, and customer stories. Think of your page as a shop with a friendly owner, not a billboard.

The fourth mistake is giving up too early. Many sellers post five or ten videos, get a few hundred views, and conclude TikTok does not work for them. That is like opening a shop for one week and closing it because the first customers did not buy everything. Give yourself at least sixty days of consistent posting before you judge. The fifth mistake is ignoring the comments. Comments are where sales happen: a person who asks "How much?" or "Do you deliver to Enugu?" is ready to buy. Reply quickly, politely, and with the exact information they asked for. Answer in public when you can, because ten other people reading the comments have the same question.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need a lot of followers to sell on TikTok in Nigeria?

No. TikTok shows your videos to strangers, not only to your followers, so a new account can reach thousands of people with its first videos. Many Nigerian sellers make their first sales with under one thousand followers.

### How many times should I post on TikTok each day?

Aim for one video a day as a minimum, and two or three a day if you can manage it. Consistency matters more than volume.

### What is the best time to post on TikTok in Nigeria?

For most audiences, the busiest times are 7am to 9am, 12pm to 2pm, and 7pm to 10pm, with weekends also strong. Check your analytics after a few weeks for the exact hours your followers are most active.

### Can I sell services on TikTok, or only physical products?

You can sell services very well — tailors, makeup artists, cleaners, photographers, tutors, and caterers all use TikTok to find customers. Show the before and after, show the process, and share customer reviews.

### Should I pay for TikTok ads as a small business?

Paid ads can help, but they are not the first step. Start with free, organic videos and learn what your audience responds to. Once a video already performs well on its own, you can put a small budget behind it. Master the free reach first.

### How do I handle customers who want to pay on delivery?

Pay on delivery is common in Nigeria, and many customers prefer it because they have been scammed before. If you can offer it, it will win you customers. If you cannot, be honest about it: show your face and your shop, share customer reviews, and always deliver what you promised.
