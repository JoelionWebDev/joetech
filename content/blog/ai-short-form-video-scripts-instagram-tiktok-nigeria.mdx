It is 7pm. You sit down to "make content," and an hour later you've scrolled your feed, matched three outfits, and posted nothing. Sound familiar? The problem is almost never your filming — it is that you don't know *what to say* in 30 seconds.

The good news: writing short-form video scripts is a learnable skill, and in 2026 AI is the difference between staring at a blank phone screen and producing a week of content in an hour. The keyword is *scripts that actually grow* — not the generic, wordy AI paragraphs that make every video feel like the same boring advertisement.

Here is the exact framework Nigerian creators and businesses use to write Reels and TikTok scripts with AI — and turn them into videos people finish, share, and follow for.

---

## Step 1: Understand what a short-form script is for

Before a single word, understand the job. A 30–60 second script has exactly three jobs:

1. **Stop the scroll.** You have 1–2 seconds to make someone pause.
2. **Hold attention.** Every line must earn the next one — or the viewer is gone.
3. **Lead somewhere.** A save, a follow, a WhatsApp click, a comment — pick one goal.

Every script decision — length, hooks, and structure — serves those three jobs. If a sentence does not stop, hold, or lead, cut it.

> An average script is a message. A great script is a journey someone wants to finish.

---

## Step 2: Write for one person, not "the algorithm"

The biggest beginner error is writing for an algorithm. Your video reaches *one human* at a time, scrolling on a phone likely during a break or commute in Lagos or Abuja.

**The question to answer for every script:** *"Why should this specific person stay for 30 seconds?"*

- Instead of "5 waste management tips," use "If you live in a Lagos flat, this one bill is probably eating your money."
- Instead of "How to start a business," use "What I'd do differently if I started my braiding business today."

AI is brilliant at generating *options* once you feed it this specificity. Vague prompts give vague scripts; specific prompts give scripts people actually watch.

---

## Step 3: Master the hook — the make-or-break of the video

The first sentence decides everything. If AI gives you "In today's video, we'll discuss…" delete it and try again until you have something that makes a Lagos viewer stop mid-scroll.

**Hook formulas that work exceptionally well in Nigerian short-form:**

- **The warning:** "Stop posting your content at 2pm — here's why it's dying."
- **The contrarian claim:** "I stopped charging by the hour, and my clients started paying *more*."
- **The specific promise:** "This one WhatsApp trick got 3 clients booked this week."
- **The story opener:** "My first client almost never paid me. Here's what I did differently."
- **The audience call:** "If you sell aso-oke bags and people keep asking for a 'price list,' do this."

**The 3-second test:** read the hook aloud. If you wouldn't stop scrolling for it, neither will your audience. Generate five hooks with AI, pick the best, keep the rest for later.

---

## Step 4: Give AI a script structure it can fill

Raw AI scripts are long and lifeless. Instead, give the AI a *skeleton* and let it dress the bones. A proven short-form structure:

**The 30-second structure:**

1. **Hook (0–2 sec):** One line that stops the scroll.
2. **Stakes (2–6 sec):** "It's costing you money/you're losing clients time." Urgency, short.
3. **Core content (6–25 sec):** 2–4 rapid, specific points. Fast cuts, one idea per breath.
4. **Result (25–28 sec):** What changed when you did this.
5. **CTA (28–30 sec):** One clear ask — follow, save, comment, or WhatsApp.

**A practical AI prompt:**

> "Write a 30-second Instagram Reel script for a Nigerian audience. Topic: [your topic]. Hook must stop the scroll in 2 seconds. Include 3 rapid specific points with Nigerian examples, an urgency line, a result line, and end with a save/comment CTA. Tone: [friendly/expert/playful]. No generic advice, no 'hello everyone', natural speech, easy to say out loud."

Then — this is the secret — **read it out loud and cut everything that sounds stiff.** AI writes for the page; you speak for the mic. Nigerian audiences respond to conversational energy, not essay sentences.

---

## Step 5: Add the Nigerian texture that makes it authentic

The fastest way to make an AI script sound like every other brand video is to strip out context. Your audience is Nigerian — let the script sound it. You don't need slang, but you do need *local truth*.

**Texture that works:**

- Real examples: naira amounts, Lagos traffic, Aba markets, "naija" data prices, Paystack/Flutterwave, WhatsApp delivery.
- Real constraints:  plan networks, weekend markets, "when data is cheap," Ajo and cooperative saving.
- Real problems: trust issues, "how much?" fear, knowing the price before ordering.

Example: an AI script about "using AI to write your CV" becomes far more valuable when it says *"a Lagos recruiter is spending 15 seconds on your CV — here's how to make those 15 seconds count for a Nigerian job."* The same framework, completely different results.

---

## Step 6: Batch-produce a week of videos in an hour

The daily "what should I post today" battle kills most creators. Solve it with a weekly batch.

**The batch workflow:**

1. **Monday (30 min):** List 5 audience questions you hear weekly. Those are your video titles.
2. **Tuesday (20 min):** For each title, generate 5 hooks + a 30-second script with AI.
3. **Wednesday (30 min):** Edit each script out loud, cut stiffness, add your local examples.
4. **Thursday–Sunday:** Record and post one per day. Same script, own delivery.

Follow this rhythm and you always know exactly what to film — no more 7pm panic. Consistency beats perfection, and a weekly batch makes consistency possible.

---

## Step 7: Stick to the retension metrics that matter

After you post, watch the numbers — not for ego, for information. The three that matter for growth:

- **Average watch time.** If people drop in the first 2 seconds, your hook is the problem.
- **Shares and saves.** Better than likes. Saves mean "I want this later" — keep breaking ideas into steps.
- **Profile visits and follows.** If watches are fine but follows are low, your CTA asks for the wrong action — or your content doesn't match your profile promise.

One video a week that finishes beats five that nobody watches. Quality, relevance, and consistency — that's the actual growth formula.

---

## Your first 7 days of scripts

- **Day 1:** List 7 real questions your audience asks you (or about your niche).
- **Day 2:** Turn each into a hook using the five hook formulas.
- **Day 3:** Generate 30-second scripts for the 3 strongest with AI.
- **Day 4:** Edit them out loud; add your Nigerian context.
- **Day 5:** Film all three (phone, natural light, speak to camera).
- **Day 6:** Post one. Keep two for later.
- **Day 7:** Study the watch time, pick your best hook, and base next week on it.
