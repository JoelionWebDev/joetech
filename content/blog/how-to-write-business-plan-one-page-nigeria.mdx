Most Nigerian small business owners never write a business plan. Not because they do not need one, but because the idea feels heavy. Twenty pages. Tables. Charts. Formal language. When you are selling food in Surulere, sewing clothes in Yaba, or running a cleaning service in Lekki, that kind of document feels like homework for someone else.

A one-page business plan solves that problem. It is exactly what it sounds like: your whole plan on a single page. The problem you solve, the people you serve, what you sell, how you make money, what it costs, and what you want to achieve in the next few months. Nothing more, nothing less.


## What a One-Page Business Plan Is — and What It Is Not

A one-page business plan is a short, working summary of your business. It fits on one A4 page. You can read it in three minutes and explain it to someone in five, without opening a laptop.

It is not a shortened version of a long plan. You are not trying to squeeze twenty pages into one. You are choosing only the essentials and writing each one in two to four lines. If a sentence does not help you make a decision, sell, or spend money wisely, it does not belong on the page.



## Before You Write: Gather Four Things

Do not start with a blank page and hope for inspiration. Spend twenty minutes gathering four things first. This makes the writing fast and honest.

First, your prices. Write down what you charge for each product or service, and what it costs you to deliver it. If you sell a plate of food for ₦3,500, how much do the ingredients, gas, packaging and transport cost? If you do not know, track one day properly this week.

Second, your customers. Who buys from you now, or who do you want to buy from you? Be specific. "Everybody" is not a customer. "Working mothers in Ikeja who order lunch for the office" is a customer.

Third, your monthly costs. Rent, transport, data, airtime, staff, packaging, ingredients, fuel or generator costs. Add them up roughly. You do not need perfect figures. You need honest figures.

Fourth, your goal for the next three to six months. One clear goal, such as reaching 40 paying customers a week, or earning ₦400,000 in monthly sales. A plan without a goal is just a description.

With these four things in front of you, writing the page takes less than an hour.

## The 9 Sections That Belong on One Page

A good one-page business plan has nine short sections. Each one earns its place because it answers a question you face every week. Keep each section to two to four lines, using short sentences and real numbers.

### 1. The Problem

What problem do your customers have, and why does it hurt? Write it in their words, not yours.

Good example: "Office workers in Ikeja struggle to find clean, tasty lunch delivered on time. Most nearby options are slow, oily, or run out before 1 pm."


### 2. Your Solution

What do you offer that fixes the problem? Describe it in one or two sentences, and say what makes it different.

Good example: "We cook fresh lunch daily and deliver to offices in Ikeja before 12:30 pm. Customers order on WhatsApp the night before and choose from three meals."


### 3. Target Customers

Name the people you serve. Be narrow on purpose. A small business that tries to serve everybody ends up serving nobody well.

Write two or three lines covering who they are, where they are, and how they buy. For example: "Office workers and small business owners in Ikeja GRA and Allen Avenue, aged 25 to 45. They order lunch on WhatsApp and pay by bank transfer. Most earn a steady salary and eat out at least four days a week."


### 4. Products and Services

List what you sell. Not everything you could sell one day. What you sell now, and what brings in the most money. Keep it to three to five items, with a price next to each one. For example:

- Jollof rice with chicken — ₦3,500
- Fried rice with turkey — ₦4,000
- Ofada rice with stew — ₦4,500
- Office lunch pack for 10 people — ₦32,000


### 5. How You Make Money

This is the section many owners skip, and it is the most important one. Answer three questions in three or four lines: what do you charge, how often do customers buy, and how much do you keep after costs?

Start with your main product and work out the profit on one sale. If a plate of food sells for ₦3,500 and costs ₦2,200 to make and deliver, you keep ₦1,300. That is your margin. Now ask how many plates you need to sell each day to cover your monthly costs and reach your goal.

Write it plainly on the page: "We sell 60 plates a day at an average of ₦3,800. Average profit per plate is ₦1,300, giving about ₦78,000 a day before rent and other fixed costs. Monthly target is ₦1,800,000 in sales."


### 6. Marketing: How Customers Find You

How will people hear about you, and how will they order? Pick two or three channels that actually work for your customers. Do not list ten.

For many Lagos small businesses, the strongest channels are WhatsApp, referrals from happy customers, and being visible where customers already gather. A food vendor might write: "Daily menu broadcast on WhatsApp to 200 contacts; referrals — every new customer who brings a colleague gets a free drink; a small banner and flyers around Ikeja offices; food tastings for two offices a month."


Write down what you will do each week, not just the channel name. "Post on WhatsApp" is a wish. "Send the menu to 200 contacts every evening by 7 pm" is a plan. Also write down how a customer orders and pays. If ordering is hard, marketing does not matter.

### 7. Costs and Money Needed

This section has two parts: what it costs to run the business each month, and what money you need to start or grow it.

List your main monthly costs with rough figures. For example, a small food business might list: "Ingredients ₦450,000; gas and transport ₦120,000; packaging ₦60,000; rent share ₦50,000; data and airtime ₦15,000; helper's pay ₦60,000. Total about ₦755,000 a month."


Then write what money you need and what it is for. Be specific: "We need ₦350,000 to buy a bigger freezer, a second gas cylinder, and packaging for one month. This will let us serve 80 plates a day instead of 60." That sentence is far more useful than "we need capital." It shows what the money does and what changes afterwards.


### 8. Goals and Milestones

Pick two or three goals for the next three to six months, and make each one measurable. A goal you cannot measure is a wish.

Good goals look like this: "Reach 80 plates sold daily by the end of March." "Get 15 offices ordering lunch at least three times a week." "Grow the WhatsApp broadcast list from 200 to 500 contacts." "Earn ₦1,800,000 in monthly sales by June."

Under each goal, add one milestone: the small step that shows you are on track. For example, under "reach 80 plates daily," the milestone could be "add two new offices this month" or "sell out by 1 pm three days a week."


### 9. What Makes You Different

This is your edge in one or two lines. Why should a customer choose you over the next seller?

It could be speed: "We deliver before 12:30 pm, every day, or the delivery is free." It could be trust: "Same cleaner every visit, with a supervisor's inspection." It could be specialisation: "We only make ready-to-wear outfits in sizes 8 to 20, so our fit is consistent."


## A Full Worked Example: One Page for a Lagos Food Business

Reading about sections is useful. Seeing a full page is better. Here is a complete one-page business plan for a fictional but realistic Lagos business: a lunch delivery service in Ikeja called Mama B's Kitchen. Imagine all of this fitted on one A4 page, with short lines and clear headings.

**The problem:** Office workers in Ikeja struggle to find clean, tasty lunch delivered on time. Nearby options are slow, run out before 1 pm, or arrive cold. Workers waste their break queuing or settling for food they do not enjoy.

**Our solution:** Mama B's Kitchen cooks fresh lunch daily and delivers to offices in Ikeja GRA and Allen Avenue before 12:30 pm. Customers order on WhatsApp the night before and choose from three meals. Food arrives hot, in sealed packs, with a drink option.

**Target customers:** Office workers and small business owners in Ikeja, aged 25 to 45. They order lunch on WhatsApp, pay by bank transfer, and eat out at least four days a week. Our best customers are offices with 5 to 20 staff who order together.

**Products and services:** Jollof rice with chicken ₦3,500; fried rice with turkey ₦4,000; ofada rice with stew ₦4,500; office lunch pack for 10 people ₦32,000; weekend party packs from ₦60,000.

**How we make money:** Average plate price is ₦3,800 and average profit per plate is ₦1,300. We sell 60 plates a day, five days a week, giving about ₦1,140,000 in monthly sales. Office packs and weekend orders add extra income on top of the daily plates.

**Marketing:** Daily menu broadcast on WhatsApp to 200 contacts by 7 pm; referrals — a customer who brings a colleague gets a free drink; flyers and a banner around Ikeja offices; free tasting for two new offices every month.

**Costs and money needed:** Monthly costs are about ₦755,000, covering ingredients, gas, transport, packaging, rent share, data, and one helper's pay. We need ₦350,000 for a bigger freezer, a second gas cylinder, and one month of packaging, which will let us serve 80 plates a day.

**Goals and milestones:** Reach 80 plates sold daily by the end of March. Get 15 offices ordering at least three times a week. Grow the WhatsApp list from 200 to 500 contacts. First milestone: add two new offices this month and sell out by 1 pm three days a week.

**What makes us different:** We deliver before 12:30 pm every day, or the delivery is free. Our menu is fixed at three meals, so the food is always fresh and never rushed.



## How to Fit Everything on One Page

Fitting nine sections on one page is the hardest part, so here are practical ways to do it.


Use numbers instead of adjectives. "Many customers" becomes "60 plates a day." "Affordable prices" becomes "₦3,500 per plate." Numbers take less space and mean more.

Use bullet points for products and costs. A list of five items with prices is easier to read and takes less space than a paragraph saying the same thing.


Use a simple layout. Put your business name and a one-line description at the top, then bold headings for each section. Use a readable font size. If you have to shrink the text until nobody can read it, you have written too much.


## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Trying to fit a 20-page plan on one page.** This is the biggest mistake. Owners cram in everything: history, market analysis, five-year dreams, and long descriptions. The result is tiny text nobody reads. A one-page plan is a choice about what matters most. Leave the rest out.

**Vague numbers.** "We will make good profit" means nothing. "We sell 60 plates a day at ₦3,800, keeping ₦1,300 per plate" means everything. If you do not know your numbers yet, spend a week tracking them, then write the page. Guessing is fine as a starting point, but label it as an estimate and update it when real figures come in.

**No costs on the page.** Many plans list income and forget costs. A business that sells ₦1,000,000 a month and spends ₦950,000 is very different from one that spends ₦600,000. Put your costs on the page, even if the figures are rough. Honest costs protect you from nasty surprises.


**No clear customer.** "Everybody in Lagos" is not a plan. Pick a group, name where they are, and describe how they buy. You can always expand later. Starting narrow helps you serve people well and spend your effort where it works.

**A plan with no goal.** A page that describes your business but sets no target gives you nothing to aim at. Always end with two or three measurable goals and a date. That turns the page from a description into a plan.


## How to Use Your Finished Page

A finished one-page plan is only useful if you use it. Here are five practical ways to put it to work.

**1. Use it to pitch your business.** When someone asks what you do, do not ramble. Walk them through the page: the problem, your solution, who you serve, and how you make money. In five minutes they will understand your business better than most owners understand their own. This works with a potential partner, a supplier, a landlord, or a programme officer.

**2. Use it when you apply for support.** Some grants, competitions, training programmes and cooperatives ask for a business plan. A clear one-page plan is often enough at the first stage, and it is far better than a long document copied from the internet. It shows you know your customers, your costs and your goals. Keep a clean printed copy and a PDF on your phone.

**3. Use it to guide daily decisions.** Before you spend money, check the page. Does this purchase help you reach your goal? Does this new product fit your target customers? Does this price keep your profit per sale healthy? If the answer is no, wait. The page is your filter for saying yes and no quickly.

**4. Use it to track progress weekly.** Pick one morning a week, even fifteen minutes, and compare the page with reality. Are you selling the number of plates you planned? Are your costs close to your estimate? Is your WhatsApp list growing? Write the real figures next to the planned ones. Over a month, you will see patterns: which days sell best, which product earns most, and where money leaks away.


Keep the page where you can see it. Pin it near your workspace, save it on your phone, or keep a copy in your bag. A plan you see daily shapes your choices. A plan you file away does not.

## Your One-Page Checklist

Before you call your plan finished, run through this checklist and tick each item honestly.

- It fits on one A4 page with readable text.
- The problem is written in the customer's words.
- The solution says what you do and what makes it different.
- Target customers are named, with where they are and how they buy.
- Products and prices are listed, with your best sellers first.
- You know your profit on one sale and how many sales you need.
- Marketing lists two or three channels and what you do on each weekly.
- Monthly costs are listed, with a total figure.
- Money needed is named, with what it buys and what changes.
- Two or three goals are measurable and have dates.
- A friend can read it in three minutes and explain your business back to you.


## Start With One Page Today

You do not need permission, software, or a perfect draft to start. Open a notebook or a document, write the nine headings from this guide, and fill each one in two to four lines. Use the figures you know and estimate the rest. Give yourself one hour.


The businesses that last in Nigeria are rarely the ones with the longest documents. They are the ones whose owners know their customers, know their numbers, and know what they are aiming at next. One page is enough to hold all three. Write yours today, and let it guide what you do tomorrow morning.
