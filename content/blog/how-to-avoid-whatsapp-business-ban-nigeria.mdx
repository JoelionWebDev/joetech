Imagine waking up, opening WhatsApp Business, and seeing a message that your number has been banned. Your chats are gone. Your customers cannot reach you. Your catalogue, your groups, and years of conversations are suddenly out of reach. For a Nigerian small business that sells mainly through WhatsApp, that is not a small inconvenience. It can stop your income for days or weeks.

It happens more often than many vendors realise. A fashion seller in Lagos loses her number after sending the same broadcast to hundreds of people. A food vendor in Abuja gets restricted after too many customers report his messages as spam. A phone accessories dealer in Ikeja finds his account blocked after using an unofficial version of WhatsApp. In each case, the owner did not set out to break the rules. They simply did not know what the rules were, or they pushed too hard to make sales.

The good news is that most bans are avoidable. WhatsApp bans numbers for clear reasons: spam, bulk messaging to strangers, misleading behaviour, and using unofficial apps. If you understand those reasons and build safer habits, you can keep your number healthy for years. This guide explains what causes bans in Nigeria, how to message customers safely, and what to do if your number is ever restricted.

## Why WhatsApp Bans Business Numbers

WhatsApp is built for personal conversation first. When a business account starts behaving like a spam machine, WhatsApp's systems notice. They look at patterns: how many new chats you start each day, how many people block or report you, how quickly you send messages, and whether the people you message have your number saved.

A ban usually comes in two forms. A temporary restriction limits what you can do for a few hours or days. You may not be able to send messages to new contacts, join groups, or use some features. A permanent ban removes your access to that number entirely. You may be able to appeal, but many permanent bans are not reversed, and you may need to start again with a new number, which means telling every customer about the change.

WhatsApp does not always warn you before a ban. Sometimes you get a notice that your account is under review. Often, the first sign is that your messages stop delivering or your account is locked when you open the app. That is why prevention matters far more than appeal. Once a ban happens, recovery is slow and uncertain.

## Use Only the Official WhatsApp Business App

The first rule is simple: use only the official WhatsApp Business app downloaded from the Google Play Store or the Apple App Store. Do not use modified versions such as GB WhatsApp, FM WhatsApp, or any app that promises extra features, hidden online status, or unlimited messaging. These unofficial apps violate WhatsApp's terms, and WhatsApp actively detects and bans numbers that use them.

Unofficial apps may look attractive. They offer themes, the ability to see deleted messages, or to send larger files. But the price is your business number. Many Nigerian vendors have lost numbers they used for years because they installed a modified app for a small extra feature. No feature is worth losing your customer base.

If you have used an unofficial app before, switch back to the official app now, before a ban happens. Uninstall the modified app, download WhatsApp Business from the official store, and register your number there. Your chats may not all transfer, so back up what you can first. It is better to lose some chat history now than to lose the number later.

## Only Message People Who Expect to Hear From You

The most common cause of bans is messaging strangers. When you send a promotional message to someone who never gave you their number or never asked to hear from you, they are likely to block you or report you as spam. A few reports will not ban you, but many reports in a short time will.

Build your contact list properly. Add customers who bought from you, enquired about a product, filled in a form, or asked to join your broadcast list. Ask for permission clearly: "Would you like us to send you new arrivals and offers on WhatsApp?" If they say yes, save their number and note their interest. If they say no, respect it.

Do not buy contact lists. Lists sold online with thousands of Nigerian phone numbers may look like a shortcut to sales, but they are a fast route to a ban. The people on those lists never asked to hear from you, they do not know your business, and they will report your messages. The seller of the list takes your money and disappears. You keep the ban.

Do not add people to groups without their consent. Adding strangers to a sales group is another common complaint. Many people leave the group immediately and report the admin. If you run a group for customers, invite people with a link and let them choose to join. A smaller group of willing customers is far more valuable than a large group of annoyed strangers.

Be careful with numbers you collect at events, markets or trade fairs. A person who gave you a business card at a trade fair did not necessarily agree to receive weekly promotions. Send one polite introductory message, remind them where you met, and ask if they want to stay in touch. If they do not reply, do not keep messaging them.

## Send Messages Like a Human, Not a Machine

WhatsApp watches for behaviour that looks automated. Sending the same message to fifty people in two minutes, copying and pasting identical text to everyone, or starting dozens of new chats in an hour are all red flags. Even if your intentions are good, the pattern looks like spam.

Slow down. Space your messages out. If you need to send a new arrival notice to customers, send it to a small group at a time over the day, not all at once. Personalise each message where you can. Use the customer's name, refer to what they bought before, or mention the product they asked about. A personal message is less likely to be reported and more likely to get a reply.

Vary your messages. Do not send the exact same broadcast text to everyone every week. Change the wording, change the image, and tailor the message to the customer. WhatsApp's systems notice identical messages sent in bulk, and customers notice too. A message that feels copied feels like spam.

Respect the time of day. Sending promotions at 2 am or repeatedly messaging someone who has not replied is a quick way to get blocked. Stick to reasonable hours, such as 8 am to 8 pm, and stop after one or two follow-ups if a customer does not respond. A single polite follow-up the next day is enough. Five messages in a row is not.

Keep your message frequency sensible. Daily promotions to the same people will tire them out, even if they liked your business at first. Most small businesses do well with two or three promotional messages a week to their broadcast list, and personal replies whenever a customer writes first. Let customers who want more frequent updates join your status viewers or your group, where they can check your offers when they choose.

## Use Broadcast Lists Correctly

Broadcast lists are a useful WhatsApp Business feature, but many Nigerian vendors use them wrongly. A broadcast sends the same message to many people individually, but it only delivers to people who have your number saved in their contacts. That rule is your protection. If someone has not saved your number, they will not receive the broadcast, and you will not know.

That is why you should ask customers to save your number. When a new customer buys from you, say: "Please save our number so you can receive our new arrivals and offers." You can also put this request in your business profile, your status, and your greeting message. The more customers who save your number, the better your broadcasts will deliver.

Keep your broadcast lists small and organised. Instead of one list of five hundred people, create smaller lists by interest or location: Lagos customers, Abuja customers, wholesale buyers, retail buyers, or people who bought a particular product. Smaller lists let you send more relevant messages, and relevant messages get fewer complaints.

Clean your lists regularly. Remove numbers that never reply, numbers that have not been active for months, and anyone who asks to stop receiving messages. If someone replies "Stop" or "Remove me", remove them immediately and apologise. Continuing to message someone who asked to stop is one of the fastest ways to earn a report.

Do not use broadcast lists for cold outreach. A broadcast is for existing customers and people who opted in. If you want to reach new customers, use Instagram, Facebook, a referral, or a proper advert, and let them come to you. Cold broadcast messages are the single most reported behaviour on WhatsApp Business.

## Keep Your Profile and Behaviour Trustworthy

WhatsApp also looks at whether your account looks like a real business. Complete your business profile fully: your business name, category, address or area, opening hours, email, website if you have one, and a short description of what you sell. Add a clear profile photo, such as your logo. A complete profile tells WhatsApp and your customers that you are a genuine business.

Use a business name that matches your other channels. If your Instagram page is "Ada's Kitchen" and your WhatsApp profile says something different, customers get confused and may report the number as suspicious. Consistency builds trust and reduces reports.

Avoid misleading messages. Do not promise free products, fake giveaways, or prizes that do not exist to get people to message you. Do not use another business's name or logo. Do not send links that look suspicious or use URL shorteners that hide where a link goes. If you share a link, use your real website or a link customers recognise.

Watch your content. Do not forward chain messages, false news, or content that violates WhatsApp's rules. Do not send adult content, hateful messages, or threats, even as a joke. Accounts that share such content are banned quickly.

Keep your phone number secure. Do not share your WhatsApp verification code with anyone. Scammers in Nigeria often pretend to be WhatsApp support and ask for the six-digit code sent to your phone. If you share it, they can take over your number and use it to send spam, which can lead to a ban on your own number. Turn on two-step verification in your WhatsApp settings and choose a PIN you will remember.

## Warm Up a New Number Slowly

If you are starting with a new business number, do not begin by messaging hundreds of people on the first day. New numbers are watched more closely. A sudden burst of activity from a fresh number looks suspicious.

Start slowly. In the first week, chat with people you know, reply to customers who message you first, and build up your activity gradually. Add your number to your Instagram bio, your flyers, and your business cards so customers come to you. Let enquiries arrive naturally.

Over the next two or three weeks, increase your activity step by step. Start a small broadcast list with your most loyal customers. Join only groups that are relevant to your business. Avoid adding many new contacts in a single day. By the end of the first month, your number will have a normal pattern of activity that looks like a real business, not a spam account.

This also applies if you change your number after a ban. Many vendors get a new number and immediately repeat the same behaviour that caused the first ban. The second ban often comes faster. Take the time to warm up the new number and fix the habits that caused the problem.

## What to Do If Your Number Is Restricted

Even careful businesses can face a restriction. If you open WhatsApp and see that your number is banned or under review, do not panic, and do not immediately try to register the same number with an unofficial app or a trick you found online. That usually makes things worse.

First, read the message on your screen. If it offers a review or appeal option, use it. Explain briefly and honestly what your business does and that you will follow the rules. Do not write a long essay. A short, polite appeal is more likely to be read. If the ban was temporary, you may be told how long to wait. Respect that waiting period.

While you wait, tell your customers through another channel. Post on your Instagram page, your Facebook page, or your status from another number that your WhatsApp is temporarily unavailable and that you will be back soon. Give them an alternative way to reach you, such as a second number, an email, or your Instagram DM. This keeps your customers informed and reduces lost sales.

Do not create many new accounts to get around a ban. WhatsApp links accounts by device and behaviour, and creating several numbers in a short time can lead to all of them being banned. If your appeal fails and the ban is permanent, accept it, set up a new number properly, warm it up slowly, and inform your customers through your other channels.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many messages can I send per day without getting banned?

There is no fixed number published by WhatsApp. The risk depends on how many people block or report you, how many of your messages go to strangers, and how fast you send them. A business that messages existing customers who reply and save its number can send far more messages safely than one that messages strangers. Focus on consent and replies, not on a daily count.

### Will using WhatsApp Business instead of regular WhatsApp protect me?

Using the official WhatsApp Business app is necessary, but it does not make you immune. Business accounts are still banned for spam, bulk messaging and misleading behaviour. The app gives you business tools, but you still need to follow the rules.

### Can I get unbanned if my number is banned?

You can appeal through the app, and temporary bans are often lifted after the waiting period. Permanent bans are harder to reverse. If your appeal is rejected, you will likely need a new number. That is why prevention is more important than appeal.

### Is it safe to send broadcasts to my customers?

Yes, if they are existing customers who gave you their number and expect your messages, and if they have saved your number. Keep lists small, send relevant messages, and remove anyone who asks to stop. Do not use broadcasts for cold outreach.

### Does using GB WhatsApp really cause bans?

Yes. Unofficial versions violate WhatsApp's terms, and WhatsApp actively bans numbers detected using them. The risk is real and well known in Nigeria. Use only the official app from the Play Store or App Store.

### What should I do if many customers report my messages?

Stop and review your messaging. Are you sending too often? Are your messages relevant? Did those customers agree to hear from you? Reduce your frequency, clean your list, and focus on customers who reply and engage. Reports are feedback that something needs to change.

## What to Do Next

1. Check that you are using only the official WhatsApp Business app from the Play Store or App Store, and uninstall any modified version.
2. Turn on two-step verification and never share your six-digit verification code with anyone.
3. Complete your business profile with your correct name, address, hours, and a clear logo or photo.
4. Clean your broadcast lists: remove people who never reply, and remove anyone who asked to stop immediately.
5. Ask your regular customers to save your number so your broadcasts deliver properly.
6. Slow down your messaging: send to smaller groups over the day, personalise messages, and stop after one polite follow-up.
7. If you have a new number, warm it up slowly over two to three weeks before increasing your activity.

Your WhatsApp number is one of the most valuable assets your business owns. It holds your customers, your orders, and your reputation. Protect it the way you would protect your shop keys. Message only people who expect to hear from you, send messages like a human, and use the official app. Do those things consistently, and your number should stay safe while your business keeps growing.
