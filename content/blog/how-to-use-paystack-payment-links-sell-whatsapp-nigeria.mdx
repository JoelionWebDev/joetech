WhatsApp is where many Nigerian small businesses already sell. A customer sees a dress on your status, a plate of food in a group, or a pair of shoes in a chat, and asks, "How much?" and "How do I pay?" Too often, the sale dies at that point. The customer is asked to copy an account number, type it carefully, send the money, and then send a screenshot. Any mistake, delay, or doubt can end the sale.

Paystack payment links solve that problem. A payment link is a simple web page for one product, or one amount, that a customer can open and pay from with a card, bank transfer, or USSD. You do not need a website, a shop, or a POS machine. You create the link in a few minutes, paste it into WhatsApp, and the customer taps and pays. You receive a notification when the payment succeeds, and the money settles into your bank account.

This guide is for sellers in Lagos, Abuja, Port Harcourt, Ibadan, Enugu, and everywhere else in Nigeria who already use WhatsApp Business or regular WhatsApp to sell. You will learn what payment links are, how to set up a Paystack account, how to create your first link, how to share it well on WhatsApp, how charges and settlement work, and how to avoid the scams that target online sellers.


## What Is a Paystack Payment Link?

A Paystack payment link is a unique web address that takes a customer straight to a secure payment page. On that page, the customer sees the product name, the price, and the payment options. They enter their name, email or phone number, choose how to pay, and complete the payment. Paystack then tells you the payment was successful.

Think of it like a small online shop for one item. If you sell Ankara fabric for ₦25,000 a yard, you can create a link called "Ankara Fabric - ₦25,000" and share it with anyone who asks. If you sell food packs for ₦8,500, you can create a link for that pack. If you run a service, such as a hair appointment for ₦15,000, you can create a link for a booking deposit. Each link can be used again and again, or you can create a new link for a special offer.

Payment links are different from sending your bank account number. With an account number, the customer must type the number correctly, choose the right bank, and send the money. Then you must check your bank app to confirm it. With a payment link, the amount is already fixed, the customer cannot pay the wrong amount by mistake, and you get a clear record of who paid, for what, and when. That record is useful when a customer says, "I have paid," and you need to check quickly.

Payment links also work for customers who do not have your bank app or who prefer to pay by card. A customer in Abuja can pay a seller in Lagos without visiting a bank. A customer abroad can pay with an international card, depending on the settings you choose. The link opens on a phone, so it works well in WhatsApp, where most customers are already chatting with you on their phones.

## Set Up Your Paystack Account Properly

Before you create a link, you need a Paystack business account. Go to the Paystack website or download the Paystack app and sign up with your business name, email address, and phone number. Use an email you check regularly, because payment notifications and settlement alerts will go there. Use your business name as customers know it, so the payment page looks familiar and trustworthy.

You will be asked to provide business details. For a registered business, this may include your CAC documents and a means of identification. For a sole trader or small seller, Paystack allows a starter account with basic details, and you can upgrade later. Follow the instructions in the app carefully and provide clear photos or scans. Blurry documents cause delays. If you are asked for a bank account for settlement, add the account where you want your money to land. Double-check the account number and bank name before you save it. A wrong digit can send your money to the wrong place or delay settlement.

Complete your business profile. Add a short description of what you sell, your business address or area, and your logo if you have one. Customers see some of this information on the payment page. A complete profile looks more professional than a blank one, and it helps customers trust the link. If you sell from home in Lekki or from a shop in Wuse, say so in a simple way. You do not need to share your exact house number on a public page, but your area and business name help customers feel safe.

Turn on notifications. In the Paystack app or dashboard, allow email and push notifications for successful payments. That way, you know immediately when a customer pays, even if you are not looking at WhatsApp. Also, learn where to find your transaction history. You will need it to confirm payments, settle disputes, and track your daily sales. Spend ten minutes exploring the dashboard before your first sale, so you are not learning it in front of a waiting customer.

## Create Your First Payment Link Step by Step

Creating a payment link is simple once your account is ready. Open the Paystack app or dashboard and look for "Payment Links" or "Create Payment Link." Tap it, and you will see a form. Fill it in carefully, because this is what your customer will see.

First, give the link a clear name. Use the product name and, if useful, the size or variant. "Ladies Handbag - Brown" is clearer than "Product 1." "Jollof Rice Pack - Large" is clearer than "Food." If you sell the same product in different sizes, create a separate link for each size, so the price is always correct. A customer should never have to guess which link is for which size.

Second, set the price in naira. Type the exact amount, for example ₦12,500. Check it twice. A missing zero can cost you money, and an extra zero can scare a customer away. If the price includes delivery, say so in the description. If delivery is extra, say that too, and explain how delivery is calculated. For example, write "Price is ₦12,500. Delivery within Lagos is ₦2,000, outside Lagos is ₦3,500." Clear pricing prevents arguments later.

Third, add a description. Write one or two sentences about the product. Mention the colour, size, material, or what is included. For a food pack, mention the portions. For a service, mention the duration and what the customer should bring. You can also add a product image if the option is available. A clear photo makes the link look more trustworthy and helps the customer confirm they are paying for the right item.

Fourth, decide the settings. You can choose whether the customer must enter their name, email, and phone number. Ask for a phone number if you need to contact them about delivery. You can also set a limit on how many times the link can be used, or an expiry date for a limited offer. For a regular product, leave it open. For a flash sale, set an expiry date so customers know the offer ends soon. When you are happy with the details, save the link. Paystack will give you a web address. Copy it and test it yourself by opening it in your browser. Check the name, price, and description. If anything is wrong, edit it before you share it with customers.

## Share the Link Well on WhatsApp

A payment link only works if customers see it at the right moment. The best time to share it is immediately after a customer asks for the price or says they want to buy. Do not send the link too early, before the customer knows what they are buying. Do not send it too late, after they have lost interest. When a customer says, "I want it," reply with a short message and the link.

A good WhatsApp message is short and clear. For example: "Thank you! The brown handbag is ₦18,000. You can pay securely here: [link]. Delivery within Lagos is ₦2,000. Please send me your delivery address after payment." That message tells the customer the price, the payment method, and the next step. Avoid long messages with too many details. The customer is on a phone, often in a hurry.

Use WhatsApp Business features to make sharing faster. Save your payment links as quick replies, so you can send them with a short code instead of copying and pasting every time. For example, save "/bag" for the handbag link and "/food" for the food pack link. You can also add the link to your catalogue. In WhatsApp Business, each catalogue item can have a link. Add the Paystack link to the product, so a customer who taps the item sees a "Buy" option. You can also post the link on your WhatsApp status with a photo of the product, but keep the status simple. One product, one price, one link. A status with five links is confusing.

When a customer pays, confirm it promptly. Check your Paystack notification, then reply on WhatsApp: "Payment received, thank you! Your order will be delivered tomorrow." That confirmation builds trust and stops the customer from worrying. If the customer says they paid but you have not received a notification, ask them to send a screenshot of the successful payment page or the debit alert, then check your Paystack transaction history. Do not dispatch goods until you have confirmed the payment in Paystack, not just in a screenshot. Screenshots can be faked, but your Paystack dashboard shows the truth.

## Understand Charges, Settlement, and Your Money

Paystack charges a fee on each payment. The fee depends on the payment method and the amount, and it can change, so always check the current charges in the Paystack app or website before you set your prices. Do not assume a fee from last year is still the same. As a guide, many Nigerian payment processors charge a small percentage plus a flat fee, with a cap on local card payments. That means a ₦10,000 payment may cost you a few hundred naira in fees, while a larger payment may hit the cap and cost less in percentage terms.

You need to decide whether to absorb the fee or add it to your price. Many sellers absorb it and treat it as a cost of doing business, like packaging or delivery. Others add a small amount to the price for card payments. Whichever you choose, be honest with customers. Do not advertise a price of ₦10,000 and then ask for ₦10,500 at payment without explaining why. If you add a fee, state it clearly before the customer taps the link.

Settlement is how the money moves from Paystack to your bank account. After a customer pays, the money is held by Paystack for a short period, then sent to your bank account. The timing depends on your settings and the payment method. Some sellers receive money the next business day, others choose a weekly settlement. Check your settlement schedule in the dashboard and plan your cash flow around it. If you need money to buy stock tomorrow, do not rely on a payment that will settle next week. Keep a small cash float for urgent purchases.

Keep a record of every payment link sale. At the end of the day, compare your Paystack transaction history with your WhatsApp chats. Write the total in your sales book or spreadsheet. Note the product, the amount, the fee, and the amount you expect to receive. This helps you track your real profit, not just the sales total. If a payment fails, check the reason in the dashboard. It may be insufficient funds, a wrong card detail, or a network issue. Tell the customer politely and offer another payment method, such as bank transfer or a different card. Do not blame the customer. A failed payment is frustrating for them too.

## Avoid Scams and Protect Your Customers

Payment links are safe when used correctly, but scammers target WhatsApp sellers. The most common scam is a fake payment alert. A customer sends you a screenshot showing a successful transfer or a Paystack payment, but the money never arrives. Always confirm in your Paystack dashboard before you dispatch goods. Do not rely on a screenshot alone. If the dashboard does not show the payment, do not send the product, even if the customer is in a hurry or sounds angry.

Another scam is a fake Paystack link. A scammer may send you a link that looks like Paystack but is designed to steal your login details. Never log in to Paystack through a link sent by a stranger on WhatsApp. Always open the Paystack app directly or type the address yourself. Turn on two-factor authentication if it is available, and do not share your password or one-time code with anyone, including someone who claims to be from Paystack support.

Protect your customers too. Only share your real Paystack link, and make sure the web address starts with the correct Paystack domain. If a customer is unsure, tell them to check that the page shows your business name and the correct price before they pay. Do not ask customers to pay through a different link or account at the last minute. That confuses them and makes them vulnerable to fraud. If you change your payment link, tell your regular customers clearly and explain why.

Keep your phone secure. Use a screen lock, do not leave your Paystack app logged in on a shared phone, and log out if you lend your phone to someone. If your phone is stolen, change your Paystack password immediately and contact your bank. A few minutes of caution can save you from losing a day's sales.

## Grow Your WhatsApp Sales With Payment Links

Once your first links are working, you can use them to grow. Create links for bundles and special offers. For example, if you sell hair products, create a bundle link for shampoo, conditioner, and oil at a discounted price. If you sell food, create a link for a family pack. Bundles increase the average order value and give customers a reason to buy more.

Use payment links for deposits and bookings. If you are a tailor, a makeup artist, or a photographer, ask for a deposit through a payment link before you start work. That protects your time and shows the customer is serious. Create a link called "Booking Deposit - ₦5,000" and share it when a customer books. When the job is done, send a second link for the balance. That system is clearer than asking for a transfer with no record of what it was for.

Promote your links beyond WhatsApp chats. Add a link to your WhatsApp Business catalogue, your Instagram bio, or your Facebook page. If you have a simple website, add the link to a product page. You can also create a QR code for a payment link and print it on a flyer or business card. A customer who scans the code can pay without typing a web address. Track which links perform best. At the end of each week, check your Paystack dashboard. Which product sold most through links? Which link was opened but not paid? If many customers open a link but do not pay, the price may be too high, the description may be unclear, or the payment page may be confusing. Try a clearer description or a smaller deposit option. Small tests lead to better sales over time.

Start with one product, one link, and one WhatsApp chat. Create the link, test it, share it, and confirm the first payment. Once you see it work, create links for your other products. Payment links will not sell your products for you, but they remove friction at the moment a customer is ready to pay. In a busy WhatsApp market, that small difference can turn a chat into a sale, and a one-time buyer into a repeat customer.
