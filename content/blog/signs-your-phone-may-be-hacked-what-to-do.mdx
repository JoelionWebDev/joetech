Your phone holds your money, your messages, your photos, and your business contacts. A hacked phone rarely shows a big warning on the screen. Instead, it shows small, strange signs — things that are easy to dismiss as a bad battery, a network problem, or a phone getting old.

One strange sign on its own usually has an innocent explanation. But when two or three appear together, it is time to take action. Here are the seven signs to watch for, and exactly what to do about them.

## 1. Your Battery Drains Much Faster Than Usual

A phone battery wears down over time, so some decline is normal. But a sudden change is different. If your phone used to last all day and now dies by early afternoon with the same usage, something may be running in the background without your knowledge — harmful apps often run constantly, sending data or tracking your location, and that hidden work drains the battery fast.

**What to do:** Check which apps use the most battery — on Android, Settings, then Battery, then Battery usage; on iPhone, Settings, then Battery. If an app you barely use is at the top, or there is one you do not recognise, remove it and watch whether the battery improves.

## 2. Your Data Finishes Suspiciously Fast

Your data bundle used to last the week. Now it is gone by Wednesday, and you have not watched more videos or downloaded anything new. Where did it go?

A compromised phone can use data in the background — uploading your photos, sending your messages somewhere else, or downloading more harmful software. Data does not vanish on its own, so this is one of the clearest signs.

**What to do:** Check your data usage by app — on Android, Settings, then Connections, then Data usage; on iPhone, Settings, then Mobile Data. Look for an app using far more data than it should, especially one you rarely open.

## 3. Your Phone Gets Hot or Slows Down for No Reason

A phone gets warm when you play games or record video. That is normal. But if your phone feels hot while sitting idle on a table, or it suddenly becomes slow at simple things like opening WhatsApp, something may be running that you cannot see.

Hidden software uses your phone's processor heavily, which creates heat and slows everything else down.

**What to do:** Restart your phone. If it stays hot and slow, remove any app you do not remember installing or no longer trust, and keep your software updated — updates close the gaps harmful apps use to get in.

## 4. Apps You Did Not Install Appear on Your Phone

You open your app drawer and there is an app you have never seen before — a strange cleaner app, a "free" game, a tool with a generic name. You did not install it, and nobody else uses your phone.

An app you did not install is a serious sign. It means something on your phone has permission to add software without asking you, usually after installing an app from outside the official store or tapping a suspicious link.

**What to do:** Uninstall the app immediately. Then check your settings for any app with permission to install other apps (on Android: Settings, Apps, Special access, Install unknown apps). Turn that permission off for every app except your official store. Never install apps from random websites or links sent on WhatsApp.

## 5. Strange Messages, Calls, or Pop-ups

Your phone shows pop-ups claiming you have won a prize, or warning that it is infected and you must tap a link now. Your call log shows calls you did not make, or your SMS app has sent messages you did not write.

Pop-ups that appear even when you are not browsing are a classic sign of an unwanted app. Messages sent from your phone without your knowledge mean something has access to your messaging.

**What to do:** Do not tap any pop-up or follow any link inside one. Note which app was open when it appeared — it is often the source. Check your sent messages and call log, warn your contacts that any strange message from your number was not from you, then find and remove the app.

## 6. Settings Change by Themselves

You notice that your Wi-Fi turns itself on, your mobile data is enabled when you switched it off, or your browser homepage is suddenly different.

Settings do not change on their own. If yours are changing, something on your phone has the power to change them — and that is not normal for an ordinary app.

**What to do:** Put your settings back, then check which apps have special permissions, especially "Device admin" or "Accessibility" access on Android. A legitimate app rarely needs those powers — if an unfamiliar app has them, remove its access and uninstall it.

## 7. Your Accounts Send Things You Did Not Send

Your friends tell you they received a strange message from your WhatsApp or Facebook account. Or you see posts, likes, or emails you did not create. Your phone itself may be fine — but your account has been taken over, often because your password was stolen or guessed.

This is common in Nigeria, where hacked WhatsApp accounts are used to ask the victim's contacts for urgent money transfers, claiming an emergency so the money is sent before the victim notices.

**What to do:** Act immediately. Change the password from a different device if you can, turn on two-step verification wherever it is available, and log out of all sessions so the intruder is kicked out. Then warn your contacts that any money request from your account should be confirmed by a phone call first.

## What to Do Right Now If You Suspect a Hack

If two or more of these signs are present, work through these steps in order:

1. **Turn on aeroplane mode.** This cuts the phone off from the internet and stops anything sending your data out while you work.
2. **Change your important passwords.** Start with your email, because it can reset almost everything else. Then your bank apps, WhatsApp, and social media. Use a different device if you can, and make each password long and unique.
3. **Turn on two-step verification** on your email, WhatsApp, and bank apps — the single most effective protection you can add.
4. **Remove suspicious apps.** Delete anything you do not recognise or trust, using battery and data usage to find the worst offenders.
5. **Update your phone.** Install the latest software update — updates close the security gaps attackers rely on.
6. **Check your bank.** Look at your recent transactions, and if you see anything you did not authorise, call your bank immediately.
7. **Factory reset as a last resort.** If the problems continue, back up your photos and contacts, then reset the phone to factory settings. This removes almost all harmful software. Set the phone up as new, rather than restoring from a backup that might contain the problem.

## How to Keep Your Phone Safe Afterwards

Once your phone is clean, keep it that way with a few habits:

- **Only install apps from the official store.** Google Play Store or Apple App Store — never from links, websites, or files sent on WhatsApp.
- **Read permissions before you tap Allow.** A torch app does not need your contacts. A wallpaper app does not need your messages. If an app asks for more than it needs, do not install it.
- **Use a screen lock.** A PIN, fingerprint, or face lock stops someone from picking up your phone and installing something on it.
- **Keep two-step verification on.** It takes a minute to set up and stops most account takeovers, even if someone learns your password.
- **Be careful with public Wi-Fi.** Avoid logging into your bank on free Wi-Fi in a public place. Use your mobile data instead for anything involving money.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does a factory reset remove a hacker?

In most cases, yes. A factory reset wipes the phone and reinstalls the operating system, which removes almost all harmful apps. After a reset, set the phone up carefully and only reinstall apps you trust.

### Can my phone be hacked through WhatsApp?

WhatsApp messages are encrypted, but your account can be taken over if someone tricks you into sharing your verification code. Never share a six-digit WhatsApp code with anyone, even if they claim to be from WhatsApp or a friend. That code is the key to your account.

### Should I install an antivirus app on my phone?

On iPhones, it is generally not needed — Apple's system is tightly controlled. On Android, a well-known security app can help, but only from the official Play Store. Avoid "free cleaner" and "free antivirus" apps from unknown developers; many of them are the problem, not the solution.

### How do I know my bank app is safe?

Download your bank's app only from the official app store, never from a link, and check that the developer name matches your bank. Use a strong password and turn on all the security features your bank offers. Never enter banking details on a website a link in a message took you to — type your bank's address yourself or use the official app.

## Your Phone Is Worth Protecting

Your phone is your wallet, your office, and your photo album in one device. The signs of a hack are usually small — a hot phone, a fast-draining battery, a strange app. Noticing them early and acting quickly is what keeps a small problem from becoming a stolen account or an emptied bank balance. Stay alert, keep your software updated, and guard your passwords and codes like the keys they are.
