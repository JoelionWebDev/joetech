If you sell food, clothes, cakes, hair, or handmade products in Nigeria, you know the problem with taking orders on WhatsApp. A customer sends a message at 8:00 a.m., another sends a voice note at 11:00 a.m., a third sends a long chat with their order hidden in the middle, and by evening you cannot remember who ordered what, who paid, and who is still waiting for a reply. Orders get lost, customers get angry, and you lose money.

Google Forms solves this problem, and it is free. It is a simple online form that you create once, share as a link, and use to collect orders in an organised way. Every order arrives in the same format, with the customer's name, phone number, order, and delivery address in the same place. You can see all your orders in a spreadsheet, count them, sort them, and mark them as delivered.

You do not need a website, an app, or any technical skill to use it. If you can send a WhatsApp message, you can build a Google Form. Many small businesses in Lagos, Abuja, Ibadan, and Port Harcourt already use it for food orders, cake orders, pre-orders for clothes, and event registrations, and their customers find it easier than a long chat.

This guide explains how to take orders with Google Forms in Nigeria, step by step. You will learn how to build an order form that customers enjoy filling, how to collect payments, how to organise the responses so nothing gets lost, and how to confirm orders and deliveries professionally.

## Why Google Forms Works for Taking Orders

Google Forms works because it brings order to a messy process. When every customer fills the same form, you get the same information from everyone, in the same order. You no longer have to ask a customer for their address three times, or search through a long chat to find the size they wanted. The form asks, and the customer answers.

## Set Up Your Google Account and Your First Form

You need a Google account to use Google Forms. If you already use Gmail, you already have one. If not, create a free Gmail account with your business name, such as lagosfoodbasket@gmail.com, so your forms and responses stay separate from your personal email.

Go to forms.google.com on your phone or computer and click on the blank form. Give your form a clear title that tells customers exactly what it is for, such as "Order Form — Ada's Small Chops" or "Christmas Cake Pre-Order". Add a short description underneath that says what you sell, your delivery areas, and the deadline for orders.

Choose your settings before you share the form. Go to the settings and make sure the form collects email addresses only if you need them, because asking for an email can put off customers who do not use email regularly. You can also turn on the option to limit each person to one response, which helps when you are taking pre-orders and do not want duplicate orders from the same person.

Test the form yourself before you share it. Fill it in as if you were a customer, submit it, and check that the response appears in your responses tab. If anything is confusing, fix it now, before your customers see it. A form that is confusing on the first day will lose you orders.

## Build the Order Form: The Fields You Need

A good order form asks for everything you need and nothing you do not. Every extra question makes a customer more likely to abandon the form halfway. Ask for the essentials, and ask them in a clear order.

Start with the customer's full name. Use a short answer question, and make it required, because you cannot deliver an order to a nameless customer. Next, ask for their phone number, and make it required too. Use a short answer question, and add a note that says "WhatsApp number", so you can reach them on WhatsApp if you need to confirm anything.

Next, ask for their delivery address. Use a paragraph question so they have room to write their full address, including the house number, street, area, and any landmark. Add a note that says "Please include your area and a landmark, for example, 12 Allen Avenue, Ikeja, opposite the filling station". Clear addresses save your rider time and save you phone calls.

Now add the order itself. The best way to do this depends on what you sell. If you sell a small number of items, use a multiple-choice question for each item, with the price in the option, such as "Jollof Rice and Chicken — ₦4,000". If you sell many items, use a checkbox question that lets the customer select several items at once. Add a quantity question next to each item, using a dropdown with numbers from one to ten.

Add a question for special instructions, such as "No pepper", "Extra chicken", or "Deliver by 1:00 p.m.". Make this optional, so customers who have no special request can skip it. Add a question for the delivery date and time, using a date question and a multiple-choice question for the time slot, such as "Morning (9 a.m. – 12 p.m.)" or "Afternoon (12 p.m. – 3 p.m.)".

Finally, add a payment question. Ask the customer to choose how they will pay, such as bank transfer, Paystack link, or pay on delivery. If you take payment before delivery, add a question that asks them to upload a screenshot of their payment receipt. Google Forms lets you add a file upload question for this, and it is one of the most useful features for a Nigerian vendor.

## Collect Payments Through Your Form

Google Forms does not collect payment itself, so you need to connect it to a payment method your customers already use. The simplest method is a bank transfer. Add your account name, account number, and bank name at the end of the form, or in the confirmation message that appears after a customer submits the form. Ask the customer to upload a screenshot of the transfer as proof of payment.

A better method is a Paystack or Flutterwave payment link. Both platforms let you create a payment link for a fixed amount, and you can add that link to your form. When a customer submits the form, they tap the payment link, pay with their card or transfer, and the money goes straight to your account. This is faster and safer than waiting for a screenshot, because you can confirm the payment in your Paystack or Flutterwave dashboard.

If you use a payment link, make sure the link matches the order total. If your prices vary, you can create a payment link for each price, or you can ask the customer to pay after you confirm the total. Many vendors confirm the order on WhatsApp first, send the exact payment link, and mark the order as paid only when the payment arrives.

## Organise Responses with Google Sheets

Every response to your Google Form is saved automatically, and you can view them in the responses tab. But the real power comes when you link your form to a Google Sheet. Click on the green Sheets icon in the responses tab, and Google will create a spreadsheet that fills itself with every new order. Each row is one order, and each column is one question.

Use the spreadsheet to manage your orders. Add a column at the end called "Status", and mark each order as "New", "Paid", "Cooking", "Dispatched", or "Delivered". This gives you a clear view of what needs attention. A vendor who cooks lunch for twenty customers can look at the sheet in the morning and see exactly how many plates to cook.

Share the sheet with your team if you have one. A staff member who packs orders can see the sheet and prepare the right items, while a rider can see the delivery addresses. Set their access to editor or viewer depending on what they need, and do not share the sheet publicly, because it contains your customers' phone numbers and addresses.

Keep the sheet as your permanent record. At the end of the week, you can count how many orders you received, which items sold most, and how much money you made. That information helps you decide what to cook more of, what to stop selling, and what to promote. A food vendor in Abuja who checked her sheet every Sunday discovered that her small chops sold twice as fast on Fridays, so she started cooking a bigger batch on Fridays and stopped wasting food on Mondays.

## Confirm Orders and Handle Delivery

When a customer submits your form, do not leave them wondering. Send a confirmation message on WhatsApp within an hour, saying you received the order, repeating the items and the total, and confirming the delivery date and time. A message such as "Thank you, Chidinma. We received your order for two plates of jollof rice and chicken, total ₦8,000 plus ₦1,500 delivery. Your order will arrive tomorrow between 12 p.m. and 1 p.m." reassures the customer and prevents mistakes.

If anything in the order is unclear, ask the customer immediately. If the address is incomplete, or the quantity is confusing, message them and confirm before you cook. It is better to ask one question now than to deliver the wrong order later.

On the delivery day, message the customer when the rider leaves, and share the rider's phone number. A customer who knows their food is on the way is patient. A customer who hears nothing for two hours will call you worried, and may order from someone else next time.

After delivery, ask the customer to confirm they received the order and that everything was correct. Update your sheet to "Delivered", and ask for feedback. A short message such as "Did you enjoy your food? We would love your feedback" opens the door to a review you can post, and to a repeat order.

If a customer does not pay, do not dispatch the order. Message them politely and remind them of the payment details. If they do not respond, mark the order as cancelled and move on. It is better to lose one order than to cook food for a customer who never pays.

## Share Your Form Where Customers Will Find It

A form that nobody sees will not bring orders. Share the link everywhere your customers already find you. Put it in your WhatsApp Business catalogue, in your Instagram bio, in your Facebook page, and in your TikTok bio. When you post a photo of your food on your WhatsApp status, add the order link so a customer can tap and order immediately.

Shorten the link so it is easy to share. Google Forms gives you a long link, and you can shorten it in the form settings or with a free link shortener. A short link is easier to type, easier to remember, and looks neater in a WhatsApp message or an Instagram bio.

You can also turn the link into a QR code and print it on your packaging, your flyer, or your shop banner. A customer who scans the code with their phone camera goes straight to your order form. There are free QR code tools online, and you can generate one in seconds. Update the link when you change the form. If you create a new form for a new week or a new product, update the link in your bio and your status. An old link that leads to a closed form will frustrate customers and lose you orders.

## Common Mistakes People Make with Google Forms Orders

The first mistake is asking too many questions. A form with twenty questions will scare customers away. Ask only what you need to fulfil the order. If you do not need the customer's date of birth, do not ask for it. If you do not need their email, do not ask for it. Keep the form short and focused.

The second mistake is making every question required. If a customer has no special instructions, they should be able to skip that question. If you make every field required, customers will fill in nonsense just to submit the form, and you will get useless information. Make only the essential fields required: name, phone number, address, order, and payment method.

The third mistake is never checking the responses. A form is only useful if you read it. Check your responses at least twice a day, morning and evening, and reply to new orders promptly. A customer who submits a form at 9:00 a.m. and hears nothing until 6:00 p.m. will assume you are not serious.

The fourth mistake is using one form for everything. A single form for food orders, cake orders, complaints, and feedback will confuse customers and make your spreadsheet messy. Create a separate form for each purpose, with a clear title, and share only the one that matches what the customer wants to do.

The fifth mistake is forgetting to close the form. If you are sold out for the day, or you are travelling, turn off the form or add a note at the top that says "We are closed this weekend, orders resume on Monday". A customer who orders on a closed form and gets no food will not trust your form again.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Google Forms really free for taking orders?

Yes, Google Forms is free with a Google account. There is no limit on the number of forms you can create or the number of responses you can collect on the free plan. You only pay if you choose to use a separate payment service such as Paystack or Flutterwave, which charge a small fee on each transaction.

### Do my customers need a Google account to fill my form?

No, your customers do not need a Google account to fill in a form, unless you turn on a setting that requires it. By default, anyone with the link can fill in the form from their phone browser, without signing in. Keep that setting off so customers can order easily.

### How do I stop people from submitting fake orders?

You can turn on the setting that limits each person to one response, which requires them to sign in with Google, but that can put off genuine customers. A better approach is to confirm every order on WhatsApp before you cook, and to ask for payment before delivery. A fake order usually stops when you ask for payment.

### Can I use Google Forms for pre-orders?

Yes, Google Forms is excellent for pre-orders. Create a form for a specific product or event, set a closing date, and share the link. Customers fill in their orders before the date, you count the responses in your sheet, and you cook or buy exactly the quantity you need. This reduces waste and guarantees sales before you spend money on ingredients.

### What should I do if a customer makes a mistake on the form?

Message the customer on WhatsApp and confirm the correct details before you cook or dispatch. You can also edit their response in the linked Google Sheet, so your record is correct. If the mistake changes the price, tell the customer the new total and confirm they are happy before you proceed.

## What to Do Next

1. Create a free Google account with your business name if you do not already have one.
2. Go to forms.google.com and create a new blank form with a clear title and a short description.
3. Add the essential fields: name, phone number, delivery address, order items, quantity, delivery date and time, and payment method.
4. Link the form to a Google Sheet so every order is saved automatically in one place.
5. Add your payment details or a Paystack or Flutterwave payment link, and ask for a payment screenshot.
6. Test the form yourself, submit a test order, and check that it appears in your sheet.
7. Share the link on your WhatsApp status, Instagram bio, and Facebook page, and add it to your WhatsApp Business catalogue.
8. Check your responses twice a day, confirm every order on WhatsApp, and update the status in your sheet as each order moves from paid to delivered.
