Konga is one of Nigeria's best-known online marketplaces. Every day, customers across Lagos, Abuja, Port Harcourt, Enugu, Kano, and other cities search Konga for phones, fashion, home appliances, beauty products, groceries, and more. For a small business owner with good products but limited foot traffic, Konga offers something a physical shop cannot: access to customers in every state, without opening a branch in each one.

Selling on Konga is not complicated, but it does require care. Your product photos, descriptions, prices, and delivery habits all affect whether customers buy from you and whether they return. A seller who lists products properly, packs orders well, and delivers on time can build a steady stream of orders. A seller who lists poor photos, sets confusing prices, or cancels orders will struggle, even with good products. This guide walks you through the full process, from registration to your first sale and beyond.

## Why Sell on Konga as a Nigerian Small Business

The first advantage is reach. A shop in Ikeja can only serve people who walk past it or hear about it. A Konga store can serve a customer in Jos who has never been to Lagos. That reach matters when your product is niche, such as handmade shoes, a particular brand of hair, or a specific phone accessory that is hard to find locally.

The second advantage is trust. Many Nigerian customers are cautious about paying a stranger on Instagram or WhatsApp. They worry about being scammed or receiving a different product from the one they ordered. Konga provides a structured platform with buyer protection, secure payment, and a returns process. That structure makes customers more willing to try a new seller.

The third advantage is that you do not need to build a website or pay for adverts to start. Konga already has traffic. You can start with ten products and grow gradually as you learn what sells.

Selling on Konga also teaches you useful business habits. You learn to take good photos, write clear descriptions, manage stock, track orders, and handle customer questions professionally. Those skills help you even if you later open your own online store.

## Step 1: Prepare Your Business Before You Register

Before you open a Konga seller account, get your basics ready. You will need a valid email address and phone number that you check regularly. Konga will send order notifications, payment updates, and important messages to these contacts. Use a business email if you have one, and a phone number you answer during business hours.

You will also need a means of identification and your Bank Verification Number. Konga may ask for a government-issued ID, such as a National Identity Number slip, a voter's card, a driver's licence, or an international passport. Have a clear photo or scan ready before you start so the process is smooth.

You will need a bank account in your business name or your personal name where Konga will pay you for completed orders. Make sure the account name matches the name you register with, and confirm that the account is active and can receive transfers. If you have a business bank account, use it. It makes your records cleaner and your business look more professional.

Prepare your product information in advance. For each product you plan to list, write down the name, brand, size or variant, colour, material, price, and quantity in stock. Take clear photos of each product before you register, so you can list quickly once your account is approved. Good preparation saves you days of back-and-forth later.

If you have a registered business name with the Corporate Affairs Commission, keep your registration details handy. Registration requirements can change, and some seller categories may ask for business documents. Having them ready means you will not be delayed.

## Step 2: Create Your Konga Seller Account

Go to the Konga website and look for the Sell on Konga link, usually found in the footer or the main menu. You will be directed to the seller registration page. Fill in your personal details carefully: your full name, email address, phone number, and business name. Choose a shop name that is easy to remember and easy to spell. A shop name like "Ada Fashion Hub" is better than a long name with numbers and symbols that customers cannot remember.

Create a strong password and keep it safe. Do not share your seller password with staff or friends. If someone helps you manage the store, ask about sub-account or staff access options rather than sharing your main login.

Submit the registration form and follow the verification steps. Konga may send a verification code to your email or phone. Enter it promptly. You may also be asked to upload your ID and other documents. Upload clear images. A blurry ID photo is one of the most common reasons for delays.

Once your account is created, complete your seller profile fully. Add your business address, your pickup location, and your bank details. Add a short description of your shop and what you sell. A complete profile builds trust with Konga and with customers. An incomplete profile can delay your ability to list products.

## Step 3: Learn the Seller Dashboard Before You List Anything

After approval, you will have access to the Konga seller dashboard. Spend time exploring it before you list your first product. Find where to add products, where to see orders, where to check payments, and where to respond to customer messages.

Look for the section on shipping and delivery. Konga uses its own logistics network, Konga Express, in many areas, and also works with other delivery partners. Understand how delivery works in your area. Find out whether you need to drop off orders at a Konga store or pickup point, or whether a rider will collect from your shop. Knowing this in advance prevents delays when your first order arrives.

Check the fees and commissions for your product category. Konga charges a commission on each sale, and the rate varies by category. Electronics, fashion, and home goods may have different rates. There may also be a fee for payment processing or for using certain services. Knowing your fees before you set prices is essential, because a price that looks profitable can become a loss once commission is deducted.

Read the seller policies on returns, cancellations, and prohibited items. Some products cannot be sold on Konga, and some categories have extra requirements. Selling a prohibited item can lead to your account being suspended. If you are unsure whether a product is allowed, check the policy or contact seller support before you list it.

## Step 4: List Your First Products Properly

Product listings are where sales are won or lost. A good listing answers every question a customer might have before they ask it.

Start with the product title. Write a clear, descriptive title that includes the brand, the product type, and a key feature. For example, "Oraimo Wireless Bluetooth Earbuds with Charging Case — Black" is far better than "Earbuds" or "Good Quality Earbuds for Sale." Customers search using specific words, and a clear title helps your product appear in those searches. Avoid writing titles in all capital letters or filling them with unrelated keywords. That looks unprofessional and can hurt your ranking.

Next, write a detailed description. Explain what the product is, what it is made of, what is included in the box, the size or capacity, and how to use it. Mention who it is for and any important warnings, such as "requires Android 8 or higher" or "hand wash only." Use short paragraphs or bullet points so the description is easy to scan on a phone. Many Konga customers shop on their phones, so long blocks of text are hard to read.

Set the correct category and attributes. If you sell a dress, choose the right fashion category, select the correct size range, and list the available colours. Wrong categories confuse customers and make your product harder to find. If a customer orders a medium dress and receives a large one because the listing was wrong, you will face a return and a bad review.

Upload high-quality photos. Konga requires clear product images, usually on a plain background. Take photos in good daylight, show the product from the front, back, and side, and include a close-up of important details such as fabric, stitching, or ports. Do not use photos downloaded from the internet that show a different product. Customers compare the photo with what they receive, and mismatches lead to returns.

Set your stock quantity accurately. If you have only three pieces left, list three, not ten. Overselling leads to cancellations, and cancellations hurt your seller rating. Update your stock immediately when you sell offline or when new stock arrives.

## Step 5: Price Your Products for Profit, Not Just for Sales

Pricing on a marketplace is different from pricing in your shop. On Konga, you must cover the cost of the product, the commission, delivery costs where applicable, packaging, and your profit. Many new sellers set prices too low to compete, make sales, and then realise they lost money on every order.

Calculate your price step by step. Start with the cost of buying the product from your supplier, including transport to your shop. Add the Konga commission for your category. Add packaging costs, such as a carton, bubble wrap, or a branded bag. Add any delivery cost you bear. Then add your profit margin. If the final price is much higher than similar products on Konga, look for a cheaper supplier or a different product. If it is much lower, you may be leaving money on the table.

Check what competitors charge for similar products, but do not copy their prices blindly. A competitor might have a cheaper supplier or be selling old stock at a discount. Compare the full offer: the product quality, the photos, the description, the delivery time, and the seller rating. A product with better photos and a higher rating can sell at a slightly higher price.

Consider offering a small discount on your first listings to attract early buyers and reviews, but keep the discount modest. A ten percent discount is enough to catch attention without destroying your margin. You can also create bundle offers, such as a phone case and a screen guard together, to increase the average order value.

Review your prices regularly. Supplier prices change, delivery costs change, and Konga fees can change. A price that was profitable three months ago may not be profitable today. Check your margins at least once a month.

## Step 6: Handle Orders Quickly and Pack Them Well

When an order comes in, speed matters. Konga tracks how quickly sellers confirm and ship orders, and slow sellers rank lower and receive fewer orders. Check your seller dashboard and your email at least twice a day, morning and evening. Confirm new orders promptly and prepare them for pickup or delivery the same day if possible.

Pack each order as if the customer were standing in front of you. Use a clean carton or a strong branded bag. Wrap fragile items in bubble wrap or soft paper. Seal the package properly so it does not open during transit. Include a small thank-you note or a business card with your shop name and WhatsApp number. That small touch encourages repeat orders and makes your brand memorable.

Double-check the order before you seal it. Confirm the product, the size, the colour, and the quantity match what the customer ordered. A wrong size or colour is one of the most common reasons for returns on Konga. A thirty-second check saves you the cost of a return and a disappointed customer.

If you need to drop off the order at a Konga store, do it on time and keep the drop-off receipt. If a rider collects from you, be available at the agreed time and hand over the package promptly. Delays at this stage affect the delivery time the customer sees and can lead to complaints.

## Step 7: Manage Payments, Returns, and Customer Messages

Konga pays sellers for completed orders after delivery is confirmed and the return window has passed. Payments are usually made to your registered bank account on a set schedule. Keep track of every payment you receive and compare it with your orders. If a payment is missing or incorrect, contact seller support with the order number and details.

Returns are a normal part of online selling. A customer may return a product because it is the wrong size, it arrived damaged, or it does not match the description. Handle returns calmly and professionally. Follow Konga's return process, accept the return if it is valid, and use it as feedback. If many customers return the same product for the same reason, fix the listing. Update the photos, correct the size chart, or improve the description.

Respond to customer messages quickly and politely. Customers often ask about size, delivery time, or whether a product is in stock before they buy. A fast, helpful reply can turn a question into a sale. A slow or rude reply will send the customer to another seller. Use clear, friendly language. If you do not know an answer, say so and promise to check, then follow up.

Never ask a customer to pay outside Konga or to cancel an order so you can sell directly. That breaks Konga's rules and can lead to your account being closed. It also removes the buyer protection that makes customers trust you in the first place.

## Step 8: Grow Your Konga Store With Reviews and Promotions

Reviews are powerful on Konga. Customers read reviews before they buy, especially when they are buying from a seller for the first time. The best way to earn good reviews is to deliver exactly what you listed, on time, and in good packaging. After delivery, you can send a polite message through Konga asking the customer to leave a review if they are happy. Do not offer payment or gifts in exchange for a review, as that can break platform rules.

Take part in Konga promotions when they fit your products. Konga runs sales events, discount campaigns, and special offers throughout the year, including festive season sales. A well-timed discount during a major campaign can bring many more orders than a normal week. Plan your stock in advance so you do not run out during a promotion.

Add new products regularly. A store with ten products will attract less traffic than a store with fifty products in the same category. Add products that complement what you already sell. If you sell phones, add chargers, cases, and earphones. If you sell dresses, add matching bags or shoes. Each new listing is another chance for a customer to find your store.

Use your Konga store link in your other marketing. Share it on WhatsApp, Instagram, and Facebook. Tell your offline customers that they can also order from you on Konga, especially if they live far away. Your social media followers who trust you already are more likely to buy from your Konga store than a stranger is.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Selling on Konga

**How much does it cost to open a Konga seller account?**

Registration itself is usually free, but you should confirm the current terms on the Konga seller page, as fees and requirements can change. Your main costs are the commission on each sale, packaging, and delivery where applicable. Always check the current commission rate for your category before you set prices.

**Can I sell on Konga without a registered business?**

Many small sellers start as individuals, but requirements can vary by category. Having a registered business name helps with trust and with opening a business bank account. Check the current seller requirements for your category before you register.

**How do I receive payment for my sales?**

Konga pays sellers to their registered bank account after an order is delivered and confirmed. Keep your bank details up to date and track each payment against your orders. If a payment is delayed, contact seller support with the order details.

## Final Thoughts

Selling on Konga gives your Nigerian small business a shop window that is open across the whole country, day and night. Success does not come from listing many products quickly. It comes from listing a few products well, with clear photos, honest descriptions, fair prices, careful packaging, and fast delivery. Start with your best products, learn from your first orders, and improve one thing each week. Over time, your store rating will grow, your reviews will build trust, and Konga will become a reliable source of orders alongside your physical shop and your social media sales.
