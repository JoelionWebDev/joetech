You want to sell online in Nigeria. You have products, customers, and ambition. But the first big question stops many business owners cold: where should you sell?

The three main options — your own website, Instagram Shop, and online marketplaces like Jumia and Konga — each have different costs, benefits, and trade-offs. There is no universal right answer. The best choice depends on your product type, your budget, your technical capability, and your long-term goals.

This guide breaks down each option so you can make an informed decision — or combine them strategically.

## Option 1: Your Own Website (Full E-commerce Store)

### What It Is

A fully customisable online store hosted on your own domain. Customers browse your catalogue, add items to cart, check out using Nigerian payment gateways (Paystack, Flutterwave, Monnify), and receive order confirmations — all within your brand experience.

### Costs

- **Development:** ₦800,000 — ₦2,500,000 one-time
- **Domain:** ₦6,000 — ₦25,000/year
- **Hosting:** ₦50,000 — ₦200,000/year
- **Payment gateway fees:** 1.5%-3% per transaction
- **Maintenance:** ₦50,000 — ₦200,000/year
- **Total first year:** ₦900,000 — ₦2,900,000

### Pros

- **Full control** — You own the customer data, the design, the checkout experience, and the relationship. No algorithm changes affect your reach. No marketplace rules dictate your policies.
- **Higher margins** — No marketplace commission fees. The only transaction cost is your payment gateway fee (1.5-3% versus marketplace fees of 10-20%).
- **Brand ownership** — Customers experience your brand fully, not as a listing within a marketplace interface. Every touchpoint reinforces your brand.
- **SEO advantage** — Your website can rank on Google for product and category keywords, bringing organic traffic that compounds over time.
- **Retargeting** — You can track visitors, capture emails, and retarget them with ads. Marketplaces limit your access to customer data.
- **Full customisation** — You decide everything: the layout, the checkout flow, the shipping options, the return policy, the promotional mechanics.

### Cons

- **High upfront cost** — Building a quality e-commerce website is expensive compared to free options
- **Traffic building is your job** — Nobody visits your website automatically. You need to drive traffic through marketing, SEO, and advertising
- **Technical maintenance** — You are responsible for uptime, security, updates, and performance
- **No built-in audience** — Unlike marketplaces, your website starts with zero traffic

### Best For

- Businesses with established brand recognition
- Products with higher price points (higher margins justify the investment)
- Businesses with repeat purchases (customer retention economics work better on your own platform)
- Brands that want to build long-term asset value
- Businesses selling unique or differentiated products (not commodities)

## Option 2: Instagram Shop

### What It Is

Instagram's built-in shopping features that let you tag products in posts and stories, create a product catalogue on your profile, and enable in-app checkout or external checkout via your website.

### Costs

- **Account setup:** Free
- **Instagram fees:** ₦0 if you redirect checkout to your website. 5% per transaction if using in-app checkout (Instagram's Nigerian rate)
- **Content creation:** ₦0 — ₦100,000/month (depending on whether you DIY or hire)
- **Instagram ads:** Optional but recommended for growth
- **Total first year:** ₦0 — ₦1,200,000 (depending on ad spend and content production)

### Pros

- **Free to start** — Zero upfront investment. If you already have an Instagram presence, you are minutes away from adding a shop
- **Built-in audience** — Access to Instagram's massive Nigerian user base
- **Visual discovery** — Instagram is designed for visual product discovery. Fashion, beauty, home decor, and food products perform especially well
- **Social proof** — Likes, comments, shares, and user-generated content serve as social proof that drives trust and sales
- **Low friction** — Customers can discover and purchase without leaving the app
- **Algorithm boost** — Shopping content gets preferential reach in Instagram's algorithm

### Cons

- **You do not own the audience** — Instagram controls the algorithm. A change in their feed algorithm can halve your reach overnight
- **Account risk** — Accounts get hacked, suspended, or shadowbanned. If that happens, your store disappears instantly
- **Limited product information** — Instagram's product pages are basic. No detailed specifications, comparison tables, size guides, or FAQ sections
- **Limited analytics** — Instagram's built-in analytics are basic compared to Google Analytics on a website
- **Cart abandonment** — No abandoned cart recovery, no email capturing, no retargeting sequences
- **Competition is visible** — Customers can easily click away to your competitors' profiles

### Best For

- Product businesses with strong visual appeal (fashion, beauty, jewellery, art, food)
- Businesses building brand awareness alongside sales
- Testing product-market fit before investing in a full website
- Low-consideration purchases (impulse buys under ₦15,000)

## Option 3: Online Marketplaces (Jumia, Konga, etc.)

### What It Is

Selling your products on established e-commerce platforms that already have millions of active buyers in Nigeria.

### Costs

- **Seller account:** Free to register
- **Commission fees:** 10-20% per sale (varies by category and platform)
- **Storage and fulfilment:** Optional paid services
- **Advertising:** Optional but recommended (pay-per-click within marketplace)
- **Total first year:** ₦0 — ₦500,000 (depending on ad spend and fulfilment costs)

### Pros

- **Massive built-in traffic** — Jumia receives millions of visits per month. Your products are immediately visible to active buyers
- **Trust factor** — Customers trust Jumia and Konga. They are more likely to buy from you on Jumia than from an unknown standalone website
- **Logistics infrastructure** — Jumia's delivery network handles shipping, returns, and cash-on-delivery collection across Nigeria
- **Easy to start** — List your products, set prices, and start selling within days
- **Payment handling** — The marketplace processes payments, reducing fraud risk
- **Mobile money and cash-on-delivery** — Marketplaces handle payment methods that are harder for individual merchants to implement

### Cons

- **High commissions** — 10-20% per sale significantly reduces your margins. On a ₦10,000 product with a 50% margin, Jumia takes 20%, leaving you with ₦3,000 after COGS — and that is before delivery costs
- **Limited brand experience** — Your products appear within the marketplace's interface. Little opportunity for brand differentiation
- **No customer ownership** — The marketplace owns the customer relationship. You cannot email them, retarget them, or build loyalty programs
- **Price competition** — Customers compare your product against identical or similar products directly. Race-to-the-bottom pricing is common
- **Restricted communication** — Marketplaces limit how you can communicate with customers to prevent you from taking them off-platform
- **Suspension risk** — Policy violations or customer complaints can get your seller account suspended, losing your revenue stream instantly

### Best For

- Physical products with broad appeal (electronics, household items, baby products, groceries)
- Testing demand for a new product category
- Businesses that lack logistics infrastructure (marketplace handles delivery)
- Commodity products where price is the primary differentiator
- High-volume, low-margin businesses that can operate at scale

## The Winning Strategy: Combination Approach

The most successful Nigerian e-commerce businesses do not choose one option — they use all three strategically.

### The Three-Tier Strategy

**Tier 1: Your Own Website (Foundation)**

This is your home base. Your full catalogue, your best prices, your best customer experience, your brand. All marketing efforts ultimately drive customers here. This is where you build long-term value.

**Tier 2: Instagram Shop (Discovery)**

Use Instagram for visual discovery and impulse purchases. Tag products in posts and stories. Drive engagement with behind-the-scenes content, user-generated content, and influencer partnerships. Link your Instagram shop to your website checkout so you capture customer data.

**Tier 3: Marketplace (Volume)**

Use Jumia or Konga for reach and volume, especially for products where you can compete on price. Accept the lower margins in exchange for volume and market share. Use the marketplace to acquire customers, then incentivise them to buy directly from your website next time (better pricing, exclusive products, loyalty programme).

### Tactical Example

A Lagos-based beauty brand uses this combination:

- **Website:** Full catalogue with detailed product information, bundle deals, subscription options, and loyalty programme. Primary checkout for all marketing campaigns.
- **Instagram Shop:** Daily Reels showing product applications, before/after transformations, and customer testimonials. Products tagged for easy shopping. Instagram ads retarget website visitors.
- **Jumia:** Selected best-selling products listed for search visibility. Higher prices on Jumia to offset commission (or exclusive bundles to differentiate). Every package includes a flyer directing customers to the website for better deals.

## How to Decide: A Decision Framework

1. **What is your budget?** If you have less than ₦500,000, start with Instagram Shop and/or marketplace. Reinvest profits into your own website later.
2. **What is your product category?** Fashion, beauty, and handmade products perform well on Instagram. Electronics, household goods, and commodity products perform better on marketplaces.
3. **What is your long-term goal?** If you want to build a brand with lasting value, invest in your own website as soon as possible. If you want quick sales with minimal investment, start with marketplaces.
4. **What is your margin?** Low-margin products (below 30%) are difficult to sell profitably on marketplaces after 10-20% commission. Higher-margin products give you more flexibility.
5. **Do you have logistics capability?** If you can handle delivery yourself, your own website or Instagram is viable. If not, marketplace logistics are valuable.
