A customer in Lagos scrolls past hundreds of products every day. On Instagram, on WhatsApp Status, on Jumia, on your website. Most of those products blur together. The ones that sell are the ones whose descriptions answer the buyer's real question in seconds: what is this, why should I buy it, and can I trust this seller?

A good product description is not fancy English. It is clear, honest, and useful. It tells the customer exactly what they will get, how it will help them, how much it costs, and how to order. In Nigeria, where many customers buy from a phone, pay on delivery or by transfer, and worry about scams, a strong description also builds trust. This guide shows you how to write descriptions that do all of that, whether you sell fashion, food, skincare, electronics, or handmade goods.

## Start With the Customer, Not the Product

Most sellers start with the product. They list what it is made of, where it came from, and what it looks like. That is useful, but it is not where the sale starts. The sale starts with the customer and their problem.

A woman buying a handbag in Abuja is not just buying leather and a zip. She is buying a bag that will carry her laptop, her phone, and her purse without looking bulky at work. A mother buying baby food in Ibadan is not just buying a jar. She is buying peace of mind that her child is eating something safe and nutritious. A young man buying sneakers in Lekki is not just buying shoes. He is buying comfort for long days and a style that fits how he wants to be seen.

Before you write a single word, answer three questions. Who is buying this product? What problem does it solve for them? What would stop them from buying it? Write those answers down. They become the backbone of your description.

For example, if you sell Ankara dresses, your customer might be a working woman aged 25 to 45 who wants a dress she can wear to church on Sunday and to the office on Monday. Her problem is finding a dress that is stylish, comfortable, and modest enough for both places. Her fear is that the dress will look different from the photo, or that the size will not fit. Your description should speak directly to those points: the fit, the fabric, the occasions, and your exchange policy.

## The Simple Structure That Works Every Time

You do not need a complicated formula. A clear structure, used the same way for every product, makes writing faster and makes your shop easier to shop from. Use this five-part structure.

First, the headline. This is the product name plus one key benefit. "Soft Cotton Ankara Midi Dress — Comfortable Fit for Work and Church" tells the customer far more than "Ankara Dress" alone. Keep it specific and honest.

Second, the opening line. In one or two sentences, say who the product is for and what it does. "Made for busy days, this dress keeps you comfortable from morning meetings to evening events." That opening line should make the right customer keep reading.

Third, the benefits. List three to five benefits, not just features. A feature is what the product has. A benefit is what the customer gets from it. "Made from breathable cotton" is a feature. "Stays cool and comfortable even in Lagos heat" is a benefit. Turn every feature into a benefit before you publish.

Fourth, the details. This is where you give the facts: size, colour, material, weight, what is included, and how to care for the product. Customers in Nigeria ask these questions constantly on WhatsApp. Answering them in the description saves you time and builds confidence.

Fifth, the call to action. Tell the customer exactly what to do next. "Send us a WhatsApp message with your size to order" or "Tap Add to Cart and pay securely with Paystack." Do not leave the customer guessing. A clear next step is what turns interest into an order.

## Features Versus Benefits: Make Every Line Earn Its Place

The difference between features and benefits is the difference between a description that informs and a description that sells. Many Nigerian sellers list features only, and then wonder why customers ask so many questions and buy so little.

Here is how to convert features into benefits. Start with the feature, then ask "so what?" until you reach the customer's life.

Feature: "This power bank has a 20,000mAh battery." So what? It can charge a phone four times. So what? You will not run out of battery during a long day in traffic, a church service, or a power outage. The benefit is freedom from battery anxiety, and that is what the customer is really buying.

Feature: "This cream contains shea butter." So what? Shea butter moisturises deeply. So what? Your skin stays soft and smooth through harmattan, without the greasy feeling some creams leave behind. The benefit is soft skin in dry season, and that is what sells the cream.

Feature: "This wig is made from human hair." So what? It can be styled with heat, washed, and worn for a long time. So what? You get a natural look that lasts, instead of buying a new wig every few weeks. The benefit is value for money and a natural look.

Write your description with benefits first, then add the features as supporting detail. The customer buys the benefit. The feature is the proof that the benefit is real.

## Write for a Phone-First Customer

Most Nigerian customers will read your description on a phone, often on WhatsApp, Instagram, or a mobile browser. Many are reading on a small screen, on a slow connection, while doing something else. Your writing must work in that reality.

Keep sentences short. Keep paragraphs to two or three lines. Use line breaks generously. A wall of text looks hard to read on a phone, and customers will skip it. Short paragraphs look friendly and easy.

Put the most important information first. Price, size, colour, and delivery information should appear near the top, not buried at the bottom. If a customer has to scroll and search for the price, many will give up and move on. In Nigeria, hiding the price is one of the fastest ways to lose a sale. Customers assume a hidden price means the price is high, or that the seller will charge different people different amounts. State your price clearly.

Use simple, everyday English. Your customer may be a graduate or a trader, a teenager or a grandmother. Simple English reaches all of them. Avoid jargon, avoid long words when a short word works, and avoid sentences that need to be read twice to be understood. If you sell to a specific community, a few familiar words in Pidgin or a local language can make the description feel warm and personal, but keep the main description in clear English so everyone can follow it.

Make the description easy to copy and share. Many customers will forward your WhatsApp message or your product link to a friend or a sister before they buy. A description that is clear and complete travels well. A description that depends on a long voice note or a private conversation does not.

## Honesty Builds Trust and Reduces Returns

In Nigerian online selling, trust is everything. Customers have been disappointed before. They have received a dress that looked nothing like the photo, a phone accessory that stopped working in a week, or a food item that arrived stale. Your description is your chance to prove you are different.

Be honest about size and fit. If a dress runs small, say so and advise customers to order one size up. If a shoe is narrow, say so. If a colour looks slightly different in real life because of lighting, mention it. This honesty may lose you one sale today, but it will save you from a return, a complaint, and a bad review tomorrow.

Be honest about what is included. If the price covers the dress only, say so. If it includes a belt or a scarf, say so. If the phone case does not include a screen guard, say so. Customers feel cheated when they discover an extra cost or a missing item after they have paid. Clear details prevent that feeling.

Use real photos and describe what the customer will actually receive. If you use a model's photo, say what size the model is wearing. If the product is handmade, say that colours and patterns may vary slightly. If you sell food, state the weight, the ingredients, and the shelf life. These details answer questions before they are asked, and they make you look professional.

State your delivery and return policy in plain language. How long does delivery take to Lagos, Abuja, or Port Harcourt? What is the delivery fee? Do you offer pay on delivery? Can the customer exchange the product if the size is wrong? Answer these questions in the description or link to them clearly. A customer who knows the rules is a customer who can buy with confidence.

## Use Words That Help Customers Find You

Search matters, even for small sellers. When a customer types "ankara dress Lagos" or "natural hair cream Nigeria" into Google or Instagram search, your product should have a chance to appear. That chance depends on the words you use.

Include the words your customer would actually type. If you sell "unisex sneakers in Lagos," use those words in your title and description. If you sell "wholesale Ankara fabric in Onitsha," use those words too. Do not use only brand names or clever phrases that no one searches for. A clever title is useless if no one can find it.

Use location words naturally. Mention Lagos, Abuja, Ibadan, or your delivery areas where it fits. Many customers search with a location because they want fast delivery or a seller they can visit. A line like "We deliver across Lagos within 24 hours and nationwide within 3 days" helps both the customer and the search engine.

Write a title that a human would understand and a search engine would understand. "Red Cotton Ankara Midi Dress for Women — Size 10 to 16" is better than "Beautiful Dress for Ladies." The first tells the customer and the search engine exactly what the product is. The second tells them almost nothing.

## Prices, Payment, and Delivery: Say It Plainly

Price is often the first thing a Nigerian customer looks for, and the last thing many sellers want to state. State it anyway, clearly and completely.

Write the price in naira, with the full figure. "₦25,000" is clearer than "25k" in a formal product page, although "25k" is fine in a casual WhatsApp message. If you offer a discount, show the old price and the new price together, and say when the discount ends. "Was ₦30,000, now ₦25,000 until Sunday" creates urgency and is honest about the offer.

Explain how to pay. Do you accept bank transfer, Paystack, Flutterwave, or pay on delivery? Say so. If you accept transfer, give the account name clearly so the customer can verify it. If you use pay on delivery, say which areas it covers and whether there is an extra fee. Customers are more likely to order when they know the payment is safe and simple.

Explain delivery in plain terms. State the delivery fee for Lagos and for other states, or say that delivery is free above a certain amount. State how long delivery takes. If you use a dispatch rider in Lagos, say so. If you use a courier for nationwide delivery, name the courier if you can. A customer who knows when their order will arrive is less likely to message you every hour asking where it is.

If you sell on WhatsApp, make the order process simple. A line like "To order, send us your name, size, colour, and delivery address on WhatsApp" tells the customer exactly what to send. The fewer steps between interest and order, the more orders you will get.

## Descriptions for WhatsApp, Instagram, and Your Website

The same product needs slightly different descriptions on different platforms, because customers behave differently on each one.

On WhatsApp, keep it short and complete. Your customer is chatting, not browsing a catalogue. Give the price, the sizes, the colours, and how to order in a few short lines, with line breaks between each point. A WhatsApp description that fits on one phone screen without scrolling is ideal. If you need more detail, send the short version first and offer to send more photos or details if the customer asks.

On Instagram, lead with the visual and the benefit. The photo or video catches the eye, and the caption explains why the product matters. Put the price and the key benefit in the first two lines, before the "more" button hides the rest. Use hashtags that your customers actually search for, like your product type and your location, rather than a long list of generic tags. End with a clear call to action, such as "Send us a DM to order" or "Tap the link in our bio."

On your website, give the full description. This is where the customer expects detail, and where search engines will read your words. Use headings, bullet points, and short paragraphs. Include a size guide, a care guide, and a delivery section. A website product page is your shop assistant. It should answer questions even when you are asleep.

Keep the core facts the same across all platforms. The price, the sizes, and the colours should not change from WhatsApp to Instagram to your website. Inconsistent information confuses customers and damages trust. Write one master description for each product, then adapt its length and tone for each platform.


## Common Mistakes to Avoid

The first mistake is copying another seller's description word for word. Customers notice, search engines notice, and it tells the customer nothing about your own product. Write your own words, even if they are simple. Simple and original is better than polished and copied.

The second mistake is exaggerating. "The best cream in the world" and "guaranteed to make you fair in three days" are claims that customers do not believe and that can get you into trouble. Make claims you can stand behind. If a cream moisturises well, say it moisturises well. If a dress is comfortable, say it is comfortable. Honest claims build a reputation that lasts.

The third mistake is forgetting the customer entirely and writing only about the product. A list of materials, dimensions, and technical terms is not a description. It is a specification sheet. Add the human side: who it is for, when they will use it, and how it will make their day easier.

The fourth mistake is writing too little. "Available in all sizes. DM to order" is not a description. It gives the customer no reason to choose you over the next seller. Give them a reason. Give them details. Give them confidence.

## Your Next Product Description: A Quick Checklist

Before you publish your next product description, run through this checklist. Does the headline say what the product is and one key benefit? Does the opening line say who it is for? Have you listed three benefits, not just features? Have you given the size, colour, material, and what is included? Have you stated the price clearly in naira? Have you explained how to pay and how delivery works? Have you told the customer exactly what to do next? Have you read it once on your phone to check how it looks?

If you can answer yes to all of those questions, your description is ready. Publish it, watch the questions customers ask, and improve it over time. Good descriptions do not just sell one product. They build a shop that customers trust, return to, and recommend to their friends.

Start with one product today. Rewrite its description using the structure in this guide. Make it clear, honest, and useful. Then do the same for the next product, and the next. Over time, your whole shop will speak more clearly, and your sales will show it.
