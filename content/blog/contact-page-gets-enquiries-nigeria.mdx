Your website can have beautiful design, strong content, and steady traffic, but if your contact page does not work well, none of that turns into business. The contact page is where a visitor decides whether to reach out or to leave and try someone else. It is the final step between interest and an enquiry, and for many Nigerian small businesses it is the weakest page on the whole website.

Think about your own behaviour online. When you want to contact a business and the page is confusing, the form asks for too much information, or there is no phone number or WhatsApp link in sight, what do you do? Most people leave. Your customers do the same.

This post explains how to build a contact page that actually gets enquiries: what to include, what to leave out, how to design the form, and how to build trust so visitors feel confident reaching out.

## Why Most Contact Pages Fail

Before fixing your contact page, it helps to understand why so many of them fail. The problems are usually simple, and they are the same across many Nigerian business websites.

The form asks for too much. A visitor who only wants a price quote should not have to fill in ten fields, upload documents, and answer questions that belong in a later conversation. Every extra field is a reason to abandon the form.


There is no reason to trust the business. A bare form on a bare page, with no address, no photo, no opening hours, and no sign of a real business behind it, does not inspire confidence. Visitors want to know who they are contacting before they share their details.


## Start With the Visitor's Goal

A good contact page starts with a simple question: what does the visitor want to do? Usually, the answer is one of three things. They want to ask a question, request a price, or book a service. Your contact page should make all three easy.



## The Essential Elements of a Good Contact Page

Here is what every effective contact page for a Nigerian small business should include.

**Multiple ways to contact you.** Offer at least three options: a phone number, a WhatsApp link, and an email address, alongside your contact form. Different customers prefer different channels, and offering a choice increases the chance that each visitor will use the one they are most comfortable with. Make your phone number clickable, so a visitor on a mobile phone can tap it to call you directly. Do the same for your WhatsApp number, so one tap opens a chat. Include your business address if you have a physical location, and your opening hours, so visitors know when to expect a reply.

**A short, simple form.** Your contact form is the heart of the page, and it should be as simple as possible. Ask only for the information you genuinely need to respond. For most businesses, that means name, phone number or WhatsApp number, email address, and a message box where the visitor can describe what they need. A service selector can also help, if you offer several services, because it lets the visitor indicate which one they are interested in. But keep the list short. Do not ask for information you do not need at this stage, such as a full address or budget. Save those questions for the follow-up conversation. Label every field clearly, and mark clearly which fields are required.

**A clear promise about what happens next.** Visitors hesitate when they do not know what will happen after they submit a form. Will someone reply today, or next week? Will they receive a phone call, or an email? Tell them. A simple line such as "We reply within one business day" sets expectations and builds confidence. If you can genuinely respond quickly, say so, because speed is one of the strongest reasons a visitor chooses one business over another. After the form is submitted, show a clear confirmation message. Thank the visitor, confirm that you received their message, and tell them when to expect a reply.

**Trust signals.** A contact page is also a trust page. Visitors are about to share their personal details with you, so give them reasons to feel safe doing so. Include a brief line about your business and your location. Add a photo of your team, your office, or yourself, because people trust a business more when they can see the real people behind it. Opening hours, a physical address, and a real phone number all signal that you are a genuine business. Do not invent testimonials, awards, or client names to fill this space. Honest, verifiable details build far more trust than impressive-sounding claims that a visitor cannot check.


## Common Contact Form Mistakes to Avoid

Asking for too much information is the biggest mistake. Each additional field reduces the number of people who complete the form. Keep it short.


Forgetting to test the form is surprisingly common. Forms break when email settings change, when a plugin is updated, or when a website is redesigned. Test your form regularly by submitting a test message yourself and confirming that it arrives. There is nothing worse than a contact page that silently fails to deliver enquiries for weeks before anyone notices.


## A Simple Contact Page Structure

If you are rebuilding your contact page, here is a structure that works well for most small businesses.

Start with a short headline, such as "Get in touch", followed by one or two sentences explaining what the visitor can expect when they contact you. Next, show your contact details prominently: phone number, WhatsApp link, email address, physical address if you have one, and opening hours.

Then place the form, with only the essential fields and a clear submit button labelled with what it does, such as "Send my enquiry" rather than a vague "Submit". Below the form, add a short trust section: a sentence about your business, a photo, and a link to your services page so visitors who are not quite ready to contact you can learn more first.


## How to Know Whether Your Contact Page Is Working

You cannot improve what you do not measure. Use your website analytics to check how many people visit your contact page, how many submit the form, and how many leave without doing anything. If many people visit the page but few submit the form, the form itself is likely the problem.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many fields should a contact form have?

As few as possible. For most small businesses, four fields are enough: name, phone or WhatsApp number, email address, and a message. Add a service selector only if it genuinely helps you respond faster. Every field you add reduces the number of people who complete the form.

### Should I include my phone number and WhatsApp on the contact page?

Yes. Many Nigerian customers prefer to call or chat on WhatsApp rather than fill in a form. Offering these options alongside the form means you capture enquiries from visitors who would otherwise leave without contacting you.

### How quickly should I reply to an enquiry?

As quickly as you can, ideally within a few hours during business hours, and within one business day at the latest. Customers often contact several businesses at once, and the first helpful reply often wins the job. Set up notifications so you never miss a new enquiry.

### What should the confirmation message say after someone submits the form?

Thank the visitor, confirm that you received their message, and tell them when to expect a reply. For example: "Thank you for your message. We have received your enquiry and will get back to you within one business day." This reassures the visitor and sets a clear expectation.

## Final Thoughts

Your contact page is not a formality. It is the place where your website earns its keep, by turning visitors into conversations and conversations into customers. A short form, multiple contact options, clear expectations, and honest trust signals are all it takes to make it work far harder for your business.

Take a few minutes this week to visit your own contact page as if you were a customer. Try to submit an enquiry from your phone. If anything feels slow, confusing, or untrustworthy, fix it. Your next customer may be the person who decides, in that moment, whether to reach out or to move on.
