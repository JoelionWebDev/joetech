A client in Abuja wants to discuss a project. A supplier in Onitsha needs to show you samples. A partner in London wants a meeting before they send payment. A few years ago, each of these meant travel, traffic, and a full day lost. Today, they can all be a video call from your phone or laptop — and the tool that does it is probably already on your device.

Google Meet is Google's free video meeting service. If you have a Gmail account, you already have it. It runs in a browser on a laptop, and as an app on Android and iPhone. There is nothing to buy, no complicated setup, and your clients do not need any special software either: they tap a link and join. This guide shows you how to set it up, run a meeting that feels professional, and handle the real-world problems Nigerian business owners face on video calls — weak network, background noise, and clients who have never joined a video meeting before.

## Why Google Meet Suits a Small Business

The first reason is the price. Google Meet's basic version is free, and it covers what most small businesses need: one-to-one meetings and group meetings with a reasonable number of people, for a reasonable length of time. For a business watching every naira, a free tool that does the job is worth more than a paid tool with features you will never use.

The second reason is that your clients already have what they need. A client does not need to create an account, download special software, or remember a password to join your meeting. You send them a link on WhatsApp or by email, they tap it, and they are in. That matters in Nigeria, where many clients are busy, many are not technical, and any friction in joining a meeting becomes a reason to postpone it. The easier it is to join, the more meetings actually happen.

The third reason is that it works on a phone. Many of your clients will join from a phone, on mobile data, from wherever they are. Google Meet is built for that. It adjusts the video quality to the network available, so a call continues on a weak connection instead of dropping entirely. And because it is tied to your Google account, it connects naturally with Gmail and Google Calendar: you can create a meeting from an email, add it to your calendar, and the link goes to everyone automatically.

## Setting Up Your First Meeting

Starting a meeting takes less than a minute. On your phone, open the Meet app and tap the option to create a new meeting. On a laptop, open your Gmail and you will find Meet in the menu, or visit the Meet website and sign in with your Google account. Give the meeting a name if you like, and you will get a link. That link is the meeting room: anyone with it can join, so send it only to the people you want in the meeting.

The better way to schedule a meeting is through Google Calendar. Create a calendar event for the meeting, add the people you want to invite, and choose the option to add a Meet link. The invitation goes out with the link included, the meeting appears on everyone's calendar, and reminders arrive automatically. This is the professional way to do it, and it is how you avoid the familiar problem of a client who forgot the meeting was today.

Before an important meeting, test your setup. Join the meeting link yourself a few minutes early and check three things: can people hear you, can they see you, and is your background acceptable. Speak a few words and watch the microphone indicator move. Look at your own video and check the lighting: a light source in front of you is good, a bright window behind you is bad, because it turns you into a silhouette. Check what is visible behind you — a tidy wall or shelf is fine, a messy room or a bed is not. These checks take two minutes and they are the difference between looking professional and looking unprepared.

## Running a Meeting That Feels Professional

A video meeting is still a business meeting, and the same manners apply. Start on time. In Nigeria, where traffic makes physical meetings start late, starting a video call on time is a quiet advantage: it tells the client you respect their time, and it sets the tone for the whole conversation.

Open the meeting with a clear purpose. "Thank you for joining. Today I want to show you the proposal, agree the timeline, and confirm the payment schedule." Thirty seconds of structure at the start saves thirty minutes of wandering later. If there are several topics, name them in order and work through them one at a time.

Keep your attention on the camera, not on yourself. It is tempting to watch your own video, but the client experiences eye contact when you look at the camera. Glance at your notes when you need to, but return your eyes to the camera when you are speaking. Keep your phone on silent and close other apps, because a notification sound or a wandering eye tells the client you are somewhere else.

Manage the conversation actively. In a group meeting, invite quiet people to speak by name: "Mrs Eze, what do you think of the timeline?" Mute yourself when you are not speaking in a larger meeting, so background noise from your side does not disturb everyone. If someone's connection is poor and their voice breaks up, say so kindly and suggest they turn their video off, which usually improves the audio. If two people talk at once, pause and let one finish — video calls make interruptions worse, because nobody can read body language as easily.

End the meeting with a summary. Before anyone leaves, say what was agreed, who will do what, and by when. "So I will send the revised quotation tomorrow, and you will confirm the design by Friday." Then send the same summary by WhatsApp or email within the hour, while the meeting is fresh in everyone's mind. That short message is what turns a good conversation into an actual agreement.

## Presenting Your Screen: Show, Don't Just Tell

One of Google Meet's most useful features for a business is screen sharing. Instead of describing a design, a document or a price list, you show it. A tailor can show fabric options. A designer can show a draft. A consultant can walk a client through a proposal page by page. A shop owner can show a catalogue. Showing is always more convincing than describing.

To present, join the meeting from a laptop or a computer if you can, because presenting from a phone is possible but harder to manage. Open the document, image or website you want to show before the meeting starts, and close anything you do not want the client to see — other tabs, personal messages, unrelated files. When you are ready, use the present option and choose whether to share your whole screen, a single window, or one browser tab. Sharing a single window or tab is usually safest, because it shows only what you intend to show.

While presenting, remember that your audience sees only the shared screen, not your face, so narrate what you are showing. "This is the homepage design. Notice the WhatsApp button at the top, so visitors can reach you in one tap." Move slowly, and pause after each point to ask if there are questions. After the presentation, stop sharing before you open anything else, so a private file never flashes onto the client's screen by accident.

## Handling Poor Network, Noise and Other Real Problems

Video calls in Nigeria come with real-world challenges, and a professional approach is to plan for them rather than pretend they do not exist. The most common is network. Before an important call, connect to the strongest network you have — office Wi-Fi if it is reliable, or mobile data on the network that works best in your location. Close other apps that use data, and ask anyone nearby not to stream videos during your meeting.

If the connection is weak during a call, turn your video off. Audio uses far less data than video, and a clear voice with no picture is far better than a frozen picture with broken audio. Tell the client what you are doing: "My network is a bit weak, so I will turn my video off to keep the audio clear." Clients understand this completely, because they face the same thing themselves.

Background noise is the second problem. If you work from a busy shop or a shared space, find the quietest corner you can, and use earphones with a microphone — even basic ones improve your audio enormously, because the microphone sits close to your mouth and picks up far less of the room. Mute yourself whenever you are not speaking in a group call. If a generator starts or a loud noise interrupts you, mute, wait, and continue. Nobody will judge you for a generator; they will judge you for talking over it.

The third problem is clients who have never joined a video meeting. Help them before the meeting, not during it. Send the link early, on WhatsApp, with a short instruction: "Please tap this link at 10am. It will open in your browser. Allow it to use your camera and microphone, then tap Join." Offer a quick test call the day before if the meeting matters. Five minutes of preparation saves a meeting that starts twenty minutes late while everyone troubleshoots.

## Recording, Notes and Follow-Up

Some meetings are worth keeping a record of. Google Meet's free version does not include built-in recording, and paid plans do, but there are honest ways to keep a record without it. The simplest is to take notes during the meeting: the decisions made, the numbers agreed, and the actions each person promised. Keep those notes in the same place every time — a notebook, a document, or the calendar event for the meeting — so you can find them later.

After the meeting, send a summary message. This is the single most useful follow-up habit in business, and it costs you five minutes. "Thank you for the meeting. As agreed: we will deliver the first draft by Wednesday, the total is as quoted, and the balance is due on delivery. Please let me know if I have missed anything." That message does three things: it confirms the agreement, it gives the client a chance to correct any misunderstanding, and it creates a written record you can both refer to later.

If a meeting is long or complex, consider recording it with your phone's screen recorder or your computer's built-in recording, but always tell the other people first and ask their permission. Recording someone without telling them damages trust. A simple "Would you mind if I record this so I can review the details later?" is enough.

## Using Meet for More Than Client Calls

Google Meet is not only for clients. It works just as well for the inside of your business. A weekly team meeting with staff who work in different locations. A training session for a new staff member. A supplier meeting to agree prices for the month. An interview with a job applicant who lives in another city. Each of these is cheaper and faster as a video call than as a journey across Lagos.

It also works for selling. A short video call with a customer who is hesitating over a big purchase can close a sale that messages alone would not.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

The first mistake is sending the link at the last minute. A meeting link sent five minutes before the meeting starts is an invitation to chaos. Send it the day before, and send it again an hour before the meeting. Two messages cost you seconds and save you the meeting.

The second mistake is joining from a noisy or dark place. A call from a car in traffic, a call with the camera pointing at the ceiling, a call where the client can hear a television in the background — each one says that the meeting did not matter enough to prepare for. Find a quiet spot, face the light, and sit somewhere stable. If you must join from an imperfect place, say so at the start, and keep the call short.

The third mistake is treating a video call like a casual chat. Keep it structured, keep it on time, and keep it to the point. A meeting that wanders for an hour when it needed twenty minutes wastes everyone's day, and the client will remember the waste more than the content.

The fourth mistake is never testing anything. The first time you use screen sharing should not be in front of your biggest client. Practise with a friend or a staff member. Join a meeting from your phone and from your laptop, share your screen, mute and unmute, turn your video on and off. Ten minutes of practice removes almost every technical surprise.

The fifth mistake is forgetting the follow-up. A meeting with no summary message is a meeting that fades. Send the summary within the hour, while the details are fresh. It is the cheapest, most reliable way to make sure what was agreed actually happens.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Google Meet really free?**

Yes, the basic version is free with any Google account, and it covers one-to-one and group meetings for a small business. Paid plans exist with longer group meetings, recording, and extra features, but most small businesses do not need them to start. Check the current limits on Google's own pages before you plan a very long group meeting, because limits can change.

**Does the client need a Google account to join my meeting?**

No. Anyone with the meeting link can join from a browser or the Meet app, without signing in, as long as you admit them. That is one of the reasons Meet works well for business: your client does not need to create anything or remember a password.

**Can I use Google Meet on my phone?**

Yes. There is a Meet app for Android and iPhone, and it works well on mobile data. You can host and join meetings, share your screen, and manage participants from your phone. For presenting documents, a laptop is easier, but for a conversation, a phone is perfectly fine.

**What do I do if the call keeps freezing?**

First, turn your video off, which usually fixes the audio immediately. Second, close other apps using data. Third, move closer to your Wi-Fi router, or switch to mobile data if your Wi-Fi is the weak link. If the problem continues, end the call politely and rejoin, or switch to a voice call to finish the conversation. A short, honest explanation keeps the client's trust.

**Can I schedule a Google Meet through WhatsApp?**

You cannot create the meeting inside WhatsApp, but you can create it in Google Calendar and share the link on WhatsApp. That combination is what most Nigerian businesses actually use: the calendar handles the scheduling and reminders, and WhatsApp handles the quick message with the link. Send the link the day before and again an hour before the meeting.

Third, set your meeting habits: join five minutes early, start with a clear purpose, end with a spoken summary, and send the written summary within the hour.
