You built a great app and submitted it to Google Play and the App Store. And then... silence. Downloads trickle in, and most people who land on your app page decide not to install.

Here is the part nobody told you before launch: submitting your app is only half the battle. The other half is making sure people can actually find it — and want it — the moment they do. That is where App Store Optimisation (ASO) comes in.

ASO is to mobile apps what SEO is to websites. It is the discipline of making your app discoverable and appealing inside the app stores, and it is often the cheapest way to grow. Here is how to approach it for an app that wants to reach users in Nigeria and abroad.

## Understanding the Two Jobs of ASO

Think of a shopper walking into a huge store looking for something specific. Your app's listing — its name, icon, screenshots, and description — is what tells them whether this is what they want.

ASO does two distinct jobs:

**1. Discovery — can people find you?** When someone searches "send money," "booking app," or "loan app," does yours appear and rank?

**2. Conversion — do people install you?** When people find you, does your page convince them to tap "Install"?

Both matter. Rank high with a weak page and you get visibility but few installs. Have a great page buried in results and you get nothing. You need both.

## The Listing Elements You Control

These are the pieces most directly in your control, and each one affects ranking or conversion.

### Your app name

Your app name is one of the strongest ranking signals. Including your most important keyword naturally — for example "QuickSend — Instant Money Transfer" — helps more than a purely generic name. Keep it clean and readable.

### Your keywords

The Play Store weights your title and short description heavily in search. Think about what users in each country actually type. A user in northern Nigeria may search "loan app," while another user elsewhere searches "lending app." Optimise for your real audience, not just for what you call the product.

### Your icon

The icon is your first impression. It must be recognisable at a small size, stand out next to competitors, and communicate the purpose of the app in one glance. Test two or three designs before committing.

### Screenshots

Most installs are decided from the first one or two screenshots. Show the core benefit within a styled, readable view. Add short overlay text at the main value proposition so users understand the value instantly.

### Your description

The first two or three lines appear before the "more" button, so put your strongest benefit and your target keywords there. The full description is read by humans who skim — write clearly, structure with short sections, and do not stuff it with keywords.

### Video preview

A short 15 to 30 second preview can lift conversion because it shows the app in action. If you can, produce one for your most compelling flow.

## Trust Signals That Lift Downloads

### Ratings and reviews

Ratings affect visibility and every potential user sees them. Build a gentle way to ask happy users for a review at a natural moment, and fix the reasons unhappy users are leaving before you chase volume.

### Install velocity

Stores pay attention to how quickly and consistently new installs arrive. A steady flow of real, engaged users is a strong signal. Early adopters, internal channels, and small organic pushes all help build this.

Do not buy fake installs. They can hurt ranking and get your app flagged or suspended. Real growth beats shortcuts.

### Localisation

Translating your listing opens new markets. For an app going abroad, localise into the languages and currencies of the countries you target. At home, keep the listing in natural English — the dominant store interface with Nigerian users.

## Setting Realistic Expectations

ASO is a slow, cumulative effort. It rarely delivers an overnight spike, but it pays off over time.

- **It works through search intent.** You reach people who are already looking for what you offer. To reach people who do not yet know they need you, combine ASO with ads and marketing.
- **The algorithms keep shifting.** The stores change ranking rules, competitors move, and your metadata grows stale. ASO is ongoing work, not a one-time task.
- **Measure what you manage.** Track impressions, page views, and install conversion rate. Never guess whether a change helped — measure it.

## How to Get Started

1. **Study competitors.** Apps ranked well make their titles, keywords, and screenshot hooks clear.
2. **Use the words real users type.** Talk to your users and note the search suggestions the store shows.
3. **Optimise the elements.** Name, icon, short description, full description, and screenshots.
4. **Answer the questions people ask.** Use the words people actually search so the algorithm understands and ranks your app.
5. **Test and keep improving.** Make a change, measure the clicks and installs, keep what works, and repeat.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**When should I start on ASO?**

Start on the day you define the app. Choose your name and icon direction early and do it well the first time — it is easier to build the whole launch plan around good decisions than to redo them later.

**Is ASO more important than paid ads?**

Both matter, but ASO is permanent while an ad campaign is temporary. Ads reach new audiences; ASO converts the search intent so you do not pay for every tap. It improves return on advertising too because you get a bigger share of search.

**Do I need a big budget?**

Not for the core. Adjusting name, icon, description, and screenshots is free. You only invest if you choose paid growth or additional advertising.

**Should ASO be combined with digital marketing?**

Yes. They work together. Marketing brings people to consider your app; ASO captures the interest in the store at the moment of need.
