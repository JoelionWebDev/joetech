Your business bio is the smallest piece of writing in your business, and one of the most important. On Instagram it is the few lines under your name that a stranger reads before deciding whether to follow you, message you, or scroll away. On WhatsApp Business it is the short description a customer sees when they open your profile to ask about a price. In both places, you have only seconds, and only a few words, to answer the customer's three questions: what do you sell, where are you, and how do I order?

Most Nigerian business bios fail at this simple job. They are full of emojis and no information, or they are a list of motivational quotes, or they say "DM for price" and nothing else. A customer who cannot tell what you sell from your bio will not spend time finding out. They will move to the next seller whose bio is clear.

This guide shows you how to write a business bio for Instagram and WhatsApp that turns visitors into customers, with a simple formula, examples for different types of businesses, and the mistakes to avoid.

## Why Your Bio Matters More Than Your Posts

Your posts are seen by people who already follow you. Your bio is seen by everyone who finds you for the first time: from a search, from a tagged photo, from a friend's recommendation, or from a WhatsApp status. It is your first impression, and for many customers, it is the only impression they will ever get.

Think about how a customer finds a vendor. A friend says, "Check Ada's page, she makes nice abayas." The customer opens the page. In two seconds, they look at the profile picture, the name, and the bio. If the bio says what she sells, where she is, and how to order, the customer taps the WhatsApp link or sends a message. If the bio says "God's grace 🙏✨💯 Blessed and highly favoured", the customer learns nothing, and they leave.

Finally, your bio tells Instagram and WhatsApp search what your business is about. When someone searches "cakes in Lekki" or " Ankara fabric Lagos", a bio that contains those words is more likely to appear. A bio with no useful words is invisible to search, no matter how good your products are.

## The Four Things Every Business Bio Must Answer

Every good business bio, on any platform, answers four questions. If your bio answers all four, it is doing its job. If it misses one, you are losing customers.

The first question is: what do you sell? Say it plainly, in the first line. "Ladies' ready-to-wear" is clear. "Quality goods at affordable prices" is not, because it could mean anything. Name your product or service in words a customer would actually search for.

The second question is: who is it for? A small detail here helps the right customers recognise themselves. "Sizes 6 to 18", "For brides and bridesmaids", "For busy mums", "Wholesale and retail". This line filters your audience, so you spend less time answering questions from people who were never going to buy.

The third question is: where are you? Customers want to know your city and area, because it tells them about delivery time and cost. "Based in Lekki, Lagos" is enough. "Deliver nationwide" tells them you can serve them wherever they are. If you have a physical shop, add the area or the street, or keep the full address in your highlights or your WhatsApp Business address field.

The fourth question is: how do I order? Tell them the next step. "WhatsApp to order", "Tap the link below to shop", "DM 'ORDER' to buy". A bio without a next step is like a shop with a locked door. The customer knows what you sell, but cannot reach you.

Write these four answers down before you touch your bio. Then arrange them into two or three short lines. That is your bio.

## Writing Your Instagram Bio: The Formula

Instagram gives you 150 characters for your bio, which is about thirty words. That sounds small, but it is enough if every word works.

Use the first line for what you sell and who it is for. "Handmade Ankara dresses | Sizes 6–20" or "Fresh cakes & pastries for birthdays & weddings". Put the most important words first, because some visitors will only read the first line.

Use the second line for your location and delivery. "Lekki, Lagos | Delivery nationwide" or "Ikeja & environs | Pickup available". This line answers the delivery question before the customer asks it.

Use the third line for how to order and any special offer. "WhatsApp to order 👇" with the link below it, or "Free delivery in Lekki this weekend". A small offer in the bio can be the push a visitor needs to tap your link.

Use your link wisely. Instagram gives you one link in your bio, or several if you use a link page. Point it to the place where customers can buy fastest, which for most Nigerian sellers is a WhatsApp chat. A link that opens a direct WhatsApp message with a pre-written text, such as "Hello, I want to order", removes a step for the customer and brings you a serious buyer.

Use line breaks to make the bio easy to scan. A bio written as one long paragraph is hard to read on a phone. A bio written as three short lines, each with its own job, is read in two seconds.

## Writing Your WhatsApp Business Bio: Short and Useful

WhatsApp Business gives you a short "About" section, also called a business description, and it works differently from Instagram. Customers usually see it when they open your chat or your profile to check who you are before they send money. That means it must build trust fast.

Keep it to two or three lines. "Ada's Cakes — fresh cakes and pastries in Surulere, Lagos. Orders: WhatsApp 0803 000 0000. Delivery in Lagos, pickup available." That is all it needs to be. It says what you sell, where you are, and how to reach you.

Include your business hours if they matter. "Open Mon–Sat, 8am–7pm" tells a customer when to expect a reply. If you reply slowly at night, say so honestly rather than leaving the customer wondering. A simple "We reply within 2 hours during business hours" sets a fair expectation.

Use the other WhatsApp Business fields too, because they work together with your bio. Fill in your business address, your email, your website if you have one, and your opening hours. These fields show on your profile and make you look established. A WhatsApp Business profile with a complete bio, an address, and a catalogue of your products looks like a real shop. A profile with a blank bio and no information looks like a personal number.

Update your WhatsApp bio when things change. If you travel, change your location, or stop taking orders for a week, say so in your bio or your status. A customer who reads "Closed for holidays, back on Monday" will wait. A customer who messages a silent number for three days will buy from someone else.

## Business Bio Examples for Nigerian Sellers

Examples help more than rules, so here are bios for common Nigerian businesses. Adapt the words to your own business, but keep the structure: what, who, where, how.

For a fashion seller: "Ready-to-wear Ankara & lace | Sizes 6–20. Based in Lekki, Lagos. Delivery nationwide. WhatsApp to order 👇" This bio answers all four questions in three lines, and the customer knows the sizes before they message.

For a food vendor: "Fresh soups & party jollof in Abuja. Bulk orders for events welcome. Same-day delivery in Wuse & Garki. Order on WhatsApp 👇" The bio says the product, the area, and the special strength: events and bulk orders.

For a beauty seller: "Original skincare & hair products. For all skin types. Lagos & nationwide delivery. Free consultation on WhatsApp 👇" The offer of a free consultation is the reason a visitor taps the link, because it promises help, not just a sale.

For a phone accessories seller: "Phone accessories & gadgets in Computer Village, Ikeja. Wholesale & retail. Nationwide delivery. Send a DM to order." The location is a strength here, because Computer Village is a known market, and wholesale buyers know exactly where to find the seller.

For a service business, such as a makeup artist: "Bridal & event makeup artist, Lagos. Home service available. Book 2 weeks ahead. WhatsApp to book 👇" The bio tells the customer the service, the city, and the important detail that booking ahead is needed.

Notice what these bios have in common: short lines, plain words, a location, and a clear next step. None of them needs a motivational quote, and none of them hides the product behind clever language.

## Words and Phrases That Help, and Ones That Hurt

Some words in a bio make customers trust you. Others make them leave. Choose carefully.

Words that help include your product name, your city, and clear promises: "fresh", "handmade", "original", "ready-to-wear", "nationwide delivery", "wholesale and retail", "book now". These words are specific, and they tell the customer something useful.

Phrases that hurt include vague claims nobody can check: "best quality in Nigeria", "number one vendor", "cheapest prices guaranteed", "100% original" with no evidence. Customers have seen these claims a thousand times, and they ignore them. If your products are original, prove it with a video or a review, rather than shouting it in your bio.

Avoid "DM for price" as your only call to action. It tells the customer that buying from you will take extra steps, and many will not bother. If your prices vary, put a starting price in the bio instead: "Dresses from ₦12,000" or "Cakes from ₦15,000". A starting price filters out buyers who cannot afford you and attracts buyers who can.

Avoid writing your bio in all capital letters. "WE SELL THE BEST SHOES IN LAGOS" is harder to read than "Quality shoes in Lagos", and it looks like shouting. Use normal capitalisation, and let your words do the work.

## Your Name, Username, and Profile Picture Work with Your Bio

Your bio does not work alone. Three other parts of your profile help or hurt it.

Your name field on Instagram is searchable, so use it well. Instead of just "Ada", write "Ada's Fashion | Ankara & Lace". Instead of just "Tunde", write "Tunde Cakes | Lagos Baker". The name field holds the words customers search for, and it works together with your bio to tell search engines and people what you do. On WhatsApp Business, your business name should match your Instagram name, so customers who find you in one place recognise you in the other.

Your username should be simple and easy to spell. A username like "adas_fashion_lagos" is easy to remember and to tell a friend. A username with random numbers, underscores, and strange spellings, like "ada_fashion_xx_247_lagos", is hard to share and hard to find. If your ideal username is taken, add your city or your product, not random numbers.

Your profile picture should be your logo or a clear photo of you. It should be readable at a tiny size, because that is how most people will see it. A logo with small writing will be unreadable. A photo where your face is clear will be recognised. Whichever you choose, use the same picture on Instagram and WhatsApp, so customers know they have found the same business in both places.

## Keep Your Bio Fresh: When and How to Update It

A bio is not written once and forgotten. Your business changes, and your bio should change with it.

Update your bio when you add a new product line. If you start selling bags as well as shoes, your bio should say so, or bag buyers will never know. Update it when you change location, when you start delivering to new cities, or when your prices change in a way that matters.

Update it when you learn something from your customers. If three customers in a week ask whether you deliver to Ibadan, add "Delivery to Ibadan available" to your bio. If customers keep asking for your price range, add a starting price. Your bio should answer the questions customers actually ask, not only the questions you thought of on the first day.

Review your bio once a month. Read it as if you were a stranger. Can you tell what the business sells? Can you tell where it is? Can you tell how to order? If any answer is missing, fix it that day. A five-minute review once a month keeps your bio working for you all year.

## Mistakes That Make Customers Scroll Past

The most common mistake is a bio that says nothing about the business. Motivational quotes, Bible verses, and personal slogans belong on a personal page, not a business page. A customer who visits a business page wants business information. Give it to them first, and save the inspiration for your posts.

The second mistake is copying another vendor's bio word for word. Customers notice, and it makes you look unoriginal. It also confuses search, because two pages with identical bios compete for the same words. Write your own bio, in your own words, about your own products.

The third mistake is an out-of-date bio. A bio that says "Christmas sale" in March, or lists a phone number you no longer use, tells the customer that the business is not paying attention. If a customer messages a dead number, they will not try again. Check your bio and your link every month, and test the link yourself by tapping it.

The fifth mistake is a bio written only for other vendors. Words like "distributor wanted" or "resellers needed" may attract other sellers, but they confuse ordinary customers. If you serve both retail customers and resellers, write the bio for the retail customer first, and mention wholesale in one line. The customer is your main audience.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Should my Instagram bio and my WhatsApp bio be the same?

They should say the same things, but they do not need to be identical. Instagram has a character limit and a link, so your bio there is a short sales pitch. WhatsApp has a short description plus fields for address, hours, and email, so your bio there can be simpler and more factual. Keep the business name, the product, and the location the same in both places so customers recognise you.

### Can I put my phone number in my Instagram bio?

Yes, but it is usually better to put a WhatsApp link instead of a plain number. A link opens a chat directly, while a number must be copied and saved first. If you do write the number, write it in full with the country code, so customers outside Nigeria can reach you too. Never put a personal number you do not want the public to see.

### What should I do if my business sells many different things?

Name the main categories, not every single item. "Fashion, bags & accessories" covers a lot without listing everything. "Foodstuffs & provisions" is enough for a general store. Use your posts, your highlights, and your catalogue to show the details. Your bio is a signboard, not a catalogue.

## What to Do Next

1. Write down the four answers for your business: what you sell, who it is for, where you are, and how to order. Then write a three-line bio from those answers, using the formula in this guide.
2. Update your Instagram bio and your WhatsApp Business bio today, and make sure your name field, username, profile picture, and link match. Tap your own link to confirm it opens your WhatsApp chat.
3. Ask three friends to read your new bio and tell you, in ten seconds, what you sell and how to order. If they cannot answer, rewrite it until they can. Then review it again next month.
