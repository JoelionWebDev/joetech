If you sell clothes, food, hair, skincare, shoes, or gadgets in Nigeria, your next customer is probably scrolling TikTok right now. Millions of Nigerians open the app every day, and they do not only watch dance videos — they watch people unbox products, try on clothes, cook with new ingredients, and review gadgets, and then they buy what they see.

TikTok works differently from Instagram or Facebook, where you mostly reach people who already follow you. On TikTok, the algorithm shows your video to strangers who have never heard of you, as long as it is interesting. A small business in Enugu or Ilorin with zero followers can reach thousands of potential buyers with a single good video — no other platform gives a small Nigerian business that kind of free reach.

But TikTok is not magic. Posting random videos and hoping for sales will not work. You need a clear setup, the right kind of videos, a simple way for people to order, and a routine you can keep up. This guide covers all of it, step by step.

## Why TikTok Works for Nigerian Sellers

Three things make TikTok powerful for selling in Nigeria. The first is reach. TikTok's "For You" page shows videos based on what people enjoy, not on who they follow. A video of a well-made dress, a delicious plate of food, or a clever gadget can travel far beyond your own followers. A single video can bring in more new customers than a month of posting on other platforms.

The second is trust. Nigerians buy from people they feel they know, and TikTok's short, informal videos let customers see your face, hear your voice, see your shop, and watch your product in real life. That feels more honest than a polished advert: when a customer watches you pack an order or try on a dress, they start to trust you, and trust turns a viewer into a buyer.

The third is cost. TikTok is still largely free to grow on: a phone, good daylight, and a clear idea are enough to start. Many Nigerian sellers have built six-figure monthly sales from videos shot in their shop or living room. The barrier is low, but the opportunity is large, and it will not stay this open forever.

## Step 1: Set Up Your Account Like a Shop, Not a Personal Page

If you already have a personal TikTok account, do not use it for business. Create a separate business account, or convert your existing one properly. A business account gives you access to analytics, so you can see which videos bring in views, followers, and enquiries. It also looks more professional to buyers.

Choose a username that matches your business name as closely as possible — if your business is "Ada's Cakes", use something like @adascakes or @adascakesng. Avoid long strings of numbers and random words: your username is what customers will search for and tell their friends.

Write a short, clear bio that says what you sell, where you are based, and how to order, in one or two lines: "Affordable wigs and hair, Lekki Lagos. Order on WhatsApp, link below. Nationwide delivery." Add your WhatsApp or website link — it is the most important part of your account, because it is how a viewer becomes a buyer.

Use a clear profile photo. Your logo works well, but a photo of your best product can work too. Whatever you choose, make sure it is sharp and recognisable at a small size. When your video appears on someone's feed, your profile photo is one of the first things they see.

Set your location and language to Nigeria and English, so TikTok shows your videos to Nigerian users first — that is where your buyers are. If you sell only in one city, say so in your bio.

## Step 2: Know What Your Customers Want to Watch

TikTok users do not open the app to watch adverts. They open it to be entertained, to learn something, or to discover something new. Your videos need to fit into one of those three reasons, or people will scroll past in two seconds.

Entertainment videos show your product in a fun, lively way. A fashion seller might do a quick try-on with music, showing five outfits in thirty seconds. A food seller might film the sizzle of meat hitting a hot pan. A skincare seller might film a satisfying before-and-after. The product is the star, but the video feels like fun, not like a sales pitch.

Educational videos teach the viewer something useful. A hair seller can show how to care for a wig so it lasts longer. A tailor can show how to measure yourself correctly before ordering. A food vendor can show how to store pepper soup so it stays fresh. When you teach, people save your video, follow you for more, and start to see you as an expert. Experts get the sale.

Discovery videos show something new. A new product, a new colour, a new style, a behind-the-scenes look at how you make your product. People love seeing how things are made. A video of you sewing a dress, baking a cake, or packing an order with care can get more views than any advert, because it feels real.

Watch what works in your niche. Search TikTok for your type of product — "wigs Nigeria", "asoebi styles", "Lagos food vendor" — and study the videos with the most views: how they start, how long they are, what music they use. Do not copy them word for word; learn the pattern, then make it your own.

## Step 3: Make Videos That Sell Without Feeling Like Adverts

The best selling videos on TikTok do not look like adverts. They look like a friend showing you something they love. Here is how to make them.

Start strong. The first two seconds decide whether someone watches or scrolls. Start with the most interesting part: the finished dress, the sizzling food, the glowing skin, the unboxed gadget. Do not start with a long greeting or your logo. Say something that makes people stop: "This wig is ₦18,000 and it looks like this", or "Watch me turn this ₦5,000 fabric into a dress", or "POV: you ordered food from us for the first time".

Show the product in real life. Customers in Nigeria are wary of buying online because they have been disappointed before. Show the product from different angles. Show it being worn, used, or eaten. Show the real colour, not a filtered one. Show the size next to something familiar. The more real your video looks, the more buyers will trust it.

Say the price. Many Nigerian sellers hide their prices and say "DM for price". That slows down sales. Customers who have to ask for a price often do not bother, and those who do ask may feel the price changes depending on who is asking. Say the price in the video or write it clearly on the screen. Transparent pricing builds trust and brings in serious buyers.

Tell people exactly what to do next. End every video with a clear call to action: "Click the link in my bio to order on WhatsApp", or "Send us a DM with the word WIG to order". Do not assume viewers know what to do. Tell them, simply and directly.

Film with your phone, in good light, with clear sound — you do not need expensive equipment. Stand near a window during the day, or film outside in the shade. Speak clearly, and use captions: many Nigerians watch TikTok with the sound off, in public transport or at work, so captions are how half your audience understands your video.

## Step 4: Post Regularly — Consistency Beats Perfection

TikTok rewards consistency. Posting one video a day beats posting ten in one day and disappearing for two weeks. Aim for one to three videos a day if you can, or at least one a day, five days a week. It gets easier once you build a routine.

Batch your filming: set aside two hours, twice a week, to film five or six videos at once, changing your outfit or background between them so they do not all look the same. Then post one each day — you are not filming daily, and you always have something ready.

Post at the times your customers are online. For most Nigerian audiences, the best times are early morning between 6 and 8 am, lunchtime between 12 and 2 pm, and evenings between 7 and 10 pm. Test different times and check your analytics to see when your own audience is most active. Your customers may be different from the average.

Do not delete a video because it got few views in the first hour — TikTok sometimes pushes a video days after you post it. A flop on Monday can take off on Thursday. Leave your videos up and keep posting; each one is a new chance to reach buyers.

Reply to comments. When someone asks a question under your video, answer quickly and warmly — other people read the comments before they buy, and your replies show how you treat customers. A friendly reply can turn a commenter into a buyer; a rude or slow one does the opposite. Pin a comment that answers a common question to the top.

## Step 5: Turn Viewers Into Paying Customers

Views are not sales. A video with 50,000 views and zero orders is entertainment, not business. The bridge between a view and a sale is a simple, fast ordering process.

Make ordering easy. Most Nigerian sellers take orders on WhatsApp, where customers are comfortable paying and sharing their address, so your TikTok bio link should open WhatsApp directly. When a customer messages you, reply quickly — one who waits six hours has often already bought from someone else. If you cannot reply instantly, set up a short automatic greeting on WhatsApp Business: "Hello, thank you for messaging us. We will reply within 30 minutes. Please send the item name and your delivery address."

Confirm the order clearly. Send a short summary: the item, the size or colour, the price, the delivery fee, and the total. Ask the customer to confirm. Then send your account details for payment. When they send the payment screenshot, confirm it and give a delivery date. This clear process prevents confusion, reduces the number of abandoned orders, and protects you if a dispute arises.

Handle payments carefully. Bank transfer is still the most common method in Nigeria, but payment links are becoming more popular because they record the payment automatically and reduce fraud. Whatever method you use, never send a product before payment is confirmed, unless you have a clear arrangement with a customer you already trust. Cash on delivery sounds attractive to customers, but it carries risks: customers may refuse the delivery, and you lose the delivery fee. If you offer it, do so only in your own city, and only for products you can easily resell.

Deliver on time, and communicate about delays. If a rider is late, tell the customer before they ask. If a parcel will take three days to reach Abuja, say so at the point of order. Customers forgive delays when they are informed. They do not forgive silence.

After delivery, follow up. A short message — "Your order has arrived. Please let us know if everything is fine" — shows care and often produces a review or a repeat order. Save every satisfied customer's number, with permission, and add them to your WhatsApp broadcast list for your next launch or sale.

## Step 6: Use Live Videos, TikTok Shop, and Paid Adverts Carefully

Once your account is growing, you can use TikTok's bigger tools. Go live once or twice a week. A live video lets customers ask questions in real time, see your products up close, and buy on the spot. You can show new stock, try on clothes, answer questions about sizes, and offer a small discount only for people watching live. Even twenty or thirty viewers on a live can produce several orders, because live viewers are your most interested audience.

TikTok Shop is available in some markets and is expanding. If it becomes available to you in Nigeria, check the fees, the rules, and the payment terms carefully before you join. It can make ordering easier, but it also takes a commission, and you must follow its policies on returns and delivery times. Do not join a platform feature until you understand what it costs you.

Paid adverts on TikTok can bring in new buyers, but treat them like any other business expense. Start with a small budget, like ₦10,000 to ₦20,000, and promote your best-performing organic video, not a brand-new one. If a video already gets good views and comments for free, an advert will amplify it. If a video performs badly for free, paying to show it to more people will not fix it. Watch the results closely, and stop any advert that brings views but no orders.

Work with small creators if you can. A micro-influencer in your city, with a few thousand engaged followers, can be more effective and far cheaper than a big celebrity. Send them your product, let them review it honestly, and agree clearly on what they will post — their followers trust them, and that trust transfers to you.

## Mistakes That Waste Your Time on TikTok

The first mistake is posting only adverts. If every video is "Buy this, buy this", people will scroll past. Mix selling videos with entertaining and educational ones. Aim for roughly one direct selling video in every three.

The second mistake is hiding your prices. "DM for price" slows down sales and makes customers suspicious. Say the price, or write it clearly on the screen.

The third mistake is slow replies. A TikTok viewer is one tap away from the next seller. Reply to messages and comments fast, or you will lose the sale to someone who does.

The fourth mistake is copying other sellers exactly. Customers can tell when a video is copied. Learn from others, but show your own face, your own shop, and your own personality. That is what makes you different.

The fifth mistake is giving up too early. Most accounts take weeks or months to find their audience. If your first ten videos get few views, that is normal. Keep posting, study your analytics, and improve one thing at a time. The sellers who succeed on TikTok are usually the ones who simply refused to stop.

## How to Know If TikTok Is Working

Check your numbers every week. How many views did your videos get? How many new followers? How many WhatsApp messages came from TikTok? How many of those became orders? How much did you sell? Write these numbers down. They tell you what is working and what is not.

Look for patterns. Which videos got the most views? Which ones brought the most messages? Was it the try-on, the tutorial, or the behind-the-scenes video? Make more of what works, and less of what does not. If your morning videos perform better than your evening ones, post in the morning. If videos where you speak to the camera outperform silent ones, speak more.

Set a simple goal for your first three months: a number of followers, a number of orders per week from TikTok, or a sales target. Review it monthly. If TikTok is bringing in orders, invest more time in it. If it is bringing views but no sales, fix your ordering process before you make more videos.

## Start This Week

You do not need a studio, a camera, or a big budget. You need a phone, a product, and a plan. Here is a simple way to start. Today, set up your business account, write your bio, and add your WhatsApp link. Tomorrow, film three short videos: one showing your best product, one teaching something useful, and one showing how you pack an order. Post the first one tomorrow evening. Post the second the next day. Post the third the day after. Reply to every comment. Save every customer who orders.

Within three months, it can become one of your most reliable sources of new customers. The sellers winning on TikTok in Nigeria are not the ones with the most money — they are the ones who started, kept posting, and made it easy for viewers to buy. Start with your first video.
