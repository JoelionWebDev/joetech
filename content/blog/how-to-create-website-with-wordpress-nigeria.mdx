Every business in Nigeria today needs a place on the internet that it owns. Social media accounts are useful, but they are rented space: the platform can change its rules, limit who sees your posts, or suspend your account without warning. A website is different. It is your own address on the internet, open twenty-four hours a day, where customers can find you, learn what you sell, and contact you — even while you sleep. And building one is far easier than most business owners think.

WordPress is the most popular way in the world to build a website, and it powers a large share of all the websites on the internet, including many Nigerian business sites. It is free software, it does not require you to write code, and you can build a professional-looking site yourself by following a series of clear steps. This guide walks through those steps one by one: choosing a domain name, getting hosting, installing WordPress, picking a theme, creating your essential pages, and launching a site you can be proud of.

## Start With a Plan — What Your Website Needs to Do

Before you buy anything or click anything, spend a little time deciding what your website is for. A website without a purpose becomes a collection of random pages that confuses visitors and brings in no business. Ask yourself three simple questions. What do you want visitors to do on your site? For most small businesses, the answer is one of these: contact you, see your products or services, or place an order. Who are your visitors? A fashion business in Lekki serves different customers from a farm produce supplier in Jos, and the words and photos on the site should speak to those particular people. And what information do those visitors need before they trust you enough to buy? Usually it is your prices or price range, your location, how to contact you, and proof that you are a real business — photos, reviews, or examples of your work.

Write down the pages your site needs based on those answers. Almost every small business website needs the same core pages: a home page that explains what you do in seconds, an about page that tells your story, a products or services page, and a contact page. Depending on your business, you may also want a gallery, a price list, a testimonials page, or a simple blog where you share tips and news. Keep the list short to start with — four or five pages is enough for a first website. You can always add more later, and a small, complete website is far better than a large, half-finished one that never gets launched.

## Choose a Domain Name and Buy Hosting

Your domain name is your website's address on the internet — for example, yourbusiness.com.ng — and it is the first thing to choose. A good domain name is short, easy to remember, easy to spell over the phone, and closely related to your business name. Avoid long names, hyphens, numbers that could be confused, and unusual spellings. If your business is called Adaeze Fashion, a domain such as adaezefashion.com.ng is far better than adaeze-fashion-2024-online-store.com. For a Nigerian business serving Nigerian customers, a .com.ng domain is a strong choice because it signals that you are a Nigerian business, and it is often available even when the matching .com is taken. A .com is also fine and is the most widely recognised ending in the world. Check whether your preferred name is available with a domain registrar, and if your first choice is taken, try a small variation — adding your city, such as "lagos", or a word that describes what you do, such as "foods" or "designs" — rather than choosing a completely different name.

Hosting is the service that stores your website's files and makes them available on the internet, twenty-four hours a day. You rent space on a company's computers, called servers, and that company keeps your site online. When choosing a hosting company, look for a few essentials: it should offer one-click WordPress installation, so you do not have to install anything manually; it should include a free security certificate, known as SSL, so your site address starts with https and visitors see a padlock; it should offer regular backups; and it should have customer support you can actually reach. Many hosting companies offer plans priced in Naira or in dollars, paid monthly or yearly, and for a small business website the cheapest plan is usually enough to start. You can upgrade later as your site grows.

## Install WordPress and Learn Your Way Around

Once your hosting is ready, installing WordPress is usually a one-click job. Log in to your hosting account, find the WordPress installer — it is often labelled "WordPress", "One-click install" or "Softaculous" — and follow the prompts. You will choose your domain name, create a username and password for your WordPress admin area, and give your site a title. Choose a strong password that is different from your hosting password, and avoid obvious usernames such as "admin". Within a few minutes, WordPress will be installed, and you will be able to log in to your admin area by going to your domain followed by /wp-admin — for example, yourbusiness.com.ng/wp-admin — and entering the username and password you created.

The WordPress admin area, called the dashboard, is where you will build and manage your site. It may look busy at first, but you only need a few sections to begin with. Posts are for blog articles and news. Pages are for the permanent parts of your site, such as your home page, about page and contact page. Media is where your photos and files live. Appearance is where you change how your site looks, including your theme and menus. Plugins are extra features you can add, such as a contact form or a security tool. Settings is where you control the basics, such as your site title and how your page addresses look.

## Pick a Theme and Make the Site Look Like Your Business

A WordPress theme controls how your site looks: the layout, the colours, the fonts and the overall style. There are thousands of free and paid themes, and choosing one is one of the most enjoyable parts of building your site — and also one of the places where beginners waste the most time. The right approach is simple: choose a theme that is clean, fast and suited to your type of business, and then stop looking. A fashion business needs a theme that shows photos beautifully; a service business needs one that makes its services and contact details easy to find; a restaurant needs one that shows its menu clearly.

Start with the free themes available inside WordPress itself, under Appearance and then Themes. Look for themes that say they are responsive, which means they look good on phones as well as on computers — this matters enormously in Nigeria, where most of your visitors will browse on a phone. Check that the theme has been updated recently and has good reviews, and preview it before you activate it. Popular free themes such as Astra, OceanWP and the default WordPress themes are solid, reliable choices for a first website. Paid themes, usually bought from a theme marketplace, offer more designs and features, but they are not necessary for a good first site.

Once your theme is active, customise it to look like your business. Under Appearance and then Customise, you can usually set your logo, your colours, and your site title. Use the colours of your business if you have them, or choose two or three colours that suit what you sell and use them consistently. Upload your logo if you have one; if you do not, your business name in a clean font is fine for now. Add a menu under Appearance and then Menus, with links to your main pages — Home, About, Services, Contact — so visitors can find their way around. Keep the design simple and clean. A website with clear text, good photos and easy navigation will bring you far more business than a crowded site full of animations and effects that slow it down and confuse visitors.

## Create Your Essential Pages

Now comes the most important part: the content. Start with your home page. A visitor should understand what you do within five seconds of arriving, so put a clear headline at the top — what you sell and who it is for — followed by a short paragraph, a button that leads to your products or your contact page, and a few of your best photos. Below that, add a short section about your main products or services, a few words about why customers choose you, and a customer review or two if you have them. Keep the home page focused; it is an introduction, not your whole business.

Your about page tells the story behind the business: who you are, when you started, what you sell, and why you care about it. Customers in Nigeria buy from people they trust, and a genuine story builds that trust far better than a list of claims. Write in plain, friendly language, include a photo of yourself or your team if you are comfortable, and mention your location. Your products or services page should list what you offer with clear photos, honest descriptions and prices or price ranges wherever possible. Customers who cannot find a price will often leave and buy from a seller who shows one.

Your contact page needs your phone number, your WhatsApp link, your email address, your location, and your business hours. Add a contact form so visitors can message you without leaving your site — the free WPForms or Contact Form 7 plugin will create one in minutes. Test the form yourself before you launch, to make sure messages actually reach you. Finally, create a privacy policy page if you collect any information from visitors, and check that every page looks good on your phone, because that is how most of your visitors will see it. Read every page aloud before you publish it; if a sentence sounds awkward when spoken, rewrite it until it sounds natural.

## Add the Plugins You Need — and Only Those

Plugins are extra features you add to WordPress, and they are one of its greatest strengths. They are also one of the easiest ways to slow your site down and create problems, because every plugin you install adds code that your site must load. The rule is simple: install only the plugins you actually need, and choose well-known, regularly updated ones.

Start with the essentials. A security plugin, such as Wordfence, helps protect your site from attacks and unauthorised logins. A backup plugin, such as UpdraftPlus, saves a copy of your site regularly so you can restore it if anything goes wrong. A search engine optimisation plugin, such as Rank Math or Yoast SEO, helps you write page titles and descriptions that Google can understand. A contact form plugin, such as WPForms or Contact Form 7, gives visitors a way to reach you. And a caching plugin, such as WP Super Cache, helps your site load faster. That is enough for most small business websites. Add other plugins only when you have a specific need — an online shop plugin such as WooCommerce if you want to sell directly from your site, or a social sharing plugin if you want visitors to share your pages.

## Launch, Share and Keep Your Website Alive

Before you tell anyone about your site, go through a final checklist. Visit every page on your phone and on a computer, and check that the text is correct, the photos load properly, the links work, and the contact form sends messages to you. Check that your site address shows a padlock in the browser, which means the security certificate is working. Ask a friend or family member to visit the site and tell you honestly what they think — a fresh pair of eyes will spot mistakes you have read past a dozen times, such as a wrong phone number or a confusing sentence. Fix everything you find, then launch.

Announce your website everywhere your customers already find you. Put the link in your WhatsApp bio and your WhatsApp Business profile, in your Instagram and Facebook bios, and in a post on each platform announcing that your website is live. Add it to your email signature, your business cards and your invoices. When customers ask for your price list or your product photos, send them to the website instead of sending the same photos for the hundredth time. Over time, your website becomes the place where all your marketing points, and where customers can find everything they need without waiting for your reply.

A website is not finished on launch day; it needs regular care. Add new products when they arrive, update prices when they change, and post a short article or update every few weeks so the site stays fresh and Google keeps noticing it. Check your site at least once a month: are the links still working, is the contact form still sending, is anything out of date? Keep your backups running and your software updated, and keep a note of your logins somewhere safe. A website that is looked after grows in value year after year, bringing you customers you never met and orders you never had to chase — and that, for a small business, is one of the best investments you can make.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does it cost to create a WordPress website in Nigeria?**

The WordPress software itself is free. Your costs are the domain name and the hosting, which you pay for yearly or monthly. Prices vary between providers and change over time, so compare a few hosting companies before you buy. For a small business website, the cheapest hosting plan is usually enough to start.

**Do I need to know how to code to use WordPress?**

No. WordPress is designed so that you can build and manage a website without writing any code. You choose a theme, add your pages and photos through the dashboard, and use plugins for extra features. Knowing a little about how websites work helps, but it is not required to build a good site.

**How long does it take to build a WordPress website?**

A simple business website can be built in a weekend if you have your content and photos ready. Give yourself a few days: one day for the domain, hosting and installation, one day for the theme and pages, and one day for checking everything and launching. Take your time with the content — it matters more than speed.

**Is WordPress safe for a business website?**

Yes, when it is looked after. Keep WordPress, your theme and your plugins updated, use strong passwords, install a reputable security plugin, and keep regular backups. Most WordPress security problems come from outdated software and weak passwords, both of which are easy to avoid.

**Can I sell products directly from my WordPress site?**

Yes. The free WooCommerce plugin turns a WordPress site into an online shop, with product pages, a cart and payment options. You can also start simpler, with a products page and a WhatsApp or payment link for orders, and add a full shop later when you are ready.
