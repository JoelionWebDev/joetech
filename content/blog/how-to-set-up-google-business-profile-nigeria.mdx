When someone in Lekki searches Google for "plumber near me", "best cake vendor in Lagos", or "pharmacy open now", Google does not just show websites — it shows a map with local businesses, their ratings, their hours and their phone numbers. The businesses on that map did not pay for the spot. They have a Google Business Profile, and it is completely free.

A Google Business Profile (formerly called Google My Business) is the free listing that puts your business on Google Search and Google Maps. For a small business in Nigeria, it is one of the most powerful free marketing tools available. Customers can find you, call you, message you, read your reviews and get directions to your shop — all without ever visiting your website. This guide walks you through setting it up properly, step by step.

## Create Your Profile the Right Way

Start by going to business.google.com and signing in with a Google account. Use the Google account tied to your business if you have one — the same one you use for your business email or YouTube channel. If you only have a personal Gmail, you can use it, but creating a dedicated business Google account keeps things tidy as you grow.

Click "Manage now" and type your exact business name — the name customers know you by, spelled exactly as it appears on your shop sign, your flyer and your social media pages. Consistency matters: if Google sees "Joetech" in one place and "JoeTech Digital" in another, it gets confused about which business is which.

Next, choose your business category. This is one of the most important choices you will make, because it decides which searches your business appears in. Pick the category that describes what you actually do — "Website Designer", "Restaurant", "Hair Salon", "Pharmacy", "Fashion Designer". Be specific rather than vague. A "Marketing Agency" will show up for marketing searches; a generic "Business Service" will show up for almost nothing. You can add extra categories later, but your primary category should be the service that brings you the most money.

Google will then ask whether you have a physical location customers can visit. If you have a shop, an office or a salon, choose "Yes" and enter the address carefully, exactly as it would appear on a delivery or a signboard. If you work from home or deliver to customers without a walk-in shop, you can choose to hide your address and set a service area instead — for example, Lekki, Victoria Island and Ikoyi. That way customers see the areas you serve without seeing your home address.

## Verify Your Business

Before your profile goes fully live, Google needs to check that your business is real. This is called verification, and it is the step where many Nigerian business owners get stuck. The most common method is verification by postcard: Google sends a physical postcard with a code to your business address, and you enter the code into your profile. The postcard usually takes one to two weeks to arrive, so be patient and do not request a new one too soon.

Other verification methods sometimes appear, depending on your business type and location — including verification by phone call, text message, email or video recording. If Google offers you one of these, take it, because it is much faster than waiting for a postcard. Follow the instructions exactly, and make sure the person verifying can show the business name, the shopfront or the workspace on camera if a video is requested.

While you wait for verification, you can still fill in the rest of your profile. Do not edit your business name, address or category during verification, because changes can restart the process. Once verified, your business can appear on Google Search and Maps, and you can reply to reviews and post updates.

## Complete Every Section of Your Profile

A half-finished profile ranks badly and looks untrustworthy. Treat every field as a chance to win a customer.

Write your business description in plain language: what you do, who you serve, and what makes you different. You have up to 750 characters, so use them well. A tailor might write: "Custom-made Ankara and corporate outfits for men and women in Lekki. We sew and deliver within 5 working days, with fitting available at our shop on Admiralty Way." That tells a customer everything they need in one paragraph.

Add your opening hours and keep them accurate. If you close on Sundays, say so — a customer who drives to a closed shop will not come back. Add special hours for public holidays when Google reminds you to. Add your phone number — the number customers should call, ideally the same WhatsApp business number you use everywhere else — and your website if you have one.

Upload good photos. Profiles with photos get far more attention than profiles without them. Add your logo, a photo of your shopfront or workspace, photos of your products or your team at work, and photos of finished jobs. Use clear, well-lit photos taken on a decent phone; you do not need a professional photographer. Update your photos every few months so your profile looks alive, not abandoned.

Add your services or products with prices where you can. A salon can list haircuts, braiding and wig installation with starting prices. A restaurant can list its menu items. This helps customers decide before they call, and it helps Google match your profile to more searches.

## Get Reviews and Respond to Them

Reviews are the heart of a Google Business Profile. When two similar businesses appear on the map, customers almost always pick the one with more reviews and a higher rating. Getting reviews takes effort, but it is the highest-return work you will do on your profile.

Ask happy customers directly. After a successful job or delivery, send a short message: "Thank you for your patronage! If you have a minute, please leave us a review on Google — it helps our small business a lot." Include your review link, which you can find in your profile dashboard under "Get more reviews". Ask in person too — a simple request at the counter works better than any automated message.

Never pay for reviews or write fake ones. Google removes fake reviews, and getting caught damages your profile. A few honest reviews are worth more than dozens of suspicious ones.

Reply to every review, good or bad. Thank happy customers by name and mention something specific: "Thank you, Mrs Ade, we are glad you loved the cake!" For a bad review, stay calm and professional: apologise, explain briefly, and invite the customer to contact you directly to fix it. Future customers read your replies as closely as the reviews themselves — a polite, helpful response to a complaint builds more trust than a perfect five-star rating.

## Keep Your Profile Active With Posts and Updates

A Google Business Profile is not a "set it and forget it" listing. Google rewards profiles that stay active. Use the Posts feature to share news, offers and new products, just like a social media update. Announce a new service, a holiday discount, a restock, or a behind-the-scenes photo. Posts expire after a while, so add a fresh one every week or two.

Keep your information updated. If you change your phone number, move to a new address, change your hours or add a new service, update the profile the same day. Outdated information is worse than no information — it sends customers to the wrong place or the wrong number.

Check your profile insights regularly. Google shows you how many people found your profile, what they searched for, whether they called you, asked for directions or visited your website. This data tells you what customers actually want. If many people search for a service you offer but never call, your photos, reviews or prices may need work. If most calls come from Google Maps, your location details matter more than your website link.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Google Business Profile really free?

Yes. Creating and managing a Google Business Profile is completely free. Google may offer paid advertising separately, but the profile itself — the listing on Search and Maps, reviews, posts and insights — costs nothing. Anyone asking you to pay for a basic profile is not acting on Google's behalf.

### Can I set up a profile if I do not have a physical shop?

Yes. If you work from home, run a mobile service, or deliver to customers, you can set up a service-area business. You hide your home address and tell Google the areas you serve, such as Lekki, Ajah and Victoria Island. Customers can still call you, message you and leave reviews.

### How long does verification take in Nigeria?

It depends on the method. Phone, email or video verification can be instant or take a day or two. Verification by postcard — the most common method — usually takes one to two weeks for the postcard to arrive at your address. Make sure your address is complete and accurate, including any landmarks the postman would need.

### What should I do if my business is already listed but I did not create it?

This happens when Google creates a listing automatically from public information. Search for your business name on Google Maps, click "Claim this business" or "Own this business?", and follow the verification steps. Once verified, the profile is yours to manage, and you can correct any wrong information on it.
