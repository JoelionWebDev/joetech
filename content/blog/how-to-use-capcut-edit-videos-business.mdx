Video is now the most powerful way for a small business to get attention online. A short video of your product, a quick explanation of your service, or a behind-the-scenes look at your work will usually reach far more people than a photo or a text post. Many business owners know this, but they hold back because video editing sounds expensive and complicated. It is not anymore. CapCut is a free video editing app made by the same company behind TikTok, and it runs on your phone and your computer. With it, you can cut clips, add captions, add music, and export a polished video in under an hour — even if you have never edited a video before. This guide shows you how, step by step.

## Why Video Works So Well for Small Businesses

Customers buy from businesses they feel they know. A video lets people see your face, hear your voice, and watch your product in action — things a photo cannot do. A tailor showing a finished dress on a real person, a food vendor showing a meal being prepared, or a phone repairer showing a cracked screen being fixed: these videos build trust quickly, because the customer can see the work with their own eyes.

Video also travels further on social media. Facebook, Instagram, TikTok and WhatsApp Status all push video to more people than other types of posts, and short videos are the easiest content for people to share. A single useful video — for example, three mistakes people make when buying a product you sell — can be shared from one person to another for days. You do not need a large advertising budget to reach new customers when your videos are genuinely useful or interesting.

The best part is that the barrier has fallen. A few years ago, making a good video needed a camera, a computer and paid software. Today your phone camera is good enough, natural light is good enough, and CapCut is free. What matters is not expensive equipment — it is a clear message, good lighting, and clean editing.

## Getting Started With CapCut

CapCut is free to download from the Google Play Store and the Apple App Store, and there is also a free desktop version for Windows and Mac. The mobile app is the easiest place to start, because you can film on your phone and edit on the same device. Install the app and create an account, or sign in with a Google, Facebook or TikTok account if you prefer.

When you open CapCut, you will see a home screen with a "New project" button. Tap it, and your phone's gallery will open. Select the video clips and photos you want to use — you can add more later — and tap "Add". CapCut will place your clips on a timeline at the bottom of the screen. The timeline is where all the editing happens: the clips sit in a row, and you drag, cut and arrange them there. Above the timeline is the preview window, which shows your video as you edit it. The toolbar below the timeline holds the editing tools: split, speed, volume, text, effects, filters and more.

Before you start editing, take a moment to check your project settings. CapCut lets you choose the aspect ratio — the shape of your video. For TikTok, Instagram Reels, Facebook Reels and WhatsApp Status, use the vertical 9:16 format, which fills a phone screen. For YouTube or Facebook feed videos, the horizontal 16:9 format works better. Choose the format that matches where you will post the video before you start editing, because changing it later means rearranging your clips.

## Your First Edit: Cutting and Arranging Clips

Editing starts with cutting out the parts you do not need. Watch your clips in the preview window and find the moments that matter. Tap a clip on the timeline to select it, then drag the white handles at either end to trim the start or the end. To remove a section from the middle — a pause, a mistake, or a long silence — move the playhead (the white line on the timeline) to the exact point where you want to cut, tap the clip, and choose "Split". This divides the clip into two pieces. Tap the unwanted piece and delete it, and the remaining pieces will join together.

Do not be afraid to cut aggressively. A video that is 30 seconds long and keeps the viewer's attention is far more effective than a two-minute video that rambles. Watch your edit from the start after every few cuts and ask yourself: does this part add anything? If not, cut it. Short, tight videos perform better on every platform.

You can also change the order of your clips by pressing and dragging a clip to a new position on the timeline. A good structure for a business video is simple: start with a hook in the first three seconds — a bold statement, a question, or your best shot — then show the product or the process, then end with a clear call to action, such as your phone number, your location, or "send us a message to order". Viewers decide in the first few seconds whether to keep watching, so put your strongest moment first, not last.

CapCut also lets you change the speed of a clip. Select a clip, tap "Speed", and choose "Normal" to speed up or slow down the whole clip, or "Curve" for speed changes within a clip. Speeding up a long process — like packing an order or setting up a display — makes for an engaging time-lapse effect, while slowing down a key moment can add emphasis. Use speed changes sparingly: one or two per video is enough.

## Adding Text and Captions

Most people watch social media videos without sound, at least at first. That is why captions matter so much. CapCut can generate captions automatically from your voice: select your video on the timeline, tap "Text", then "Auto captions", choose the language, and CapCut will listen to the audio and add the words as text on screen. Check the captions afterwards, because automatic captions sometimes mishear words — especially names and Nigerian place names — and correct any mistakes by tapping on the caption text.

You can also add your own text: headings, prices, your business name, or a call to action. Tap "Text", then "Add text", type your words, and choose a font, colour and size. Keep text big and bold — small text is unreadable on a phone screen. Use high-contrast colours, like white text with a dark outline, so it stays readable over any background. Place text where it does not cover the important part of your video, usually the top or bottom third of the screen.

A useful habit is to put your key message in text as well as in voice. If your video says "we deliver anywhere in Lagos", put those words on screen too. That way, viewers who watch without sound still get the message, and viewers who watch with sound get it reinforced.

## Adding Music and Sound

Music sets the mood of your video. CapCut has a built-in library of free music and sound effects: tap "Audio", then "Sounds", and browse by mood or genre. Choose something that fits your business — upbeat music for a fashion or food video, calm music for a professional service. Keep the volume low enough that your voice is still clear: select the music track on the timeline, tap "Volume", and lower it to around 20 to 30 percent if you are talking over it.

You can also record a voiceover directly in CapCut. Tap "Audio", then "Record", and speak while the video plays. This is useful for explaining what is happening on screen, or for adding a personal message to a video of your products. Record in a quiet room, hold the phone a short distance from your mouth, and speak clearly. If you make a mistake, you can re-record just that section.

Be careful with copyrighted music. Music you download from elsewhere and add to a video can get your video muted or removed on some platforms. CapCut's built-in library is designed for use in videos, which is one reason it is safer than adding songs from other sources. If you use music from outside CapCut, make sure you have the right to use it.

## Effects, Transitions, Filters and Stickers

Once your clips are cut and your captions are in place, you can polish the video with effects. Transitions sit between two clips and control how one flows into the next. Tap the small icon between two clips on the timeline to add one — a simple fade or a quick slide works well. Avoid flashy transitions: they distract from your product and can make a business video look unprofessional. One simple transition style, used consistently, looks far better than a different effect between every clip.

Filters change the colour and mood of your video. Tap "Filters" and try a few — a subtle warm or bright filter can make a product video look more appealing. Do not overdo it: a filter should make your video look cleaner, not change the colour of your product so much that customers are surprised when it arrives. For product videos, accuracy matters more than style.

CapCut also offers effects, stickers and animations, and it is tempting to use them all. For a business video, restraint is better. A simple animated text entrance, a subtle zoom on a product shot, or a clean sticker with your business name is enough. The product should be the star of the video, not the effects.

## Exporting Your Video the Right Way

When your video is ready, tap the export button — usually an arrow at the top right of the screen. CapCut will ask you to choose the resolution and frame rate. For most social media posts, 1080p resolution at 30 frames per second is a good choice: it looks sharp and the file size stays manageable. Higher settings like 4K make larger files that take longer to upload, which matters when you are uploading on mobile data.

Before you export, watch the whole video from start to finish, with sound on, and check for mistakes: captions with errors, a clip that is too long, music that drowns out your voice, or a sudden jump where a cut went wrong. It is much easier to fix these in CapCut than to re-upload a video after posting. When you are happy, export and save the video to your phone. CapCut also lets you share directly to TikTok, Instagram or Facebook, but saving it first gives you a copy you can post anywhere.

One video can work on several platforms. A vertical video made for TikTok can also go on Instagram Reels, Facebook Reels and WhatsApp Status. That means one editing session can give you content for the whole week, if you plan it well.

## Practical Tips for Business Videos

Film in good light. Natural daylight near a window is the best free lighting there is — avoid filming in a dark room with a single bulb, which makes videos look grainy. Film during the day when you can, and keep the light in front of you, not behind you, so your face and your product are lit rather than in shadow.

Keep your phone steady. Rest it on a table, a stack of books, or a small tripod if you have one. Shaky video looks unprofessional and is tiring to watch. If you are filming yourself talking, look at the camera, not at your own image on the screen, and speak as if you are talking to one customer standing in front of you.

Plan before you film. Write down the three or four points you want to make, and film each one as a separate short clip. Editing is far easier when you have short, focused clips than when you have one long recording that rambles. A simple plan — hook, product, price or benefit, call to action — covers most business videos.

Stay consistent. Post videos regularly, even if it is only two or three times a week, and use a similar style each time: the same colours, the same kind of captions, the same closing message. Over time, people begin to recognise your videos before they even see your name, and that recognition builds trust — and trust brings orders.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is CapCut free?

Yes, CapCut is free to download and use on phones and computers. It has a paid Pro version with extra effects and templates, but the free version includes everything you need to edit good business videos: cutting, captions, music, filters and exporting in high quality.

### Can I use CapCut on my computer as well as my phone?

Yes. CapCut has a free desktop app for Windows and Mac, and a web version that runs in your browser. Your projects do not automatically move between your phone and your computer, so it is best to start and finish a video on one device.

### What video size should I use for TikTok and Instagram Reels?

Use the vertical 9:16 format, which fills a phone screen. In CapCut, set the aspect ratio to 9:16 before you start editing. For YouTube or Facebook feed videos, use the horizontal 16:9 format instead.

### How do I add captions to my video in CapCut?

Select your video clip on the timeline, tap "Text", then "Auto captions", choose the language, and CapCut will generate captions from your voice automatically. Always read through them afterwards and correct any words it misheard, especially names.

### Can CapCut remove the background from a video?

Yes. CapCut has a background removal tool that can cut out the background behind a person in a video, similar to a green screen effect, without needing a physical green screen. It works best when the person is clearly separated from the background and the lighting is good.

### Will my video have a CapCut watermark?

Videos exported from CapCut do not carry a watermark by default, as long as you do not use the ending template that says "CapCut" at the end. If your video has that ending clip, you can delete it from the timeline before exporting.

### What is the best length for a business video?

For TikTok, Instagram Reels and Facebook Reels, 15 to 60 seconds usually works best. That is long enough to show a product or make a point, and short enough to hold attention. Longer videos work on YouTube or Facebook feeds, where people expect more detail.

### Do I need a good camera to make business videos?

No. A modern smartphone camera is good enough for social media videos. Good lighting, a steady phone and clear sound matter far more than an expensive camera. Film in daylight, keep the phone still, and record in a quiet place.
