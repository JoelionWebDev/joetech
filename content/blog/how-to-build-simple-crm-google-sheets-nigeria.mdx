Most Nigerian small businesses manage customers from memory and WhatsApp chats. You remember Mrs Adeyemi likes the large size. You scroll through three weeks of chats to find what Mr Okonkwo ordered. You forget to follow up with the woman who asked about prices on Tuesday. And every forgotten follow-up is money left on the table.

A CRM — customer relationship management system — sounds like expensive software for big companies. It is not. At its simplest, a CRM is just an organised list of your customers, what they bought, what they owe, and when to talk to them next. You can build that in Google Sheets, for free, on the phone you already own.

This guide walks you through building a simple CRM step by step. No coding, no subscriptions, no laptop required. By the end, you will have a system that remembers every customer so you do not have to.

## Why Google Sheets Works for a Nigerian Small Business

Before we build, here is why Google Sheets is the right tool for most Nigerian SMEs right now:

- **It is free.** A Google account costs nothing. Paid CRM tools charge ₦15,000 to ₦60,000 or more per month — money a small business can spend on stock instead.
- **It works on your phone.** The Google Sheets app runs on Android and iPhone, and works with MTN, Airtel, Glo or 9mobile data. You can update a customer record while sitting in traffic on the Lekki-Epe Expressway.
- **It works offline.** Enter data without network and it syncs when your connection returns. This matters when NEPA takes light, your router dies, and you are running on phone data.
- **It is easy to share.** Give your staff or assistant access with one link, and everyone sees the same up-to-date information. No more "I wrote it in my own notebook."
- **It grows with you.** Start with twenty customers. When you reach two thousand, the same sheet still works — and by then you will know exactly what you need from a paid tool, if you ever need one.

The honest limit: Google Sheets is not a full sales platform. It will not send automatic emails or run complex marketing campaigns. For a business doing tens or hundreds of orders a month, it is more than enough. If you later outgrow it, your data exports cleanly into almost any paid CRM.

## What Your CRM Needs to Track

A CRM that tracks everything tracks nothing well. Keep it focused on five things:

1. **Who the customer is** — name, phone number, location, and how they found you.
2. **What they bought** — product, quantity, amount, and date.
3. **What they owe or what you owe them** — payments, balances, and delivery status.
4. **When to follow up** — the next action and its date.
5. **What they like** — preferences, sizes, and notes that make them feel remembered.

Everything else is decoration. If a column does not help you sell more, serve better or get paid faster, leave it out.

## Step 1: Set Up Your Google Sheets CRM

Open sheets.google.com on your phone or computer and create a new blank spreadsheet. Name it something clear, like "Joetech Customers CRM" — replace Joetech with your business name.

You will build four tabs. Tabs sit at the bottom of the sheet. Rename the first tab "Customers" and add three more: "Orders", "Follow-ups" and "Payments". Each tab has one job, and together they give you the full picture.

**Customers tab — one row per customer.** Set up these column headings in row 1:

- A: Customer ID (C001, C002, C003...)
- B: Full name
- C: Phone number (WhatsApp)
- D: Location (Lekki, Ikeja, Abuja, Port Harcourt...)
- E: How they found you (Instagram, Referral, Walk-in, Facebook, Google)
- F: First order date
- G: Total spent (₦)
- H: Last order date
- I: Notes (preferences, sizes, birthday, anything useful)

Freeze row 1 so the headings stay visible while you scroll: select row 1, tap View, then Freeze, then 1 row. On the phone app, long-press the row number and choose Freeze.

**Orders tab — one row per order.** Column headings:

- A: Order ID (ORD-001, ORD-002...)
- B: Date
- C: Customer name
- D: Customer phone
- E: Product or service
- F: Quantity
- G: Unit price (₦)
- H: Total amount (₦)
- I: Amount paid (₦)
- J: Balance (₦)
- K: Delivery status (Pending, Delivered, Cancelled)
- L: Notes

**Follow-ups tab — one row per action.** Column headings:

- A: Date to follow up
- B: Customer name
- C: Phone
- D: Reason (New enquiry, After delivery, Payment reminder, Restock alert, Birthday)
- E: What to say (a short note to yourself)
- F: Status (Pending, Done)

**Payments tab — one row per payment received.** Column headings:

- A: Date
- B: Customer name
- C: Order ID
- D: Amount received (₦)
- E: Payment method (Transfer, Cash, POS, Paystack, Flutterwave, Moniepoint)
- F: Reference or notes

That is the whole structure. Four tabs, about thirty columns total. It takes twenty minutes to set up and will save you hours every week.

## Step 2: Enter Your Existing Customers

Do not try to enter five years of history. Start with your active customers — people who bought in the last three months, people with outstanding balances, and people who enquired recently but have not bought yet.

For each one, fill in what you know and leave the rest blank. A CRM with twenty complete records beats a CRM with two hundred half-finished rows you never trust.

Where do you find the details? Your WhatsApp chats, your bank statements, your delivery waybills and your Instagram DMs. Set aside one quiet evening, put on music, and work through your recent chats customer by customer. Most sellers finish this in two to three hours.

Give every customer an ID number. It sounds formal, but it saves confusion when you have three customers named Blessing or two Mrs Adeyemis. Use the phone number as the backup identifier — in Nigeria, the phone number is the most reliable way to identify a customer.

## Step 3: Add Simple Formulas That Do the Maths

Formulas turn your sheet from a notebook into a system. You only need four, and you can copy them down the column once and forget them.

**Order total.** In the Orders tab, column H (Total amount), enter `=F2*G2` — quantity times unit price. Copy it down the column.

**Balance owed.** In column J (Balance), enter `=H2-I2` — total minus amount paid. If the balance is zero, the customer has paid in full. If it is above zero, they still owe you. Use conditional formatting to turn positive balances red so unpaid orders jump out at you.

**Total spent per customer.** In the Customers tab, column G, use `=SUMIF(Orders!C:C, B2, Orders!H:H)`. This adds up every order total for that customer automatically. It tells you at a glance who your best customers are.

**Total business revenue.** At the bottom of the Orders tab, or on a small summary area at the top, use `=SUM(H:H)` for total sales and `=SUM(J:J)` for total outstanding balances. Two numbers, updated automatically, that tell you how the business is doing.

If formulas feel intimidating, start with just the first two and add the rest later. Even a CRM without formulas is far better than memory and scattered chats.

## Step 4: Build a Daily Follow-Up Habit

A CRM only works if you open it. Build a five-minute daily habit around the Follow-ups tab:

1. **Every morning**, open the Follow-ups tab and filter for today's date. Those are your calls and messages for the day.
2. **Work through the list** — send the messages, make the calls, and mark each row Done with today's date.
3. **Every evening**, add new follow-ups: everyone who enquired today, everyone whose order was delivered today (follow up in three days), and everyone who owes a balance (follow up on the agreed date).
4. **Every Friday**, review the week: how many follow-ups were completed, how many turned into orders, and which customers have gone quiet for 30 days.

The follow-up reasons that matter most for Nigerian businesses:

- **New enquiry follow-up (1 day later):** "Good morning, Mrs Eze. You asked about the Ankara gown yesterday — the price is ₦35,000 and we have your size in stock. Shall I reserve one for you?" Most enquiries die because nobody follows up. This single habit can lift your sales by a visible margin.
- **After-delivery check (3 days later):** "Hope the package arrived well. How is the product serving you?" This catches problems early and often earns a testimonial or a referral.
- **Payment reminders (on the agreed date):** Polite, short, and factual. "Good afternoon sir, a gentle reminder that the balance of ₦15,000 for order ORD-042 is due today. Thank you." Never rude, never in a group chat.
- **Restock alerts:** When a regular customer's favourite product returns, tell them first. They feel valued, and you sell faster.

## Step 5: Use Filters and Sorting to Find Answers Fast

Google Sheets has built-in filters that answer everyday business questions in seconds. Select your header row, tap Data, then Create a filter. Now you can:

- **See who owes you money:** filter the Orders tab where Balance is greater than zero. That list is your cash collection plan.
- **Find your best customers:** sort the Customers tab by Total spent, largest first. Your top ten customers deserve your best attention.
- **See what sells:** sort the Orders tab by product and count. The products at the top deserve more stock and more marketing.
- **Find customers by location:** filter by Lekki, Abuja or Port Harcourt when you plan a delivery run or a location-based promo.
- **Track where customers come from:** filter by "How they found you" and count. If Instagram brings most customers, spend more effort there. If referrals bring the best buyers, invest in your referral reward.

These answers used to require guesswork. Now they take ten seconds.

## Step 6: Protect Your Data

Your customer list is one of the most valuable things your business owns. Protect it.

- **Back it up.** Google Sheets saves automatically to your Google account, but keep a monthly copy: File, then Make a copy, and name it with the month. If something is deleted by mistake, you have a fallback.
- **Control who can edit.** Share the sheet with staff as Editors only if they need to change data. Assistants who only need to view it should be Viewers. Check the sharing settings monthly.
- **Do not share the link publicly.** A sheet link posted in a WhatsApp group can expose your customers' phone numbers. Share only with named people.
- **Use two-step verification on your Google account.** If someone gets into your Gmail, they get your CRM, your business email and your YouTube channel. Turn on 2-step verification in your Google account settings today if you have not already.
- **Respect customer privacy.** Customer phone numbers and addresses are not for sharing, selling or adding to random broadcast lists without consent. Treat their data the way you would want yours treated.

## Real Example: A Hair Vendor in Surulere

Consider Adaeze, who sells hair in Surulere, Lagos. Before her CRM, she kept customer details in her head and in scattered WhatsApp chats. She forgot to follow up on enquiries, lost track of who owed balances, and reordered the wrong lengths because she could not remember what sold.

After building her four-tab sheet, her routine changed. Every morning she checks the Follow-ups tab. She messages three customers whose hair arrived three days ago. She reminds two customers of balances due. She sees that her top customer, Mrs Bankole in Lekki, has spent ₦180,000 in four months — so when a new luxury bundle arrives, Mrs Bankole hears about it first.

Within two months, Adaeze noticed three things: she recovered ₦85,000 in forgotten balances, her repeat orders rose because follow-ups were no longer forgotten, and she stopped over-ordering slow products because the Orders tab showed clearly what actually sold. The sheet did not sell the hair for her. It stopped her from losing sales she had already earned.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Making it too complicated.** Twenty columns you never fill are worse than eight columns you use daily. Start simple and add columns only when you feel the lack of them.
- **Entering data in batches weeks late.** A CRM updated monthly is a history book, not a tool. Enter orders the same day they happen. It takes two minutes per order.
- **Two versions of the truth.** If some orders live in the sheet and others live only in your head, you will trust neither. Commit to the sheet as the single record.
- **Never cleaning it up.** Once a month, remove duplicate customer rows, fix wrong phone numbers and archive customers who have not bought in a year. A clean CRM is a trusted CRM.
- **Sharing passwords instead of sharing the sheet.** Never give a staff member your Google password. Share the sheet with their own Google account and remove their access when they leave.

## When to Upgrade From Google Sheets

Google Sheets serves most small businesses well for a long time. Consider a paid CRM when:

- You have more than 2,000 active customers and the sheet feels slow.
- You need automatic emails or WhatsApp messages sent without you doing it manually.
- Three or more staff need to update customer records at the same time, all day.
- You want detailed sales reports, pipelines and team targets built in.

When that day comes, tools like HubSpot's free plan, Zoho CRM or Freshsales are reasonable next steps — and because your data is already organised in Sheets, moving it over takes an afternoon instead of a month. But do not rush to pay for software to solve a discipline problem. If you do not update a free sheet, you will not update a paid CRM either.

## A 7-Day Plan to Get Your CRM Running

- **Day 1:** Create the sheet and the four tabs with the column headings above.
- **Day 2:** Enter your twenty most active customers in the Customers tab.
- **Day 3:** Enter the last month's orders in the Orders tab, with balances and delivery status.
- **Day 4:** Add the four formulas and test that totals calculate correctly.
- **Day 5:** Fill the Follow-ups tab with everyone who enquired this week and every delivery from the last three days.
- **Day 6:** Use the sheet for a full day of real work — every order and every follow-up goes in the sheet.
- **Day 7:** Review the week. Check your total outstanding balances, your top five customers and your follow-up completion rate. Fix whatever felt awkward.

After seven days, the habit is formed. After thirty days, you will wonder how you ran the business without it.
