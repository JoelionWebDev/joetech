A customer sends you a message: "I paid for the item on your page, but nobody is replying." You check, and there is no order. Then the customer sends a screenshot, and your heart sinks. The page in the screenshot uses your business name, your logo, and your photos, but it is not your page. Someone copied your business, collected money from your customer, and disappeared.

Fake pages and impersonators are a real problem for Nigerian businesses, especially small ones that are growing fast on Instagram, Facebook, and WhatsApp. The people behind them copy your identity, offer your products at lower prices, and collect payments from customers who think they are dealing with you. The money is lost, the customer is angry, and your reputation takes the damage. This guide explains how to spot these pages, report them, warn your customers, and make your real business harder to copy.

## Know how impersonators work

Impersonators rarely build anything original. They copy your profile picture, your business name with a small change, your photos, and even your captions. The changes are often tiny: an extra letter, an underscore, a number, or a word like "official" or "real" added to the name. A customer scrolling quickly will not notice the difference.

They usually attract customers in two ways. First, they run adverts or post in groups offering your products at prices far below yours. Second, they reply to people who comment on your real posts, telling them to send a message to the fake page for a discount. Some even copy your giveaway posts and tell people they have won, then ask for a fee to claim the prize.

Understanding this matters because it tells you what to look for. The fake page will often have few posts, most of them stolen from you, and the account will be new. It will push customers to pay quickly, often by transfer to a personal account, and it will avoid phone calls or video calls. Real businesses welcome questions and checks. Fake pages rush the payment.

## Search for copies of your business regularly

You cannot report a fake page you do not know exists, so searching for copies should be a regular habit. Once a week, search your business name on Instagram, Facebook, and TikTok, and check the results. Look for accounts with similar names, the same profile picture, or your photos. Search for your phone number and your unique product photos too, because impersonators sometimes keep your name but use a different spelling.

Set up a simple routine for this. Pick one day a week, spend ten minutes searching, and keep a note of anything suspicious. Ask your staff to do the same, and ask loyal customers to tell you if they see a suspicious page. Customers are often the first to spot a fake, because they are the ones being targeted. Make it easy for them to report it to you, by telling them where to send a screenshot.

Also protect your own accounts with two-factor authentication and strong passwords that are not shared with staff. For more on keeping your business safe online, read our guide on [cybersecurity for small businesses in Nigeria](/blog/cybersecurity-small-business-nigeria).

## Report fake pages the right way

When you find a fake page, report it through the platform's own tools. On Instagram and Facebook, go to the fake profile, open the menu, and choose the option to report it as pretending to be someone else, then select your business. On TikTok and WhatsApp, the steps are similar. Give the platform the link to your real page, so it can compare the two.

Reporting works, but it takes time, and it often takes more than one report. Ask your staff, your family, and your loyal customers to report the same page. Platforms act faster when several people report the same account for the same reason.

## Warn your customers clearly and often

Your customers are your best defence, but only if they know what to look for. Make a clear post on your real pages explaining how to identify your genuine account. State your exact username, your real phone number, and your real payment details. Tell customers that you never ask for payment through a different account, and that you never ask anyone to pay a fee to claim a prize.

Pin that post to the top of your profile, so every new visitor sees it first. Repeat the warning regularly, especially during sales and giveaways, when impersonators are most active. Add the warning to your website too, on your contact page or in your footer, so customers who find you through Google see it as well.

Use the tools that make your real page easy to recognise. A consistent profile picture, a clear bio with your website link, and regular posts all help customers tell the real page from a copy. If the platform offers verification for businesses, apply for it. A verified badge is not available to every small business, but where it is possible, it is the clearest signal a customer can see.

## Make your business harder to copy

Some simple habits make impersonation harder. Watermark your product photos with your business name or website address, placed where it cannot be cropped out easily. When you post a price, include your contact details in the same image, so a stolen photo still carries your real information. These steps do not stop a determined copier, but they make the copied version less useful to them.

Keep your contact details consistent everywhere. One phone number for orders, one bank account for payments, and one website address. When your details never change, customers learn them, and a fake page asking for payment to a different account stands out immediately.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What should I do first when I find a fake page using my business name?**

Take screenshots of the fake profile and its posts, then report it through the platform as pretending to be your business. After that, warn your customers on your real pages with a clear post showing your genuine username and contact details. Do not message or argue with the impersonator.

**Can I get a fake page removed quickly?**

It depends on the platform. Some fake pages are removed within days, while others take longer or come back under a new name. Multiple reports from different people help, and keeping screenshots lets you report again quickly if the page reappears. There is no guaranteed timeline, which is why warning your customers matters as much as reporting.

**How can my customers tell my real page from a fake one?**

Tell them exactly what to check: your exact username, your real phone number, your website address, and your real payment details. Warn them that you never ask for payment to a different account, and never charge a fee for a prize. Pinning a warning post to the top of your profile makes this check easy for every visitor.

**Should I report fake pages to the police?**

If a fake page has collected money from your customers, you can report the matter to the police or to the relevant authorities, with your screenshots and any evidence from affected customers. Keep copies of everything. A police report may help in serious cases, but for most small businesses, reporting to the platform and warning customers is the fastest first step.

## Protect the name you built

You worked hard to build your business name, and a fake page can damage it in a day. Search for copies every week, secure your real accounts, report fakes through the platform, and warn your customers clearly and often. Make your real business easy to recognise, with consistent details and a watermark on your photos. Your customers want to buy from the real you. Give them every way to be sure they have found you.
