WhatsApp is the busiest marketplace in Nigeria. It is where customers in Lagos, Abuja, Enugu and Ibadan check on friends, catch up on family news, and, increasingly, decide what to buy. Your status sits at the top of that daily habit. Unlike Instagram or Facebook, where an algorithm decides who sees your post, WhatsApp status goes directly to the people who saved your number. They chose to see it. That is a powerful advantage, and most Nigerian sellers waste it.

Many sellers treat status like a notice board: they post ten blurry product photos in a row, with no prices, no clear offer, and no reason to reply. Viewers swipe past, and the seller concludes that WhatsApp does not sell. The truth is different. WhatsApp status sells very well when it is run like a shop, with a plan, a schedule, prices, and follow-up. This guide shows you how to run WhatsApp status sales that actually bring orders, step by step, using tools and habits that work on Nigerian data budgets and Nigerian buying behaviour.

## Why WhatsApp Status Works for Nigerian Sellers

First, the audience is already warm. The people who view your status are contacts, past customers, friends of friends, and people who saved your number after an enquiry. They already know you exist. You do not need to pay for adverts to reach them, and you do not need to fight an algorithm. If your status is good, they will see it.

Second, buying on WhatsApp feels safe to many Nigerians. The customer can ask questions in a private chat, negotiate, see your profile picture and status updates, and judge whether you look like a real person with a real business. That matters in a market where online scams are common. A seller who posts regularly, answers quickly and shows real products builds trust over weeks, and that trust turns into repeat orders.

Third, WhatsApp is cheap to use. A customer on MTN or Airtel with a small data bundle can view your status without opening a heavy website or app. A short video or a clear photo loads quickly, even on a slow connection. WhatsApp status meets customers where they already are.

## Set Up WhatsApp Business Before You Start Selling

If you are serious about selling, switch from regular WhatsApp to WhatsApp Business. It is free, it works with the same number, and it gives you tools that regular WhatsApp does not have. Download WhatsApp Business from the Play Store or App Store, verify your number, and set up your business profile properly. Do not skip this step.

Your business profile is your shop front. Add your business name exactly as customers know it, choose the right category such as Clothing Store, Food and Drink, Beauty Salon or Electronics, and write a short description that says what you sell and where you deliver. For example: "Ready-to-wear gowns and two-pieces in Lekki. Nationwide delivery. Payment on delivery in Lagos." Add your address or area, your working hours, and your email if you have one. Add a clear profile photo, ideally your logo or a clean photo of your best product, not a personal selfie that confuses customers.

Next, set up a catalogue. The catalogue is the most underused feature in WhatsApp Business, and it changes how customers buy from you. Add each product with a clear photo, a name, a price in naira, and a short description. A customer who sees your status can tap through to your catalogue, see the full price list, and message you about a specific item. Without a catalogue, every enquiry starts with "How much is this one?", and you spend your day answering the same question instead of closing sales.

Create quick replies for the questions you answer every day. In WhatsApp Business, go to Business Tools, then Quick Replies, and save answers for "What is your delivery fee?", "Do you deliver to Abuja?", "What is your account number?" and "Is this still available?". Each quick reply gets a shortcut, such as /delivery or /account. When a customer asks, you type the shortcut and send a complete, correct answer in seconds. This matters when ten customers message you at once after a good status post.

Finally, organise your chats with labels. WhatsApp Business lets you label chats as New Customer, Order Placed, Payment Received, Delivered, or any label you create. After a busy day, you can open the "Payment Received" label and see exactly who has paid and what you still need to send. Without labels, paid orders get lost among casual chats, and customers who paid you start chasing you for goods you forgot to send.

## Build an Audience That Actually Buys

A status sale only works if the right people see it. Having 2,000 contacts who never buy is worse than having 300 contacts who buy regularly. Your first job is to grow an audience of buyers, not just viewers.

Start with your existing customers. Every time someone buys from you, ask politely if you can save their number and if they would like to see new stock on your status. Most will say yes. Save their number with their name and what they bought, for example "Ada — Ankara gown". That note helps you message them personally when similar stock arrives.

Grow beyond your phone contacts by sharing your WhatsApp link in places your customers already gather. Add it to your Instagram bio, your Facebook page, your TikTok bio, and your business flyers. When you post in a Facebook group or a Telegram group, include the link with a clear reason to join: "Chat me on WhatsApp to see today's new stock." Do not spam groups with your link. Share it where it is welcome, with a product people actually want.

Join and participate in groups where your customers spend time, but sell with sense. A food vendor who shares a useful cooking tip in a mums' group, then occasionally mentions that she takes weekend orders, will sell more than a vendor who drops ten adverts a day and gets removed by the admin. Give value first, sell second.

## Plan What to Post: The Daily Status Mix

Random posting does not sell. A planned mix does. Think of your status as a small daily TV channel with different segments, so viewers have a reason to watch every day instead of skipping your updates.

Start the day with a product post. This is your main selling slot, posted between 7am and 9am when people check their phones before work, school runs and the morning commute. Show one product clearly, with its price, sizes or options, and a call to action. One strong product per status slide is better than a collage of six products squeezed into one image. If you have five products, post five slides, each with one product.

Midday, post social proof. Share a screenshot of a happy customer, a photo of a parcel being packed, a delivery rider leaving with goods, or a short video of a customer wearing or using your product. Blur the customer's phone number and any private details before you post a screenshot. Social proof answers the question every Nigerian buyer asks: "Has anyone else bought from this person and received their goods?" A steady stream of proof posts builds trust faster than any advert.

In the afternoon, post behind the scenes. Show your shop, your cooking, your sewing machine, your stock arriving, or you preparing an order. Customers love seeing the real work behind a business. It proves you are not a middleman using stolen photos, and it makes your business feel human. A 10-second video of you packing an order with care will do more for trust than a polished flyer.

In the evening, between 6pm and 9pm, post your second selling slot. This is when people are home, relaxed, scrolling, and most likely to buy. Repeat your best product of the day, post a limited offer, or announce tomorrow's new stock. Evening is also the best time for a short video, because people have more time to watch it.

A practical daily plan is four to eight status slides per day, spread across the day, not posted all at once. Posting twenty slides at once overwhelms viewers, and most people will skip the whole batch. Space them out, and let each slide breathe.

## How to Price on Status So People Message You

Price is the biggest reason status viewers do not become buyers. Hide the price and you get many "How much?" messages from people who cannot afford it, and few orders. Show the price and you get fewer messages, but far more of them are from people ready to buy.

Always show the price on the status image itself, not only in the caption. Many viewers watch status on mute, quickly, without reading a long caption. Put the price in large, bold text on the image: "₦18,500". Add the delivery fee separately, so there is no surprise later: "Delivery in Lagos ₦2,000 – ₦3,500 depending on area. Nationwide delivery available." Customers in Abuja or Port Harcourt need to know you can reach them, and customers in Lekki need to know the fee before they commit.

Use simple, honest pricing tactics. Show a discount as a clear comparison: "Was ₦25,000, now ₦19,500 — this weekend only." Offer a bundle where it makes sense: "Buy 2 gowns, get a free scarf." State a minimum order for free delivery if you offer it: "Free delivery in Lagos on orders above ₦50,000." Each of these gives the customer a reason to buy now and to buy more.

Be careful with "DM for price". There are rare cases where it makes sense, such as custom work where the price genuinely depends on size, design or quantity. Even then, give a starting price: "Custom cakes from ₦35,000. DM for a quote." A starting price respects the customer's time and filters out enquiries you cannot serve.

State your payment terms clearly on your status at least once a week. Nigerians have learned to be careful about paying first. Tell customers whether you accept transfer, Paystack or Flutterwave payment links, or payment on delivery. If you accept payment on delivery in Lagos, say so prominently, because it removes the biggest fear for first-time buyers. If you require full payment before delivery, say so, and explain why: "Full payment confirms your order. We send your tracking number once payment is received." Honesty about payment terms prevents arguments later.


## How to Write Status Captions That Get Replies

A status image catches the eye, but the caption starts the conversation. Keep captions short, specific and written the way Nigerians actually talk. Long, formal captions get skipped. A good caption is two to four lines: what it is, how much it costs, and what to do next.

Use this simple formula for product captions: product plus price plus benefit plus action. "Ankara two-piece, ₦22,000. Soft cotton, sizes 8 to 16. Send a screenshot to order." "Smoky jollof, ₦4,500 per pack. Available today in Lekki. Order before 11am for lunch delivery." "Solar power bank, ₦28,000. Charges two phones at once. Limited stock, chat me now." Each caption gives the customer everything they need in one glance.

Use Nigerian English and local references naturally. "Available now", "No wahala", "We deliver sharp sharp" and "Send your location" are phrases your customers use every day. You do not need to sound like a corporate advert. You need to sound like a real seller they can talk to. At the same time, keep your spelling correct on prices, dates and phone numbers, because errors there cost you orders.

Always end a selling status with a clear next step. "Screenshot and send to me", "Chat me on WhatsApp to order", "Tap the catalogue to see more sizes" — pick one. A customer who likes your product but does not know what to do next will keep scrolling and forget about it.

## Reply Fast, Follow Up, and Close the Sale in Chat

Posting is only half the job. The sale happens in the chat. Many Nigerian sellers post excellent status updates, then reply to messages three hours later, by which time the customer has bought from someone else. Speed is a competitive advantage you can control for free.

Aim to reply within five to ten minutes during working hours. If you cannot be online all day, set your working hours clearly on your profile and use the greeting message to tell customers when you will reply. When you do reply, answer the question directly. If a customer asks "Is the red one available in size 12?", do not reply "Yes, we have many colours." Reply: "Yes, red is available in size 12. Price is ₦22,000, delivery in Lekki is ₦2,000. Would you like me to reserve it for you?" That answer moves the sale forward.

Ask for the order. Many sellers answer questions politely and then wait, hoping the customer will say "I want to buy". Customers often need a gentle push. After answering their questions, ask: "Should I package one for you now?", "What area should I deliver to?", "Would you like to pay by transfer or on delivery?" A question is easier to answer than a vague "Let me know". It turns a chat into a sale.

After a customer pays, confirm quickly and keep them updated. Send a message when you receive payment, when the order is packed, when the rider leaves, and when it is delivered. Each update reduces anxiety and builds the trust that brings the customer back. After delivery, ask for feedback and, if they are happy, ask if you can share their review on your status. That review becomes your next social proof post.

## Mistakes That Kill WhatsApp Status Sales

Posting too much is the most common mistake. Twenty slides a day, every day, trains your viewers to skip you. Keep it to four to eight slides, and make each one worth watching. If you have nothing useful to post, post nothing. Silence for a day is better than noise that makes people mute you.

Posting without prices is the second mistake. It wastes your time and the customer's time, and it signals that the price might be a secret or a scam. Show the price, show the delivery fee, and let serious buyers come to you.

Using other people's photos is the third mistake. Customers who receive a product that looks nothing like the photo will demand a refund, leave a bad review, and warn their friends. Use your own photos, even if they are simple. A real photo of your real product, taken in daylight, will outsell a stolen studio photo every time.

Ignoring viewers is the fourth mistake. If someone views your status every day but never messages, they are interested but not yet convinced. Post social proof, answer common questions on your status, and make it easy for them to start a chat. A status that says "Many people ask if we deliver to Abuja — yes, we do, delivery is ₦4,500" can turn silent viewers into buyers.

## A Simple Weekly Routine

Run your status sales on a weekly rhythm. On Sunday evening, plan the week: which products you will push, which days you will offer a discount, and when new stock arrives. On Monday morning, post your best seller with its price. On Wednesday, post social proof and a behind-the-scenes video. On Friday, post a weekend offer with a clear deadline. On Saturday, post your delivery updates and happy customers. Each day, reply fast, follow up once, and update your labels so you know who has paid and who is still waiting.

Check your numbers once a week. WhatsApp Business shows how many people viewed each status. Note which products got the most views and the most messages. If a particular gown got 200 views and 15 messages, stock more of it. If another product got 40 views and no messages, change the photo, change the price, or stop pushing it. You do not need complicated analytics. Views, messages and orders are enough to tell you what works.

WhatsApp status will not make you rich overnight, but run properly, it becomes a steady daily sales channel that costs you nothing except consistency. Post one clear product at a time, show the price, prove you deliver, reply fast, and follow up once. Do that every day for a month, and your status will stop being a notice board and start being the shop your customers check first.
