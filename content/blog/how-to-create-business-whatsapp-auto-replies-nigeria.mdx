If you run a small business in Nigeria, your WhatsApp is your shop front, your receptionist, and your cashier all in one. Customers message you at 7 a.m. before work, at 2 p.m. during lunch, and at 11 p.m. when they finally have time to shop. You cannot reply instantly to every message — and you do not have to. WhatsApp Business has built-in auto-reply tools that answer customers for you, day and night, even while you are cooking, driving, or asleep.

This guide shows you exactly how to set up WhatsApp auto-replies for your business: the greeting message, the away message, quick replies, labels, and the catalogue. It is written for Nigerian realities — customers who ask "Is this available?" ten times a day, buyers who pay by bank transfer, and the MTN or Glo data bundles you are running on. No technical skill is needed, and everything here is free.

## Why Auto-Replies Matter for a Nigerian Small Business

Speed wins customers in Nigeria. When a buyer messages three sellers about the same human hair, bag, or plate of food, the seller who replies first usually gets the sale. The others get "seen" and forgotten. An auto-reply does not close the sale by itself, but it keeps the customer warm for the few minutes or hours until you can reply in person.

Auto-replies also make a small business look professional. A customer who messages at midnight and immediately receives a polite greeting with your opening hours and a link to your catalogue trusts you more than a business that leaves them on "delivered" until morning. That first impression matters, especially when the customer found you through Instagram, Facebook, or a friend's recommendation and has never bought from you before.

There is a third benefit that owners often miss: auto-replies save your time and your temper. If you answer "How much is delivery to Lekki?" forty times a week, a quick reply lets you answer in two taps instead of typing the same paragraph forty times. That time goes back into running the business.

The tools in this guide are all inside WhatsApp Business, the free app from WhatsApp itself. If you are still using the regular WhatsApp app for your business, the first step is to switch. Your chats, contacts, and groups move with you, and you gain the business features this guide covers.

## Step 1: Install WhatsApp Business and Set Up Your Profile

Download WhatsApp Business from the Google Play Store or Apple App Store and register it with the phone number you use for the business. Use a dedicated business number if you can — a second SIM is cheap, and it keeps business messages separate from your personal chats. Many Nigerian business owners use a dual-SIM phone for exactly this reason.

Once installed, fill in your business profile properly. Tap the three dots at the top right, then Settings, then your business name. Add:

- **Your business name**, exactly as customers know it.
- **A profile photo**, ideally your logo or a clear photo of your best product.
- **Your address**, even if it is just the area — "Lekki Phase 1, Lagos" is enough to start.
- **Your business category**, such as Retail, Food and Drink, or Beauty.
- **Your opening hours**, for example Monday to Saturday, 8 a.m. to 8 p.m.
- **Your email address**, if you have one.

A complete profile builds trust before a customer types a word. A buyer who sees a logo, an address, and opening hours is far more likely to pay by bank transfer than one who sees a blank profile with no photo.

Also set your business description to one or two lines that say what you sell and how to order. For example: "Affordable human hair and wigs in Lagos. Nationwide delivery. Pay on delivery in Lagos." That single line answers the three questions most new customers ask.

## Step 2: Write a Greeting Message That Sells

The greeting message is the automatic reply a customer receives the first time they message you, or after 14 days of silence. It is your shop's welcome, and it should do three things in a few lines: greet the customer warmly, say what you sell, and tell them what to do next.

To set it up, open WhatsApp Business, tap the three dots, go to Business tools, then Greeting message. Switch it on. You can choose to send it to everyone, or only to people who are not in your contacts. Sending it to everyone is usually best for a small business, because most new customers are not yet in your contacts.

Here is a greeting message template you can adapt:

"Hello and welcome to Adaeze Fashion Hub. Thank you for messaging us. We sell quality female clothes and bags at affordable prices. Please send the screenshot of what you like, or type CATALOGUE to see our products. We reply within 1 hour during business hours."

Keep it short. A greeting message that runs for ten lines will not be read. Three or four lines is enough. Use the customer's name automatically if you can — WhatsApp lets you insert it — but a warm, general greeting works fine.

Do not use the greeting message to list your prices. Prices change, and a greeting message with old prices creates arguments. Instead, point the customer to your catalogue or to a simple action, like sending a screenshot of the product they want.

Review your greeting message every month. If you change your products, your opening hours, or your delivery policy, update the message the same day. An outdated greeting message is worse than none at all, because it gives wrong information with confidence.

## Step 3: Set an Away Message for Nights and Busy Hours

The away message answers customers when you are not available — at night, on Sundays, or during your busiest hours. Without it, a customer who messages at 11 p.m. sees nothing until morning and may buy from a competitor who replied faster.

To set it up, go to Business tools, then Away message. Switch it on and choose a schedule: always send, on a custom schedule, or outside business hours. The best option for most small businesses is "outside business hours," using the hours you set in your profile. That way the away message only appears when you are genuinely closed.

A good away message is honest and helpful:

"Hello, thank you for messaging Adaeze Fashion Hub. We are currently closed and will reply as soon as we open at 8 a.m. You can browse our catalogue now and send your order — we will confirm it first thing in the morning. Thank you for your patience."

Notice what this message does: it tells the customer when you will reply, and it gives them something useful to do in the meantime. A customer who browses your catalogue at night is a customer who may have already chosen what to buy by morning.

If you close for a public holiday or travel, update the away message for that period. "We are closed for the Christmas holiday and will reopen on January 2nd. You can still place orders and we will deliver when we return." That single message prevents dozens of confused follow-up messages.

## Step 4: Build Quick Replies for the Questions You Answer Daily

Quick replies are short, saved answers you can send with two taps. They are the biggest time saver in WhatsApp Business, and most Nigerian business owners underuse them.

To create one, go to Business tools, then Quick replies, then the plus button. Write the message, then give it a shortcut — a short word starting with a slash, like /price or /delivery. When a customer asks the question, you type the shortcut and WhatsApp suggests the full message.

Here are the quick replies almost every Nigerian small business needs:

**Price list.** "/price" — a short list of your main products and prices. Update it whenever prices change.

**Delivery fees.** "/delivery" — your delivery charges by area. For example: "Delivery within Lekki: ₦1,500. Lagos mainland: ₦2,500. Abuja: ₦3,500. Other states: ₦4,000 to ₦5,000. Delivery takes 24 to 48 hours."

**Payment details.** "/pay" — your account number, bank name, and account name, plus a note to send proof of payment. Keep this one ready at all times, because the customer who asks for account details is ready to buy.

**Opening hours.** "/hours" — your working days and hours, and the best time to reach you.

**Order confirmation.** "/confirm" — "Thank you, your order is confirmed. Your total is ₦X, including delivery. Please send proof of payment and we will dispatch today."

**Out of stock.** "/soldout" — a polite message for when an item is finished: "Sorry, that item is currently sold out. We expect new stock on Friday. Would you like us to reserve one for you?"

Write each quick reply in your own voice, short and polite. Avoid long paragraphs; customers read WhatsApp messages quickly and skip anything that looks like an essay. Test each one by sending it to yourself or a staff member before you rely on it.

You can create as many quick replies as you like, but start with six to ten. More than that becomes hard to remember, and the shortcuts stop saving time.

## Step 5: Use Labels to Organise Your Customers

Labels are coloured tags you attach to chats so you can find customers quickly. They are simple, free, and surprisingly powerful once you start using them.

To add a label, long-press a chat, tap the label icon, and choose or create a label. WhatsApp Business comes with five default labels — New customer, New order, Pending payment, Paid, and Order complete — and you can create your own.

A practical labelling system for a Nigerian small business looks like this:

- **New customer** — someone who has messaged for the first time.
- **Pending payment** — a customer who has ordered but not yet paid.
- **Paid** — payment confirmed, order being prepared or dispatched.
- **Order complete** — delivered and done.
- **VIP** — a repeat customer who orders often and should get priority replies.
- **Lagos** and **Outside Lagos** — useful when you batch deliveries by location.

Labels change how you work. Each morning, open the label view and see all your "Pending payment" chats in one place. Send them a polite reminder with your payment quick reply. At the end of the week, open "Order complete" and send those customers a thank-you message or a notice about new stock. That is follow-up marketing, done in minutes, with no extra app.

Labels also stop you losing orders. A chat that slips down your list is easy to forget; a labelled chat is easy to find. In a busy week with 50 or more customer chats, that difference is worth real money.

## Step 6: Add a Catalogue So Customers Can Browse Without You

The catalogue is a mini shop inside your WhatsApp profile. Customers tap your business name, open the catalogue, and see your products with photos and prices — without sending a single message. It sells for you while you are busy or asleep.

To set it up, go to Business tools, then Catalogue, then add an item. For each product, add a clear photo, the product name, the price in naira, and a short description. Add a link if you sell on a website or Instagram shop as well.

Take good photos. You do not need a professional camera; a phone camera in daylight works well. Photograph the product against a plain background, in good light, from two angles if possible. A blurry photo taken at night under a yellow bulb will not sell a ₦25,000 wig, no matter how good the wig is.

Keep the catalogue current. Remove items that are sold out, and add new stock the day it arrives. A customer who orders from your catalogue and is told the item finished two weeks ago will not trust the next listing. Price changes belong in the catalogue the same day they happen.

The catalogue also works with your auto-replies. Your greeting message can point new customers to it, and your quick replies can reference it. When a customer asks "What do you have?" you can send one message — "Please check our catalogue here" — instead of sending ten photos one by one.

## Step 7: Combine Auto-Replies With WhatsApp Status and Broadcasts

Auto-replies handle incoming messages, but you also need to reach out to customers. WhatsApp Status and broadcast lists do that, and they work well alongside your auto-replies.

Post on your WhatsApp Status every day, or at least three times a week. A photo of new stock, a short video of a product, or a customer's review keeps your business in front of people who already have your number. Post in the morning before 9 a.m., when people check their phones before work, and in the evening after 6 p.m.

Use broadcast lists to send updates to many customers at once. A broadcast goes to each person as a private message, unlike a group where everyone sees each other. Create lists by customer type — for example, one list for Lagos customers and one for customers outside Lagos — so your messages stay relevant.

A few rules for broadcasts: send at most two or three a week, keep each one short, and always include a clear action, like "Reply YES to order." Too many broadcasts feel like spam, and customers will mute or block you. Every broadcast should give value — new stock, a discount, or useful information — not just ask for a sale.

When a broadcast brings replies, your quick replies and labels take over: quick replies answer the common questions, and labels track who ordered, who paid, and who is still deciding.

## Step 8: Common Mistakes That Push Customers Away

Auto-replies are powerful, but they can also annoy customers if used badly. Avoid these mistakes.

**Sounding like a robot.** A greeting message that reads like a formal letter feels cold. Write the way you would speak to a customer in your shop: warm, short, and human.

**Leaving auto-replies on during business hours.** The greeting message is for first contact, but your away message should only run when you are actually away. A customer who messages at noon and gets "we are closed" will think you do not want their money.

**Never replying in person.** An auto-reply is a holding message, not a replacement for you. If a customer gets only automatic messages for a whole day, they will leave. Reply personally as soon as you can, even if it is just to confirm an order.

**Outdated prices and information.** An auto-reply that quotes last month's price will cause an argument when the real price is higher. Update your quick replies and greeting message the day anything changes.

**Too many messages.** Do not set up an auto-reply for every situation. A customer who receives three automatic messages in a row — greeting, away message, and a catalogue link — feels spammed. One clear message is enough.

## A Simple 7-Day Plan to Set It All Up

Day one: install WhatsApp Business and complete your business profile with your name, photo, address, hours, and description.

Day two: write and switch on your greeting message. Keep it to three or four lines, and point customers to your catalogue.

Day three: set your away message on a schedule that matches your real opening hours, and write it to tell customers when you will reply.

Day four: create your six most important quick replies — price list, delivery fees, payment details, opening hours, order confirmation, and out of stock.

Day five: label your existing chats — new customers, pending payment, paid, order complete — and start checking the pending payment label each morning.

Day six: build your catalogue with photos and prices for your ten best-selling products. Remove anything that is out of stock.

Day seven: post your first WhatsApp Status of the week, send one broadcast to your customers about new stock, and review every auto-reply you set up. Fix anything that is outdated or unclear.

After this week, the system runs itself. Your greeting message welcomes every new customer, your away message covers the nights, your quick replies answer the same questions in seconds, your labels keep your orders organised, and your catalogue sells while you sleep. Your job is to reply personally when it matters, keep your information current, and let the automatic tools handle the rest. That is how a one-person business in Lagos, Abuja, or Enugu can serve 50 customers a day without hiring a receptionist.
