You have a product or service, and you have a Facebook Page or Instagram account. You have seen the "Boost Post" button many times. But boosting a post is not really advertising — it is the shallow end. A proper ad, set up in Meta Ads Manager, gives you control over who sees it, what they see, and what happens when they click. And it is far simpler than it looks.

This is a genuine beginner walkthrough. In about 30 minutes, you can have your first real campaign live.

## Before You Start: What You Need

You need three things before you open Ads Manager.

First, a Facebook Page for your business. Ads run from a Page, not a personal profile. If you only have a personal profile, create a Page first — it takes a few minutes and it is free.

Second, an Instagram account connected to that Page, if you want your ad to appear on Instagram as well. You can connect it from your Page settings. This is optional for a first ad, but most Nigerian businesses get good results from Instagram, so it is worth doing.

Third, a payment method. Meta accepts cards, and you will add yours in Ads Manager. Your first campaign does not need a large budget — you can start small and learn, which is exactly what this guide recommends.

Finally, decide what you want the ad to do. Do you want people to send you a WhatsApp message? Visit your website? Follow your Page? That single decision shapes everything else, so make it before you start.

## Step 1: Open Ads Manager and Create a Campaign

Go to business.facebook.com and open Ads Manager. If you have never used it, Meta will walk you through creating an ad account — just follow the prompts and choose your business details and time zone carefully, because the time zone is hard to change later.

Click the green "Create" button. The first thing Ads Manager asks is your campaign objective — what you want the ad to achieve. For a first campaign, pick the objective that matches your goal:

- **Traffic**, if you want people to visit your website or landing page.
- **Engagement**, if you want likes, comments, and shares on a post.
- **Leads**, if you want people to fill in a form with their details.
- **Sales**, if you want people to buy directly from your website or shop.

For most small businesses running a first ad, Traffic or Engagement is the simplest place to start. If your main goal is WhatsApp chats, you can set up a campaign that sends people to WhatsApp — look for the messaging option when choosing your objective.

## Step 2: Set Up Your Ad Set — Audience, Placements, and Budget

After the campaign, Ads Manager asks you to create an ad set. This is where you decide who sees the ad, where it appears, and how much you spend.

### Audience

This is the most important part of your first ad. You can target by location, age, gender, interests, and behaviours. For a first campaign, keep it simple:

- **Location:** your city or state — Lagos, Abuja, Port Harcourt, or wherever your customers are. If you deliver nationwide, you can target Nigeria as a whole, but starting with your strongest market usually works better.
- **Age:** the age range of your real customers.
- **Interests:** things your customers care about. If you sell fitness products, target people interested in fitness and healthy living. If you run a hotel, target people interested in travel and events.

Do not make the audience too narrow on your first try. An audience of a few hundred thousand people in your area is a healthy starting size. If it is too small, your ad may barely run; if it is far too broad, your money spreads too thin.

### Placements

Placements are where your ad appears — Facebook feed, Instagram feed, Stories, Reels, and more. For a first campaign, the automatic option, called Advantage+ placements, is a sensible choice. Meta shows your ad where it is most likely to perform, and you learn which placements work for your business.

### Budget and schedule

Set a daily budget — the amount you are willing to spend each day. For a first test, a modest daily budget is enough to learn whether your ad works. Start small, watch the results for two or three days, and only increase the budget when the ad is performing.

Set a start date of today and, for a first test, an end date a few days away. A short test with a clear end date keeps you in control and stops an ad running unnoticed for weeks.

## Step 3: Create the Ad — Creative and Copy

Now the fun part: the ad itself. You need an image or video, a headline, and a short piece of text.

**Creative.** Use a clear, bright image or a short video that shows your product or service in use. Real photos of your actual product, your shop, or your work beat generic stock photos, because people can tell the difference. Keep text on the image to a minimum — a short headline is fine, but a paragraph on the image is not.

**Primary text.** This is the text above the image. Write like you talk. Say what you offer, who it is for, and what the reader should do next. Two or three short sentences are usually enough. For example: "Fresh, ready-to-wear styles for Lagos women, delivered to your door. Send us a message on WhatsApp to order today."

**Headline.** The bold line under the image. Make it specific: "New collection now available" is vague; "Ankara dresses from ₦25,000 — order on WhatsApp" tells the reader exactly what to expect.

**Call to action.** Choose the button that matches your goal: "Send WhatsApp Message", "Learn More", "Shop Now", or "Sign Up". The button should lead somewhere you are ready to handle — if it opens WhatsApp, make sure someone is ready to reply quickly.

Before you publish, use the preview on the right of the screen to check how the ad looks on a phone. Most of your audience will see it on a phone, so if it looks wrong there, fix it before it goes live.

## Step 4: Publish and Watch the First Results

Click "Publish". Meta reviews your ad before it goes live, which usually takes from a few minutes to a few hours. If it is rejected, Meta will tell you why — usually a policy issue such as a claim the ad cannot support — and you can edit and resubmit.

Once the ad is running, give it time. Do not judge it after one hour. Let it run for at least two or three days, then open Ads Manager and look at the basics: how many people saw it, how many clicked, and how much each click cost. These numbers tell you whether the ad is working.

## Mistakes Beginners Should Avoid

- **Boosting posts instead of using Ads Manager.** Boosting is quick, but it gives you almost no control over audience, objective, or placements. Ads Manager is the real tool, and it is worth the extra few minutes.
- **Targeting everyone.** "All of Nigeria, ages 18 to 65" is not a target audience. A clear audience is cheaper and more effective than a vague one.
- **No clear next step.** An ad that does not tell people what to do — message us, visit the site, order now — wastes the attention it earns.
- **Expecting instant sales.** A first ad is a test, not a money machine. Its job is to teach you what your audience responds to, so your next campaign performs better.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much should I spend on my first Meta ad?

Start with a small daily budget you can afford to lose while you learn — the amount depends on your business, but the principle is the same for everyone: test small, learn what works, and only scale up when the numbers make sense.

### How long should my first campaign run?

Two to three days is enough for a first test. That gives Meta time to show the ad to enough people for the results to mean something.

### Why was my ad rejected?

The most common reasons are claims Meta cannot verify — such as guaranteed results or income promises — and images with too much text. Read the reason Meta gives, fix that specific issue, and resubmit.

### Should I run ads on Facebook, Instagram, or both?

For a first campaign, let Meta's automatic placements decide. After a few days, check which placement brought the best results for your money, and you can focus your budget there next time.

## Your First Ad Is a Lesson, Not a Lottery

Nobody's first ad is perfect. The businesses that win with Meta ads are the ones that test small, read their numbers, and improve a little with every campaign. Set up your first ad today, watch what happens, and let what you learn shape the next one.
