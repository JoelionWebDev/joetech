Getting a new phone should feel exciting. Then you remember: years of WhatsApp chats are on the old one. Customer conversations, family group chats, important voice notes, photos you will never get back. The fear of losing all of it keeps some people using a cracked, dying phone for months longer than they should.

The good news is that moving WhatsApp is straightforward — if you do it in the right order. The one rule that matters most is this: back up first, then move. Every failed transfer starts with someone who skipped the backup.

## Before You Start: The One Thing That Saves Everything

WhatsApp does not store your chats on its own servers. Your messages live on your phone. That is why a backup matters so much: it is the copy that lets you rebuild everything on a new device.

On Android, WhatsApp backs up to Google Drive. On iPhone, it backs up to iCloud. Before you do anything else, check that your backup is fresh:

1. Open WhatsApp on your old phone.
2. Go to **Settings**, then **Chats**, then **Chat backup**.
3. Look at the date and time of the last backup. If it is not today, tap **Back up** now.
4. Choose whether to include videos. They make the backup bigger, so it is your call.
5. Stay on Wi-Fi while the backup runs and keep the phone charged — a backup over mobile data can eat a large part of your bundle.

Do this on the old phone before you even unbox the new one. It is the single most important step in this whole guide. Just confirm one detail: the backup is tied to the account you are signed in with — your Google account on Android, your Apple ID on iPhone — and you will need the same account on the new phone.

## Moving from Android to Android

This is the easiest move of all.

1. On your old phone, run a fresh backup to Google Drive, as described above.
2. On your new phone, sign in with the **same Google account** you used for the backup.
3. Install WhatsApp and open it.
4. Verify your phone number — use the same number as before.
5. WhatsApp will find your Google Drive backup and offer to restore it. Tap **Restore** and wait. Do not close the app or switch off the phone while it works.
6. Once the chats appear, WhatsApp will finish downloading your photos and videos in the background.

## Moving from iPhone to iPhone

The iPhone-to-iPhone move works the same way, using iCloud instead of Google Drive.

1. On your old iPhone, go to WhatsApp, then **Settings**, **Chats**, **Chat backup**, and tap **Back up Now**. Wait for it to finish.
2. On your new iPhone, sign in with the **same Apple ID**.
3. Install WhatsApp, verify your number, and tap **Restore Chat History** when it appears.
4. Keep the phone on Wi-Fi and charging until the restore is done.

One thing to watch: iCloud backups need enough free iCloud storage. If your backup is large and your iCloud is full, the backup will fail quietly, so check your storage in your iPhone settings before you start.

## Moving from Android to iPhone

For years this was the hardest move, but WhatsApp now has an official way to do it.

1. On your old Android phone, run a fresh backup first, just in case.
2. Install the **Move to iOS** app on your Android phone, and set up the iPhone until you reach the "Transfer your data" screen.
3. On the Android phone, open Move to iOS, connect to the iPhone, and choose **WhatsApp**.
4. Open WhatsApp on the Android phone and follow the transfer screen. Your chats will move directly from one phone to the other.
5. Once it finishes, install WhatsApp on the iPhone, sign in, and your chats will be there.

This needs both phones, charged and close together, and it can take a while if you have years of chats. Do not start it when you are in a hurry.

## Moving from iPhone to Android

Going the other way is also supported now, usually with a cable.

1. Connect the two phones with a USB-C to Lightning cable.
2. On the Android phone, during setup, choose to transfer data from your iPhone.
3. When asked what to transfer, select **WhatsApp**.
4. On the iPhone, scan the QR code on the Android phone, then open WhatsApp and start the transfer.
5. Keep both phones connected and unlocked until it completes, then finish setting up WhatsApp on the Android phone.

If your new Android phone does not offer the transfer during setup, you can do it later through the phone's data transfer app. The steps vary a little by brand — Samsung, Tecno, Infinix, and others each have their own — but all of them support moving WhatsApp now.

## What About Photos, Videos, and Voice Notes?

Your photos, videos, and voice notes move with your chats, as long as they were part of the backup or the transfer. Two things to check:

- **Media is big.** A backup that includes videos can be several gigabytes. Make sure your Google Drive or iCloud has enough space, and use Wi-Fi for the restore so you do not burn through your data.
- **Old media may already be gone.** If you once deleted a photo from your phone, WhatsApp cannot bring it back. The backup only contains what is still on the phone, so check that a photo you care about is still in the chat before you switch.

After the move, give your new phone some time. WhatsApp restores the chat text first, then downloads media in the background. If a photo looks blurred for a while, it is still downloading.

## If Something Goes Wrong

Most transfers work the first time. If yours does not, work through this checklist before panicking:

- **Wrong account.** The new phone must use the same Google account (Android) or Apple ID (iPhone) as the backup. This is the most common cause of a "no backup found" message.
- **No backup existed.** If you never turned backups on, there is nothing to restore. If the chats are still on the old phone, run a backup now and try again.
- **Different phone number.** If you changed your number, change it inside WhatsApp on the old phone first (Settings, Account, Change number), then move.
- **Not enough space.** A restore needs free space roughly equal to the size of the backup. Delete apps or files you do not need.
- **Old phone is lost or broken.** If the old phone is gone and there was no cloud backup, the chats are gone too. That is why automatic backups are worth turning on today, not after a problem.

## A Simple Checklist Before You Switch

Run through this list and you will be fine:

- Backup is fresh — made today, on Wi-Fi, with videos if you want them.
- You know which Google account or Apple ID holds the backup.
- Your new phone is charged and has enough free space.
- Your old phone is still working and nearby until the transfer is confirmed.
- Your phone number is the same, or you have already changed it inside WhatsApp.
- You have turned on automatic backups on the new phone, so you never have to worry about this again.

To turn on automatic backups, go to WhatsApp, **Settings**, **Chats**, **Chat backup**, and choose **Daily** or **Weekly**. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Will my chats transfer if I change my phone number?

Yes, but change the number inside WhatsApp on your old phone first. Go to Settings, Account, Change number, and follow the steps. Then move to the new phone and verify the new number. If you skip this, WhatsApp may not find your backup.

### How much data does a WhatsApp backup use?

It depends on your media. A text-only backup is small, often under 100 MB. With photos and videos, it can be several gigabytes. Always back up and restore on Wi-Fi to avoid using up your mobile data.

### Can I transfer my chats without a backup?

Yes, using the direct transfer methods — Move to iOS for Android to iPhone, or a cable for iPhone to Android. These move chats directly between the phones. But a cloud backup is still the safest option, because it also protects you if a phone is lost or stolen.

### What if my old phone is lost or broken before I back up?

If there was no cloud backup and the old phone is gone, the chats cannot be recovered — WhatsApp does not keep a copy of your messages. Turn on automatic daily backups now, so the next switch is painless.

## Switch Phones Without the Fear

A new phone should be a fresh start, not a fresh loss. Back up first, use the same account and number, and give the transfer time. Do those three things and your chats, photos, and voice notes will be waiting for you on the new phone exactly where you left them. Then take two minutes to turn on automatic backups.
