Every website has one, and almost every visitor has seen one. It is the page that appears when something goes wrong: a mistyped address, a deleted page, an old link from years ago. It usually says "404 Not Found" in plain text, offers no help, and leaves the visitor with only one real choice, which is to leave.

That page is your 404 page, and it is one of the most neglected pages on most Nigerian business websites. Yet it is also one of the easiest pages to improve, and a good one can turn a frustrating moment into a second chance to win a customer.

This post explains what a 404 page is, why the default version costs you visitors, and how to design one that actually helps people find what they were looking for.

## What Is a 404 Page?

When someone tries to visit a page on your website that does not exist, your server responds with a status code of 404, which means "not found". Your website then shows a 404 page to explain what happened.

Visitors arrive at a 404 page in many ways. They might mistype your web address, click an old link from a social media post, follow a link from another website to a page you have since deleted, or click a bookmark to a page whose address changed during a redesign. Sometimes there is nothing they did wrong at all. The link itself was simply out of date.


## Why the Default 404 Page Costs You Visitors

Most websites show a default 404 page provided by their hosting platform or website builder. It usually contains a short error message, a plain design, and no links back to the rest of the site. Sometimes it does not even look like the rest of your website, so the visitor wonders whether they are still on your site at all.

This is a problem: it offers no next step. A visitor who lands on a dead end has to work out what to do next on their own. Most will not bother. They will close the tab and search for a competitor instead.



## What a Good 404 Page Should Do

A good 404 page has one job: to help the visitor get back on track as quickly as possible. It should do three things well.

**Explain what happened in plain language.** Most visitors do not know what "404" means, and they do not need to. Use simple, friendly words. Something like "Sorry, we cannot find the page you are looking for" is far better than a technical error code on its own.

**Look like the rest of your website.** Your 404 page should carry your logo, your colours, and your normal navigation menu. When it looks like the rest of your site, the visitor feels reassured that they are still in the right place and can find their way around.

**Offer clear next steps.** This is the most important part. Give the visitor somewhere useful to go. Include a search box, so the visitor can search for what they were looking for, and links to the most important pages on the site, such as the homepage, the services page, and the contact page. A visitor who was looking for a service and landed on an error page should be one click away from finding it.


## What to Include on Your 404 Page

Here is a practical checklist for building a helpful 404 page for a Nigerian small business website.

- A clear, friendly headline, such as "This page seems to have gone missing".
- A short sentence explaining what may have happened, in plain language.
- A search box so visitors can search your site directly.
- A button linking back to your homepage.
- Links to your main service or product pages.
- A link to your contact page, so visitors can ask for help.
- Your normal header and footer, including your phone number and WhatsApp link.
- Links to two or three of your most popular blog posts or products.


## How to Create a Custom 404 Page

How you create a custom 404 page depends on how your website was built.

If your website runs on WordPress, your theme may already include a 404 template that you can edit, or you can use a plugin to design one without writing code. Check your theme settings first, because many modern themes allow you to customise the 404 page directly.

If your website was built with a website builder such as Wix or Squarespace, look in the site settings for a 404 page option. Most builders let you edit the text and add links, even if the design options are limited.

If your website was custom-built by a developer, ask them to design a 404 page that matches the rest of your site and includes the elements listed above. It is a small job for a developer, and it is worth doing properly.

Whatever platform you use, test the page after creating it. Visit a web address on your site that you know does not exist, such as your domain followed by a random word, and confirm that your new 404 page appears, that it looks right on a phone as well as a computer, and that every link on it works.

## How to Reduce 404 Errors in the First Place

A good 404 page is a safety net, not a substitute for fixing the underlying problems. You should also reduce how often visitors encounter 404 errors at all.

First, set up redirects when you rename, move, or delete a page. A redirect sends visitors from the old address to the new one automatically, so they never see an error page. This is the single most effective way to prevent 404 errors on your own site.

Second, check your website for broken links regularly. Internal links that point to deleted pages are a common cause of 404 errors, and they are easy to find with a broken link checker and easy to fix once found.

Third, check Google Search Console for pages that return 404 errors. Google reports the addresses it tried to visit but could not find, which helps you identify the pages that need redirects or fixes. Prioritise the ones that receive the most traffic.


If you are seeing a large number of 404 errors, or if visitors keep landing on your 404 page from links inside your own website, it may be a sign of a wider problem. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Will a custom 404 page improve my Google rankings?

A custom 404 page on its own will not directly raise your rankings, but it improves the experience for visitors who land on an error, which keeps them on your site longer. 

### Should I redirect all 404 errors to my homepage?

No. Redirecting every missing page to your homepage confuses visitors, because they expected specific content and arrived somewhere unrelated. It also hides the problem, so you may never notice which pages are missing. Only redirect a missing page to a specific, relevant page.

### How do I know how many visitors see my 404 page?

Your website analytics tool, such as Google Analytics, can show you how often the 404 page is viewed and which addresses visitors were trying to reach. Google Search Console also reports pages that return 404 errors. Checking these reports regularly helps you spot the missing pages that matter most.

### Can a 404 page include a contact form?

It can, but a simple link to your contact page is usually better. A full form on an error page can feel out of place and distract from the main goal, which is helping the visitor find what they were looking for. Keep the page focused, and make the contact link easy to find.

## Final Thoughts

Nobody plans to visit a 404 page, but every website will send some visitors there. The question is whether those visitors meet a dead end or a helpful guide. A clear message, a familiar design, a search box, and links to your best pages are all it takes to turn a frustrating moment into a second chance.

Take a few minutes this week to visit your own 404 page. If it is a bare error message with no way forward, improve it. Your visitors, and your business, will be better for it.
