# How to Create Service Packages That Sell More in Nigeria

A customer messages a Lagos cleaning company and asks, "How much do you charge?" The owner replies with a long list: deep cleaning ₦45,000, sofa cleaning ₦15,000, fumigation ₦25,000, window cleaning ₦10,000. The customer reads the list, says "Okay, I will get back to you," and never returns. The business did nothing wrong — except it made buying feel like hard work.

Now imagine the same company replies differently: "We have three packages. Essential Home Clean ₦60,000 — full deep clean of a 3-bedroom flat. Family Fresh ₦95,000 — deep clean plus sofa and mattress cleaning. Total Home Care ₦140,000 — everything plus fumigation and window cleaning." The customer picks one in two minutes and books. Same services, same business, completely different result.

That is the power of a service package. A package takes your separate services, bundles them into a clear offer with one price, and makes the buying decision easy. Nigerian customers love packages because they remove confusion, feel like better value, and let the buyer compare options instead of negotiating every item. Businesses love packages because they raise the average sale, reduce endless price haggling, and make marketing simpler.

This guide shows you how to build service packages for your own business — whether you run a salon in Abuja, a catering company in Port Harcourt, a digital agency in Lekki, or a cleaning service in Ikeja. You will learn how to choose what goes in each package, how to price them, how to name them, and how to present them so customers buy.

## What a Service Package Is — and What It Is Not

A service package is a fixed bundle of services sold together at one price, with a clear name and a clear list of what is included. A bridal makeup artist might offer a "Classic Bride" package: makeup, gele tying, lashes, and a touch-up kit for ₦120,000. The customer knows exactly what she gets and what she pays. There is no back-and-forth, no surprise charges, and no confusion.

A package is not a discount list. Slashing your prices and calling the result a package is not packaging — it is a sale, and it trains customers to wait for cheaper prices. A real package adds value by combining services that make sense together, not by cutting your rates to the bone.

## Why Packages Sell More in Nigeria

Packages work particularly well in the Nigerian market for several reasons.

First, Nigerian customers hate uncertainty. Many have been burned by service providers who quoted one price and charged another at the end. A package with a fixed price and a written list of inclusions builds trust before the work starts. The customer knows what she is paying for, and you know what you must deliver. That clarity removes the biggest reason people delay buying.

Second, packages make comparison easy. When a customer can see three options side by side, she compares them with each other instead of comparing your price with a competitor's price. That is a much better conversation for you to be in. Instead of "your price is higher than the salon down the road," the conversation becomes "should I take the Classic or the Premium package?" You have moved the decision inside your own business.

Third, packages raise your average sale without pressure. A customer who came for a ₦15,000 haircut might choose a ₦45,000 package that includes the cut, treatment, and styling — because the package feels like a better deal than buying each service separately. You did not upsell aggressively; the package did the work for you.

Fourth, packages are easier to advertise. A single service is hard to promote — "we do facials" is not a compelling advert. A package is a story: "Glow Up Package — facial, massage, and pedicure for ₦35,000 this weekend." That is a message you can put on a flyer, in a WhatsApp broadcast, or in an Instagram post, and people understand it instantly.

Fifth, packages make your business look professional. A business with named packages and clear prices looks organised and established. A business that quotes a different price every time looks like a hustle. In a market where trust is everything, packaging is a signal of seriousness.

## Step 1: List Everything You Sell

Before you build packages, write down every service you offer, with two numbers next to each one: what it costs you to deliver, and what you currently charge. Include your time, your materials, transport, and any helper you pay. Many Nigerian service businesses discover at this stage that some services lose money — for example, a free delivery that costs ₦5,000 in fuel on a ₦20,000 order. Those services should not go into a package until their cost is covered.

Next, mark which services customers usually buy together. A photographer will notice that clients who book a birthday shoot often ask for extra edited photos and a photo book. A caterer will notice that clients who order small chops also ask for drinks and servers. These natural combinations are the raw material for your packages.

Also mark your most profitable services and your most popular services. Your packages should be built around the popular ones — that is what pulls customers in — and should include the profitable ones, because that is where your margin comes from.

Write this list on paper or in a spreadsheet. You need it in front of you for the next steps, and you will be surprised how much clearer your business looks once everything is written down with real numbers.

## Step 2: Understand Your Customers' Budgets

A package only sells if its price fits what your customers can actually pay. A package priced at ₦500,000 will not sell to customers whose entire event budget is ₦200,000, no matter how good it is.

Think about your last twenty customers. What did most of them spend? If most spent between ₦30,000 and ₦80,000, your middle package should sit in that range. Your cheapest package should be reachable for a first-time buyer who is testing you, and your most expensive package should be within reach of your best customers, not priced for a fantasy client who may never come.

Ask your customers directly. After a job, ask: "If we offered a package that included this service plus two others, what would you expect to pay?" Customers will tell you, and their answers are more useful than any pricing theory.

Also consider how your customers pay. Many Nigerian customers pay in instalments, especially for weddings and big events. A ₦300,000 package with a 60% deposit and the balance a week before the event will sell better than a ₦300,000 package that demands full payment upfront. Build your payment terms into the package description so customers know from the start.

## Step 3: Design Three Packages — Good, Better, Best

The simplest and most effective structure is three tiers: a basic package, a standard package, and a premium package. Three is the magic number. Two options feel like a choice between cheap and expensive. Four or more options create confusion. Three gives the customer a clear middle to land on — and the middle package is the one most customers will choose, so design it with care.

Start with the middle package, because it is your main seller. It should contain your most popular services, deliver clear value, and be priced where most of your customers already spend. This is the package you will promote most.

Then design the basic package. It should be a genuine, useful service — not a stripped-down version that feels like a punishment for being broke. It should solve the customer's core problem at the lowest sensible price, and it should leave room for the customer to upgrade later. A cleaning company's basic package might cover a full deep clean of the home without the extras; a salon's basic package might cover a wash, cut, and style without colour or treatment.

Then design the premium package. This is for customers who want the best and are willing to pay for it. It should include everything in the middle package plus extras that feel luxurious: priority booking, a dedicated attendant, premium materials, free delivery, or a follow-up service. The premium package does not need to sell every day. Its job is to make the middle package look like good value by comparison, and to catch the big spenders when they come.

Here is an example for a makeup artist in Lagos:

- **Everyday Glam — ₦60,000:** Soft glam makeup, lashes, and lip touch-up kit. Perfect for birthdays, photoshoots, and owambe guest looks.
- **Classic Bride — ₦150,000:** Full bridal makeup, gele tying, lashes, touch-up kit, and a trial session before the day.
- **Royal Bride — ₦250,000:** Everything in Classic Bride, plus makeup for the mother of the bride, a second look for the reception, and a dedicated assistant on the day.

Notice how each tier is a complete, sensible offer on its own, and how the jump from one tier to the next is clear. A bride can see exactly what the extra ₦100,000 buys her.

## Step 4: Price for Profit, Not Just for Sales

The most common pricing mistake is building a package by adding up your normal prices and then giving a big discount. A 30% discount on a bundle might win the sale and lose you money. Price your packages from your costs upward, not from your prices downward.

Here is a simple method. For each package, add up the true cost of everything inside it: materials, transport, your time, and any staff you pay. That is your cost. Then decide the profit margin you need. For most service businesses, a margin of 40% to 60% on top of cost is reasonable — for example, if a package costs you ₦40,000 to deliver, a price of ₦70,000 to ₦80,000 gives you a healthy margin. If your price comes out lower than that, something is wrong: either your costs are too high, or the package includes too much.

Check the price against your customers' budgets from Step 2. If the price is above what your customers can pay, do not just cut the price — cut the contents. Remove one service, use a simpler material, or reduce the time involved, and recalculate. A smaller package that makes a profit is always better than a big package that loses money.

Also check the price against your competitors, but do not copy them. If a competitor's similar package is ₦50,000 and yours is ₦80,000, you need to be able to explain the difference: better materials, more experience, faster delivery, or extra services. If you cannot explain the difference, your package is overpriced for your market.

Finally, round your prices to clean numbers. ₦75,000 is easier to say, remember, and pay than ₦73,500. Clean prices also look more professional on a flyer or a WhatsApp price list.

## Step 5: Name Your Packages Well

A package name should tell the customer who it is for or what it does. "Package A," "Package B," and "Package C" tell the customer nothing. "Starter," "Growth," and "Premium" are better, but they are generic and forgettable.

Good names describe the result or the customer. A caterer could use "Small Chops Delight," "Owambe Feast," and "Grand Celebration." A cleaning company could use "Fresh Start," "Family Fresh," and "Total Home Care." A digital agency could use "Launch," "Grow," and "Scale." The best names make the customer feel something — the premium package should sound like an experience, not just a bigger list.

Keep names short, easy to pronounce, and easy to type in a WhatsApp message. Avoid names that need explaining. If a customer has to ask what "Package X" means, the name has failed.

Use the same naming style across all three packages so they feel like a family. Mixing "Basic," "Royal Bride," and "Package 3" looks disorganised. Pick a style — descriptive, aspirational, or customer-based — and use it consistently.

## Step 6: Present Your Packages Clearly

How you show your packages matters as much as what is inside them. A customer who cannot understand your packages in thirty seconds will not buy.

Create a single page or image that shows all three packages side by side, with the middle package highlighted as "Most Popular" or "Best Value." Each column should list what is included as short bullet points, with the price at the bottom. The customer should be able to compare the three columns at a glance and see what the extra money buys.

Put this price list everywhere your customers already look: as a pinned post on Instagram, as an image in your WhatsApp Business catalogue, as a PDF you send when someone asks for prices, and on your website if you have one. When someone asks "how much?" in a DM, send the package image instead of typing a long reply. It is faster, clearer, and more professional.

In your captions and adverts, lead with the middle package. That is your main seller, and it should be the one customers see first. Mention the basic package as an affordable starting point and the premium package as the full experience, but give the middle package the spotlight.

Also, put a clear next step under every package: "DM us to book," "Pay 50% to secure your date," or "Tap the WhatsApp link in our bio." A package without a next step is just information. A package with a next step is a sale waiting to happen.

## Step 7: Test, Listen, and Adjust

Your first version of your packages is a guess. A good guess, but still a guess. The real work starts after you launch them.

Track which package sells most. If everyone buys the basic package and nobody buys the premium, your middle package may be too expensive or too similar to the basic one. If everyone buys the premium, your premium may be underpriced — customers are telling you they would pay more. If nobody buys at all, the problem may be the price, the contents, or simply that customers do not know the packages exist.

Ask customers who did not buy. When someone asks for your price list and disappears, send a polite follow-up: "Thank you for checking our packages. Was there a package that came close to what you needed, or would a smaller option help?" Their answers will tell you whether to add a cheaper tier, change the contents, or adjust the price.

Review your packages every three months. Update prices when your costs rise — fuel, materials, and transport costs in Nigeria change often, and a package priced a year ago may now lose money. Add new services that customers keep asking for, and remove services nobody chooses. A package that never sells is taking up space in your marketing; replace it with something customers actually want.

One caution: do not change your prices silently for existing customers. If a customer booked a package at an old price, honour it. Announce price changes clearly on your page and in your price list, and give regular customers a short notice period. Trust is hard to win and easy to lose over a surprise price change.

## Your Package-Building Checklist

Work through this list and you will have packages ready to sell this week:

- I have listed every service I offer, with its true cost and current price.
- I know which services customers usually buy together.
- I know what my last twenty customers spent, and what my customers can afford.
- I have designed three packages — basic, middle, and premium — starting with the middle.
- Each package is priced from its costs, with a healthy profit margin, and rounded to a clean number.
- Each package has a clear name that describes who it is for or what it does.
- All three packages fit on one page or image, with the middle one highlighted.
- The price list is saved in my WhatsApp catalogue, pinned on Instagram, and ready to send as a PDF.
- I have a clear next step under every package, and payment terms customers can understand.
- I will track which package sells most and review all three packages in three months.

Service packages are not a trick. They are simply a clearer, kinder way to sell: clearer for the customer, who can see exactly what she gets and what she pays, and kinder for you, because you stop negotiating every job from scratch. Build your three packages, put them where your customers can see them, and let the packages do the selling for you.
