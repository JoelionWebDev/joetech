You need a website, an app, or a digital product built. The first decision is not which platform or technology to use — it is who will build it. And the three options — freelancer, agency, and in-house — are very different in cost, quality, reliability, and outcome.

There is no universally right answer. Each option is best for different stages of business, different project types, and different budgets. This guide will help you match your situation to the right choice.

## Freelancers

### What They Are

Independent professionals who work alone or with a small network of collaborators. They range from junior developers charging ₦100,000 per project to senior specialists charging ₦500,000+.

### When Freelancers Are the Right Choice

**You have a small, well-defined project.** A simple 5-page website, a landing page, a WordPress customization, or a small feature addition to an existing site. Projects where the scope is clear and unlikely to change.

**Your budget is tight.** Freelancers have lower overhead than agencies and can often deliver comparable work at 40-60% of the cost.

**You know exactly what you want.** You have a detailed specification, wireframes, or a reference site you want replicated. Freelancers are execution-focused — they do best when the requirements are already defined.

**You have time to manage the project.** You or someone on your team will need to define requirements, review progress, provide feedback, and test the output. Freelancers typically do not provide project management.

### Pros

- **Lower cost** — Lower rates than agencies, no overhead for office space, management, or benefits
- **Direct communication** — You talk directly to the person doing the work
- **Flexible engagement** — Hire for one project, scale up or down easily
- **Access to specialists** — You can find a freelancer who specializes exactly in what you need (e.g., a Nigerian developer who specializes in Paystack integration)

### Cons

- **Single point of failure** — If the freelancer gets sick, disappears, or takes another project, your project stops
- **Limited capacity** — One person can only do so much. Complex projects take longer
- **Variable quality** — Quality varies significantly between freelancers. Hiring the wrong person costs time and money
- **No backup** — If your freelancer moves on after the project, maintaining or updating the work requires finding someone new
- **Less accountability** — Freelancers are not always available for urgent fixes or follow-up questions months later

### Red Flags When Hiring Freelancers

- No portfolio or portfolio with inconsistent quality
- Cannot provide references
- Asks for 100% payment upfront
- Does not use contracts
- Promises unrealistic timelines
- Does not ask questions about your business

### How to Hire a Good Freelancer

1. Check their portfolio thoroughly — do the sites work? Are they well designed? Do they load fast?
2. Ask for 2-3 client references and call them
3. Start with a small paid test project before committing to a large engagement
4. Use a contract that covers scope, timeline, payment milestones, and IP ownership
5. Use a project management tool (Trello, Notion, or Asana) to track progress

## Agencies

### What They Are

A team of professionals with defined roles — project managers, designers, developers, QA testers, and sometimes SEO specialists, content writers, and marketing experts. Agencies range from small studios (3-10 people) to large firms (50+ people).

### When Agencies Are the Right Choice

**Your project is complex or multi-faceted.** An e-commerce website with payment integration, a mobile app with a backend system, a complete brand identity plus website — projects that need multiple skill sets working together.

**Your timeline is tight.** Agencies have the capacity to dedicate multiple people to your project simultaneously. A project that would take a freelancer 12 weeks can often be done by an agency in 6-8 weeks.

**You want a single point of accountability.** Instead of managing a designer, a developer, a QA tester, and a copywriter separately, you deal with one agency that manages the entire process.

**You need ongoing support.** Agencies typically offer maintenance, support, and retainer agreements. They do not disappear after the project launches.

**You value process and reliability.** Agencies have established workflows, quality assurance processes, and backup plans. If one team member is unavailable, another can step in.

### Pros

- **Full team access** — Designers, developers, project managers, QA — all working on your project
- **Accountability** — One contract, one point of contact, one invoice. The agency owns the outcome
- **Reliability** — If someone is sick or leaves, the agency has backup. Your project does not stop
- **Process and quality** — Established workflows, code reviews, QA testing, and project management
- **Post-launch support** — Maintenance, updates, and support are typically available
- **Broader expertise** — Agencies see many different projects and industries, bringing diverse experience to your project

### Cons

- **Higher cost** — Agency rates are higher than freelancer rates due to team overhead
- **Less direct access** — You communicate through a project manager, not directly with the developers
- **May assign junior staff** — Some agencies sell based on senior expertise but assign junior team members to the actual work
- **Less flexible** — Agencies have processes that may feel bureaucratic for small projects

### Red Flags When Hiring Agencies

- No case studies or portfolio with measurable results (not just screenshots)
- Unclear about who will actually do the work (ask for specific team members' names and experience)
- Vague scope or pricing that changes after you commit
- No contract or terms that heavily favor the agency
- No post-launch support plan
- Promises that sound too good to be true (₦150,000 for a full e-commerce site delivered in 3 days)

### How to Choose a Good Agency

1. Review their portfolio for projects in your industry or similar complexity
2. Ask for case studies that show process, challenges, and results
3. Request to speak with past clients
4. Ask specifically who will work on your project and their experience level
5. Review their contract carefully — ensure you own the code, domain, and IP
6. Clarify post-launch support terms before signing
7. Look for agencies that ask detailed questions about your business before quoting

## In-House Teams

### What They Are

Employees who work exclusively for your business. For digital projects, this typically includes designers, frontend developers, backend developers, and sometimes a project manager or product owner.

### When In-House Teams Are the Right Choice

**Digital is core to your business.** If you are a tech company, a SaaS business, or a company where your website/app is your primary product, in-house development makes sense.

**You have ongoing development needs.** If you need continuous feature development, updates, and maintenance over years rather than a one-time project, an in-house team provides continuity.

**You have the budget and management capacity.** A single senior developer in Lagos costs ₦500,000 — ₦1,500,000/month in salary plus benefits, equipment, and management overhead. A full team costs significantly more.

### Pros

- **Full control** — The team works exclusively on your projects, aligned with your priorities
- **Institutional knowledge** — They understand your business, your codebase, and your customers deeply
- **Immediate availability** — No scheduling conflicts with other clients; they work on your projects when you need them
- **Cultural alignment** — Your team understands your company culture and communication style
- **Long-term continuity** — The same people who build your product maintain and improve it over years

### Cons

- **Highest cost** — Salaries, benefits, equipment, office space, training, and management overhead add up significantly
- **Hard to hire** — Finding and retaining good technical talent in Lagos is competitive and time-consuming
- **Limited perspective** — An in-house team works only on your products and may lack exposure to different approaches and industries
- **Capacity constraints** — If you need to scale up quickly, hiring is slow. If you have a quiet period, you are paying for idle capacity
- **Management burden** — Technical teams require technical management, which most non-technical founders cannot provide directly

## Decision Framework

Use this framework to decide:

### Choose a Freelancer If:
- Your project is small and well-defined (under ₦500,000)
- You have a clear specification and can manage the project yourself
- Your timeline is flexible
- You have a low budget
- You do not need ongoing support

### Choose an Agency If:
- Your project is complex or requires multiple skills
- You have a tight timeline and need a team working in parallel
- You want a single point of accountability
- You need ongoing support and maintenance
- Your budget is ₦1M+ and quality/reliability matters more than cost
- You want proven process and established expertise

### Choose an In-House Team If:
- Digital products are your core business
- You have continuous development needs over years
- You have the budget for salaries (₦500K — ₦1.5M/month per person)
- You have technical management capability
- You can afford the time to hire and onboard

## What We Recommend

For most Nigerian businesses building their first or second digital project, the agency model offers the best balance of quality, reliability, and value. You get a team with diverse expertise, established processes, project management, and post-launch support — without the cost and complexity of building an in-house team.

Freelancers are excellent for small, well-defined projects where you can manage the process. If your project is under ₦500,000 and you have the time to manage it, hiring a good freelancer can be cost-effective.

In-house teams make sense only when digital is your primary business and you have the budget and technical leadership to manage them effectively. Most Nigerian businesses should start with freelancers or agencies and hire in-house only when the business justifies it.
