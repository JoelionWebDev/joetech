## Why a Return Policy Matters More Than You Think

Adaeze sells Ankara dresses from her shop in Yaba Market and on Instagram. For months, her biggest problem was not finding customers. It was the message that arrived after every sale: "Sis, if it does not fit, can I return it?" She had no answer written down. Sometimes she said yes, sometimes she said no, depending on her mood and how busy the day was. Customers noticed the inconsistency, and a few stopped buying altogether.

Then she wrote a simple policy, pinned it to her Instagram highlights, and added it to every WhatsApp order confirmation. Sales did not fall. They rose. Customers who had been hesitating now ordered, because they knew exactly what would happen if the dress did not fit. A return policy is not an invitation for people to return your goods. It is a promise that removes fear from buying.

Nigerian online shoppers have good reason to be careful. Many have paid for an item that arrived in the wrong colour, the wrong size, or a quality far below the photo. Some have paid and received nothing at all. When a new customer lands on your page, they are weighing two things: how much they want your product, and how much they trust you. A clear, fair return policy tips that balance in your favour.

## What a Return and Refund Policy Actually Is

A return and refund policy is a short, plain document that answers four questions before a customer has to ask them. Can I return this item? Within how many days? In what condition? And will I get my money back, an exchange, or store credit?

It is not a legal textbook. It does not need long sentences or difficult words. The best policies read like a conversation with a fair shop owner. If a customer can read it in two minutes and understand it fully, it is doing its job. If they need a lawyer to understand it, it is working against you.

Your policy should live in places where customers can find it without asking. Put it on your website, pin it on Instagram and Facebook, save it as a WhatsApp catalogue note, and send it with every order confirmation. When a customer asks about returns, you should be able to send them the link or the saved message in seconds, not type a fresh answer each time.

Think of the policy as part of your product. You would not sell a shoe without telling the buyer the size. Do not sell anything online without telling the buyer what happens if it does not work out. That single habit separates serious sellers from the ones customers describe as "I am not sure about that vendor."

## Start With Your Products, Not With a Template

Before you copy any policy from the internet, look at what you actually sell. A policy that works for a phone accessories seller in Computer Village will not work for a baker in Surulere, and neither will work for a thrift seller in Yaba. Your products decide your rules.

Start by sorting your products into three groups. The first group is items that can be returned safely: clothes, shoes, bags, phone cases, home items, and anything that can be checked, cleaned, and resold. The second group is items that can only be exchanged, not refunded: items you ordered specially for a customer, or items in a size or colour you cannot easily resell. The third group is items that cannot be returned at all: food, drinks, opened cosmetics, underwear, swimwear, and anything made to order with a customer's name on it.

Write the groups down on paper. For example, Tunde sells sneakers from a shop in Lekki Phase 1. His policy is simple: unworn sneakers in the original box can be exchanged within three days; worn sneakers cannot be returned; and limited-edition pairs ordered specially are final sale. Customers accept this because it is clear and because it is written before they pay, not invented after they complain.

Food sellers need a different approach. If you sell chin chin, cakes, or small chops in Lagos, a customer cannot return a half-eaten cake. Your policy should focus on what happens if the order is wrong or damaged on delivery: a replacement, a re-delivery, or a refund of the affected portion. Chiamaka, who runs a small bakery in Ikeja, promises that any cake delivered damaged or with the wrong flavour is replaced free of charge if the customer sends a photo within two hours of delivery. She has replaced three cakes in a year, and each of those customers has ordered again.

## Choose a Return Window That Works in Nigeria

The return window is the number of days a customer has to ask for a return after receiving the item. Big international stores offer 30 days. That rarely works for a small Nigerian business. Your stock moves fast, prices change, and an item returned after a month may already be out of fashion or out of season.

For most products, three to seven days is realistic. Clothes, shoes, and accessories work well with three days for exchange and seven days for a refund where you offer one. Electronics and gadgets work well with 48 hours for a defect check, because a fault usually shows up quickly. Custom and made-to-order items usually carry no return window, but you should still promise to fix genuine mistakes.

Be specific about when the window starts. "Within three days of delivery" is clear. "Within three days" alone is not, because a customer may think it means three days from the day they ordered, while your dispatch rider was still on the road. State the start point, and state whether you count calendar days or working days. In Lagos, where a delivery to Badagry can take a day longer than a delivery to Victoria Island, that detail prevents arguments.

Also decide what happens on weekends and public holidays. If your shop is closed on Sundays, say so. If a customer receives an item on Saturday and your window is three days, tell them whether Sunday counts. Small details like this are where most return disputes begin, and they are the easiest to prevent.

## Decide What Condition Items Must Be In

Every fair policy sets a condition for returns. The standard is simple: the item must be unused, unworn, unwashed, and in its original packaging, with tags attached and any free gifts included. Write this in plain words, and give one example for your main product.

For clothes, say the dress must not have been worn outside, washed, or altered. For shoes, say they must not have scuff marks on the sole. For electronics, say the item must be returned with its charger, box, and receipt. Customers understand rules better when they can picture them. A sentence like "If you have worn the shoe to a party, we cannot accept it back" is clearer than a paragraph about "items in resalable condition."

There is one exception you should always make: faulty items. If a shoe sole peels after one day, or a blender stops working on first use, the condition rule does not apply in the same way. The customer did not damage the product; the product failed them. Your policy should say that faulty or damaged items can be returned even if the packaging is opened, as long as the customer reports the fault quickly and provides a photo or short video. This is the clause that builds the most trust, because it tells customers you stand behind what you sell.

## Exchange, Refund, or Store Credit: Pick Your Promise

You do not have to offer full cash refunds on everything. Many successful Nigerian sellers offer exchanges first, store credit second, and cash refunds only for faulty or wrong items. What matters is that the customer knows the promise before they pay, and that you keep it every time.

An exchange is the easiest option for your cash flow. The customer brings back the size 38 shoe and takes the size 39. No money leaves your account, and the customer still leaves with a product. For fashion sellers, exchanges solve most return requests, because most returns happen when the size or colour is wrong, not when the customer has changed their mind about buying.

Store credit works well when the customer wants something different but you do not have the exact replacement in stock. Give them credit for the value of the returned item, valid for 30 days, and let them choose something else. Put the credit in writing, even if it is only a WhatsApp message with the amount and the expiry date. A customer who feels their money is safe will come back to spend it.

Cash refunds should be reserved for clear cases: the item is faulty, the wrong item was delivered, or the item never arrived. When you refund, state the timeline honestly. A refund to a bank account or through Paystack or Flutterwave can take one to three working days, depending on the bank. Tell the customer the day you sent it, and send them a screenshot of the transfer. That small act of proof ends most refund arguments before they start.

## Who Pays for Return Delivery?

Return delivery is the question that causes the most friction, so answer it directly in your policy. There are three fair options, and you should choose one and stick to it.

The first option is that the customer pays for return delivery, and you pay for sending the replacement. This is the most common choice for small sellers, and customers accept it when the reason for the return is their choice, such as a wrong size. The second option is that you pay both ways when the mistake is yours: you sent the wrong colour, the item is faulty, or the item arrived damaged. The third option is that you cover return delivery for all returns, which is generous but can be expensive if you sell low-priced items.

Write it plainly. For example: "If you are exchanging for a different size, you cover the cost of sending the item back to us, and we cover the cost of sending your replacement. If we sent the wrong item or a faulty item, we cover all delivery costs." A customer reading that knows exactly where they stand.

For Lagos deliveries, use dispatch riders you trust and agree on the return fee in advance. A return from Ajah to Yaba should cost the same as the original delivery, and the rider should confirm receipt of the item before you send the replacement. For deliveries outside Lagos, tell the customer which courier to use and ask them to keep the waybill number. Never ask a customer to send a return by a method you cannot track, and never accept a return without checking the item first.

## How to Handle a Return Step by Step

A good policy is only as good as the process behind it. When a return request comes in, follow the same steps every time, so no customer feels they were treated differently from another.

Step one is to acknowledge the message quickly. Even a short reply helps: "Thank you for letting us know. Please send a photo of the item and your receipt so we can help you." A customer who waits a whole day for a reply will assume you are ignoring them, and that assumption turns a small problem into a public complaint.

Step two is to check the facts. Look at the photo, check the date of delivery, and confirm the item matches your records. If the item is faulty, ask for a short video showing the fault. This is not distrust; it is good record-keeping, and most honest customers are happy to provide it.

Step three is to confirm the solution in writing. Tell the customer what will happen, by when, and what they need to do. For example: "We will exchange your size 38 for size 39. Please send the shoes back with our rider on Thursday, and your replacement will arrive on Friday." Written confirmation prevents the "but you said" arguments that follow verbal promises.

Step four is to inspect the item when it arrives, then act the same day. Do not keep a returned item in a corner for a week while the customer waits. Check it, confirm it is in the promised condition, and send the exchange or refund immediately. Speed is the difference between a customer who says "they handled it well" and one who says "they wasted my time."

## Write the Policy in Plain Language

Now it is time to write the actual document. Keep it to one page if you can, and use short sections with clear headings. Start with a friendly opening, then cover the return window, the condition of items, the options available, who pays for delivery, how refunds are processed, and what cannot be returned. End with how to start a return: a phone number, a WhatsApp link, or an email address.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long should my return window be in Nigeria?

For most products, three to seven days after delivery works well. Three days is enough for clothes and shoes, while seven days suits home items and accessories. Electronics usually need a shorter window of 48 hours for fault checks. Choose a window you can honour, state when it starts, and apply it the same way to every customer.

### Do I have to offer cash refunds?

No, you do not have to offer cash refunds on every return. Many Nigerian sellers offer exchanges and store credit as the standard options, and reserve cash refunds for faulty items, wrong deliveries, or orders that never arrived. What matters is that the customer knows this before paying, and that you follow the policy you published.

### Who should pay for return delivery?

If the return is because the customer changed their mind or picked the wrong size, it is fair for the customer to pay for sending the item back, while you pay for the replacement. If the mistake is yours, such as a wrong item or a faulty product, you should cover all delivery costs. State the rule clearly in your policy so there is no argument.

### Can a customer return food, cosmetics, or customised items?

In most cases, no. Food, drinks, opened cosmetics, underwear, and items made to order with a customer's name cannot be returned for hygiene and resale reasons. Your policy should list these items clearly, and still promise to replace or refund an item that arrives damaged or different from what was ordered.

### What if a customer returns an item after the window has closed?

You can politely refuse, pointing to the written policy, and offer a small goodwill gesture such as a discount on the next order. If the item is faulty and the fault could not have been seen earlier, consider making an exception and note the reason in your records. Consistency matters, so do not refuse one customer and accept another in the same situation.

### Should I ask for a photo before accepting a return?

Yes. Asking for a clear photo, and a short video for electronics, helps you confirm the item, the fault, and the condition before the customer spends money on return delivery. Most honest customers are happy to send one. It also gives you a record if there is ever a disagreement about the state of the item.

## What to Do Next

1. Sort your products into three groups: returnable, exchange-only, and non-returnable, and write the list down today.
2. Choose your return window, your refund options, and your delivery rule, and write them in one page of plain language.
3. Ask a friend or a regular customer to read the draft and explain it back to you, then fix anything they found confusing.
4. Publish the policy on your website, pin it on Instagram and Facebook, and save it as a WhatsApp quick reply.
5. Add a short version of the policy to every order confirmation and receipt, so customers see it before they need it.
6. Train anyone who answers your business phone or works in your shop on the policy, so every customer gets the same answer.
7. Start a simple return record book, and review it at the end of each month for patterns in sizes, faults, or suppliers.
8. Review and update the policy every three months, or whenever you add a new product, a new delivery area, or a new payment method.
