You took a beautiful photo of your product. You sent it to a customer on WhatsApp. And when they opened it, it was blurry, dull, and half the size it should be. Or you recorded a video, sent it, and the customer could barely read the text in it.

That is WhatsApp compression. Every photo and video you send the normal way gets squeezed to save data, and the quality drops. For a casual chat with a friend, that is fine. For a business — product photos, design work, documents, videos a client needs to see clearly — it is a real problem.

The good news is that you can send files on WhatsApp without losing quality, and there are easy options for files too big for WhatsApp altogether. This guide shows you how.

## Why WhatsApp Shrinks Your Files

WhatsApp was built to be fast and light on data. When you send a photo or a video through the gallery, WhatsApp reduces its size and resolution before sending it. A photo that was 5 MB on your phone might arrive as a 200 KB file. A video gets compressed even harder.

This is useful when you are chatting with friends on a slow connection. But when the file itself matters — a product photo a customer wants to see clearly, a design a client must approve, a video with text in it — compression ruins the result. The customer sees a blurry version and thinks your work looks poor, when the real file is fine.

WhatsApp's document limit is generous: you can send any file up to **2 GB** as a document. The trick is knowing which button to press.

## Method 1: Send It as a Document (Best for Quality)

This is the single most important trick in this guide. When you send a photo or video as a **document**, WhatsApp does not compress it at all. The file arrives exactly as it left your phone — same size, same quality, same detail.

Here is how to do it on Android:

1. Open the chat and tap the **paperclip** icon.
2. Choose **Document**, not Gallery.
3. Find the photo or video in your files and select it.
4. Tap **Send**.

On iPhone:

1. Open the chat and tap the **+** button.
2. Choose **Document**.
3. Find the photo or video in your Files app and select it.
4. Tap **Send**.

Two things to know: the file arrives as a downloadable file, not as a picture inside the chat, so the other person has to tap it to open it. And the file must be under 2 GB. For almost every photo and most videos, that is plenty.

Tell the other person what you are doing: "I am sending this as a document so it keeps its full quality — tap it to open it." Otherwise they may wonder why the photo did not appear in the chat.

## Method 2: Use HD Quality (Quick, But Not Perfect)

WhatsApp now offers an **HD** option for photos and videos. When you select a photo to send, tap the **HD** button at the top of the screen before you send it. The photo arrives clearer than the standard version.

HD is better than the normal send, but it is still not the original file. WhatsApp still compresses the photo, just less aggressively. Use HD when you want a quick improvement and the file does not need to be perfect. Use the document method when quality really matters — for a client approving a design, a product photo for a catalogue, or anything that will be printed.

## Method 3: Send a Cloud Link (Best for Big Files)

For files over 2 GB, or for a set of many files, do not send the files themselves. Send a link instead.

Upload the files to Google Drive, Dropbox, or OneDrive, then share the link in the WhatsApp chat. The other person taps the link and downloads the original files at full quality, and nothing is stored on their phone until they choose to download it. Google Drive gives you 15 GB of free space, which is enough for most jobs.

This method is best for:

- **A full set of product photos.** Upload the whole folder to Drive, share one link, and the customer downloads what they need.
- **Long videos.** A 4K video of a wedding or an event is often over 2 GB. A link handles it easily.
- **Folders of documents.** A quotation, a catalogue, and a price list in one folder, shared with one link.

To share a link from Google Drive: open the Drive app, find the file or folder, tap the three dots, tap **Share**, then **Copy link**. Paste that link into the WhatsApp chat. Make sure the link setting allows the other person to view it — if it says "restricted", change it to "anyone with the link can view".

## Method 4: WeTransfer for One-Off Large Files

WeTransfer is a free website for sending large files without an account. Go to wetransfer.com, upload your files — the free tier handles files up to 2 GB — enter the other person's email address, or copy the download link and paste it into WhatsApp. The files stay available for seven days on the free plan.

This is useful when you need to send a large file once and do not want to set up a cloud folder. The other person gets an email or a link, downloads the file, and that is it.

## Method 5: Telegram for Very Large Files

Telegram, the messaging app, allows files up to 2 GB per file for free users, and it does not compress photos and videos sent as files. If you and your customer both use Telegram, you can send large files there without any of WhatsApp's limits. It is not a replacement for WhatsApp — most Nigerian customers are on WhatsApp — but it is a useful backup when a file is too big.

## Which Method Should You Use?

- **One photo or video, quality matters:** send it as a document in WhatsApp.
- **One photo, quick and easy:** use the HD option.
- **A set of photos, a long video, or anything over 2 GB:** upload to Google Drive and send the link.
- **A one-off large file for someone who does not use cloud storage:** use WeTransfer.
- **You and the other person both use Telegram:** send it there as a file.

## Tips for Sending Files Like a Professional

**Check the file size first.** Before you send anything, look at the size. On Android, long-press the file in your file manager. On iPhone, check in the Files app. If it is under 2 GB, the document method works. If it is over, use a link.

**Name your files clearly.** "Product-Photo-Blue-Dress-Front.jpg" is far better than "IMG_4829.jpg". The other person will know what they received without opening it, and they can find it again later.

**Compress a set of files into one ZIP.** If you are sending ten photos, put them in one folder and compress it into a single ZIP file, then send the ZIP as a document. The other person downloads one file instead of ten. Most phones can open a ZIP file without any extra app.

**Warn the other person.** A document arrives as a file to download, not as a picture in the chat. A short message — "Sending the photos as documents so they stay sharp, tap each one to download" — stops confusion.

**Use Wi-Fi for large uploads.** Uploading a 2 GB file on mobile data will eat your bundle. Send large files when you are on Wi-Fi, and let the other person know the file is large so they can download it on Wi-Fi too.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why do my WhatsApp photos look blurry?

Because WhatsApp compresses photos sent through the gallery to save data. The photo is reduced in size and detail before it is sent. To keep the original quality, send the photo as a document instead of from the gallery.

### What is the largest file I can send on WhatsApp?

2 GB per file, when sent as a document. Photos and videos sent the normal way through the gallery are compressed and capped much lower. For anything over 2 GB, send a cloud link instead.

### Does HD quality send the original file?

No. HD is clearer than the standard send, but WhatsApp still compresses the file. Only the document method sends the file exactly as it is, with no compression at all.

### Will sending as a document use more of my data?

Yes, because the file is larger. A photo sent as a document might be 5 MB instead of 200 KB. That is the price of full quality. Send large files on Wi-Fi when you can.

### Can I send a whole folder on WhatsApp?

Not directly. Compress the folder into a single ZIP file first, then send the ZIP as a document. Or upload the folder to Google Drive and share the link. Both work well.
