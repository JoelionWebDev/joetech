Somewhere last month you opened a competitor's page, admired their pricing, and thought, "Why can't we do that?" Then you copied their offer sheet and called it research. You're not alone.

But the brands that grow ten times over in Nigeria don't copy what's visible. They research what's invisible — the funnel, the pricing logic, the message, the gap the competitor is leaving behind. That's what we're building here: a repeatable, ethical eye on your market so you compete with a map instead of a guess.

> Research your market like an investor, not a spy. You're not taking their ball; you're building your own stadium.

---

## Step 1: Define "ethical" with three hard borders

Before a single tab opens, set the rules so you never drift to the wrong side:

1. **Don't lift their words or layout.** Copying content or re-skinning a design is copyright infringement, and in a market as tight as Lagos, everyone spots the copy. It also builds your reputation on sand.
2. **Public only.** Anything you can see without logging in or pretending to be someone else — pages, prices, reviews, posts — is fair game. Anything you must fake a login, a purchase, or a scrape to reach is off-limits.
3. **Benchmark, don't become.** Study the structure: the offer, the outcome, the pacing, the pricing model. Then build something recognisably yours that is better. That's competition, not theft.

> Ethical research feels like watching the match from the stands: you study the play, you don't steal the referee's whistle.

---

## Step 2: Pick five rivals — not twenty

You don't need a folder of fifty competitors. You need five strategic ones, each answering a different question:

- **1 direct local rival** — same city, same offer (e.g., you and the other web designer in Ikeja).
- **1 indirect rival** — same problem, different method (a no-code site builder vs a custom development studio).
- **1 market leader** — the national name everyone cites as the ceiling.
- **1 fast mover** — a newcomer who jumped the ranks in 6–18 months.
- **1 aspiration** — the competitor you'd love to become in two years.

Write all five into one Google Sheet with columns: name, URL, audience, offer, price, channels, content cadence, notes. This sheet is your war room; every future audit feeds back into it.

---

## Step 3: The pricing window — read the market, set your own number

Never copy a competitor's price. A price is a decision, not a fact to borrow. Your job is to understand *why* that number exists:

- What's **included** — logo, revisions, hosting, delivery time, WhatsApp support?
- What's **extra** — which add-ons signal what buyers love?
- Is the price built on **cost-plus** (their expenses + margin), **value** (an outcome they sell), or **fear-of-losing** (matching the market's lowest number)?
- What would a newcomer be stupid to chase and smarter to avoid?

Then decide **your** number with a reason: position higher with a sharper bundle if their price is a race to zero, or take the low-end specialist seat if the market is fragmented. What they charge is not what you must charge — it's only your starting truth.

> A competitor's price is a data point, not a command. You choose the turn.

## Step 4: The SEO autopsy — win what they haven't noticed

Free tools give you x-ray vision over which words your rivals win. Your stack:

- **Google** — type your topic; the results page *is* a ranking report.
- **`site:competitordomain.ng`** — uncovers every indexed page of theirs.
- **Google Keyword Planner** — free volume data per keyword.
- **Localised search** — search "your topic + Nigeria" to see exactly who owns the space.

Record two buckets:

- **Words they rank for with thin content** — you can outwrite them and take that spot.
- **Words they ignore** — long-tails like "cheap web designer in Port Harcourt" or "makeup studio with WhatsApp booking" — your blue ocean.

Each unlocked keyword becomes a blog post, a services page, or a product section. The gaps are the strategy.

---

## Step 5: The content audit — steal the format, not the text

Your rival's content calendar is the most public playbook they own. Track:

- **Formats** — Reels vs carousels, weekly blogs vs daily posts.
- **Winners** — which posts earned comments and shares, and why (the insight, the number, the personal angle).
- **Funnel preview** — which "free" content piece actually feeds their sales.
- **Timing and replies** — when they post and how they engage.

Then build **your** content column with the same rhythm and formats — but your voice, your data, your sharper frame. No paragraphs lifted, ever.

> Don't copy the fish. Learn how that fish gets caught, then go fish somewhere else.

---

## Step 6: The audience sniff — read their reviews like a goldmine

Reviews and comments are the bluntest data available. Sort everything:

- **What customers loved** — "fast delivery!", "best SEO in Abuja", "responded within an hour" — that's the buyer's exact language. Rephrase those outcomes into *your* marketing, in your own sentences.
- **What customers hated** — "slow replies," "extra charges," "took longer than promised" — every negative is a positioning slot. Build your pitch around being the fix.
- **What they wish for** — features nobody offers yet → your differentiator.

The ethical test: you're turning public opinion into a *different*, *better* offer, not a clone.

---

## Step 7: Turn the map into 30 days of action (C.L.E.A.N.)

Distil the sheet into five buckets:

- **C — Confidence:** what they bet on, and where your nerve to compete grows.
- **L — Leaks:** their weaknesses (reviews, gaps, slow service) — your sharpest target.
- **E — Experiences:** their genuine strengths (speed, range, support) — the standard to match or beat.
- **A — Availability:** white spaces (keywords, audiences, angles) that belong to no one yet.
- **N — New ground:** the segment they ignore that you can claim first.

Then pick **only three actions** for the next 30 days:

1. One gap-beating piece — a post or page written for an unlocked word.
2. One service fix — resolve the complaint that's practically waiting to be solved.
3. One new claim — an audience they never spoke to.

Everything else stays in the sheet and sleeps. That's how research stops being a hobby and becomes a weapon.

## Your first 60 minutes of research

1. Build the 5-row sheet for your rivals (10 min).
2. Run one `site:` audit and read one of their top three posts (20 min).
3. Read five reviews per competitor and log three take-aways (20 min).
4. Write your three C.L.E.A.N. actions for the month (10 min).

That's how you go from "their price frightens me" to "I see their stage, and I've chosen a different one."

## FAQ

| Question | Short answer |
| -------- | ------------ |
| **Isn't competitor research just copying?** | No. If you study structure and reinvent your own words, that's learning. If you lift their copy and re-skin their look, that's copying. Research ethically — build your own. |
| **What can and can't I use from a rival?** | Everything public — pages, prices, reviews, posts, ranking data. Anything gated or requiring a fake identity is off-limits. |
| **Do I need paid SEO tools?** | Not to start. Google Trends, Keyword Planner and plain search cover most of it. Buy a tool only when the free route stops answering the question. |
| **How often should I audit competitors?** | A light pass each month, a deep audit every quarter, plus a quick check before launching anything new. |
| **Can AI do my competitor research?** | Yes — ChatGPT or Gemini can summarise pages and draft heuristics. But the final strategic choice — your angle, your nerve — should always stay human. |
