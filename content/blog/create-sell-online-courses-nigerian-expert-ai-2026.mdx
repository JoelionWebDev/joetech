> *Turn the skill you already have into a digital product that sells while you sleep — no fancy studio needed.*

Somewhere in Lagos, a woman who has run a successful food business for eight years is about to upload her first step-by-step "How to Run a Catering Business in Lagos" course to WhatsApp. A young man in Ibadan who fixed laptops for his neighbourhood all through NYSC is pricing his "Phone & Laptop Repair from Zero" training. And a teacher in Abuja who has coached students into JAMB success for a decade is deciding whether to record her first paid lesson.

Each of them already owns the hardest thing in online education: **a skill other people would pay to learn.** What they think they lack is a studio, a platform, and marketing muscle. In 2026, all three of those have been replaced by free AI tools and a smart plan. This guide is that plan — how to choose your topic, design a curriculum with AI, record and edit on a phone, host and price in naira, sell through WhatsApp and Instagram, collect payments with Paystack, and support your students like a pro.

## Why Now Is the Best Time to Be a Nigerian Course Creator

Nigeria has a young population hungry to learn, a smartphone penetration that keeps climbing, and one stubborn truth: **tens of thousands of people search daily for skills education, and the money is moving from classrooms to chat.** Three reasons this works in your favour in 2026:

- **AI has removed the production barrier.** Writing scripts, making slides, creating graphics, and even generating practice questions used to take weeks and money. Free AI handles that scaffolding — you supply the expertise.
- **Payments are finally frictionless.** Paystack and Flutterwave let any small creator collect card or transfer payments in naira without a foreign merchant account.
- **Distribution is built-in.** WhatsApp, Telegram, and Instagram are where your students already spend their day. You do not need an SEO miracle to make your first sales — just a clear offer and a warm list.

## Step 1 — Choose a Course Topic That Actually Sells

The topic decides everything, so choose smart. A course idea is worth selling when it passes this test:

1. **You could teach it without notes.** Your experience is the asset.
2. **People already pay for it.** Can they pay someone to solve this problem today?
3. **The result is clear.** Students finish with a skill, certificate, or transformation they can name.
4. **There is searchable demand.** Nigerians are asking about it — on YouTube, forums like Nairaland, Reddit, or in your own DMs.

If you are stuck, ask the AI to help you find the gap:

> "I am an expert in [your field]. Brainstorm 10 sellable online course topics for Nigerian beginners. For each: the clear result a student would get, who would buy it, and a realistic naira price. Then rank them by demand and competition."

Avoid the trap of "I will teach everything." Beginners teaching beginners to be beginners earns nothing. Pick one painful, specific problem you solve effortlessly.

## Step 2 — Design the Curriculum With AI (Not Without It)

Your curriculum is the skeleton. Have AI draft it, then spend your real expertise filling the bones. The prompt:

> "Design a step-by-step curriculum for a [beginner/intermediate] online course called '[Title]' for Nigerian students. Break it into modules and lessons. Every lesson should end with an action students take. Include practice exercises, a quiz per module, and a final capstone project. Keep language simple and encouraging. Output as a course outline I can follow."

Then do the expert pass: cut anything that is not essential, add your own war stories, local examples (Lagos market prices, Naira budgets, real client scenarios), and the mistakes you made so your students do not. **This pass is why they pay you and not the AI.** For each lesson, draft a script — the AI can write a first version, but read it aloud and rewrite it in your voice. Your real-life examples, not polished prose, are what students remember.

## Step 3 — Record and Edit Without a Studio

Perfectly adequate quality in 2026 = a smartphone, natural light, and a free editing app. The checklist:

- **Sound first:** record in a quiet room, a few feet from your phone, with a keyboard stop. Audio quality matters more than video quality for most courses.
- **Light:** face a window or a simple ₦5,000–₦15,000 ring light. Daylight is the cheapest studio.
- **Record in segments:** film lesson by lesson (2–7 minutes each). Short, focused lessons are easier to edit, re-record, and consume on mobile data — your students are on MTN or Airtel, not fibre.
- **Edit with free tools:** CapCut (mobile) or DaVinci Resolve (desktop) handle cutting pauses, adding captions, and normalising sound. Captions double accessibility and make lessons watchable on silent office breaks.
- **Use AI for extras:** generate slide decks with AI tools, clean up background audio with free AI denoisers, and create a study guide PDF with ChatGPT from your own scripts in minutes.

**Data reality check:** keep each lesson short (5–10 MB range where possible) and offer downloadable versions so students on limited bundles do not burn their data on your course. It is a small kindness that lowers churn dramatically.

## Step 4 — Host Your Content and Take Payments (Naira-Friendly)

You have options, from simplest to most structured:

| Option | Cost | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| WhatsApp/Telegram + Paystack/Flutterwave link | Free (plus payment fees) | First sales, small lists, maximum familiarity |
| Gumroad or Payhip | Free tier + fees | Digital products, simple checkout, affiliate links |
| Coursify/Selar-style Nigerian platforms | Low monthly or small fee | Structured course pages, Nigeria-focused payment |
| Thinkific/Teachable (free/paid) | Free tier or paid | Professional look, drip content, certificates |

**The smart 2026 play:** run your first launch on WhatsApp + a Paystack payment link (fastest to a sale, zero platform learning curve), then move a second course onto a proper platform once you have proof of demand. ## Step 5 — Price Your Course in Naira (Without Guilt)

Fair Nigerian pricing follows your audience and the transformation:

- **Budget-friendly skill course** (basics, no support): ₦5,000–₦15,000.
- **Results-focused course with support and feedback:** ₦20,000–₦60,000.
- **Premium with mentoring/group calls/certification:** ₦60,000–₦200,000.

Use **anchoring and scarcity ethically**: publish a real launch price, then an early-bird discount. Never fake countdown timers — in 2026, savvy Nigerian buyers check screenshots and group chats, and trust is your currency. Offer a bulk/group price for offices, churches, or WhatsApp communities; group sales are a huge Nigerian market.

## Step 6 — Market It Through WhatsApp, Telegram, and Instagram

Your first buyers are people who already trust you. Sequence the launch:

1. **Warm-up (5–7 days before):** share 3 short free tips from your course on your WhatsApp status and Instagram stories. Build anticipation, not spam. Ask "what would you pay to learn this?" and let the answers sharpen your title.
2. **Launch day:** post your offer, price, and a genuine 60-second video of you explaining the change the student gets. Reply to every comment and DM quickly — speed is the conversion weapon.
3. **Follow-up (48 hours):** Facebook "I added a Q&A because three people asked..." — an excellent excuse to both add value and re-contact the curious.
4. **Leverage communities:** offer a student-discount code to WhatsApp community admins and Telegram groups in your niche; ask to share a free preview lesson in exchange.
5. **Capture proof:** collect screenshots of happy students within the first week — testimonials are your next launch's best asset.

Your AI can generate caption ideas, post variations, and a 7-day content calendar; your job is to supply the *human* moments — your voice, face, and story.

## Step 7 — Support Students Like a Pro (Retention = Referrals)

Your finished students advertise your next course for free — if you take care of them:

- **Answer questions within 24 hours** (even a group Q&A weekly counts).
- **Give honest feedback** on student submissions; automation is fine for templates, not for your judgment.
- **Issue completion certificates** (free with Canva) — Nigeria loves certificates.
- **Invite graduates to a private alumni group** and announce your next course there first.

A satisfied student from your first batch is the cheapest, highest-converting marketing you will ever buy.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Teaching to impress instead of to transform.** Focus on the student's outcome, not your knowledge.
- **Perfectionism before completion.** A good course launched beats a perfect course never finished. You can refine in v2.
- **Skipping the human voice.** Students pay for you. Read your own scripts, add your examples — AI drafts, you deliver.
- **Ignoring mobile UX.** Most access is on phones with average data. Short lessons, readable slides.
- **No community.** A course without any feedback loop dies in retention. Add at least a WhatsApp group.
- **Fake scarcity.** Overpromising countdowns or "only 3 seats left" when it is not true destroys long-term trust.
