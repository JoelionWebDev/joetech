If you run a local business in Lagos, your Google Business Profile is the most powerful free marketing tool you have. It determines whether you appear when someone searches for your service in their area. It puts you on Google Maps. It shows your reviews, your photos, your hours, and your contact information directly in search results.

And most Lagos businesses are using it wrong — or not using it at all.

A fully optimized Google Business Profile is the single highest-ROI activity for local SEO. It is free, it takes a few hours to set up properly, and it can drive a steady stream of customers to your business for years.

This guide walks you through exactly how to optimize your Google Business Profile so it works for your Lagos business.

## Why Your Google Business Profile Matters

### The Local Pack

When someone searches for a local service in Lagos — "plumber in Lekki," "makeup artist in VI," "restaurant in Surulere" — Google often shows a map with three business listings at the top of the search results. This is called the Local Pack.

The Local Pack gets the majority of clicks. If your business is not in the Local Pack, you are invisible to most people searching for your service in your area.

### Google Maps Visibility

When people search Google Maps for businesses near them, your profile determines whether you appear. A complete, optimized profile ranks higher than a sparse, unoptimized one.

### Trust and Credibility

A complete Google Business Profile with photos, reviews, and accurate information signals to potential customers that your business is real, established, and trustworthy. An incomplete or unclaimed profile has the opposite effect.

### Free Website Alternative

For very small businesses that cannot afford a website, a well-optimized Google Business Profile serves as a mini-website. Customers can see your photos, read your reviews, find your hours, get directions, and call you — all without visiting a website.

## Step 1: Claim and Verify Your Profile

If you have not claimed your Google Business Profile, start here.

Go to google.com/business and click "Manage now." Search for your business. If it appears, claim it. If it does not, create a new listing.

Verification is typically done by postcard. Google sends a postcard with a verification code to your business address. This can take 5-14 days to arrive in Nigeria. Once you receive it, enter the code in your dashboard.

Some businesses can verify by phone or email. If these options are available, use them — they are faster.

**Important:** Use a physical address where you actually conduct business. P.O. Boxes and virtual offices without physical presence are not accepted. If you are a service-area business (you go to customers rather than them coming to you), you can hide your address and set a service area.

## Step 2: Complete Every Field

Google rewards complete profiles with higher rankings. Fill in every field:

### Business Name

Use your exact real-world business name. Do not add keywords or location descriptors. "Best Restaurant in Lekki — Lagos Delights" violates Google's guidelines and can get your profile suspended. Use "Lagos Delights Restaurant" and let your category and content communicate the rest.

### Category

Choose the most specific category that fits your business. A salon should select "Hair salon" or "Beauty salon," not just "Salon." If you offer multiple services, you can add secondary categories (up to 10).

The primary category is the most important for ranking. Choose carefully.

### Address

Your physical business address. If you serve customers at their location (plumber, electrician, mobile makeup artist), you can set a service area instead of displaying your address.

### Phone Number

Use a local Lagos number with a 070, 080, 081, or 090 prefix. Google uses your area code as a local relevance signal.

### Website

Link to your website. If you do not have one, you can leave this blank, but having a website significantly improves your profile's effectiveness.

### Hours

Set your regular business hours accurately. Update them for public holidays and special occasions. Customers who arrive at a closed business because your hours were wrong will not come back.

### Description

Write a compelling business description (up to 750 characters). Include relevant keywords naturally, but write for humans first. Explain what you do, what makes you different, and what customers can expect.

**Example for a Lagos restaurant:** "Lagos Delights Restaurant serves authentic Nigerian cuisine in the heart of Lekki. From our signature jollof rice to our grilled suya platters, every dish is prepared fresh daily using locally sourced ingredients. Dine in, take away, or order delivery. Open for lunch and dinner, seven days a week."

### Services and Products

Add specific services or products. A beauty salon lists "Bridal Makeup," "Hair Styling," "Manicure & Pedicure," "Facial Treatments." A restaurant lists popular menu items.

### Attributes

Mark relevant attributes. These are specific features customers can filter by:
- "Free Wi-Fi"
- "Outdoor seating"
- "Wheelchair accessible"
- "Offers delivery"
- "Has parking"
- "Accepts credit cards"

### Questions and Answers

Google allows customers to ask questions on your profile. Monitor this section and answer questions promptly. You can also pre-populate it with common questions and answers.

## Step 3: Add High-Quality Photos

Businesses with photos on their Google Business Profile get 42% more requests for directions and 35% more click-throughs to their website.

### Types of Photos to Add

**Exterior photos:** Show your storefront or building so customers can recognize it when they arrive. Multiple angles if possible.

**Interior photos:** Show your space — seating area, reception, treatment rooms, display areas. Help customers imagine themselves in your business.

**Product photos:** Showcase what you sell. For a restaurant, photos of signature dishes. For a salon, photos of completed styles. For a retail store, photos of products.

**Team photos:** Photos of you and your staff. Customers like to know who they will be dealing with.

**Action shots:** Photos of your team serving customers, preparing food, or delivering services.

**Before and after:** For beauty, fitness, cleaning, or renovation businesses, before/after photos are powerful social proof.

### Photo Best Practices

- Use high-resolution images (not blurry or pixelated)
- Upload at least 10-15 photos initially, then add more regularly
- Use real photos, not stock images
- Show diversity in your customers and team
- Add new photos at least once per month
- Respond to Google's prompts to add photos

### Video

Google now supports video uploads on Business Profiles. Short videos (30-60 seconds) showing your business in action can significantly boost engagement. A restaurant might show the kitchen in action. A salon might show a time-lapse of a service.

## Step 4: Get and Manage Reviews

Reviews are the most influential ranking factor for local SEO. They also directly impact whether customers choose your business over a competitor.

### How to Get More Reviews

**Ask at the right time:** Ask for a review immediately after a positive interaction — right after a completed service, a purchase, or a compliment. The experience is fresh and they are more likely to say yes.

**Make it easy:** Send a direct link to your review page. This link format works: https://search.google.com/local/writereview?placeid=YOUR_PLACE_ID. You can find your Place ID using Google's Place ID finder tool.

**Train your staff:** If you have employees, train them to ask for reviews at the right moment. A simple "If you enjoyed your experience, we would love a Google review" works.

**Follow up:** Send a follow-up message after service thanking the customer and gently asking for a review if they were satisfied.

**Put up a sign:** If you have a physical location, put up a sign asking customers to leave a Google review. Include the direct link as a QR code.

### How to Respond to Reviews

**Positive reviews:** Thank the reviewer. Mention something specific from their review to show you actually read it. Keep it warm and genuine.

Response template: "Thank you so much, [Name]! We are thrilled you enjoyed [specific item or service]. Your kind words mean the world to our team. We look forward to serving you again!"

**Negative reviews:** Respond professionally and constructively. Apologize for their experience. Offer to make it right offline. Never argue with a reviewer publicly.

Response template: "Thank you for your feedback, [Name]. We are sorry your experience did not meet your expectations. We take your comments seriously and would like to learn more. Please contact us at [phone or email] so we can make things right."

**Fake or unfair reviews:** Flag them to Google. Do not engage publicly. Google has systems for detecting and removing fake reviews, though the process can be slow.

### Common Review Mistakes

- Buying fake reviews (Google detects and penalizes this)
- Asking only your friends and family (reviews from people who have not actually visited violate guidelines)
- Not responding to reviews at all (signals disengagement)
- Responding defensively to negative reviews (turns off potential customers)
- Offering incentives for reviews (against Google's policies)

## Step 5: Post Regularly

Google Business Profile allows you to publish posts — updates that appear in your profile and in search results. Posting regularly signals to Google that your business is active and engaged.

### Types of Posts

**Offers and promotions:** "20% off all services this weekend only."
**Events:** "Join us for our grand reopening celebration on Saturday."
**New products or services:** "Introducing our new delivery service — order online now!"
**Blog posts or articles:** Share links to your latest blog content.
**General updates:** "Happy New Year from all of us at Lagos Delights!"

### Post Best Practices

- Post at least once per week
- Include a compelling image
- Keep text concise and scannable
- Include a clear call to action (Call Now, Learn More, Get Offer)
- Use relevant keywords naturally
- Track which posts get the most engagement

## Step 6: Monitor Your Insights

Google provides detailed analytics for your Business Profile. Review them regularly:

**How customers find you:** Search (people searching for your category or service) vs Maps (people browsing maps in your area)

**Customer actions:** Website visits, phone calls, direction requests, and messages

**Search queries:** What terms people used to find your business. This is gold for understanding what your customers are searching for.

**Photo views:** How many times your photos were viewed compared to competitors

**Review trends:** Number of reviews received over time and average rating

Use these insights to refine your strategy. If a particular search query is driving traffic, create more content around that topic. If your photo views are low, add more photos.

## Common Google Business Profile Mistakes

### Using a P.O. Box Address

Google requires a physical street address where customers can visit. P.O. Boxes are not accepted. If you work from home but do not want your home address public, set yourself as a service-area business and hide your address.

### Choosing the Wrong Category

Your primary category is critical for ranking. A restaurant that selects "Food & Beverage" instead of "Nigerian Restaurant" will not rank for relevant searches. Choose the most specific category available.

### Keyword Stuffing in Business Name

Adding keywords to your business name ("Best Plumber in Lekki — John's Plumbing") is against Google's guidelines and can result in suspension. Use your real business name and let your content and categories handle keywords.

### Neglecting the Profile After Setup

An optimized profile requires ongoing attention. Post regularly, respond to reviews, add photos, update hours for holidays, and monitor insights. An abandoned profile sends the wrong signal to both Google and potential customers.

### Not Responding to Reviews

Every review deserves a response. Google considers responsiveness when ranking profiles. Responding to reviews also shows potential customers that you care about feedback.

## Advanced Optimization Tips

### Use Posts Strategically

Google Posts are not just for promotions. Use them to target specific keywords. A post titled "Best Jollof Rice in Lekki" can help you rank for that search term.

### Link to Relevant Pages

If you have a website, link each post to the most relevant page — not just your homepage. A post about a specific service should link to that service page.

### Add Products

If you sell products, use the Products section to showcase them with prices, descriptions, and buy buttons. This is especially useful for restaurants, retail stores, and service businesses with packaged offerings.

### Use the Q&A Section Proactively

Google allows you to add questions and answers to your own profile. Add common questions your customers ask (pricing, hours, parking, delivery areas) and answer them thoroughly. This appears in search results and reduces repetitive inquiries.

### Encourage User-Generated Content

When customers post photos of your business on Google, it adds social proof and fresh content to your profile. Encourage customers to share photos.
