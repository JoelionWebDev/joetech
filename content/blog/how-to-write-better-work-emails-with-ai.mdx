You open your inbox and there it is: an email you have been avoiding. A client needs a follow-up. A supplier needs a firm but polite reminder. A quotation needs to go out before the close of business. You know what to say, but finding the right words takes time you do not have.

AI can help. Tools like ChatGPT, Google Gemini, and Microsoft Copilot can draft an email in seconds. The problem is that an untouched AI draft usually reads like a robot wrote it — stiff, wordy, and full of phrases no real person would ever say.

The trick is not to let AI write your emails for you. It is to let AI do the heavy lifting, then make the result sound like you. Here is how.

## What AI Is Good at in Emails

AI is genuinely useful for the parts of email writing that slow most people down.

**Structure.** Give AI a messy set of points and it will arrange them into a clear beginning, middle, and end. That alone saves several minutes of staring at a blank screen.

**Grammar and spelling.** If English is not your first language, or you simply write in a hurry, AI will clean up the grammar and catch the typos before your client does.

**Tone.** Need the same message formal for a bank and friendly for a regular client? AI can shift the tone in seconds, and you pick the version that fits.

**Summarising.** Paste a long thread into AI and ask for a summary, and you will get the key points in a few lines — useful when you need to reply to a conversation that has run for days.

## What AI Gets Wrong

Knowing where AI fails is just as important as knowing where it helps.

**It is generic.** Left alone, AI writes the same email for everyone — the same polite phrases, the same structure, the same safe wording. Your client has probably received a dozen emails that sound exactly like that this week.

**It is too formal.** AI leans towards long, stiff sentences. Real people write shorter sentences and get to the point. A draft that says "We would like to take this opportunity to inform you that your request has been duly noted" could simply say "Noted — I will send the update by Friday."

**It invents details.** If you do not give AI the facts, it may fill in the gaps itself — wrong dates, wrong amounts, wrong names. Never trust an AI draft's details. Always check them against your own records.

**It has no relationship with your client.** AI does not know that this client prefers short messages, or that you have worked together for three years. Only you know that. The human context is your job, not the AI's.

## A Simple Process That Works

Here is a process that takes about five minutes and produces an email that is both fast and human:

### Step 1: Write your points first

Before you open any AI tool, write down what the email must say, in plain language — just bullet points. For example: thank the client for the meeting, confirm the price of ₦350,000, say the website will be ready in three weeks, ask for the logo and photos by Friday. This step puts you in charge: you decide the facts, and AI only helps with the wording.

### Step 2: Ask AI to draft it

Paste your bullet points into the AI tool and ask for a draft, being specific about tone and length. For example: "Turn these points into a short, polite business email, about 120 words, friendly but professional."

### Step 3: Edit it in your voice

This is the step most people skip, and it is the most important one. Read the draft out loud. If a sentence sounds like something you would never say, change it. Cut any phrase that adds nothing, and add one personal line — a reference to your last conversation, or a detail about the client's business. That one line is what makes the email feel human.

### Step 4: Check the facts

Read the draft once more, slowly, checking every date, amount, name, and promise against your own records. AI is confident even when it is wrong, and your name is on the email — so the facts are your responsibility.

### Step 5: Send it yourself

Never let an AI tool send an email without you reading it first. The final read-through is your quality control, and it takes less than a minute.

## Good Prompts to Use

The quality of an AI draft depends on the quality of your instruction. Vague instructions produce vague emails. Here are prompts that work well — adapt them to your situation:

- "Write a short, polite follow-up email to a client who asked for a quotation last week and has not replied. Keep it under 100 words. Friendly tone, no pressure."
- "Rewrite this email to sound more confident and direct, but still polite. Keep all the facts the same."
- "Draft an apology email to a customer whose delivery will be two days late. Be honest, take responsibility, and offer a clear new date."

Notice what these prompts have in common: they state the purpose, the tone, and the length. The more specific you are, the less editing you will need afterwards.

## How to Keep Your Voice

The difference between an AI email and a human email is usually small — a few sentences, a personal detail, a natural rhythm. Here is how to keep yours:

**Read it out loud.** If you stumble over a sentence, or it sounds like a speech, rewrite it the way you would say it to the person face to face.

**Cut the clichés.** Delete phrases like "I hope this email finds you well" and "Please do not hesitate to contact me". Replace them with something real — "Hope the week is going well", "Let me know if you have questions" — or simply nothing at all.

**Add one specific detail.** Mention the client's project by name, or refer to something from your last conversation. One specific sentence does more for trust than ten polite ones.

**Keep sentences short.** AI loves long sentences joined by commas. Break them up — short sentences are easier to read on a phone, and most of your clients will read your email on a phone.

## Email Types AI Handles Well

Some emails are easier for AI than others. These are the ones where it saves you the most time:

- **Follow-ups.** "Just checking in on the quotation I sent last week" — short, polite, and easy to personalise.
- **Quotations and proposals.** Give AI your prices and scope and it will lay them out clearly. You check the numbers.
- **Apologies and delays.** AI finds polite words for an awkward situation. You add the honest reason and the real new date.
- **Meeting requests.** AI can draft a clear request with a time and agenda in seconds.
- **Thank-you messages.** After a meeting or a completed job, AI can help you write a warm thank-you.

For sensitive emails — a dispute, a price increase, bad news about a project — write the first draft yourself. AI can polish the wording afterwards, but the thinking and judgement should be yours.

## Mistakes to Avoid

A few warnings, because AI makes it easy to get careless:

- **Sending without reading.** Never send an AI draft you have not read. It may contain a wrong fact, a wrong name, or a tone that does not fit.
- **Pasting private information.** Do not paste a client's personal details, your bank information, or confidential business data into a free AI tool. Add sensitive details yourself after the draft is written.
- **Letting AI invent facts.** If AI adds a date, a price, or a promise you did not give it, remove it. AI fills gaps with guesses, and those guesses can cost you money or trust.
- **Using the same draft for everyone.** If every client receives an email that looks identical apart from the name, they will notice. A minute of personalisation is worth more than a perfect template.
- **Forgetting the subject line.** AI drafts often come with a boring subject line, or none at all. Write your own: short, specific, and honest about what the email contains.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Will clients know I used AI to write the email?

Not if you edit it well. An AI draft you have read, corrected, and personalised reads like a good email, not like a machine wrote it. Clients notice robotic emails because of the clichés and generic tone — both of which disappear once you edit in your own voice.

### Which AI tool should I use for work emails?

ChatGPT, Google Gemini, and Microsoft Copilot all handle email drafting well, and all have free versions that are enough for most people. Pick the one you already use. The process matters more than the tool.

### Is it safe to paste work details into an AI tool?

Be careful. Do not paste a client's personal information, bank details, passwords, or anything confidential into a free AI tool. Share only the facts needed to draft the email, and add sensitive information yourself afterwards.

### Can AI write a full email for me?

It can write a draft, but it should not write the final email on its own. You are the one who knows the client, the history, and the facts. Use AI for speed and structure, then bring your own judgement and voice to the final version. That combination beats writing alone and beats AI alone.

## Faster Emails, Still Human

AI will not replace the way you communicate with your clients. It will replace the blank screen, the slow start, and the ten minutes spent finding the right words. Use it for that — and keep the thinking, the facts, and the voice for yourself.

Write your points, let AI draft, edit it until it sounds like you, check the facts, and send. Five steps, five minutes, and an email your client will actually want to read.
