## Why Google Maps Matters More Than a Flyer

When someone in Lekki searches for "plumber near me", "best suya in Abuja", or "fashion designer in Ikeja", they rarely scroll through ten websites. They tap the map, look at the businesses listed nearby, check the reviews and photos, and call or message the one that looks trustworthy. If your business is not on that map, you are invisible at the exact moment a customer is ready to buy.

Google Business Profile is the free tool that puts you on Google Maps and in local search results. It is the listing that shows your name, address, phone number, hours, photos, reviews, and directions. For a small business in Nigeria — a salon in Surulere, a restaurant in Wuse 2, a pharmacy in Ibadan, a laundry service in Port Harcourt — it is often the cheapest and most effective marketing you will ever do.

This guide shows you how to create and verify your profile, fill it in properly, use photos and reviews to win trust, and keep it updated so Google keeps showing you to nearby customers.

## What Google Business Profile Is

Google Business Profile, often shortened to GBP, is the free listing Google creates for a business on Search and Maps. When you claim and manage it, you control what customers see: your correct name, your exact location, your phone number and WhatsApp link, your opening hours, your services, and your photos.

A complete profile does three things. First, it helps Google understand what you do and where you are, so it can show you when someone nearby searches for that service. Second, it gives customers the information they need to choose you without visiting your website. Third, it collects reviews, which are often the deciding factor between two similar businesses.

You do not need a website to have a profile, although having one helps. You do not need to pay Google to appear on Maps. The basic listing is free. What it does need is accurate information, regular updates, and real engagement with customers.

## Step 1: Create or Claim Your Profile

Start by searching Google for your business name and location. If a listing already exists — perhaps Google created one automatically, or a customer suggested it — you will see an option to claim it. If nothing appears, you can create a new profile from the Google Business Profile website or app.

You will need a Google account. Use a business email you check daily, ideally the same one you use for other business accounts, so your team can find it later. Enter your exact business name as customers know it. Do not add extra keywords or your location to the name unless that is genuinely part of how you trade. Google may reject a name that looks stuffed with keywords.

Next, choose your business category. This is important. A "Beauty Salon" will show up for different searches than a "Hair Salon" or a "Nail Salon", so pick the category that matches your main service. You can add extra categories later for other services, but start with the one that brings in most of your income.

Then add your location. If customers visit you — a shop, a clinic, an office, a restaurant — enter your full address, including the street number, area, and city. A clear example is "14 Admiralty Way, Lekki Phase 1, Lagos". If you work from home and do not want your home address shown, you can hide it and list the areas you serve instead. If you deliver or visit customers, such as a mobile barber or a cleaning service, choose the service-area option and list the neighbourhoods you cover, like Lekki, Victoria Island, and Ajah.

## Step 2: Verify Your Business

Google needs to confirm that your business is real and that you are authorised to manage it. Verification is usually done by postcard, phone call, text message, email, or video, depending on the business type and location. In Nigeria, the postcard method is common for businesses with a physical address, while phone or email verification may be offered for some service businesses.

If you are asked for a postcard, Google will send a card with a code to your business address. It can take days to arrive, so be patient and do not change your address while you wait. When it arrives, log in to your profile and enter the code exactly as shown. If you are offered phone or video verification, follow the instructions on screen and have your shop signage, business documents, or premises ready to show.

Do not try to skip verification or pay a third party who promises instant verification. A verified profile ranks better, can reply to reviews, and can post updates. An unverified profile is limited and may not show fully on Maps. Complete verification once, properly, and you will not need to repeat it unless you move or change key details.

While you wait for verification, you can still fill in the rest of your profile. Do not leave it empty. Add your phone number, your hours, your services, and your photos so that the moment verification is complete, your listing is ready for customers.

## Step 3: Fill In Every Section Properly

A half-filled profile looks neglected, and Google is less likely to show it. Work through each section carefully, using the same details everywhere else online — on your website, Instagram bio, and WhatsApp Business profile — so customers are never confused.

Add a phone number that someone actually answers during business hours. If you use WhatsApp Business, make sure the number on Google matches the WhatsApp number, so a customer who taps to chat reaches you directly. Add your website address if you have one. If you do not, a link to your Instagram page or a simple landing page is better than no link at all.

Set your opening hours accurately, including any differences for weekends and public holidays. In Nigeria, many businesses close early on Sundays or open later on Saturdays. If your hours on Google say you are open and your shop is shut, you will lose trust and may get a negative review from a customer who travelled to meet you.

Write a short business description in plain language. Say what you do, who you serve, and what makes you a good choice. For example: "We are a family restaurant in Garki, Abuja, serving fresh Nigerian dishes, grilled fish, and small chops for dine-in and takeaway." Avoid vague claims and avoid stuffing the description with keywords. One clear paragraph is enough.

List your services with prices where it makes sense. A salon can list haircuts, braiding, and manicures with starting prices. A mechanic can list oil changes, diagnostics, and brake repairs. Prices help customers decide before they call, and they reduce the number of enquiries that go nowhere.

## Step 4: Add Photos That Make People Visit

Photos are often what makes a customer choose one business over another. Google allows you to add photos of your shop front, your interior, your team at work, your products, and your food or finished work. Add them, and keep adding new ones regularly.

Start with a clear photo of your shop front or signage, so customers can recognise you when they arrive. A customer driving along Allen Avenue in Ikeja should be able to match the photo to the building. Add photos of the inside of your shop, clean and well lit, so people know what to expect. If you sell food, add photos of real dishes you serve. If you are a tailor, add photos of finished outfits, not stock images from the internet.

Use real photos taken with a good phone camera in daylight. Avoid dark, blurry, or sideways images. Do not use photos that belong to another business. Customers notice when the food in the photo does not match what arrives on the table, and that mismatch leads to bad reviews.

Add a short video if you can — a 30-second walkthrough of your shop or a clip of your team at work. Videos help customers feel familiar with your business before they visit, and they make your profile look active and cared for.

## Step 5: Use Posts, Offers, and Updates

Google Business Profile lets you publish short posts that appear on your listing. Use them like a notice board. Announce a new service, a weekend offer, a change in hours for a public holiday, or a new product in stock. A restaurant in Lekki could post its weekend menu. A pharmacy could post that a popular medicine is back in stock. A boutique could post new arrivals.

Keep posts short, clear, and useful. Include a photo, a sentence or two, and a call to action such as "Call now" or "Visit us today". Posts expire after a while, so publish something new at least once a week. A profile with fresh posts looks alive; a profile whose last post was months ago looks closed, even if it is not.

If you run a genuine offer, state the terms clearly. "20% off all braids this Saturday only" is clear. "Huge discount, call for price" is not. Customers in Nigeria are used to checking prices before they commit, and a clear offer will bring more calls than a vague one.

## Step 6: Get Reviews and Reply to Every One

Reviews are the heart of a Google Business Profile. A business with twenty honest, positive reviews will usually win over a similar business with none. Ask happy customers to leave a review, and make it easy for them.

The simplest way is to share your review link. Google gives you a short link you can put on a receipt, a business card, a WhatsApp message, or a poster in your shop. After a good service — a finished haircut, a completed repair, a delivered cake — ask politely: "If you are happy, please leave us a review on Google. It helps our small business a lot." Most satisfied customers will do it if you ask at the right moment.

Never pay for reviews, and never write fake reviews for yourself. Google can detect them, and customers can usually tell. A few honest reviews are worth more than dozens of fake ones. If a customer had a bad experience, do not argue with them online. Reply calmly, apologise for the experience, and invite them to contact you directly so you can fix it. A thoughtful reply to a negative review often impresses future customers more than a perfect five-star record.

Reply to positive reviews too, with a short thank you. "Thank you, Mrs Ade, we are glad you enjoyed the meal. See you again soon." That kind of reply shows that a real person runs the business, and it encourages others to leave a review.

## Step 7: Help Customers Find You on Google Maps

Your profile is only useful if customers can actually reach you. Check your location on Google Maps yourself. Search for your business, tap directions, and see whether the pin is in the right place. If the pin is on the wrong street or in the middle of a junction, request a correction in your profile. A wrong pin is one of the most common reasons customers give up and go elsewhere.

Add landmarks in your description or in your replies to customers. In Lagos, directions often rely on landmarks rather than house numbers: "opposite the filling station on Chevron Drive" or "two streets after the Ikate market". Use those landmarks when you give directions on WhatsApp or on the phone, and make sure your map pin matches them.

If your business is inside a plaza, a mall, or an estate, add that detail to your address or description. "Shop 12, Palms Shopping Mall, Lekki" is far clearer than a street name alone. Customers who know the mall will find you faster, and Google will show you more accurately for searches in that area.

Encourage customers to save your location and to use the "Call" or "Directions" buttons on your profile. Those taps are signals to Google that your listing is useful, and they make it more likely that Google will keep showing you to other nearby searchers.

## Step 8: Keep Your Information Consistent Everywhere

Google checks whether your business name, address, and phone number match across the internet. If your website says one phone number, your Instagram bio says another, and your Google profile says a third, Google becomes less confident about which details are correct, and customers become confused.

Pick one version of your name, address, and phone number, and use it everywhere. If you move, change your address on Google first, then update your website, social media, and WhatsApp Business profile the same day. If you change your phone number, update it everywhere at once.

List your business in a few trusted Nigerian directories as well, such as your local business association or a reputable online directory, using the same details. You do not need to be listed everywhere. A few consistent listings are better than dozens of outdated ones.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

The most common mistake is creating a profile and then forgetting it. Hours become outdated, photos become old, and reviews go unanswered. Set a reminder to check your profile once a week, even if it is only for ten minutes.

Another mistake is using the wrong category or a misleading business name. If you are a bakery, do not list yourself as a restaurant just to appear in more searches. You will attract the wrong customers and disappoint them when they arrive.

Do not ignore negative reviews. A single unanswered complaint can sit at the top of your profile for months and put off new customers. Reply quickly, politely, and honestly. Do not use your profile to argue, advertise other businesses, or post personal content. Keep it focused on helping customers find you, trust you, and visit you.

Finally, do not pay someone who promises to put you at the top of Google Maps overnight. There is no shortcut. A complete profile, real photos, honest reviews, and regular updates are what move you up over time.

## Your Google Maps Checklist

Create or claim your profile, verify it, and fill in every section. Add your correct phone number, hours, services, and a clear description. Upload real photos of your shop, your work, and your products. Check your map pin and correct it if it is wrong.

Share your review link with happy customers and reply to every review you receive. Publish a short post at least once a week. Check your profile weekly for new questions, reviews, and messages, and update your hours before public holidays.

## Final Thoughts

Google Maps and Google Business Profile are free, and they work while you sleep. A customer searching at 9pm for a service they need tomorrow morning can find you, see your reviews, and save your number — all without you spending a naira on advertising.

Start today. Claim your profile, verify it, add your photos, and ask your next three happy customers for a review. Small, steady effort on your listing will bring you customers from your own neighbourhood first, and then from across your city. That is how a small Nigerian business gets found, trusted, and chosen.
