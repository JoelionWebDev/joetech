You have probably seen it happen to a business you follow. They announce a giveaway, the comments explode, their follower count jumps by hundreds in a day, and everyone celebrates. Then a week later, nothing has changed: no new orders, no new enquiries, just a follower list full of people who only came for the free item. The giveaway worked as a show. It failed as a business decision.

A good Instagram giveaway is not about giving something away. It is about using a prize to attract the right people, get them to notice what you sell, and turn a few of them into paying customers. This guide walks you through each step, with costs in naira, so you can run a giveaway that actually pays for itself.

## Start With a Goal, Not a Prize

Before you pick a prize or write a caption, decide what you want the giveaway to achieve. "More followers" is not a goal. It is a number that looks good and means very little on its own. A real goal sounds like this: get 50 new WhatsApp enquiries from people in Lagos who need what I sell, or sell 20 pieces of a new product in the week after the giveaway ends.

Your goal decides everything else. If your goal is sales, your prize should be something your ideal customer wants, not something everyone wants. If your goal is local awareness, your entry rules should favour people in your city. Write your goal down in one sentence, and check every later decision against it.

Also decide how you will measure success. Count the things that matter: how many entrants messaged you on WhatsApp, how many visited your shop or website, and how many bought something within two weeks of the giveaway ending.

## Choose a Prize Your Real Customers Want

The prize is the most important decision in the whole giveaway, and it is where most small businesses go wrong. The common mistake is to offer something everyone wants — a brand-new phone, a large cash prize, or a popular gadget. Those prizes attract thousands of entries, but they attract the wrong people. Someone who enters to win a phone does not care about your hair products, your food, or your fashion items.

Choose a prize that only your ideal customer would want. If you sell skincare products, give away a skincare hamper worth ₦50,000, not a phone. If you run a restaurant, give away a dinner for two at your restaurant, not cash. If you sell shoes, give away a pair of your best-selling shoes. The right prize does two jobs at once: it excites the people who might actually buy from you, and it quietly ignores everyone else. A smaller, well-targeted entry list is worth far more than a huge list of strangers.

Keep the prize value sensible: between ₦20,000 and ₦100,000 is a good guide, depending on the size of your business and the price of what you sell. The prize should be valuable enough to feel exciting, but not so expensive that you need a huge number of sales to recover the cost. Remember the prize is not your only cost: add design, ads and delivery before you commit.

Offering your own product or service as the prize is usually the smartest choice. It costs you its wholesale or production cost rather than its retail price, and it puts your product directly into the hands of someone who will use it, photograph it, and tell their friends about it.

## Set Entry Rules That Filter Out Freebie Hunters

Your entry rules decide who enters and what they do for you. The classic rules — follow the page, like the post, tag two friends, and share to your story — are popular for a reason, but each rule should earn its place. Every rule you add is a small barrier, and barriers cost you entries, so keep the rules to three or four at most.

A strong set of rules looks like this. First, follow your page, so entrants stay connected after the giveaway ends. Second, like the post and comment with the answer to a simple question about your product, such as which of your products they would love to try. A question like that makes people think about your products instead of typing "done" or tagging random accounts. Third, tag two friends who would genuinely like your products. Fourth, share the post to their story and tag your page, so the giveaway travels beyond your own followers.

Be careful with rules that invite cheating. If you allow unlimited entries by tagging more friends, people will tag dozens of accounts, including celebrities and accounts that do not exist, just to increase their chances. Limit entries to one per person, or allow a small number of extra entries for one specific action, such as sharing to a story. State the limit, the closing date and time, and who can enter in the caption, so nobody can argue about it later.

One rule many Nigerian businesses forget is the delivery rule. State upfront whether the winner pays for delivery or you cover it, and whether the prize can only be collected in your shop or delivered within Lagos or Abuja.

## Write a Caption That Sells While It Announces

Your giveaway caption is not just an announcement. It is the only sales message most entrants will ever read from you, so it should do more than list the rules. Start with the prize and why it is worth wanting. Instead of writing "We are giving away a hamper", write what is inside the hamper, what it is worth in naira, and who it is perfect for. People enter giveaways because they can picture themselves enjoying the prize. Help them picture it.

Then explain the rules in short numbered steps, one action per line, so nobody has to read a long paragraph to understand how to enter. After the rules, add one or two lines about your business: what you sell, where you are located, and how people can order even if they do not win. This is the part most businesses skip, and it is the part that turns entrants into customers. Someone who does not win the hamper might still buy one if your caption tells them the price and how to order.

End the caption with the closing date, the date you will announce the winner, and how the winner will be chosen. Transparency builds trust. If you say the winner will be picked at random, say how — a random picker tool, a live draw on Instagram, or a number drawn from the comments. If the best answer wins, say so, and say who will judge it. Never leave the selection method vague.

Use a clear photo of the actual prize for the post, with only the prize, its value and the closing date as text on the image. Put everything else in the caption.

## Promote the Giveaway Beyond Your Own Page

Posting the giveaway and hoping people find it is not a plan. Instagram shows your post to only a fraction of your followers, and a new giveaway from a small page will not reach far on its own. You need to push it. The cheapest way is to ask your existing customers and friends to share it. Send the post to your loyal customers on WhatsApp and ask them to share it to their status. People trust recommendations from people they know.

Consider spending a small amount on Instagram ads to promote the giveaway post. Even ₦5,000 to ₦15,000, targeted at people in your city who are interested in what you sell, can multiply your reach. Set the ad to run for three or four days, and target it narrowly: your city, the right age range, and interests that match your products.

Timing matters too. Run the giveaway for five to seven days. Shorter than that, and not enough people will see it. Longer than that, and people forget about it or lose interest before it ends. Start on a Friday or Saturday, when people have time to scroll.

Post reminders while the giveaway is running, but do not spam your page with the same post every day. Two or three reminders are enough: one in the middle, and one on the last day. Use Instagram Stories for reminders, with a countdown sticker to the closing time. Stories disappear after a day, so they keep your page clean, and the countdown creates a gentle sense of urgency.

## Pick and Announce the Winner Transparently

How you pick and announce the winner is where trust is won or lost. Many small businesses damage their reputation here, not because they cheated, but because they were not transparent enough for people to believe they did not. Pick the winner exactly the way you said you would in the caption. If you promised a random draw, use a random comment picker tool and record your screen while you do it. If you promised the best answer wins, announce who judged it and why that answer won. Keep the evidence. A short screen recording of the draw, posted to your Stories, ends arguments before they start.

Announce the winner on the day you promised, and do it publicly. Post the winner's name or username on your feed or your Stories, and tag them so people can check that they are a real person. Give them a clear deadline — for example, 48 hours — to respond and claim it. If they do not respond, say in advance what will happen: you will pick another winner. State this rule in the original caption, so nobody can call it unfair when you use it.

Be prepared for complaints. Some entrants will say the draw was rigged, especially if they entered many times and still lost. Stay calm, polite, and factual. Point them to the screen recording of the draw, and thank them for entering.

If the winner is in your city, invite them to collect the prize from your shop and take a photo with them. That photo is gold. Post it, with their permission, and tag them. It proves the giveaway was real, it shows a happy customer holding your product, and it gives your page fresh content that advertises your business without feeling like an advert.

## Follow Up With Everyone, Not Just the Winner

The biggest mistake after a giveaway is to forget about everyone who did not win. Those people are your real prize. They followed your page, they looked at your products, and they told you what they wanted by entering. If you do nothing with them, they will drift away, and your giveaway will have bought you a week of attention and nothing else. If you follow up well, a good share of them will become customers.

Start the follow-up within 48 hours of announcing the winner, while the giveaway is still fresh in people's minds. Message the entrants, or at least the people who commented with real answers, thanking them for entering and offering them something for taking part. The most effective offer is a small discount or bonus that is only available for a short time — for example, 10% off their first order in the next seven days, or free delivery on orders above ₦20,000 this week.

WhatsApp is the best place for this follow-up in Nigeria, because it is where your customers already talk to you and where orders actually happen. If entrants gave you their WhatsApp number, or messaged you during the giveaway, send them a short, friendly message with the offer and a prompt to order. Keep it personal, not a broadcast that looks like spam.

Finally, look at what the giveaway taught you. Which prize got the most entries? Which entry rule brought you the most WhatsApp messages? How many entrants actually bought something, and what did they buy? Write these answers down. They are the plan for your next giveaway.

## What a Good Giveaway Budget Looks Like

A giveaway does not need to be expensive, but it is not free either, and you should know the full cost before you start. The prize itself, if it is your own product, may cost you ₦15,000 to ₦50,000 at production or wholesale cost. A graphic for the post, if you hire a designer, may cost ₦5,000 to ₦15,000, or nothing if you design it yourself. Promoting the post with Instagram ads may cost ₦5,000 to ₦15,000. Delivering the prize to the winner may cost ₦2,000 to ₦5,000 within Lagos, or more if the winner is in another state. Altogether, a sensible total is between ₦30,000 and ₦80,000 for a giveaway that can bring you dozens of genuine leads and several paying customers.

Compare that cost with what you earn back. If the follow-up offer brings you 10 new customers, and each one spends an average of ₦15,000, you have earned ₦150,000 from a giveaway that cost you ₦50,000. That is a good return. Budget for the follow-up too — not in naira, but in time. Set aside two or three hours after the giveaway ends to message entrants, answer questions, and take orders. That time is where the profit is.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long should an Instagram giveaway run?

Five to seven days is the sweet spot. Shorter than five days, and too few people will see it. Longer than seven days, and people lose interest or forget about it.

### What is the best prize for a small business giveaway?

Your own product or service, chosen so that only your ideal customer would want it. A skincare brand should give away a skincare hamper, a restaurant should give away a dinner for two, and a fashion brand should give away one of its best-selling items. Prizes like phones and cash attract huge numbers of people who will never buy from you.

### How do I stop people from unfollowing me after the giveaway?

You cannot stop it completely, but you can reduce it sharply. Choose a prize your real customers want, so the people who follow you are people who like what you sell. Follow up with entrants quickly, offer them a real reason to stay, and keep posting useful content after the giveaway ends.

### Should I pay for Instagram ads to promote my giveaway?

A small ad budget of ₦5,000 to ₦15,000 can help a lot, if you target it narrowly at people in your city who are interested in what you sell. A broad ad wastes money on people who will never buy from you.

### What should I do if the winner does not respond?

Give the winner a clear deadline, such as 48 hours, to respond and claim the prize, and state this rule in your original caption. If they do not respond within that time, pick another winner using the same method you used the first time, and announce the new winner publicly.

### Can a giveaway really bring me paying customers?

Yes, but only if you follow up. The giveaway itself brings you attention. The follow-up — a thank-you message, a time-limited discount, and an easy way to order on WhatsApp — is what turns that attention into sales. Businesses that skip the follow-up get followers. Businesses that do it well get customers.

## What to Do Next

First, write down your giveaway goal in one sentence, and pick a prize that only your ideal customer would want. Keep the prize budget between ₦20,000 and ₦100,000, and work out the full cost — prize, design, ads, and delivery — before you commit to anything.

Second, write your caption with clear numbered rules, the closing date, the delivery terms, and how the winner will be chosen. Photograph the prize properly, schedule the post for a Friday or Saturday, and plan two or three Story reminders, including one on the last day with a countdown sticker.

Third, plan your follow-up before the giveaway even starts. Prepare a short WhatsApp message for everyone who enters, with a time-limited discount or bonus offer, and set aside two or three hours after the winner is announced to send those messages, answer questions, and take orders. That follow-up is where your giveaway turns into customers.
