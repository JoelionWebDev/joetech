Your customers see your flyer before they ever meet you. In Nigeria, that first meeting usually happens on a phone screen — inside a WhatsApp status, an Instagram story, or a group chat forwarded three times before breakfast. A flyer that looks rushed tells people your business is rushed too, even when it is not. The good news is that you no longer need a laptop, a design course, or a big budget to make something that looks professional. The phone in your hand is enough, if you know how to use it well.

Many small business owners in Lagos, Abuja, Port Harcourt and smaller towns already run their whole business from a phone. You can create a promo flyer on Friday morning, share it by noon, and start receiving orders before evening. This guide walks you through the whole process: which free apps work best, how to plan a flyer before you open any app, how to choose colours and fonts people can read, how to add your own photos, and how to share your flyer so it still looks sharp. By the end, you will be able to design a flyer for a promo, a new product, or an event — and do it again next week without starting from scratch.

## Start With the Message, Not the App

The biggest mistake people make is opening a design app before they know what the flyer should say. A flyer is not decoration. It is a message with a job to do. Before you touch your phone, write down the answers to four questions. What are you announcing? Who is it for? What should they do next? And when does the offer end?

Take a hair vendor in Lekki running a weekend sale. The announcement is the sale. The audience is women in Lagos who buy hair online. The action is to send a WhatsApp message to order. The deadline is Sunday night. Once those four answers are clear, the flyer almost designs itself. The headline becomes the sale, the photo becomes the hair, and the bottom carries the WhatsApp number and the deadline.

If you cannot answer those questions, your flyer will try to say everything and end up saying nothing. A flyer listing ten products, three phone numbers, and a motto in one small square is confusing, and people will scroll past it in two seconds. One flyer, one message, one action — if you have three things to announce, make three flyers over three days.

Write your words before you design. Keep the headline short — five to eight words at most. "Weekend Bone Straight Sale — 20% Off" works. A long sentence about amazing quality hair at affordable prices does not, because nobody will finish reading it. Your headline should be readable from arm's length, even on a small phone screen. The details — prices, sizes, delivery areas — belong in smaller text below, where interested people will look for them.

## Choose the Right App and the Right Size

You do not need five apps. You need one good one that you learn properly. For most Nigerian business owners, Canva is the best place to start. It is free, it works on Android and iPhone, and it has thousands of ready-made flyer templates you can edit. You can change the words, swap the photo, change the colours to match your brand, and export the result in a few minutes. Canva also lets you save your brand colours and your logo, so your second flyer is faster than your first.

Adobe Express is another solid free option, similar to Canva, with templates and easy text tools. If you prefer working with your own photos and want more control over text effects, PixelLab is popular among Nigerian designers and works well even on phones with limited storage. Before you settle on an app, check two practical things: how much storage it needs, and whether it works well on your network. Most design apps need the internet to load templates and save your work, so if your MTN or Airtel connection is slow, load templates while the connection is good. A flyer that fails to save is a flyer you have to design twice.

Size matters just as much as the app. A square flyer, 1080 by 1080 pixels, is the safest all-round choice for WhatsApp status, Instagram feed posts, Facebook, and group chats. A portrait flyer, 1080 by 1350 pixels, fills more of the phone screen on status and Instagram, which makes it harder to ignore. If you plan to print the flyer, use A5 or A4 size in your app, and remember that what looks bright on your phone screen may print slightly darker, so keep your text large and your colours strong. In Canva, search for "Instagram post" and the app sets the square size for you. Always leave a margin: keep your important text and your logo at least a finger's width away from the edges, because WhatsApp and Instagram sometimes crop the edges of an image, and a phone number that is half cut off is useless.

## Use Colours and Fonts People Can Actually Read

Colour is what makes people stop scrolling. It is also what makes a flyer unreadable when it is done badly. The rule is simple: use two or three colours at most, and make sure your text stands out clearly against the background. Dark text on a light background, or white text on a dark background, is almost always safe. Yellow text on a white background, or light grey text on light pink, is almost always a mistake.

Choose colours that fit your business — warm reds for food, soft pinks or gold for skincare, blue and black for a repair shop — and use the same colours on every flyer. Over time, your customers will start recognising your flyers before they even read the words. That recognition is the beginning of a brand.

Fonts matter just as much. Use one font for your headline and one for your body text — two at most. Your headline font should be bold and easy to read from a distance. Avoid fancy script fonts for important information like prices and phone numbers. A beautiful font that nobody can read is a failed font. Avoid using all capital letters for long sentences too; capitals are fine for a short headline, but a whole paragraph in capitals is hard to read and feels like shouting. Test your flyer the way your customer will see it: export it, hold the phone at arm's length, and check whether you can read the headline in two seconds. Then look at it as a small thumbnail, the way it appears in a WhatsApp chat list. If the main message is still clear, your design works.

## Add Photos That Sell the Product

People buy with their eyes first, especially on WhatsApp and Instagram. A flyer with a clear, bright photo of the real product will always outperform a flyer with a stock photo or no photo at all. Take your photos in daylight, near a window or outside, and keep the background clean. A plain wall, a neat table, or a simple cloth background is better than a cluttered room. Wipe your camera lens before you shoot — a smudged lens is the reason many phone photos look dull.

Use your own photos whenever you can. Customers in Nigeria have become very good at spotting a photo taken from the internet, and when they do, they wonder whether the product you will deliver looks anything like the picture. Your own photo builds trust because it is honest. If you sell food, photograph the actual food you cooked. If you sell shoes, photograph the actual pair in your shop.

When you add a photo to your flyer, do not stretch it. Stretching makes people and products look distorted, and customers notice. Crop the photo to fit the space instead. Most design apps let you drag the corners to crop without stretching. Keep the most important part — the food, the face, the product — in the centre, and cut away the empty edges. If you must use a photo from the internet, choose one that genuinely looks like what you sell — the gap between the picture and the reality is where disappointed customers come from.

## Design It Step by Step, Then Write Words That Make People Act

Here is a workflow you can follow every time, using Canva as the example. The same steps apply in other apps, with slightly different buttons. Open Canva, tap the plus button, and choose Instagram Post for a square flyer. Search the templates for your type of business — type "food sale", "fashion sale", or "church programme" and browse what appears. Pick a template that is clean and close to what you want, not one that is already crowded.

First, change the headline. Tap the main text, delete it, and type your own short headline, bold and large enough to fill the top third of the flyer. Next, replace the photo with your own, adjusting it so the product is centred. Then change the colours to your brand colours — in Canva you can save them once and reuse them. After that, edit the smaller text: the price, the sizes, the delivery areas, and the deadline, keeping each detail on its own short line. Then add your contact details at the bottom — your WhatsApp number and your Instagram handle — in a size people can read without zooming. When everything looks right, tap Share, then Download, and choose PNG for the sharpest result. Save the design in the app too, so next week you can reopen it, change the price and date, and export a new flyer in five minutes.

A flyer without a clear next step is just a picture. Every business flyer needs a call to action — a simple instruction telling the reader exactly what to do. "Send us a WhatsApp message to order" is a call to action. "Call 0803 123 4567 to book" is one. The best calls to action are specific and easy. Include your WhatsApp number in full, with the Nigerian country code if you serve customers outside your city. If you use WhatsApp Business, you can add a short link like wa.me/2348031234567, which opens a chat directly. If you also want a scannable code for that link on printed flyers, you can make one with Joetech's free QR code generator at https://joetech360.ng/tools/qr-code-generator. Add a deadline when there is one — "Offer ends Sunday" creates urgency — but only use a real deadline. Customers remember when a business says "last day" every week for a month.

Keep your prices honest and clear. If the price is ₦15,000, write ₦15,000, not "DM for price" unless you have a real reason to hide it. Many customers scroll past "DM for price" because they assume the price will change depending on who is asking. Showing the price attracts people who can afford it, which saves you hours of chatting with people who were never going to buy.

## Share It Well and Avoid the Amateur Mistakes

A beautiful flyer that nobody sees is wasted work. In Nigeria, WhatsApp is usually the most powerful place to share a business flyer. Post it on your WhatsApp status in the morning, when people are checking their phones, and again in the evening. Send it to your broadcast list, not as a group message, so each customer receives it personally. If you have a WhatsApp Business catalogue, update the product there too, so the flyer and the catalogue tell the same story. Instagram is the second home for your flyer: post it as a feed post with a short caption that repeats the offer, and share it to your stories as well. Use a few relevant hashtags — your location and your product — but do not fill the caption with thirty hashtags. Five to ten honest ones are enough.

Do not share the same flyer in ten WhatsApp groups at the same time. Group admins may remove it, members may report it as spam, and WhatsApp may temporarily limit your account if you send the same image too quickly to too many people. Share it in groups where you are a known, welcome member, and where the group rules allow business posts. Timing matters too: Friday and Saturday mornings suit weekend promos, while weekday evenings suit services. Test different times and repeat what gets the most replies.

The fastest way to improve your flyers is to stop making the same mistakes most phone designers make. The first is too much text: a flyer is not a price list, so pick the three products that matter for this offer and let customers ask about the rest on WhatsApp. The second is low-quality images — a blurry photo or a screenshot of another flyer makes your whole business look careless. If a photo is blurry, take a new one. The third is clashing colours and too many fonts: three colours and two fonts are enough. The fourth is forgetting the contact details, or making them so small nobody can read them. Your phone number is the most important line after the headline. The fifth is copying another business's flyer word for word, including their phone number or prices by accident. Before you share any flyer, read every word on it slowly. Check the number, the price, the date, and the spelling of your business name. One wrong digit in a phone number means your orders go to a stranger.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which free app is best for designing a flyer on a phone in Nigeria?

Canva is the best starting point for most people because it is free, has ready-made templates, and works on Android and iPhone. Adobe Express is a good alternative. PixelLab is popular if you want more control over text effects. The best app is the one you will actually learn — pick one, practise with it for a week, and judge it by whether your flyers keep getting better.

### How do I make my flyer look professional and not amateur?

Keep it simple. Use one clear headline, one good photo, two or three colours, and no more than two fonts. Leave empty space around your text, use your real product photos, check your spelling and your phone number before you share, and export at the highest quality. Professional does not mean complicated. It means clean, readable, and consistent.

### What size should my flyer be for WhatsApp status?

Use a square size of 1080 by 1080 pixels, or a portrait size of 1080 by 1350 pixels. Square works everywhere — status, feed posts, and group chats. Portrait fills more of the screen on status and Instagram. Avoid a wide landscape shape for WhatsApp status, because it will appear small with empty space above and below it.

### Can I design a flyer without using my data?

Most design apps need the internet to load templates and save your work, though apps like PixelLab work largely offline once installed. Design on Wi-Fi when you can, or buy a data bundle before you start — an unsaved design is a wasted design.

### How often should I post a new flyer for my business?

Post a new flyer when you have something real to announce — a new product, a price change, a promo, an event, or a restock. For most small businesses, one or two flyers a week is enough. Posting the same flyer every day will annoy your audience and make them mute your status. A fresh flyer with a real reason behind it will always perform better than the same old flyer shared again and again.

## What to Do Next

First, pick one app — Canva is the safest choice — install it today, and design one flyer for something real in your business, even if it is just a "We are open" flyer with your products and your WhatsApp number.

Second, take five good photos of your best-selling product in daylight this week, save them in a "Business Photos" folder, and use them in your next three flyers. Good photos are the biggest free upgrade most Nigerian business flyers need.

Third, save your finished flyer as a template inside your app, share it on your WhatsApp status and your Instagram, and write down how many people reply in the first day. That number is your baseline. Next time, change one thing — a stronger headline, a clearer price, or a better photo — and see if the replies go up.
