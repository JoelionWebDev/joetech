If you run a business in Nigeria and you still do not have a website, you are losing customers every single day. Not because your product is bad, but because people cannot find you when they search, cannot check your prices when they are ready to buy, and cannot trust a business they cannot verify online.

Think about how you shop. When someone recommends a tailor in Yaba, a caterer in Lekki, or a phone accessories shop in Computer Village, what is the first thing you do? You search for the name on Google or Instagram. If you find a proper website with clear photos, prices, a phone number and a location, you feel confident. If you find nothing, or only a half-empty Instagram page last updated six months ago, you move on to the next seller.

A website is your shop that never closes. It works while you sleep, while NEPA takes light, while you are stuck in Lagos traffic on the Third Mainland Bridge. It answers the same questions again and again — what do you sell, how much does it cost, where are you located, how can I pay — so you do not have to repeat yourself on WhatsApp fifty times a day.

The good news is that you do not need to learn coding, and you do not need to pay hundreds of thousands of naira to a developer for a simple site. Modern no-code tools let you build a clean, professional website in a weekend, using drag and drop. This guide shows you exactly how, step by step, with Nigerian realities in mind: naira payments, WhatsApp orders, dispatch riders, generator costs, and customers who want to see a price before they chat you up.

## Why Your Business Needs a Website Now

Many Nigerian small business owners rely only on Instagram and WhatsApp. Those platforms are useful, but they are not yours. Instagram can restrict your account, WhatsApp can ban a number, and an algorithm change can hide your posts from the very people who follow you. Your website is the one place online that you fully control.

A website also builds trust. A customer in Abuja who has never met you is more likely to pay ₦45,000 for a bag from a seller with a proper website, clear return policy and a verifiable address than from a page with no website and only a personal bank account. The website does not make you honest — but it makes your honesty visible.

Search is another reason. Every day, Nigerians type things into Google like "best cake baker in Ikeja", "affordable Ankara tailor in Surulere", "solar installer in Lekki", and "where to buy human hair in Lagos". If you have no website, you cannot appear in those results. Your competitor with a simple one-page site can, and will take that customer.

You do not need a big website. For most small businesses, five pages are enough to start: Home, About, Products or Services, Contact, and a simple way to order.

## What You Need Before You Start Building

Do not open a website builder yet. Spend one hour preparing your content first. Building without content is like opening a shop with empty shelves — you will stall, get frustrated, and abandon the project.

First, write down what your business does in one clear sentence. Not "we offer quality services", but something specific: "We make ready-to-wear Ankara dresses for working women in Lagos, from size 8 to size 20, starting at ₦35,000." A customer should understand your offer in five seconds.

Second, gather good photos. Use your phone in daylight, near a window or outside, with a plain background. Take photos of your real products, your real shop, and yourself at work. Customers in Nigeria are rightly careful about online scams, and real photos build far more trust than stock images of strangers. Take at least ten good photos: your best-selling products, your shop front, your team, and a photo of you.

Third, decide your prices and write them down. Many Nigerian sellers hide prices because they fear competitors or want to force a chat. In practice, hiding prices loses more sales than it gains. Customers who cannot see a price assume it is expensive or that you will charge them based on how they look. Put at least a starting price on your site: "Cakes from ₦25,000", "Braids from ₦15,000", "Delivery within Lagos from ₦2,500".

Fourth, sort out your contact details. You need a business phone number on WhatsApp, an email address, and your address or area. If you work from home and do not want to publish your house address, write your area and a landmark: "Ikeja, near Allen Avenue" or "Lekki Phase 1, off Admiralty Way". Add your opening hours too.

Fifth, decide how customers will pay and receive their goods. Will you accept bank transfer, Paystack, or Flutterwave? Do you deliver within Lagos only, or nationwide? Which dispatch rider do you use, and what does delivery to Ikeja, Surulere, or Abuja cost? Write these answers down — they will become pages on your site.

## Choose the Right No-Code Platform for Your Business

There is no single best platform. The right one depends on what you sell and how comfortable you are with technology. Here are the options most Nigerian small businesses actually use well.

WordPress with a page builder is the most flexible option. It powers a large share of business websites in Nigeria, works well with Nigerian payment plugins, and is good for Google search. The trade-off is that you need hosting, a domain name, and a little patience to learn it. If you plan to blog, publish many products, or grow the site over time, Wordspace is a strong choice. Budget roughly ₦25,000 to ₦60,000 per year for decent shared hosting and a .com.ng or .ng domain, plus your time to set it up.

Wix and Squarespace are easier for a complete beginner. You pick a template, drag blocks into place, and publish. They handle hosting for you, so there is less to break. The monthly cost in dollars can add up when converted to naira, so check the current price before you commit, and confirm that you can connect your own domain name.

Shopify is the best choice if you mainly sell physical products online and want a proper shop with a cart, stock control and discount codes. It is built for selling. It costs more per month than a simple site, and you will still need Paystack or Flutterwave for naira payments, but for a fashion, beauty, or food business doing steady online sales, it often pays for itself.

Google Sites and Carrd are free or very cheap options for a simple one-page site. If all you need is a page with your services, prices, photos and a WhatsApp button, a one-page site can be live in an afternoon. You can upgrade later. Do not let the search for a perfect platform stop you from launching a simple page now.

Whichever platform you choose, make sure of three things before you pay: you can use your own domain name, you can add a WhatsApp button, and you can connect a Nigerian payment method or at least a bank transfer option. If a platform fails any of these three, pick another one.

## Buy Your Domain Name and Hosting

Your domain name is your address on the internet, like joetech360.ng or yourbusinessname.com.ng. Pick a name that is short, easy to spell over the phone, and matches your business name. Avoid numbers, hyphens and clever spellings that people will mishear. If your business is "Adaeze Skincare", try adaezeskincare.com.ng or adaezeskincare.ng before you settle for a long variation.

Buy the domain from a reputable registrar. In Nigeria, many business owners buy through their hosting company, Namecheap, or a local registrar. A .com.ng domain is often cheaper than a .com and clearly signals a Nigerian business. A .ng domain is shorter but usually costs more. Either works. What matters is that the domain is registered in your name and your email, not in a developer's name. If a developer registers it for you, ask for the login details in writing. You must own your domain.

Hosting is the space where your website's files live. If you use Wix, Squarespace or Shopify, hosting is included. If you use WordPress, you need separate hosting. Choose a shared hosting plan from a company with support that answers quickly, daily backups, and a free security certificate, which is the padlock that shows HTTPS in the browser. Without that padlock, browsers warn visitors that your site is not secure, and many customers will leave.

Do not buy the most expensive plan. A basic shared hosting plan is enough for a small business site with a few hundred visitors a day. You can upgrade when your traffic grows. What you should not skip is the security certificate and backups. Ask the host before you pay: "Is SSL included free, and do you back up my site daily?" If the answer is no, look elsewhere.

Set aside a small budget for the first year: domain, hosting, and perhaps a premium template if you want one. For many businesses, ₦40,000 to ₦80,000 covers the first year of a WordPress site. A Wix or Shopify site will cost more in monthly naira terms, so do the maths for twelve months, not one month, before you decide.

## Build Your Website Step by Step

Now you are ready to build. Work in this order, and do not skip ahead to colours and fonts before your words and photos are in place.

Step one: pick a template made for your type of business. If you sell food, pick a food template. If you are a photographer, pick a photography template. A good template already has the right sections in the right order. Change the photos and words, keep the structure.

Step two: write your homepage headline. This is the first thing a visitor reads, and it must say who you help and what you offer. "Fresh, custom cakes for birthdays and weddings in Lagos — delivered to your door" is better than "Welcome to our website". Add one short sentence underneath that explains the benefit, then a clear button: "Order on WhatsApp" or "See Our Prices".

Step three: add your products or services with real prices. Use clear names, one good photo per item, a short description, and a price. For a service business, list three to five packages. For example, a cleaning company in Lekki could list: "One-bedroom flat cleaning — ₦35,000", "Three-bedroom flat cleaning — ₦65,000", "Office cleaning, per visit — ₦80,000". A customer who sees a package can decide quickly.

Step four: add your About section. Customers buy from people they trust. Write three or four short sentences about who you are, where you are based, how long you have been doing this, and why you care about quality. Add a real photo of yourself or your team. Chidi, a phone repair technician in Ikeja, added a photo of his workbench and a line about his five years repairing phones in Computer Village. Customers told him the photo was what made them choose him over cheaper shops they had never seen.

Step five: add your contact and order section. Put your WhatsApp number as a large button, your email, your area, and your opening hours. Add a simple contact form as well, because some customers prefer to type a message than to open WhatsApp. Test the form yourself before you launch — fill it in and confirm that you receive the message.

Step six: add delivery and payment information on its own section or page. State your delivery areas and fees plainly: "Delivery within Lekki — ₦2,500. Delivery to Ikeja and Surulere — ₦4,000. Delivery to Abuja and Port Harcourt — ₦6,500, two to three days." State your payment options: bank transfer, Paystack, or Flutterwave. State your policy on refunds and exchanges in simple language. Clear policies prevent arguments later.

Step seven: connect your payment method. If you use Paystack or Flutterwave, follow their setup guide for your platform. You will need your business name, bank account details, and a means of identification for verification. If that feels heavy for your first version, start with bank transfer and a WhatsApp order button, then add card payment in your second version. A simple site that takes bank transfer orders today is better than a perfect shop that never launches.

Step eight: check your site on your phone. Most of your customers will visit from a phone, not a laptop. Open the site on your own phone and on a friend's phone. Check that the text is readable, the buttons are big enough to tap, the photos load quickly, and the WhatsApp button works. If a photo is too large and slow to load, resize it before you upload it. Aim for photos under 300 kilobytes each.

Step nine: launch, then tell people. Share the link on your WhatsApp status, your Instagram bio, your Facebook page, and your Google Business Profile. Send it to five regular customers and ask them to try ordering through it. Their feedback will show you what to fix first.



## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does it cost to create a simple website in Nigeria?

For a WordPress site, expect roughly ₦40,000 to ₦80,000 for the first year, covering a domain name and shared hosting. A one-page site on a free or low-cost builder can cost almost nothing to start. Wix, Squarespace and Shopify charge monthly, so calculate the cost for twelve months in naira before you choose, and remember that you can start simple and upgrade later.

### Do I need to know how to code?

No. No-code builders use drag and drop, ready-made templates, and simple settings. If you can use WhatsApp and Instagram, you can build a simple website. You will need patience for the first few hours while you learn where the buttons are, but you do not need to write a single line of code.

### Which platform is best for a beginner?

If you sell products and want a proper shop, consider Shopify. If you want flexibility, good Google search results, and lower yearly costs, consider WordPress. If you want the easiest start and only need a simple page with your services, prices and WhatsApp button, try Wix or a one-page builder first. The best platform is the one you will actually finish and publish.

### Can customers pay me through my website?

Yes. You can add Paystack or Flutterwave to accept card payments, bank transfers and transfers in naira. If you are not ready for that step, start with bank transfer and a WhatsApp order button. Many Nigerian small businesses start that way and add card payments once orders become regular.


### How long will it take to build my website?

A simple one-page site can be ready in one day if your photos and prices are prepared. A five-page site usually takes a weekend. Most of the time goes into writing your words and choosing photos, not into the technical building. Prepare your content first, and the building will go much faster.



## What to Do Next

1. Write your one-sentence business description, your price list, and your delivery and payment details on paper today.
2. Take ten clear photos of your best products, your shop or workspace, and yourself at work in daylight.
3. Pick one platform — WordPress, Wix, Shopify, or a one-page builder — based on what you sell and your budget for twelve months.
4. Buy your domain name in your own name and set up hosting or your chosen builder account.
5. Build your five core pages: Home, About, Products or Services, Contact, and Delivery and Payment.
6. Add a WhatsApp button, your prices, your area, and your opening hours, then test the site on your phone.
7. Share the link on WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook, and your Google Business Profile, and ask five customers to try it.
8. Check the site weekly, update prices and photos, and add card payments once orders become regular.
