When a customer in Lekki types "plumber near me" or "best suya spot in Abuja" into Google, the businesses that appear in the map box at the top of the results win the call. That map box is powered by Google Business Profile, and it is completely free. You do not need a big advertising budget, a fancy office, or a website with thousands of pages. You need a complete, honest, well-managed profile, and a habit of keeping it fresh. This guide walks you through every step, from creating the profile to turning it into a steady source of customers, with Nigerian examples, realistic costs, and the mistakes that quietly cost small businesses money every week.

## What Google Business Profile Is and Why It Matters in Nigeria

Google Business Profile, often shortened to GBP, is the free listing Google shows when someone searches for your business name, or for the kind of business you run in a particular area. It is the panel with your photos, opening hours, phone number, address, reviews, and a button that opens Google Maps directions to your door. If you run a salon in Surulere, a restaurant in Wuse 2, a pharmacy in Ikeja, or a phone repair shop in Computer Village, your profile is often the first thing a new customer sees, long before your Instagram page or your website.

Nigerians search with intent. A person searching "tailor near me" or "emergency electrician Lekki" is not browsing for fun. They have money in hand and a problem to solve today. Data from Google consistently shows that a large share of local searches lead to a visit or a call within a day, and in Nigeria that behaviour is even stronger because WhatsApp and phone calls are how most small transactions actually happen. A customer who finds your number on Google and calls you is far more valuable than a hundred people who merely liked a post.

## Step 1: Create Your Profile the Right Way

Start by going to business.google.com and signing in with a Google account you control. Use a business email if you have one, such as yourname@yourbusiness.com.ng, rather than a personal Gmail you might lose access to. If several people manage the business, the owner account should belong to the owner, and staff can be added later as managers. Losing access to the owner account is one of the most common and painful problems Nigerian businesses face with Google, so write the login details down somewhere safe from day one.

Enter your exact business name as it appears on your signboard and your CAC documents. Do not add keywords to the name, such as "Best Hair Salon Lagos Cheap Wigs", because Google penalises that and customers distrust it. Your real name, exactly as people know it, is the right choice. If your registered name is "Adaeze Fashion Hub" but customers know you as "Adaeze Wigs", use the trading name customers actually search for, and keep the registered name for your legal documents.

Choose the correct category. This is more important than most people realise. Google uses your primary category to decide which searches you appear for, so pick the closest match, not the most general one. A business that sells wigs should choose "Wig Shop" or "Hair Salon" rather than just "Shop". A restaurant that serves amala and ewedu should choose "Nigerian Restaurant" if available, or "African Restaurant" as a close second. You can add secondary categories afterwards, up to nine, but the primary one carries the most weight, so choose carefully and change it only if your business genuinely changes.

Next, decide how customers reach you. If customers visit a physical shop, enter the full address, including the street number, area, and city, exactly as a delivery rider would need it. "14 Allen Avenue, Ikeja, Lagos" is useful. "Ikeja, Lagos" is not. Google will show your location on Maps, and wrong or vague addresses lead to lost customers and bad reviews from people who could not find you. If you run a home-based business and do not want your home address public, choose the option to hide your address and set a service area instead, such as "Lekki, Lagos" or "Abuja Municipal Area Council". Many Nigerian home-based vendors, from bakers to wig sellers, use this option safely.

Add your phone number and WhatsApp number. In Nigeria, this is often the single most important field on the whole profile. Most customers will call or message before they visit, so use a number that is actually answered during business hours, and keep it charged. If you use a separate business line, put that one first. If your only number is your personal line, consider getting a second SIM for the business. A dedicated business line costs little, often under ₦5,000 for the SIM and a basic data bundle, and it protects your privacy while making you look professional.

## Step 2: Verify Your Business

Google needs to confirm that your business is real and that you control it, and verification is the step where many Nigerian businesses get stuck. The most common method is a postcard sent by post to your business address, containing a five-digit code. In Nigeria, postal delivery can be slow or unreliable, so expect the card to take two to four weeks, and check with your estate security or reception regularly. Do not request a new code too quickly, because each new request cancels the previous one.

Depending on your business type and location, Google may offer other methods, including verification by phone call, text message, email, or video. Video verification has become more common, and it usually involves recording a short walkthrough showing your signboard, your workspace, and proof that you operate there, such as equipment, stock, or branded materials. Prepare for this by making sure your signboard is visible, your shop is tidy, and you have a utility bill, CAC certificate, or tenancy agreement nearby in case it is needed. A short, honest video is enough. You do not need professional filming.

## Step 3: Write a Description That Sells Without Shouting

Your business description is limited to 750 characters, so every word must earn its place. Write in plain language, as if you were explaining your business to a new customer standing in front of you. Say what you do, who you serve, and what makes you different, and include the area you serve naturally.

A weak description reads: "We are the best salon in Lagos. We offer quality services at affordable prices. Come and patronise us." It says nothing specific, and every salon in Lagos says the same thing.

A strong description reads: "Adaeze Hair Studio in Surulere styles braids, wigs, and natural hair for working women and students. Walk-ins welcome Monday to Saturday, 9am to 7pm. Home service available in Surulere and Yaba. Call or WhatsApp 0803 123 4567 to book." That version tells a customer exactly what to expect, when to come, and how to reach you, in the same number of words.

## Step 4: Add Photos That Make People Choose You

Photos are the most underused weapon on Google Business Profile in Nigeria. Many profiles have no photos at all, or a single blurry picture taken years ago. Meanwhile, customers decide with their eyes. A restaurant with bright, recent photos of its food will beat a restaurant with a better menu but no pictures, because the customer can already imagine eating there.

Upload at least ten good photos to start, and add new ones every week or two. Your first photo should be your exterior, so customers can recognise your shop from the street. The second should be your interior, so they know what to expect inside. Then add photos of your products, your team at work, and happy customers where you have their permission. A tailor should show finished outfits, not just fabric. A baker should show the actual cake, sliced and served, not just a stock photo from the internet. A mechanic should show a clean, organised workshop, because that is what a nervous car owner wants to see.

Use real photos of your real business. Stock photos are easy for customers to spot, and they create a gap between expectation and reality that leads to disappointment. You do not need a professional camera. A clean phone camera, good daylight, and a tidy background are enough. Wipe the lens, stand still, take the photo near a window or outside, and avoid clutter behind the subject. If you sell food, photograph it on a clean plate with natural light. If you sell clothes, photograph them on a person or a proper hanger, not crumpled on a bed.

## Step 5: Get Reviews and Answer Every Single One

Reviews are the engine of Google Business Profile. They influence your ranking, they influence whether a stranger trusts you, and they give you free feedback about what your business does well and badly. A business with fifty genuine reviews will almost always beat a business with three, even if the second business is better in real life, because Google and customers can only judge what they can see.

The simplest way to get reviews is to ask, at the right moment. When a customer smiles, compliments your work, or says they will come back, that is the moment. Say something simple: "Thank you so much. If you have a minute, a review on Google would really help our small business. I can send you the link on WhatsApp." Then send the link immediately, while the good feeling is fresh. Waiting until next week means the customer has forgotten, and the review never happens.

Create a short review link from your Google Business Profile dashboard and save it where you can reach it quickly, such as a pinned note on your phone or a saved message on WhatsApp. You can also print a small card or a QR code at your counter that says "Enjoyed your visit? Please leave us a Google review", with the code linking to your review page. A printed card costs under ₦5,000 at a local printer, and it works quietly all day without you remembering to ask.

Never buy reviews, and never ask friends or family to write fake reviews from their homes. Google detects patterns such as many reviews from the same device, the same IP address, or accounts that only review your business. Fake reviews can get your profile suspended, and even when they survive, customers can often tell they are fake because they are vague and overly enthusiastic. One honest review that says "The braids lasted three weeks and the stylist was patient with my daughter" is worth ten fake reviews that say "Best salon ever, five stars."

Answer every review, good and bad, within a day or two. For a good review, thank the customer by name and mention something specific: "Thank you, Mrs. Adeyemi, we are glad the aso-ebi came out well. We look forward to your next order." That personal reply shows future customers that you pay attention. For a bad review, stay calm, apologise for the experience, and invite the customer to resolve it privately: "We are sorry the delivery arrived late on Saturday. Please send us a WhatsApp message on 0803 123 4567 so we can make it right." Never argue in public, never insult the reviewer, and never reveal private details. Future customers read your replies to bad reviews more carefully than the reviews themselves, because they want to know how you behave when things go wrong.

## Step 6: Use Posts, Offers, and Questions to Stay Visible

Google Business Profile lets you publish short posts, similar to a social media update, that appear on your profile for seven days. Most Nigerian small businesses never use this feature, which is exactly why it works. A post takes five minutes and keeps your profile looking alive.

Post about what is new and useful. A restaurant can post its weekend menu or a new dish. A fashion shop can post a new arrival with its price. A pharmacy can post that it now stocks a particular product. A gym can post a new class time. Keep the post short, add a clear photo, and include a call to action such as "Call to order" or "Visit us today". A post that says "New stock alert: human hair wigs from ₦45,000, available in Surulere today, call 0803 123 4567" will bring more enquiries than a vague post that says "We sell quality wigs."

## Step 7: Keep Your Information Accurate, Always

The fastest way to lose customers on Google is to let your information go stale. A phone number that no longer works, hours that say open when you are closed, or an address for a shop you moved out of a year ago will generate bad reviews from people who tried to reach you and failed. Those reviews are often harsher than a complaint about your product, because the customer feels misled before they even met you.

Set a monthly reminder, on the first Saturday of the month for example, to check your profile from a customer's point of view. Search for your business name on Google, read the whole panel, click the phone number, and follow the map directions. Fix anything that is wrong. Check that your WhatsApp link works, your website link opens, and your holiday hours are correct. This ten-minute check is one of the highest-value habits a small business owner in Nigeria can build.

## Step 8: Understand What Google Ranks and How to Improve

Google ranks local businesses using three factors: relevance, distance, and prominence. Relevance is how well your profile matches what the person searched for. Distance is how close you are to the searcher or to the place they searched for. Prominence is how well known and trusted your business appears, based on reviews, links, articles, and your overall online presence.

You cannot control distance. A customer searching in Ikeja will see Ikeja businesses first, and that is fair. You can control relevance and prominence, and that is where your effort belongs. Relevance improves when your category is correct, your description is specific, your services are listed completely, and your posts and photos use the words customers actually search for. Prominence improves when you collect genuine reviews, when other websites link to you, when local blogs or directories mention you, and when your profile is active and complete.

Complete every field Google offers. Add your services one by one, with prices where you are comfortable sharing them. A salon that lists "braids ₦15,000, wig installation ₦10,000, wash and set ₦5,000" helps customers decide before they call, and it helps Google match you to searches for those exact services. Add attributes that apply to you, such as "wheelchair accessible", "free Wi-Fi", "outdoor seating", or "women-led". These small details help you appear in filtered searches and show customers that you thought about their needs.

Add your service area if you deliver or travel to customers. A Lagos plumber who serves Lekki, Ajah, and Victoria Island should say so. A caterer in Abuja who delivers to Wuse, Garki, and Maitama should list those areas. This does not replace a physical address, but it tells Google and customers where you are willing to work, and it prevents enquiries from places you cannot reach.





## Conclusion

Google Business Profile is free, it is built for exactly the kind of customer a Nigerian small business needs, and it rewards the businesses that treat it seriously. Create the profile correctly, verify it patiently, fill in every field honestly, add real photos, collect genuine reviews, answer every review, post regularly, and keep your information accurate. Do those things consistently for ninety days, and you will see the difference in calls, visits, and direction requests. Then keep going. Your competitors are either ignoring this tool or using it badly. A complete, active, well-reviewed profile is one of the cheapest advantages a small business in Nigeria can build, and it compounds every month you maintain it. Start today, with the ten minutes it takes to claim your listing and correct your hours. Your next customer may already be searching.
