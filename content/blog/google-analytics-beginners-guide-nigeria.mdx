You built a website. You installed Google Analytics. Now what?

Most Nigerian businesses install Google Analytics during the website build, get the confirmation code, and never look at it again. The data accumulates — who visited, where they came from, what they did — but nobody uses it to make business decisions.

That is a missed opportunity. Google Analytics is free, and it gives you answers to some of the most important questions about your business: Where are my customers coming from? What content do they care about? Why are they leaving without buying? Which marketing channels actually work?

This guide will teach you the basics of Google Analytics — what to look at, what it means, and how to use the data to grow your business.

## Getting Started with Google Analytics

Before you can analyze data, you need Analytics installed on your website. If you do not have it yet:

1. Go to analytics.google.com and create an account
2. Set up a property for your website
3. Install the tracking code on your website

If your developer built your website, ask them to install Google Analytics. Most developers include this as standard. If you built the site yourself on WordPress, you can use a plugin like Site Kit by Google or insert the tracking code manually.

Google Analytics 4 (GA4) is the current version. If you are still using Universal Analytics, migrate to GA4 — Universal Analytics stopped processing data in 2023.

## The Google Analytics Dashboard: What Matters Most

When you open Google Analytics, you see a lot of numbers. Here is what to focus on as a beginner.

### Real-Time Report

This shows what is happening on your website right now — how many active users, which pages they are viewing, and where they are located. It is useful for confirming that Analytics is working and for monitoring traffic spikes from social media posts or email campaigns.

### Acquisition Overview

This answers the question: "How did people find my website?" It shows traffic broken down by channel:

**Organic Search:** Visitors who found you through Google, Bing, or other search engines. This is your SEO traffic. If this number is growing, your SEO efforts are working.

**Direct:** Visitors who typed your URL directly into their browser or used a bookmark. High direct traffic indicates strong brand recognition or offline marketing driving people to your site.

**Social:** Visitors from social media platforms — Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn, TikTok, Twitter. This shows which social platforms are driving traffic to your website.

**Email:** Visitors who clicked a link in your email marketing campaigns. This requires you to tag your email links correctly (UTM parameters).

**Referral:** Visitors from other websites that link to yours. This includes directories, partner sites, and media coverage.

**Paid Search:** Visitors from Google Ads or other paid search campaigns.

For most Nigerian businesses, Organic Search and Social will be the largest channels. If Direct is very high, it may mean some traffic is miscategorized due to missing analytics on certain pages.

### Engagement Overview

This answers: "What did visitors do on my website?"

**Average Engagement Time:** How long users spend on your site. Higher is generally better, but it depends on the page type. A blog post should have 2-4 minutes of engagement. A contact page might have 30 seconds.

**Views per User:** How many pages a typical visitor views. Higher engagement usually means visitors find your content valuable.

**Bounce Rate:** The percentage of visitors who leave after viewing only one page. A high bounce rate (over 70%) may indicate that visitors are not finding what they expected. However, some pages naturally have high bounce rates — a blog post that answers a specific question may have a 90% bounce rate because the visitor got their answer and left satisfied.

### Pages and Screens Report

This shows your most popular pages. It tells you what content your audience values most. Look at your top 10 pages and ask: Are these the pages I want people to see? If not, adjust your content strategy to promote more important pages. Are there any surprises? Pages you did not expect to perform well that are getting traffic — invest more in similar content.

## Key Metrics to Track Weekly

You do not need to track every metric. Focus on these:

### Users

The number of unique visitors to your website. Track this weekly. Is it growing? What drove spikes or drops? A growing user count indicates your marketing and SEO are working.

### Traffic Sources

Which channels drive the most traffic? If Organic Search is growing, your SEO is working. If Social is flat, try new content formats or platforms. If Direct is your biggest channel, your brand recognition is strong.

### Top Pages

Which pages get the most traffic. This tells you what content your audience values. Create more content like your top-performing pages. If your top pages are blog posts, your content strategy is working. If your top page is your contact page, people are ready to reach out.

### Conversion Rate

The percentage of visitors who complete a desired action — filling out a contact form, making a purchase, signing up for your newsletter, or calling your phone number. Track this weekly. If it drops, investigate. If it rises, identify what changed and do more of it.

### Bounce Rate by Page

Identify pages with unusually high bounce rates. A blog post with a 90% bounce rate may be fine (they read and left). Your pricing page with a 90% bounce rate is a problem — it means people look at your prices and leave without taking action.

## How to Set Up Conversion Tracking

Conversions are the most important metric in Google Analytics. Without conversion tracking, you cannot measure ROI.

### What to Track as Conversions

**Form submissions:** When someone fills out your contact form.
**Phone calls:** When someone clicks your phone number on mobile.
**Purchases:** When someone completes a purchase on your e-commerce site.
**Newsletter signups:** When someone joins your email list.
**Button clicks:** When someone clicks key buttons (Get a Quote, Book Now).
**Page views:** When someone reaches a specific page (Thank You page after form submission).

### How to Set It Up

In GA4, go to Configure > Events > Create Event. You can track:

- Page views (automatic)
- Scroll depth (how far users scroll)
- Outbound clicks (clicks to other websites)
- Site search (what users search for on your site)
- Form interactions (when users engage with forms)

For e-commerce sites, GA4 has built-in e-commerce tracking. Ask your developer to implement it.

## Using Data to Make Business Decisions

Here is how to turn analytics data into action:

### Which Marketing Channel Should You Invest In?

Look at your acquisition report. Which channel drives the most traffic that converts? If Email has a 5% conversion rate and Social has a 0.5% conversion rate, you should invest more in email marketing, even if social drives more total traffic.

A common mistake Nigerian businesses make is focusing on the channel that drives the most traffic rather than the channel that drives the most conversions.

### Which Content Should You Create More Of?

Look at your top pages by engagement. What topics perform best? Create more content on those topics. If your top blog post is "How to Choose a Web Developer in Lagos," create related content like "Questions to Ask Before Hiring a Web Developer" and "Web Development Pricing in Nigeria Explained."

### Why Are Visitors Leaving?

If your homepage has a high bounce rate, visitors may not understand what you offer within the first few seconds. Improve your headline and value proposition. If your product pages have low engagement, improve the product descriptions and images. If your checkout page has a high abandonment rate, simplify the checkout process.

### Are Your Ads Working?

If you run Google Ads or social media ads, use UTM parameters to track their performance in Google Analytics. Compare conversion rates and cost per conversion across campaigns. Stop spending on campaigns that do not convert.

## Reports You Should Run Monthly

### Monthly Traffic Report

- Total users and sessions
- Traffic by channel (Organic, Social, Direct, Referral, Email)
- Top 10 pages by views
- Average engagement time
- Conversion rate and total conversions
- Compare to previous month and same month last year

### Content Performance Report

- Top 10 blog posts by traffic
- Top 10 blog posts by engagement time
- Pages with highest bounce rates
- Pages with lowest engagement
- Identify content gaps (topics your audience searches for that you have not covered)

### Conversion Report

- Total conversions by type (form submissions, purchases, calls)
- Conversion rate by traffic source
- Conversion rate by landing page
- Conversion rate by device (mobile vs desktop)
- Identify pages with high traffic but low conversion (optimization opportunities)

## Common Google Analytics Mistakes

### Not Setting Up Goals

Without conversion tracking, Google Analytics is just a visitor counter. Set up at least one goal within your first week of using Analytics.

### Misinterpreting Bounce Rate

Bounce rate is not inherently bad. A blog post with a 90% bounce rate is fine if visitors read the article and left satisfied. A product page with a 90% bounce rate is a problem. Context matters.

### Comparing Apples to Oranges

When comparing metrics month-over-month or year-over-year, ensure you are comparing similar timeframes. A 30-day month versus a 31-day month will show different numbers. Compare the same day of the week.

### Ignoring Mobile vs Desktop

Over 70% of Nigerian web traffic is mobile. If your mobile conversion rate is significantly lower than desktop, your mobile experience needs improvement. Check your mobile vs desktop performance in Analytics regularly.

### Not Acting on Data

Analytics is useless if you do not act on it. Review your key metrics weekly and make at least one data-driven decision per week. Test, measure, and iterate.

## Tools That Work with Google Analytics

These free tools complement Google Analytics:

**Google Search Console:** Shows your search performance — which keywords drive traffic, your average position in search results, and technical issues Google found on your site. Connect it to Analytics for richer data.

**Google Tag Manager:** Makes it easier to add tracking codes to your website without editing code every time. Useful for setting up conversion tracking, remarketing tags, and third-party analytics.

**Google Data Studio:** Turns your Analytics data into visual dashboards and reports. You can build a dashboard that shows your key metrics at a glance and share it with your team.

**PageSpeed Insights:** Not part of Analytics, but essential for understanding how page speed affects your traffic and conversions.

## Getting Started: Your First Week with Analytics

**Day 1:** Confirm Analytics is installed and collecting data (check Real-Time report).
**Day 2:** Set up conversion tracking for your most important action (form submission, purchase, or phone call).
**Day 3:** Review your Acquisition report. Identify your top 3 traffic sources.
**Day 4:** Review your Pages report. Identify your top 5 pages.
**Day 5:** Set up a monthly dashboard in Google Data Studio or export your key metrics to a spreadsheet.
**Day 6:** Schedule a weekly 30-minute analytics review on your calendar.
**Day 7:** Make your first data-driven decision based on what you learned.
