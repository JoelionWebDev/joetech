Ask a tailor in Yaba for the receipt from a job she finished in March, and watch what happens. If her Drive is like most small business Drives, she opens her phone, scrolls past a photo of her nephew, a church flyer, three versions of her logo, and a file called "final final 2", and after ten minutes she gives up and says, "I will send it tomorrow." Tomorrow never comes. The customer stops asking, and quietly stops trusting her.

That tailor does not have a storage problem. She has a system problem. Google Drive gives every business 15 GB of free space, and most small businesses fill it with chaos: files dumped in the root folder, names that mean nothing, folders shared with the wrong people, and a clean-up that never happens because nobody knows where to start.

## The Folder Tree: Build It Before You Move Anything

The mistake almost everyone makes is moving files first and organising later. Do it the other way round. Create the folders empty, agree the structure, and only then start moving things in. An empty folder tree takes twenty minutes. A full Drive reorganised by guesswork takes a weekend and still ends up wrong.

Start with one top folder named after your business, for example "Adaeze Hair — Business". Everything business-related lives inside it, and nothing else does. Your personal photos and family documents stay in a separate top folder, or better, in a separate Google account.

Inside the business folder, create six main folders. Six is enough for almost any small business, and few enough that you can remember them without thinking:

- **01 Customers** — one subfolder per customer or per job, with their orders, measurements, receipts and chats saved as PDFs.
- **02 Money** — invoices, receipts, bank statements, expenses, and tax records, split by year and then by month.
- **03 Suppliers** — one subfolder per supplier, with price lists, invoices, delivery notes and contact details.
- **04 Products and Services** — your price lists, product photos, descriptions, and the master copy of your catalogue.
- **05 Marketing** — your logo files, flyers, social media graphics, and the photos and videos you post.
- **06 Admin** — your CAC certificate, contracts, staff records, policies, and anything legal.

The numbers at the start — 01, 02, 03 — are not decoration. They force the folders to stay in the order you chose, instead of jumping around alphabetically every time you add a new one. Every folder in the system gets a number. Inside 02 Money, the year folders are "2025" and "2026", and inside each year, the months are "01 January", "02 February", all the way to "12 December". A year from now, when you need a March invoice, you will walk straight to 02 Money, then 2026, then 03 March. No searching, no guessing, no scrolling.

Inside 01 Customers, name each subfolder with the customer's name and the job, like "Funke Adeyemi — Wedding Dress May 2026" or "Dele Okafor — Monthly Catering". Inside 03 Suppliers, use the supplier's business name: "Alhaji Musa Fabrics — Balogun". Inside 06 Admin, keep it tight: "CAC Documents", "Contracts", "Staff Records", "Policies". Staff Records is the one folder in your whole Drive that nobody but you should ever open, and we will come back to that under sharing.

Keep the tree shallow. No folder should sit more than three levels deep: business folder, main folder, subfolder. The moment you have a folder inside a folder inside a folder inside a folder, you have built a maze, and mazes are where files go to die.

## The Naming System: A File Should Explain Itself

A good file name answers three questions before you open it: what is this, whose is it, and when is it from? If a file name cannot answer those, it is a bad name, and every bad name costs you minutes later.

The convention is simple: date first, then what it is, then whose it is. Dates go year-month-day, always with two digits, like 2026-11-15. That format has a hidden superpower: Drive sorts it correctly, oldest to newest, automatically. Dates written as "15 Nov" or "Nov 15" sort into nonsense.

Here is the pattern, with real examples from a real business:

- Invoices you send: `2026-11-15_Invoice-014_Funke-Adeyemi_28000NGN.pdf`
- Receipts you receive: `2026-11-14_Receipt_Alhaji-Musa-Fabrics_45000NGN.jpg`
- Customer measurements: `2026-11-10_Measurements_Funke-Adeyemi_Wedding-Dress.docx`
- Contracts: `2026-10-20_Contract_Dele-Okafor_Monthly-Catering.pdf`

Use hyphens between words and underscores between the parts. Never use slashes — Drive will not allow them anyway — and never, ever name a file "final", "new", or "updated". There is no file in the history of small business that stayed final. When a document changes, add a version number instead: `Price-List_v3.pdf` replaces `Price-List_v2.pdf`, and the old one moves to an "Archive" subfolder inside the same folder, not the trash.

Kill the camera names on sight. "IMG_4521" tells you nothing, and a year from now it tells you less. The day you take a product photo, rename it before you upload it, or the rename never happens. It takes ten seconds on your phone. A photo named `2026-11-12_Product_Ankara-Dress-Blue_Back-View.jpg` can be found by typing "Ankara" or "blue" into Drive search. A photo named IMG_4521 can be found by nothing except luck.

For folders, the same rule applies. "Stuff", "New Folder", "Misc", "Old" — delete these names from your vocabulary. A folder called "Misc" is where files go when you have given up on them.

## Sharing Rules: Who Gets What, and What They Can Do

Drive sharing has three levels — Viewer, Commenter, Editor — and most small business disasters come from giving the wrong level to the wrong person. Here is the rule for each kind of person in your business.

**Customers get Viewer, on single files, never on folders.** When Funke asks for her invoice, you share that one invoice file as Viewer, or you send her the link to that file. She can see it and download it, and she cannot change a single figure on it. Never share a whole folder with a customer. A folder contains other customers' files, and one wrong tap by a curious customer is a privacy problem you cannot undo.

**Staff get Editor, on one folder only — their own work folder.** Your tailor needs to edit the measurement sheets and the production list. She does not need to edit your Money folder, your Staff Records, or your supplier price lists. Share the specific folder she works in, as Editor, and nothing else. If your delivery rider only needs to see the day's delivery list, share that one file as Viewer.

**Suppliers get Viewer, on the files that concern them.** Your fabric supplier can view the order list you sent him. He cannot view your other suppliers' prices. Keep supplier subfolders unshared, and share individual files when you need to.

**Accountants and helpers get Viewer on 02 Money, and nothing else.** If your accountant needs your 2026 invoices, share the 2026 folder inside Money as Viewer. Do not share the whole Drive. Do not share the business folder itself. Sharing from the top down is the single most common mistake in this whole guide, because it looks convenient — one share, everything covered — and it hands your customer list, your staff salaries, and your supplier prices to one person in a single click.

Two more rules that save businesses every year. First, turn off link sharing by default. A link set to "Anyone with the link can view" is a copy of your file floating around WhatsApp groups forever, and you cannot call it back. Share with named email addresses instead, and use link sharing only for public things like your price list. Second, check your sharing once a month. When someone leaves your business, remove their access the same day.

## The Weekly Ten-Minute Tidy

A filing system dies without maintenance, and maintenance dies when it takes too long. So the routine is ten minutes, once a week, same time every week. Friday afternoon works for most businesses, when the week's jobs are done and the shop is quiet. Set a phone alarm. Ten minutes, no more.

Minute one to three: open Drive and look at the root of your business folder. Anything sitting loose there — files that were uploaded in a hurry during the week — gets moved into its proper folder right now. A receipt photo goes to 02 Money, then the current year, then the current month. A customer measurement goes to that customer's folder. If a file does not have a proper name, rename it now, using the convention above.

Minute four to six: check the week's money. Every invoice you sent this week should be in 02 Money under this month, named properly. Every receipt you collected should be there too. If a job is finished and its invoice is missing, write it now — do not wait, because Monday-you will not remember what Friday-you charged.

Minute seven to eight: empty the duplicates. Search "copy of" to find files uploaded twice, and delete the older copy. Check storage too: if you are close to the 15 GB limit, move old videos to an external drive this week.

Minute nine to ten: star the files you will need next week. Your current price list, the delivery list for Monday, the customer folder for the big job coming up. Starred files sit in one place under the Starred menu, and reaching them takes two taps on a busy morning instead of a five-minute hunt.

Once a month, add a thirty-minute session to the routine: open the sharing list and remove anyone who should no longer have access, move last month's finished job folders into an Archive folder, and check that your Google account has two-step verification switched on. Your whole business now lives in this account. A strong password and two-step verification are the lock on the shop door.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is 15 GB really enough for a small business?

For documents, receipts, photos and spreadsheets, yes — that is thousands of files. Videos are what eat storage. Keep your raw video files on your phone or an external drive, and keep only the finished, posted videos in Drive.

### What if my staff do not have Gmail addresses?

They can open a free Gmail account in five minutes, or you can share files with any email address — Drive will prompt them to create a Google account. Either way, share with a named address, never with a public link, so you know exactly who has access.

### What happens if my phone is stolen?

Nothing, as long as the files are in Drive. Sign in on a new phone and everything is there. This is also why two-step verification matters: your files are only as safe as your Google account.

## Build It This Weekend

Open Drive, create the business folder, and build the six numbered folders inside it. Then take the loose files sitting in your Drive root and move them, ten at a time, renaming each one as you go. It will feel slow for the first twenty files. By the fiftieth you will be fast, and by the end you will find any receipt in under thirty seconds.

Start with the money folder, because that is where lost files cost you actual naira. Then the customers folder. Then the rest. And when Friday comes, set the alarm for ten minutes, and keep the system alive.
