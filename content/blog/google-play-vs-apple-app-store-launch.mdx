You have built your app. You have tested it thoroughly. Now it is time to share it with the world. But before users can download your app, it needs to pass the review processes of Google Play and the Apple App Store.

Both stores have different requirements, submission processes, and common rejection reasons. Understanding these differences before you submit saves you the frustration of rejection and the delay of resubmission.

This guide covers everything Nigerian developers need to know about launching on Google Play and the Apple App Store.

## Developer Accounts

Before you can submit apps, you need a developer account for each platform.

Google Play requires a one-time registration fee of  (approximately ₦20,000). The account is valid for as long as Google Play exists. You can publish unlimited apps under one account.

Apple requires an annual fee of  (approximately ₦80,000) for individual developers and  for organizations. The account must be renewed every year. You can publish unlimited apps.

Both require verification of your identity. Google sends a verification code to your phone. Apple requires identity verification that can take several days. Start this process early.

## Submission Requirements

### App Icon

Both stores require app icons in multiple sizes. Google Play requires a 512x512 pixel icon and a feature graphic (1024x500). Apple requires icons at various sizes from 20x20 to 1024x1024.

Your icon should be simple, recognizable, and distinctive. Avoid text in icons — it becomes unreadable at small sizes.

### Screenshots

Both stores require screenshots showing your app in action. Google Play requires at least 2 screenshots for phones and 2 for tablets. Apple requires screenshots for each supported device size (iPhone and iPad).

Screenshots should show your app's best features. Use captions to explain what the user is seeing. The first screenshot is the most important — it appears in search results and determines whether users click through to view your app.

### App Description

Your app description is critical for App Store Optimization (ASO). The first few lines are the most important — they appear in search results before users click "read more."

Write a clear, compelling description that explains what your app does, who it is for, and why it is valuable. Include relevant keywords naturally. Avoid keyword stuffing — both stores penalize this.

### Privacy Policy

Both stores require a privacy policy. This is one of the most common reasons for rejection, especially for Apple. Your privacy policy must clearly state what data your app collects, how it is used, whether it is shared with third parties, and how users can request data deletion.

Many Nigerian developers use generic privacy policy generators, which is acceptable for basic apps. However, if your app handles sensitive data (payment information, health data, location), your privacy policy should be reviewed by a legal professional.

### Age Rating

Both stores require you to specify an age rating for your app. Be honest. Misrepresenting your age rating can lead to rejection or removal. If your app has user-generated content that may include mature themes, rate it accordingly.

### Content Guidelines

Google Play and Apple both reject apps that contain illegal content, hate speech, violence, harassment, or sexually explicit material. Apps that promote misinformation, deceptive practices, or harmful activities are also rejected.

## Common Google Play Rejections

Google's review process is largely automated, though some apps receive manual review. Common rejection reasons include:

Minimum functionality violations occur when Google considers your app too simple to provide value. An app that is just a web view wrapper around an existing website is often rejected. Your app should provide unique functionality that justifies its existence as a native app.

Deceptive behavior includes apps that mislead users about their functionality, use deceptive marketing claims, or impersonate other apps or brands. Be honest about what your app does.

User data violations happen when your app collects personal data without proper disclosure, uses data in ways not disclosed in the privacy policy, or does not provide a way for users to delete their accounts and data.

Spam and minimal functionality violations include apps with no real functionality, apps that exist primarily to display ads, and apps that duplicate existing apps with minimal changes.

## Common Apple App Store Rejections

Apple's review process is manual and thorough. Guidelines are strict and change frequently.

Incomplete information is the most common rejection reason. Missing privacy policy, incomplete app description, placeholder content, broken links, or test accounts that do not work all lead to rejection. Before submitting, check every link, every piece of text, and every configuration setting.

UI and design violations occur when your app looks outdated, does not follow iOS design conventions, or has UI elements that are confusing or non-standard. Apple pays close attention to design quality.

Performance issues include crashes on launch, excessive battery drain, slow response times, and memory usage spikes. Apple reviews apps on actual devices and will reject apps with performance problems.

Placeholder content is an automatic rejection. Your app must be fully complete — no "coming soon" pages, no dummy text, no unimplemented features.

Apps that are primarily web views are often rejected. Apple wants apps to provide native functionality, not just wrap a website. If your app is essentially a web view, consider building a Progressive Web App instead.

Uncommon API usage can trigger rejection. Apple restricts access to certain private APIs. Ensure your developer is not using any private or undocumented APIs.

## Submission Timeline

Google Play's review typically takes 1-3 hours for apps reviewed by automated systems. Apps flagged for manual review can take 2-7 days. Updates to existing apps are usually reviewed within 1-2 hours.

Apple's review typically takes 1-3 days for initial submissions. Updates are usually reviewed within 1-2 days. Apps that require additional review (due to content concerns or specific functionality) can take 7-14 days.

Plan your launch date with these timelines in mind. Always submit at least 2 weeks before your planned launch date to allow for rejection and resubmission.

## Post-Launch Considerations

After launch, both stores continue to review your app. Significant updates that change functionality, add new features, or modify data collection practices trigger a new review.

Both stores also monitor your app after launch. Apps that introduce new issues through updates, receive a high volume of negative reviews for specific issues, or violate guidelines discovered after launch can be removed.

Maintaining good standing requires responding to user reviews, fixing bugs promptly, updating your app for new OS versions, keeping your privacy policy current, and complying with evolving store guidelines.

## ASO Basics

App Store Optimization (ASO) is the process of improving your app's visibility in store search results. Key factors include the app title and subtitle, keywords (Apple allows you to specify keywords in your metadata), rating and reviews, download velocity (apps with more downloads rank higher), and screenshots and preview video.

For Nigerian-focused apps, include Nigeria-specific keywords. A local delivery app should include terms like "delivery in Lagos," "Nigeria delivery," and "order food Lagos."
