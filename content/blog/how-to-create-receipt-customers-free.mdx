A customer pays you. You say thank you. They walk away. That is where most small businesses stop. But there is one small step that separates a business that looks serious from one that looks like a side hustle: handing the customer a proper receipt.

A receipt is a simple document. It says: this business received this amount, from this person, for this thing, on this date. It takes two minutes to create, and it does more for your business than most people realise. It builds trust, it prevents arguments, it keeps your records straight, and it makes customers comfortable spending more with you next time.

## Why a Receipt Matters More Than You Think

The first reason is trust. When a customer holds a receipt with your business name, the date, the amount, and a description of what they paid for, they feel safe. They know they can come back if something is wrong. They know you are a real business with records, not someone who will deny the payment ever happened. That feeling is what turns a first-time buyer into a repeat customer.

The second reason is protection. Disputes happen, even between honest people. A customer forgets they paid a deposit and not the full amount. A customer claims they paid for two items when they paid for one. A customer says they paid last month when they paid three months ago. A receipt settles all of these in seconds, because the facts are written down. Without a receipt, it is your word against theirs, and even when you are right, the argument costs you the customer.

The third reason is your own records. At the end of the month, how much did you actually earn? Which product sold best? Which customer owes you a balance? If you issue a receipt for every payment, you can answer all of these questions. If you do not, you are guessing. A business that keeps records makes decisions based on facts. A business that does not keeps guessing.

## What Every Professional Receipt Must Contain

A good receipt is simple, but it must be complete. If any of these details are missing, the receipt is weaker. Get into the habit of including all of them, every time.

**Your business name and contact details.** Put your business name at the top, with your phone number and, if you have one, your address. This is what turns a scrap of paper into a document from a real business. Customers keep receipts, and every receipt carrying your name is a small advert in their bag or drawer.

**The word "Receipt" and a unique receipt number.** Label the document clearly, and give it a number. Use a simple system, such as R-001, R-002, and so on, or a date-based number like 2026-11-001. Numbering matters because it stops duplicates and makes it easy to find a transaction later. When a customer calls and says they paid on the 12th, you should be able to find their receipt in seconds.

**The date and the customer's name.** Write the full date the payment was received, and the full name of the person who paid. A receipt without a date or a name is almost useless in a dispute, because nobody can prove when the payment happened or who made it.

**A clear description of what was paid for.** Write exactly what the customer paid for. Instead of "goods", write "2 yards of Ankara fabric and sewing service" or "1 HP air conditioner installation, Lekki Phase 1". Be specific enough that anyone reading the receipt six months later knows what the payment covered. If the payment is a deposit, say so clearly, and write the total job value and the balance remaining.

**The amount, in figures and in words.** Write the amount in numbers, and also in words — for example, "₦150,000 (One Hundred and Fifty Thousand Naira Only)". Writing the amount in words prevents anyone from altering the figures later, and it is standard practice in formal Nigerian business documents. If you charged VAT or any tax, show it as a separate line.

**The payment method.** Note how the customer paid: cash, bank transfer, POS, or cheque. If it was a transfer, write the bank name and the transaction reference. This helps you match the receipt to your bank statement, and it helps the customer prove payment if they ever need to.

**Your signature, and a stamp if you have one.** Sign the receipt. A signature shows that the business received the money and stands behind the document. If you have a business stamp, use it too. A stamp is not expensive to make in Nigeria, and it makes your documents look far more official.

## Receipt vs Invoice vs Quotation: Do Not Mix Them Up

These three documents look similar, but they do different jobs, and mixing them up confuses customers.

A **quotation** comes before the work. It tells the customer what the job will cost. A **receipt** comes after payment. It proves the customer paid. An **invoice** is a request for payment — it tells the customer what they owe and by when.

A common mistake is to send an invoice when the customer has already paid, or to give a receipt before any money has changed hands. Each document has its moment. ## Common Receipt Mistakes That Cost You Money

The first mistake is writing vague descriptions. "Goods" or "services" tells nobody anything. Six months later, neither you nor the customer will remember what "goods" meant. Be specific on every receipt.

The second mistake is not numbering your receipts. Without numbers, you cannot find a transaction quickly, and you cannot prove that a receipt is genuine. A customer could present a forged receipt and you would have no way to check it against your records. Numbering is free protection.

The third mistake is forgetting deposits and balances. Starting work without a deposit is one of the fastest ways to lose money in Nigeria, and a receipt that does not show the balance is the reason most deposit disputes happen. Every receipt for a part payment must show the total, the amount paid, and the balance remaining.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need to charge VAT on my receipts?

Most small businesses in Nigeria do not charge VAT unless they are registered for it. If you are not VAT-registered, do not add VAT to your receipts. If you are registered, show the VAT as a separate line so the customer can see the price before tax and the tax amount. If you are unsure whether you need to register, ask an accountant — it depends on your turnover and your business structure.

### Can I send a receipt on WhatsApp, or does it need to be printed?

You can send a receipt on WhatsApp, and most customers in Nigeria expect that. The important thing is that it is a proper document, not a plain text message. Generate a clean PDF with your business details, the items, the total, and your signature, and send that PDF as a document on WhatsApp. It looks professional, the customer can save it, and you have a record of exactly what was sent.
