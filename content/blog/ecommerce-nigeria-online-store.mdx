Nigeria's e-commerce market is projected to reach $75 billion by 2027. The COVID-19 pandemic permanently shifted consumer behaviour toward online shopping. Jumala and Konga proved the model exists. Now, the opportunity is for independent merchants to build their own online stores.

But selling online in Nigeria is different from selling online anywhere else. The platforms, payment methods, logistics, and customer expectations are unique.

Here is a comprehensive guide to building an e-commerce store that succeeds in the Nigerian market.

## Why E-Commerce Works in Nigeria Now

Several factors have aligned to make this the right time:

- **Smartphone penetration** exceeds 40% and growing rapidly
- **Digital payment adoption** — Paystack and Flutterwave made payments easy
- **Logistics improvement** — last-mile delivery companies now serve most urban areas
- **Trust infrastructure** — more people have bought online successfully
- **Youth population** — 70% of Nigerians are under 30, and they shop online

## Choosing Your E-Commerce Platform

### Option 1: Dedicated E-Commerce Platform (Best for Most Businesses)

**Shopify**
- Global platform, works well in Nigeria
- Integrates with Paystack and Flutterwave
- Monthly cost: $39/month (₦30,000+)
- Best for: Businesses with budget, who want a professional store quickly

**WooCommerce (WordPress)**
- Free plugin (hosting: ₦50,000-150,000/year)
- Full control over design and functionality
- Integrates with Paystack, Flutterwave, and local payment gateways
- Best for: Businesses that want full control and have some technical support

**Selar** (Nigerian platform)
- Built for African creators and sellers
- Very easy to set up
- Handles payments and delivery digitally
- Commission-based (5% + ₦100 per sale)
- Best for: Digital products, courses, and simple physical products

### Option 2: Marketplace (Best for Testing the Market)

**Jumia** — List your products on Africa's largest e-commerce platform
**Konga** — Nigeria's second-largest marketplace
**Instagram/Facebook Shops** — Sell directly through social media

**Strategy:** Start on a marketplace to validate demand, then build your own store for higher margins and customer ownership.

## Setting Up Payments

### Essential Payment Integrations

Your store must offer Nigerian-preferred payment methods:

1. **Paystack** — Cards, transfers, USSD (most popular)
2. **Flutterwave** — Alternative with strong international support
3. **Bank transfer** — Many Nigerians still prefer direct bank transfers
4. **Pay on delivery** — Some customers will not pay upfront (manage carefully)

**Pro tip:** Offer at least 3 payment options. A customer who cannot pay their preferred way will abandon their cart.

## Logistics and Delivery

### The Biggest Challenge in Nigerian E-Commerce

Delivery in Nigeria is complex. Poor road infrastructure, inconsistent address systems, and security concerns in some areas make logistics the hardest part of e-commerce.

### Delivery Options

**Self-delivery** — Use your own team for local deliveries (within your city)
- Best for: Hyperlocal businesses
- Cost: ₦500-2,000 per delivery depending on distance

**Third-party logistics (3PL):**
- **GIG Logistics** — Nationwide coverage, reliable
- **Sendbox** — Good for smaller merchants
- **Max.ng** — Fast delivery within Lagos
- **DHL / UPS** — For international shipping

### Delivery Strategy

- Offer free delivery above a certain order value (₦15,000+)
- Clearly communicate delivery timelines (Nigerian customers are used to 1-5 days)
- Provide tracking when possible
- Partner with a pickup point for customers who want to collect

## Building Trust With Nigerian Customers

Trust is the biggest barrier to e-commerce in Nigeria. Scams have made customers cautious.

### Trust-Building Strategies

1. **Real contact information** — Display your phone number, address, and email prominently
2. **Customer reviews** — Show real reviews with real names
3. **Social proof** — Share customer photos using your products
4. **Transparent policies** — Clear return, refund, and delivery policies
5. **Secure checkout** — Show security badges (Paystack, SSL certificate)
6. **WhatsApp availability** — Most Nigerian customers want to talk before buying
7. **Business registration** — Display your CAC registration number

## Marketing Your Nigerian E-Commerce Store

### What Works in Nigeria

**WhatsApp Marketing** — The most effective channel. Build broadcast lists, share product updates, and handle customer service.

**Instagram Shopping** — Tag products in posts and stories. Nigerian consumers browse Instagram to discover products.

**Influencer Marketing** — Nigerian micro-influencers (5K-50K followers) have high engagement and are affordable (₦10,000-100,000 per post).

**Facebook Ads** — Still effective for targeting Nigerian demographics. Start with ₦50,000-100,000/month.

**SEO** — Optimise for Nigerian search queries: "buy [product] in Lagos," "[product] price in Nigeria."

### Content Marketing for E-Commerce

- Product demos and tutorials (video is huge in Nigeria)
- Customer testimonials and unboxing videos
- Behind-the-scenes content (builds trust)
- Educational content about your product category

## Common Mistakes Nigerian E-Commerce Businesses Make

- **Poor mobile experience** — 60%+ of Nigerian web traffic is mobile. If your store does not work perfectly on phones, you lose.
- **Ignoring customer service** — Slow WhatsApp responses kill sales. Aim for response time under 5 minutes during business hours.
- **Bad product photos** — Nigerian customers need to see what they are buying. Invest in good photography.
- **Complicated checkout** — Every extra step reduces conversion. Minimise form fields, offer guest checkout.
- **Not preparing for peak seasons** — Black Friday, Christmas, Back to School — stock up and prepare logistics in advance.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need a CAC registration to sell online in Nigeria?**

Yes, for credibility and legal compliance. Registration costs ₦15,000-50,000 for most small businesses. It takes 2-4 weeks. You can start selling informally while the registration processes.

**Should I use Jumia or my own store?**

Both. Use Jumia for reach and validation. Use your own store for higher margins and customer ownership. Smart merchants use the marketplace to acquire customers and the own store to retain them.

**How do I handle returns in Nigeria?**

Have a clear return policy. Offer return within 7 days for defective items. Customer pays return shipping unless the item was wrong. Build return cost (5-10% of revenue) into your pricing.

**What payment method do most Nigerians prefer?**

Bank transfers are still the most used, but cards (debit cards) are growing fastest. Pay on delivery remains popular for first-time buyers. Offer all three.
