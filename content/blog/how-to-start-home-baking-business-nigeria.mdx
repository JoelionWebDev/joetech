Every weekend in Lagos, Abuja, Port Harcourt and Ibadan, thousands of birthday cakes, wedding cakes, cupcakes, doughnuts and loaves of bread change hands. Behind many of them is not a big factory but a home baker working from a kitchen in Lekki, Surulere, Gwarinpa or Bodija. Home baking is one of the most realistic small businesses you can start in Nigeria today: the demand is constant, the startup cost is low compared with opening a shop, and you can grow it one order at a time while keeping your day job.

## Why a Home Baking Business Works in Nigeria

Nigerians celebrate a lot. Birthdays, weddings, naming ceremonies, graduations, church anniversaries, office send-offs, Valentine's Day, Christmas and Eid all need cake, small chops or pastries. In Lagos alone, a single weekend can see hundreds of events happening at the same time, and most of them need a cake.

There are three reasons home baking is a smart starting point. First, the entry cost is low. You do not need to rent a shop, which in Lekki or Ikeja can cost ₦1,500,000 to ₦3,000,000 a year before you sell a single cake. You start from your kitchen. Second, the demand is repeatable. A customer who loves your cake for a birthday will come back for a wedding, a baby's naming ceremony and a Christmas hamper. Third, the business is visible on social media. A beautiful cake photographs well, and a single viral reel of your decorating process can bring in more orders than a month of flyers.

## Step 1: Decide What You Will Bake

Do not try to bake everything. Pick a lane and get very good at it before you expand. Your niche determines your equipment, your ingredients, your prices and your customers.

Here are the most profitable niches for a Nigerian home baker:

**Celebration cakes.** Birthday and wedding cakes are the highest-margin products. A well-decorated 8-inch birthday cake can sell for ₦35,000 to ₦60,000 in Lagos, while the ingredients may cost ₦12,000 to ₦20,000. Wedding cakes start from around ₦150,000 and can reach ₦500,000 or more for multi-tier designs. The downside is that this work is seasonal and deadline-driven. You must deliver on the exact day, and mistakes are very visible.

**Cupcakes, doughnuts and pastries.** These sell in volume. A box of six cupcakes goes for ₦6,000 to ₦12,000 depending on your area and decoration. Doughnuts sell for ₦800 to ₦2,000 each in Lagos. Offices, schools and churches buy these in bulk for events and meetings. The margins per item are smaller, but the orders are steadier.

**Small chops and snacks.** Spring rolls, samosas, puff puff, meat pies and chicken pies are staples at Nigerian parties. A pack of 20 small chops sells for ₦8,000 to ₦15,000. Many home bakers combine this with cakes, because a customer planning a party often needs both.

## Step 2: Learn the Skill Properly

Talent helps, but training is what makes a business. Before you take paid orders, invest in learning. A cake that sinks in the middle or tastes of raw flour will cost you more in lost customers than any training ever would.

You can learn in several ways. YouTube has excellent free tutorials on sponge cakes, buttercream, fondant and Nigerian-style cake decorating. Channels that focus on home baking with a hand mixer are more useful than professional studio videos that assume you own a ₦500,000 oven. Paid online classes from Nigerian bakers on WhatsApp or Instagram cost between ₦10,000 and ₦50,000 and often include recipes, costing sheets and a support group. Physical baking classes in Lagos or Abuja cost ₦50,000 to ₦150,000 for a weekend course, and the hands-on practice is worth it if you can afford it.

One skill many new bakers skip is cake costing. You must know the exact cost of every cake you sell. If you guess, you will either overprice and lose customers or underprice and work for nothing. A simple costing sheet lists every ingredient, its quantity, its price per unit and its cost in that cake, plus the box, board, gas, electricity and your delivery cost. We will work through a pricing example later in this guide.

## Step 3: Write a Simple Business Plan

You do not need a 30-page document. A one-page plan is enough. Write down your niche, your target customers, your starting budget, your prices and your monthly target.

Your target customers matter more than your recipes. A baker selling to bankers in Victoria Island can charge ₦60,000 for a cake that would sell for ₦25,000 in a smaller town. Decide who you are baking for: working mothers ordering birthday cakes, brides planning weddings, offices ordering pastries, or churches ordering for events. Each group orders differently, pays differently and finds you in different places.

Set a realistic first-month target. A sensible goal for a new home baker is 10 to 15 orders in the first month, earning ₦150,000 to ₦300,000 in revenue. After your ingredient costs, gas, packaging and delivery, you should aim to keep 40 to 50 percent as profit. If you earn ₦200,000 in revenue and spend ₦110,000 on costs, your profit is ₦90,000. That is a solid start, and it grows as your customer base grows.

## Step 4: Sort Out the Legal and Safety Basics

A home baking business is a food business, and food safety is not optional. You are feeding people, including children. Customers must trust that your kitchen is clean and your ingredients are safe.

Register your business name with the Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC). A business name registration costs around ₦10,000 to ₦15,000 and gives you a certificate you can show customers and use to open a business bank account. It also makes you look professional when a corporate client asks for an invoice. You can register online through the CAC post-incorporation portal, or through an agent if you prefer help with the process.

Keep your kitchen clean and separate your baking tools from everyday cooking where possible. Store flour, sugar and butter in sealed containers, away from heat and pests. Check expiry dates on baking powder, yeast and food colouring. Buy your ingredients from trusted suppliers, not from the cheapest roadside seller whose flour may be adulterated.

Open a separate bank account or a dedicated mobile money wallet for the business. Mixing business money with your personal spending is the fastest way to lose track of whether the business is actually making money. When a customer pays, the money goes into the business account. When you buy flour, it comes out of the same account. At the end of each month you can see exactly what came in and what went out.

## Step 5: Buy the Right Equipment

You do not need a professional bakery setup to start. Begin with the essentials and add equipment as your orders grow. Buying everything at once is one of the biggest mistakes new bakers make.

**The essentials.** A reliable oven is your most important purchase. A gas oven with a good thermostat is popular in Nigeria because it works during power outages, which is a real problem with electric ovens. A decent tabletop gas oven costs ₦150,000 to ₦300,000. If you already have a working oven, start with it. A stand mixer or a strong hand mixer costs ₦45,000 to ₦150,000 depending on brand and size. You will also need baking pans in 6-inch, 8-inch and 10-inch sizes, a turntable for decorating, piping bags and nozzles, a spatula, measuring cups and a kitchen scale. A kitchen scale, which costs around ₦8,000 to ₦15,000, is not optional. Baking by weight is far more accurate than baking by cups, and accuracy is what makes your cakes consistent.

**Power reality.** Nigeria's power supply is unreliable, and baking cannot wait for the electricity distribution company. Budget for gas as your primary fuel and keep a small generator or an inverter as backup for your mixer and fridge. A small generator that can run a mixer and a fridge costs ₦150,000 to ₦250,000. Factor fuel into your prices. If you spend ₦5,000 on fuel for a weekend of baking, that cost belongs in your prices, not in your pocket.

Here is a realistic startup budget for a home baking business in Lagos:

- Oven (gas, tabletop): ₦200,000
- Mixer (hand mixer to start): ₦60,000
- Baking pans, turntable and tools: ₦70,000
- Kitchen scale and measuring tools: ₦15,000
- Initial ingredients stock: ₦80,000
- Packaging (boxes, boards, ribbons): ₦30,000
- CAC business name registration: ₦12,000
- Branding (logo, stickers, flyers): ₦25,000
- **Total: roughly ₦492,000**

## Step 6: Source Ingredients Smartly

Your profit lives or dies on ingredient costs. Buy from wholesale markets where possible. In Lagos, Mile 12, Oyingbo and the big markets in Ikeja offer flour, sugar and butter cheaper than supermarkets, especially when you buy in bulk. A 50kg bag of flour costs significantly less per kilogram than small retail packs. If you bake every week, bulk buying pays for itself quickly.

Build relationships with two or three reliable suppliers. A supplier who knows you will call you when prices drop or when a fresh batch arrives. Keep a simple price list of what each supplier charges for flour, sugar, butter, eggs, milk, cocoa powder and fondant. Prices move often, so check them monthly and update your cake prices when your costs rise. If butter jumps from ₦6,000 to ₦8,000 per kilogram, a cake that used to cost you ₦12,000 to make may now cost ₦14,500. If you do not adjust your price, your profit disappears quietly.

## Step 7: Price Your Cakes Properly

Pricing is where most home bakers fail. Many charge what a neighbour charges, or what feels fair, without ever adding up their costs. The result is a busy baker who earns almost nothing.

Use this simple formula: **Price = ingredient cost + packaging + power and gas + delivery + your labour + profit margin.**

Let us work through a real example. A standard 8-inch vanilla birthday cake with buttercream, serving about 15 people:

- Flour, sugar, eggs, butter, milk, vanilla, baking powder: ₦9,500
- Buttercream ingredients: ₦4,000
- Cake box and board: ₦2,500
- Gas and power share: ₦1,500
- Delivery (within your area): ₦2,000
- Your labour (3 hours at ₦2,000 per hour): ₦6,000
- **Total cost: ₦25,500**

If you want a 30 percent profit margin, your price should be around ₦35,000. If you sell it for ₦25,000 because that is what a competitor charges, you are paying the customer to eat your cake. Labour is not free. Your time has value, and the hours you spend baking are hours you cannot spend on anything else.

Set a minimum order value. Many bakers refuse orders below ₦15,000 or ₦20,000 because small orders consume the same time as big ones. Take a 50 percent deposit before you start baking, and the balance on delivery. The deposit protects you if a customer cancels after you have bought ingredients. State this clearly when a customer orders: "A 50 percent deposit confirms your order." Customers respect a baker who is organised.

## Step 8: Brand and Package Your Cakes

Your cake will be photographed before it is eaten. In Nigeria, a birthday cake is a status symbol. The person who ordered it will post it on Instagram and WhatsApp status before anyone cuts it. Your packaging and presentation are free advertising.

Choose a business name that is easy to remember and easy to spell. Avoid long names and complicated spellings. Get a simple logo made. A clean logo on a cake box, a sticker on the box and a thank-you card inside the box cost very little and make a big difference. A branded box that costs ₦2,000 looks like a ₦40,000 cake. A cake handed over in a plain nylon bag looks like an afterthought.

Invest in good cake boxes. Sturdy boxes protect your cake during delivery on Nigerian roads, which are not gentle. A collapsed cake is a refund, a bad review and a lost customer. Test your boxes on a bumpy road before you trust them with a wedding cake.

## Step 9: Find Customers on WhatsApp and Instagram

You do not need a shop to find customers. You need a phone, good photos and consistency.

**WhatsApp is your shop.** Set up WhatsApp Business, which is free. Fill in your business name, your address area, your opening hours and a catalogue of your products with prices. Post your cakes on your WhatsApp status every day, or at least three times a week. Your status is seen by people who already know you, and they are your warmest audience. When a customer orders, save their number and their birthday. A month before their next birthday, send a friendly message: "Your birthday is coming up. Would you like to order your cake early?"

**Instagram is your showroom.** Post clear, well-lit photos of your cakes. Natural daylight near a window beats a dark kitchen photo every time. Film short reels of your decorating process. A 30-second video of buttercream being smoothed onto a cake, or fondant being rolled out, regularly gets more views than a still photo. Post consistently, at least four times a week, and use location tags for your area in Lagos, Abuja or wherever you are based. Customers search for "baker in Lekki" or "cakes in Abuja", and location tags help them find you.

Reply to enquiries fast. In Nigeria, a customer who messages three bakers will order from the one who replies first with a clear price. Keep a saved reply with your price list and your ordering process, so you can respond in seconds. Something like: "Hello, thank you for reaching out. Our 8-inch birthday cake is ₦35,000 and serves 15 people. A 50 percent deposit confirms your order. What date do you need it?" Fast, clear replies win orders.

## Step 10: Deliver Reliably

A beautiful cake that arrives late or damaged is a failure. Delivery is part of your product, so treat it with the same care as baking.

For orders within your neighbourhood, deliver yourself if you can. It is cheaper and safer. For longer distances in Lagos or Abuja, use a trusted dispatch rider. Test a rider with a small order before you trust them with a wedding cake. Agree on the fee before the order is placed, and build it into your price or charge it separately and clearly.

Always confirm the delivery time with the customer the day before. A short message works: "Good evening, your cake will be delivered tomorrow by 11 am. Please confirm someone will be available to receive it." That message prevents missed deliveries and wasted trips.

## Step 11: Manage Your Money Like a Business

A baking business that cannot track its money will not survive. Keep it simple but consistent.

Every week, record your income and your expenses in a notebook or a simple spreadsheet on your phone. Write down every order, what you charged, what you spent on ingredients, gas, packaging and delivery, and what was left. At the end of the month, add it all up. If you earned ₦300,000 and spent ₦180,000, your profit is ₦120,000. That number tells you whether the business is working.

Pay yourself a salary. Decide on a fixed amount you take from the business each month, and leave the rest in the business account to buy ingredients and equipment. Many small businesses fail because the owner spends the revenue as if it were profit. The money in your business account is not your salary until you have paid for flour, gas and boxes.

As your orders grow, consider a simple invoicing tool. A free online invoice generator can help you send professional invoices to corporate clients who need documentation before they pay. Keep your records clean from the start, because clean records make it easier to get a loan, a grant or a bigger corporate client later.

## Conclusion

Starting a home baking business in Nigeria does not require a fortune. It requires a skill, a clean kitchen, a reliable oven, honest pricing and the discipline to reply to customers quickly and deliver on time. Start with one niche, master it, charge properly, take deposits and let your work speak for itself. Your first ten customers will come from your neighbours, your church, your WhatsApp status and your Instagram page. Your next hundred will come from their recommendations.

The cakes are already being ordered every weekend in your city. The question is whether the next order goes to you or to someone else. Set up your WhatsApp Business profile today, bake your best cake this week, photograph it in good light and post it. Your baking business starts with that first order.
