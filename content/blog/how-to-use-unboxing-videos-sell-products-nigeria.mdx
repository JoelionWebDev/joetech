## Why Unboxing Videos Sell So Well

There is a simple reason unboxing videos are so popular in Nigeria: they answer the question every online buyer asks before they pay. "Is this product real? Will it look like the photo? Is it worth the money?" A photo can be edited. A description can be exaggerated. But a video of someone opening a real package, holding the product, and showing it from every angle is much harder to fake — and customers know it.

When a buyer in Abuja watches a seller in Lagos open a box of hair, shoes, a skincare set or a phone, they are inspecting the product, not just watching entertainment. They are checking the packaging, the colour, the size, the quality of the stitching, and the way the product feels in someone's hands. That inspection builds trust, and trust is the biggest barrier to online sales in Nigeria.

Unboxing videos also work because they create excitement. The slow reveal, the sound of the box opening, the first look at the product — all of it taps into the same feeling a customer gets when their own order arrives. If you can recreate that feeling on video, the viewer starts to imagine the product in their own hands. That imagination is often what turns a viewer into a buyer.

You do not need a studio, an expensive camera, or a professional editor. A smartphone, good light, a clean background and a clear plan are enough.

## Choose the Right Product to Unbox

Not every product needs an unboxing video, and not every product makes a good one. Start with the products that customers ask the most questions about, or the products where quality is hard to judge from a photo alone.

Good candidates include:

- **Fashion items:** wigs, shoes, bags, dresses and Ankara outfits, where customers want to see the true colour, the fabric and the fit.
- **Beauty products:** skincare sets, perfumes and makeup, where customers want to see the packaging, the size and the texture.
- **Electronics and gadgets:** phones, earphones, smart watches and chargers, where customers want to see what is in the box and how the product works.
- **Food and gift hampers:** cakes, snacks and gift boxes, where the presentation is part of the product.
- **New arrivals:** any product you have just restocked, so customers can see it is available now.

Start with one product. A focused video about a single item is clearer than a rushed video that tries to show everything you sell. Once you are comfortable, you can film unboxings for your other best sellers, one at a time.

### Show the Product Customers Are Hesitant About

If you notice that customers keep asking the same questions — "Is the colour really like the photo?", "Is the shoe leather or synthetic?", "How big is the bag?" — that product is a perfect candidate for an unboxing video. The video can answer those questions visually, so you spend less time replying to the same messages and more time taking orders. Save the questions you receive on WhatsApp for a week, then choose the product with the most questions and film it first.

## Plan Before You Press Record

A good unboxing video looks natural but is planned. Before you film, write down the three or four things you want to show. For a wig, that might be the packaging, the hair texture, the length, and how it looks when worn. For a shoe, it might be the box, the shoe from the front and side, the sole, and the inside. For a skincare set, it might be each item in the set, the size of each bottle, and the texture of the cream.

Keep videos short. For Instagram Reels and TikTok, 30 to 60 seconds is often ideal. For a WhatsApp status, even shorter works — 15 to 30 seconds. For YouTube Shorts, aim for under 60 seconds. Customers in Nigeria scroll quickly, and a video that takes too long to get to the product will lose them. Show the product in the first three seconds. Do not spend the first ten seconds introducing yourself or explaining what you are about to do. Start with the box in your hands and open it.

### A Simple Script You Can Follow

You do not need to memorise a script, but a simple outline helps you stay on track:

1. **Opening (0–3 seconds):** Hold up the package. "This is the luxury wig our customers have been asking about."
2. **The unboxing (3–20 seconds):** Open the box slowly. Show the packaging, any extras, and the product itself.
3. **The details (20–40 seconds):** Hold the product close to the camera. Show the texture, the colour, the size, and any important features.
4. **The result (40–50 seconds):** Show the product in use — worn, applied, or placed where it belongs.
5. **The call to action (50–60 seconds):** Tell the viewer the price, how to order, and that stock is limited if it is true.

Practise the sequence once without recording, so you know where each item is and you do not fumble on camera. A smooth, confident unboxing looks more professional than a perfect script delivered nervously.

## Film With Your Phone: Light, Sound and Background

You do not need professional equipment, but you do need three things: good light, clear sound, and a clean background.

### Light

Natural daylight is your best friend. Film near a window during the day, or outside in soft shade. Avoid harsh midday sun, which creates strong shadows, and avoid filming at night under a single yellow bulb, which makes colours look wrong. If you must film indoors, use two light sources — one from the front and one from the side — so the product is evenly lit. Before you record, take a test photo and check whether the product colour looks true. If a red dress looks orange on camera, your light is the problem, not the dress.

### Sound

Customers need to hear you clearly. Film in a quiet room, away from traffic, generators, music and talking. Speak in a natural, friendly voice, as if you are talking to one customer, not performing for a crowd. If you are filming on a busy street in Lagos, the background noise will distract viewers and make your video feel unprofessional. A quiet corner of your shop or your home is better than a noisy market, even if the market feels more authentic.

If you add background music, keep it low so it does not drown out your voice.

### Background

A clean, uncluttered background makes your product stand out. A plain wall, a clean table, or a simple cloth in a neutral colour works well. Remove anything that distracts from the product: dirty surfaces, unrelated items, or a messy room. If you sell fashion, a tidy shelf or a clothing rail in the background can add context without distracting. If you sell food, a clean kitchen counter works. The rule is simple: the viewer should notice the product first, not the background.

## Be Honest: Show the Real Product, Not a Fantasy

The fastest way to destroy trust with unboxing videos is to show one thing and deliver another. If the product in the video is a premium version and the product you send is a cheaper one, customers will notice, they will complain, and they will warn others. In Nigeria's close-knit online shopping community, a bad review on WhatsApp status or TikTok can travel far and fast.

Film the exact product you sell, in the exact packaging the customer will receive. Show the real colour, the real size, and any imperfections honestly. If the bag has a small inner pocket, show it. If the shoe runs small, say so. That honesty may lose you a sale from a customer who wanted a different size, but it will save you from a return, a refund, and a damaged reputation.

### Let Real Customers Unbox Too

One of the most powerful forms of unboxing video is not filmed by you at all — it is filmed by your customer. When a happy customer receives their order and films themselves opening it, that video carries more trust than any video you make yourself, because it is independent proof that your product is real and your delivery works.

Encourage customers to share their unboxing. You can include a short note in the package: "We would love to see your unboxing! Tag us or send us the video." Offer a small thank-you, such as a discount on their next order, for customers who share a video. Always ask permission before you repost a customer's video on your own page, and always credit them. A reposted customer unboxing, with the customer's own words, is one of the most persuasive sales tools a Nigerian small business can have.

## Edit Simply and Post Where Your Customers Are

Editing does not need to be complicated. Most phones have a built-in editor that can trim the start and end of a video, adjust the brightness, and add text. Use it to cut out pauses, mistakes and fumbling. Add text on the screen for the key details: the product name, the price, and how to order. Many viewers watch videos without sound, especially on public transport or at work, so on-screen text ensures your message still gets through even when the sound is off.

Keep the pace lively. Cut between shots — the box, the product close-up, the product in use — rather than holding one long shot for a minute. A video that changes angle every few seconds holds attention better than a static shot. End the video with a clear call to action on screen as well as spoken: "Order now on WhatsApp — link in bio" or "Send a DM to order — only 10 pieces left."

### Where to Post

Post your unboxing video where your customers already spend time:

- **Instagram Reels:** good for fashion, beauty and lifestyle products, and for reaching new customers through discovery.
- **TikTok:** good for short, fun, energetic unboxings that can reach a wide audience quickly.
- **WhatsApp Status:** good for your existing customers and contacts, who are the most likely to buy. Post the video on your status the day the product arrives in stock.
- **Facebook:** good for reaching an older audience and for sharing in groups where your customers gather.
- **YouTube Shorts:** good for building a library of unboxing videos that customers can find when they search for your product.

You do not need to post everywhere at once. Start with the platform where most of your customers already find you. Once the video is working there, repurpose it for the other platforms. The same 45-second video can be posted on Reels, TikTok and Shorts with only small changes to the caption.

## Write Captions That Turn Viewers Into Buyers

A video without a good caption is a missed opportunity. The caption should do three things: describe the product, state the price, and tell the viewer exactly how to order.

Keep captions short and clear. For example: "Luxury bone-straight wig, 22 inches, ₦85,000. Comes with a free wig cap. Send a DM or WhatsApp 0803 123 4567 to order. Delivery available in Lagos and nationwide." That caption gives the viewer everything they need. They do not have to ask for the price, and they do not have to guess how to order.

Pin a comment with your WhatsApp number or ordering link, so it is easy to find. Reply to comments quickly, especially in the first hour after posting, when the platform is deciding whether to show your video to more people. A video with comments and replies is more likely to be promoted than a video with no engagement.

### Use Hashtags Sparingly

Hashtags can help new customers find your video, but too many look desperate. Use three to five relevant hashtags, such as #LagosVendor, #WigsInLagos, or #NigerianBusiness, rather than twenty generic ones. Location hashtags help, because many Nigerian customers search for sellers in their city. A hashtag like #AbujaVendor or #PHVendor tells a buyer in that city that you are close enough to deliver quickly.

## Measure, Learn and Improve

After you post an unboxing video, pay attention to what happens. How many views did it get? How many comments and shares? How many people messaged you to order? How many actually bought? Those numbers tell you whether the video worked.

If a video gets many views but few orders, the problem may be the price, the call to action, or the product itself. Perhaps viewers liked the video but did not know how to order. Make the ordering step clearer next time. If a video gets few views, the problem may be the opening — the first three seconds did not grab attention. Try starting with the product already out of the box, or with a bold statement: "This is the shoe everyone is asking about."

Film regularly, not just once. A seller who posts one unboxing video a week will learn faster and build a stronger presence than a seller who posts one video and waits for results. Each video is a chance to improve: better light, clearer sound, a smoother reveal, a stronger call to action. Over time, you will develop a style that your customers recognise and look forward to.

### A Simple Weekly Routine

Set aside one hour a week for unboxing videos:

- **Monday:** Choose the product to feature, based on customer questions or new stock.
- **Tuesday:** Plan the shots and write your simple outline.
- **Wednesday:** Film the video in daylight, in a quiet, clean space.
- **Thursday:** Edit the video on your phone, add text, and write the caption with price and ordering details.
- **Friday:** Post it on your main platform, share it to your WhatsApp status, and reply to comments quickly.

That routine takes little time, costs almost nothing, and gives you a steady stream of fresh content that directly supports sales. After a month, you will have four videos, and you will know which products and which styles your customers respond to best.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Filming in poor light.** A dark or yellow-tinted video makes even a beautiful product look cheap. Film in daylight whenever you can.

**Talking too much and showing too little.** Customers want to see the product. Keep your talking short and let the visuals do the work.

**Hiding the price.** A video that never mentions the price will generate many "How much?" comments and few orders. State the price clearly, in the video or the caption.

**Using a product you do not actually sell.** Showing a sample that is better than what you deliver is dishonest and will damage your reputation.

**Posting once and stopping.** One video rarely changes a business. Consistency is what builds trust and sales over time.

**Ignoring comments.** A viewer who asks a question and gets no reply is unlikely to buy. Reply quickly, politely and helpfully.

## Conclusion: Let Customers See Before They Buy

Unboxing videos work because they remove doubt. They let a customer in Port Harcourt see what a seller in Lagos is really offering, before any money changes hands. They build trust, create excitement, and give customers a reason to choose you over a seller who only posts still photos.

You do not need expensive equipment or professional skills to start. Choose a product your customers ask about, plan the shots, film in good light with a clean background, be honest about what you show, and end with a clear price and a simple way to order. Post where your customers are, reply to their comments, and learn from each video.

Film your first unboxing this week. It need not be perfect, just real. Your customers will notice the difference, and your sales will show it.

## Checklist: Unboxing Videos That Sell

- Choose a product customers ask questions about or a new arrival.
- Plan the shots: packaging, product details, product in use, call to action.
- Show the product in the first three seconds and keep the video under 60 seconds.
- Film in natural daylight, in a quiet place, with a clean background.
- Show the exact product and packaging the customer will receive.
- Add on-screen text with the product name, price and how to order.
- Post on the platform where most of your customers already find you.
- Write a short caption with price, ordering details and a clear next step.
- Encourage happy customers to share their own unboxing videos.
- Track views, messages and sales, and improve one thing in each new video.
