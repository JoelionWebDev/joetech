Instagram is where many Nigerian customers discover new businesses. A woman in Lekki scrolling through her feed in the evening might see a dress, a plate of food, a cleaning service or a phone accessory and decide to buy it the same day. A young man in Abuja might save a post from a barber or a shoe seller and visit the shop that weekend. That is why Instagram advertising matters for a small business: it puts your product in front of people who are already interested, in the places they already spend their time.

Many small business owners in Nigeria know Instagram ads exist, but they are not sure where to start. Some have boosted a post once, spent money, and got likes but no sales. Others worry that ads are too expensive, too technical, or only for big companies. The truth is simpler. Instagram ads work best when you have a clear goal, a clear audience, a good picture or video, and a small budget you can test and learn from. You do not need a marketing degree. You need a plan, a little patience, and the discipline to check your results.

This guide walks you through the whole process, step by step. You will learn how to prepare your Instagram account, how to set up your first ad properly, how to choose who sees it, how much to spend in naira, what kind of creative works in Nigeria, and how to tell whether your ad is working. By the end, you should be able to run your first small campaign with confidence and improve it week by week.

## Start With a Clear Goal Before You Spend One Naira

Every good Instagram ad starts before you open the Ads Manager. It starts with a simple question: what do you want this ad to do? If you cannot answer that question clearly, you will spend money on the wrong thing and blame the platform when nothing happens.

Most Nigerian small businesses want one of four things. The first is more WhatsApp messages. A food vendor, a tailor, a makeup artist or a phone seller often wants customers to tap a button and start a chat. The second is more profile visits and followers, which suits a new business that needs people to discover it. The third is more website visits or orders, which suits a business with an online shop or a booking page. The fourth is more awareness in a local area, which suits a salon, a restaurant or a shop that depends on people nearby.

Pick one goal for your first campaign. Do not try to get followers, sales, website visits and WhatsApp messages from one ad at the same time. A single goal makes your ad easier to design and easier to judge. If your goal is WhatsApp messages, your ad should show your product clearly and tell people to tap to chat. If your goal is profile visits, your ad should show what your page is about and why someone should follow it.

Write your goal down in one sentence. For example: "I want 30 WhatsApp chats from people in Lagos who want to order lunch packs this week." That sentence is specific, it names the place, the action and the time. It also gives you something to measure. At the end of the week, you can count the chats and decide whether the ad worked.

## Prepare Your Instagram Account Properly

Before you run an ad, your Instagram profile must be ready to receive the people the ad brings. An ad that sends people to a confusing or empty profile wastes money. People decide in a few seconds whether to trust a business page, so make those seconds count.

First, switch to a professional account if you have not done so already. Go to your profile settings, choose "Switch to professional account", and pick either Business or Creator. A business account gives you access to insights, contact buttons and advertising tools. It is free, and it takes only a few minutes.

Second, fix your bio. Your bio should say what you sell, who it is for, and where you are based, in plain language. For example: "Ready-to-wear dresses for women in Lagos. Delivery nationwide. Chat on WhatsApp to order." Add your location, such as Lekki, Ikeja, Wuse or Port Harcourt, and a working WhatsApp link or shop link in your bio.

Third, make sure your last nine posts look good. When someone taps your ad and visits your profile, they will see a grid of your recent posts. If the grid is empty, blurry or full of random reposts, they will leave. Post at least nine clear photos or videos of your real products, your shop, your work or your happy customers before you run ads. Use natural light where you can, and show the product the way a customer would actually receive it. A food vendor should show the real plate, not a stock photo of food from another country. A tailor should show real clothes on real people.

Fourth, sort out your contact options. Add a WhatsApp button or a clear WhatsApp number in your bio. Many Nigerian customers prefer to chat on WhatsApp before they buy. They want to ask about price, size, delivery or availability. If they cannot reach you easily, they will move to the next seller. Decide who will reply to messages and how quickly. A reply within an hour is good. A reply the next day often means a lost sale, because the customer has already bought from someone else.

Finally, connect your Instagram account to a Facebook Page. Instagram ads run through Meta Ads Manager, which is the same system that runs Facebook ads, and it works best when your Instagram account is linked to a Facebook Page for your business. You can do this from your Instagram settings under "Accounts Centre" or from your Facebook Page settings. If you do not have a Facebook Page for your business, create a simple one with your business name, your logo or a clear photo, and your contact details. You do not need to post on Facebook every day, but the connection makes advertising much smoother.

## Understand the Two Ways to Run an Instagram Ad

There are two main ways to run an Instagram ad in Nigeria, and it helps to know the difference before you choose.

The first way is to boost a post directly from the Instagram app. You pick a post that is already on your page, tap "Boost post", choose a goal, choose an audience, set a budget and a duration, and publish. This is the easiest way to start. It takes only a few minutes, and you do not need to learn a new dashboard. Boosting works well for a simple goal, such as getting more people to see a new product or to send you a WhatsApp message.

The second way is to use Meta Ads Manager, either on your computer at business.facebook.com/adsmanager or through the Meta Ads Manager app. This is more powerful. It gives you more control over who sees your ad, where it appears, how much you spend, and what you measure. You can run several ads at once, test different pictures against each other, and see detailed results. It takes longer to learn, but it is the better choice once you are spending regularly.

One practical note on payment: Instagram ads are paid for in naira through a debit card or a virtual dollar card, depending on what Meta accepts for your account at the time. Many Nigerian business owners use a naira debit card from their bank or a virtual card from a fintech service. Make sure the card you add has enough funds for your budget, and keep an eye on your bank alerts so you know exactly what was charged. If a card is declined, Ads Manager will pause your ads until you fix the payment method, so check your payment settings before you launch.

## Choose the Right Post or Creative for Your Ad

Your ad creative is the picture or video people see, plus the words that go with it. This is the most important part of your ad. Good targeting cannot save a bad picture, and a big budget cannot save a confusing message. Spend most of your preparation time here.

Use a real photo or video of your product or service. Customers in Nigeria are quick to spot a fake or borrowed image, and they trust real photos far more. Show the product clearly, in good light, against a clean background. If you sell food, show the food in the container the customer will receive. If you sell clothes, show them worn by a person, with the size and fit visible. If you offer a service, such as cleaning, hair styling or car repair, show a before-and-after or show your team at work.

Video often performs better than a single photo, especially a short video of 15 to 30 seconds. A video can show a dress moving, a meal being packed, a haircut taking shape, or a product being used. You do not need a professional camera. A good phone, natural daylight and a steady hand are enough to start. Film in a bright place, keep the background tidy, and speak clearly if you add a voiceover. Add captions or text on the video, because many people watch without sound.

Your caption should be short and direct. Say what the product is, who it is for, how much it costs if you are comfortable sharing the price, and what the person should do next. For example: "Soft cotton kaftans for everyday comfort. Sizes M to XXL. 18,000 naira. Tap the WhatsApp button to order. Delivery in Lagos in 24 hours." That caption answers the main questions a buyer will ask. Avoid long paragraphs and vague claims. One clear call to action is better than three competing ones.

## Target the Right People, Not Everyone

One of the biggest mistakes Nigerian small businesses make with Instagram ads is targeting everyone. They set the location to all of Nigeria, the age to 18 to 65, and the interests to everything, and then they wonder why the ad brings likes from people who will never buy. Instagram will happily show your ad to thousands of people, but only a small number of them are your real customers. Your job is to narrow the audience until it matches the people most likely to buy.

Start with location. If you run a salon in Surulere, a restaurant in Port Harcourt, or a shop in Wuse, most of your customers will come from nearby. Target your city, or even a radius around your area, rather than the whole country. If you sell online and deliver nationwide, you can target Lagos, Abuja and Port Harcourt first, because those cities have large numbers of online shoppers, and then expand to other cities once you know what works. Delivery costs also matter: if delivery to a far city costs almost as much as the product, it makes little sense to advertise there.

Next, set an age range that matches your buyers. A business selling school uniforms will target parents, often aged 28 to 50. A business selling trendy sneakers will target younger buyers, often aged 18 to 35. A business selling office wear or home appliances will often target adults aged 25 to 55. Think about who actually pays for your product, not just who likes it. Teenagers may like your sneakers, but their parents may be the ones who pay, so consider who should see the ad.

Then add interests and behaviours that match your product. If you sell fitness wear, target people interested in fitness, gyms and healthy living. If you sell baby clothes, target people interested in parenting and baby care. If you sell phone accessories, target people interested in smartphones and gadgets. Keep your interests focused. Three to five related interests are usually better than twenty unrelated ones, because a focused audience is easier for Instagram to understand and easier for you to judge.

You can also use detailed targeting to exclude people who are unlikely to buy. For example, if you sell premium dresses at 60,000 naira, you may want to focus on people with interests in premium shopping and fashion, rather than a broad audience. If you sell budget items, a broad audience may be fine. There is no single right answer. Start narrow, watch the results, and widen the audience slowly if the ad performs well and you want more reach.

## Set a Budget You Can Afford to Test

Budget is where many small business owners feel nervous, and that is understandable. Money is tight, and nobody wants to waste it. The good news is that you can start small. Instagram ads do not require a large budget to teach you something useful.

A sensible starting budget for a first test is 5,000 to 15,000 naira over three to five days. That is roughly 1,000 to 5,000 naira per day, depending on how long you run it. This is enough to reach a few thousand people in your target area and to see whether they tap, message or follow. It is not enough to judge a business in one day, so give the ad at least three days before you decide it has failed. Instagram needs time to learn who responds to your ad, and the first day is often the weakest.

Set a daily budget rather than a lifetime budget for your first campaign, so you control how much is spent each day. In Ads Manager, you can set a daily budget of, say, 2,000 naira and a duration of five days. That gives you a total spend of 10,000 naira and a clear end date. In the Instagram app, boosting also lets you set a total budget and a number of days. Pick a duration of at least three days. One-day ads rarely give you enough data to learn anything.

Do not increase your budget too quickly. If an ad is working, increase it gradually, for example by 20 to 50 percent, and watch whether the results stay good. If you double or triple the budget overnight, the ad may start reaching less interested people and your cost per message or per click may rise. Grow the budget in steps, and keep testing new creative alongside it.

If an ad is not working after three to five days and a reasonable spend, pause it. Do not keep spending in hope. Change one thing at a time: a new picture, a clearer caption, a narrower audience, or a different goal. Test the change for another three days and compare. This slow, steady testing is how small businesses get better at advertising without burning money.

## Measure Results and Improve Every Week

After your ad has run for three to five days, check the numbers in Ads Manager or in the Instagram app. Look at how many people saw the ad, how many tapped or messaged, and how much each message or click cost. Then compare that with your sales. Ask the customers who message you how they found you, and note which ad brought them. A simple notebook record of date, budget, messages and sales will teach you more than any guesswork.

If an ad brings many messages but few sales, the problem may be your price, your reply speed, or your product photos, not the ad itself. If it brings few messages, try a clearer picture, a simpler caption, or a narrower audience. Change one thing at a time and test again for a few days.

## Your First Campaign Checklist

- Pick one clear goal, such as WhatsApp messages or profile visits.
- Fix your bio, your last nine posts, and your WhatsApp link.
- Connect your Instagram account to a Facebook Page.
- Use a real photo or short video and a short, direct caption.
- Target your city and the age group most likely to buy.
- Start with 5,000 to 15,000 naira over three to five days.
- Check your results, keep the winner, and test one new idea next week.

Instagram ads do not need a big budget to work for a Nigerian small business. They need a clear goal, a ready profile, a real picture of your product, the right audience, and the patience to test and improve. Start small this week, learn from the numbers, and let each campaign teach you how to run the next one better.
