Choosing a school is one of the most careful decisions a parent makes. Long before a family visits in person, they will visit the school website. They will look for what the school teaches, what it costs, how admission works, and whether the school feels safe and serious. A website that answers those questions clearly will earn enquiries. One that hides them will lose families to schools that explain themselves better.

This post explains how to build that stage well: programmes explained in plain language, fee information structured honestly, a simple enquiry flow, and trust signals that help parents feel confident taking the next step.

## Programmes Explained in Plain Language

Parents do not choose a school from a list of programme names alone. They want to understand what their child will learn, how the school day is structured, and what support exists for different ages and needs. Programme pages should answer those questions directly.

For each level or programme, explain who it is for, what subjects or activities it covers, how classes are organised, and what a typical day looks like in broad terms. Avoid jargon and long mission statements that say little. A parent reading on a phone should be able to understand, within a minute, whether this programme fits their child's age and needs.

Also explain progression: how a child moves from one level to the next, what assessments or reports parents receive, and how the school supports children who need extra help or extra challenge. Parents worry about fit as much as about quality. A programme page that addresses fit honestly will earn more serious enquiries than one that only praises the school.

## Fee Information, Structured Honestly

Fees are often the page parents look for first, and the page schools are most tempted to hide. Hiding fee information does not stop parents asking about cost. It only moves the question to a phone call, where an unprepared answer can end the conversation. A clear fee structure, on the other hand, filters for families who can proceed and builds trust with those who enquire.

A good fee page explains the structure without needing to list every possible variation: what the main fee covers, which items are charged separately, such as transport, feeding, or examinations where they apply, and how payment is scheduled across the term or year. It should also explain, in general terms, how to ask about payment plans or available support, where the school offers them, without promising arrangements that do not exist.

Keep the fee page up to date. Nothing damages trust faster than a parent arriving for a visit with fee figures from the website that no longer apply. Review fees each term, update the page at the same time as any change takes effect, and date the information so parents know it is current.

## An Admission Enquiry Flow Parents Can Finish

The enquiry form is where browsing becomes admissions. It should be short, clear, and reassuring. Ask for the parent's name, phone number or WhatsApp contact, the child's age or intended class level, and an optional message. Avoid long forms that ask for detailed academic history, documents, or payment before a family has even spoken to the school. Those steps belong later in the process, once interest is confirmed.

Place the enquiry option where parents naturally look: on programme pages, on the fee page, and on the admissions page itself. On a phone, the enquiry button should be easy to reach without scrolling through long pages. After submission, tell the parent exactly what happens next: that the enquiry has been received, when the school will respond, and whether the next step is a call, a visit, or an application form. A confirmation message plus a WhatsApp or SMS acknowledgement makes the enquiry feel received rather than lost.

The school's response matters as much as the form. Whoever replies should be able to answer common questions about programmes, fees, and visit arrangements, or to connect the parent quickly with someone who can. A slow or vague reply after a good website wastes the work the website did.

## Admissions Steps, Shown Clearly

Many parents abandon an enquiry simply because they do not understand the process. A short, plain-language admissions timeline removes that uncertainty. Show the steps in order: enquiry, visit or interaction where offered, application, assessment where applicable, offer, and enrolment. For each step, state what the parent needs to do and what the school will do in return.

Keep the timeline honest about timing. If visits happen on certain days, say so. If assessments apply only to certain levels, say so. If documents are needed at application stage, list them plainly so families can prepare. Clarity here reduces repeated phone calls and helps families plan, especially those comparing several schools at once.

Also explain key dates where they exist, such as application windows, open days, and term start dates, and keep them updated. An admissions page with last year's dates still showing tells parents the school does not maintain its own information, which is a poor first impression for an institution built on care and detail.

## Trust Signals That Matter to Parents

Parents look for safety, care, and credibility. A school website should show those qualities through real evidence, not decoration. Use genuine photos of classrooms, play areas, libraries, and school life, with permission where children appear, and keep them current. Show the school's location, contact details, and leadership in plain terms, so parents know who they are dealing with.

Where the school holds accreditations, affiliations, or inspection outcomes, state them factually on an about page, without exaggeration. Where former parents or alumni are willing to share experiences, those stories can help, provided they are genuine and shared with permission. Avoid invented claims about results or rankings. Parents compare schools carefully, and a single claim that does not hold up can undo an otherwise strong impression.

Safety information also belongs on the website in general terms: how visitors are received, how children are collected, and how the school communicates with parents during the day. Parents may not ask these questions first, but they notice whether the school has thought about them.

## What to Update Each Term

School websites go out of date faster than many businesses realise, because programmes, fees, dates, and staff change each term. Review the website at the start of every term: programme descriptions, fee information, admissions dates, contact details, and photos. Remove last term's announcements from the homepage, or archive them where parents can still find them without confusion.

Also review enquiries. Which pages produce the most enquiries, and which produce visits? Which questions do parents ask repeatedly? Answer those questions directly on the relevant pages, so each term's website gets clearer. A school that maintains its website term by term signals the same care parents hope to see in its classrooms.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Should a school website list fees openly?**

Listing the fee structure openly usually earns more serious enquiries than hiding it. Parents who cannot find fee information often assume the school is out of reach or that costs will change later. A clear structure, with what is included and what is charged separately, builds trust and saves time for both sides.

**How long should the enquiry form be?**

As short as possible: parent name, phone or WhatsApp contact, child's age or intended level, and an optional message. Detailed information and documents belong later in the process, once the family has spoken to the school and decided to proceed.

**What photos work best on a school website?**

Genuine, current photos of classrooms, learning activities, play areas, and school life, shared with appropriate permission. Avoid stock images that show unfamiliar buildings or children, because parents quickly sense the difference and trust real photos more.

**How important is mobile access for school websites?**

Very important. Many parents will browse on a phone, often while comparing several schools. Programme pages, fee information, and the enquiry button must all work clearly on a small screen, or the school will lose enquiries to competitors whose sites do.

## Help Parents Move From Browsing to Applying

A school website supports admissions when it respects how parents decide: clear programmes, honest fee structure, a visible admissions process, a short enquiry flow, and trust built on real information.

If your website already attracts visitors but few enquiries, start with the enquiry flow and the fee page. Make it easy to ask, easy to understand the cost, and easy to trust the next step, and the same visitors will start becoming applicants.
