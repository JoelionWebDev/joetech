## The Hidden Cost of Shipping an Unconfirmed Order

Every online seller in Nigeria has lived through it. A customer orders a dress on WhatsApp, you package it beautifully, you pay a dispatch rider ₦2,500 to deliver it to Lekki — and when the rider arrives, the customer's phone is switched off. Or the customer says they have changed their mind. Or they say they are not at home and will "call you back," and they never do. The rider returns with your goods, you have lost the delivery fee, and you have lost a day of selling time.

Do that a few times a week and the losses add up fast. A seller who ships ten unconfirmed orders a week and has three of them fail is losing ₦7,500 a week in delivery fees alone — ₦30,000 a month, ₦360,000 a year — before counting the cost of returned goods that come back damaged, or the orders that were never really orders at all.

The fix is simple, but most sellers skip it because it feels like extra work: confirm every order before you ship it. A five-minute confirmation process — a call, a message, a payment check — can save you thousands of naira every week and hours of frustration every month.

This guide gives you a complete order confirmation system: what to check, how to check it, what to say to customers, and how to handle payment, delivery and the awkward cases.

## Why Orders Fail in Nigeria

Before you fix the problem, it helps to understand why it happens. Failed deliveries in Nigeria are rarely about bad luck. They usually come from one of a few causes, and each one has a different solution.

### The customer was never serious

Some people place orders casually, the way they would save an item to a wish list. They are not sure they want it, they are not sure they can afford it, and they have not thought about whether they will be at home to receive it. When the rider calls, they simply do not answer. These customers are not bad people — they just never committed.

### The customer cannot pay

Pay-on-delivery is common in Nigeria, and it creates a specific problem: the customer orders when they are hopeful, but by the time the goods arrive, the money is not there. They may have spent it on something else, or their salary has not come, or the price was higher than they expected once the delivery fee was added. The order fails not because they do not want the product, but because the money is not ready.

### The details were wrong

A wrong address, a wrong phone number, a wrong size, a wrong colour. The customer ordered a size 42 shoe and you sent size 41. The address was "near the filling station in Ikeja" — which filling station, and which Ikeja? Vague details cause failed deliveries every day.

### The customer changed their mind

Between ordering and delivery, the customer found the same product cheaper elsewhere, or a friend talked them out of it, or they simply lost interest. The longer the gap between order and delivery, the more likely this becomes.

### The customer is testing you

A small number of people place fake orders deliberately — to waste your time, to test whether you are real, or as part of a scam. This is rare, but it happens, and a confirmation process catches it early.

Every one of these causes is preventable. The confirmation process below is designed to catch them all before your goods leave your shop.

## Step 1: Take the Order Properly

Confirmation starts at the moment the customer places the order. If you take the order carelessly, you will spend the rest of the process trying to fix the gaps.

### The five details every order needs

For every single order, before you do anything else, collect and write down these five things:

1. **The customer's full name.** Not just "Ada" — "Adaeze Okonkwo." You need the full name for the rider, for your records, and for any payment verification.
2. **A working phone number.** Ideally two numbers — the customer's main number and an alternate. Call the number once while the customer is chatting with you, just to confirm it is correct and that the phone is on.
3. **The exact product, size, colour and quantity.** Repeat it back to the customer: "So that is one Ankara gown, size 14, in the blue and yellow print, for ₦28,000. Is that correct?" Let them confirm before you proceed.
4. **The exact delivery address.** Ask for a full address with a landmark: house number, street, area, and a nearby landmark the rider can find. "Number 12, Admiralty Way, Lekki Phase 1, opposite the supermarket" is an address. "Lekki" is not.
5. **The total amount, including delivery.** Tell the customer the product price plus the delivery fee before they commit. Surprising a customer with a delivery fee at the door is one of the fastest ways to get a rejection.

Write all of this down in a notebook, a spreadsheet, or a simple order form on your phone. Do not rely on memory, and do not rely on scrolling back through a long WhatsApp chat to find the details when the rider is already waiting.

### Use an order template

Create a simple template message you send to every customer after they order. It looks professional, and it makes sure nothing is missed:

"Thank you for your order! Please confirm the details:

- Name: _____
- Product: _____
- Size/Colour: _____
- Quantity: _____
- Delivery address: _____
- Phone number: _____
- Total (including delivery): ₦_____

Please reply 'Confirmed' if everything is correct, and let me know your preferred payment method. Thank you!"

When the customer replies "Confirmed," you have a written record that they agreed to the order, the price, and the address. That record protects you if there is a dispute later.

## Step 2: Confirm With a Call, Not Just a Message

A WhatsApp message is a good start, but a phone call is a far stronger confirmation. A customer who answers a call, speaks to you, and confirms their order out loud is much more likely to receive the delivery than one who simply tapped a reply.

### How to make the confirmation call

Keep it short and friendly — two minutes at most:

"Good afternoon, Mrs. Eze, this is Chiamaka from Chiamaka's Fashion Store. I am calling to confirm your order for the blue Ankara gown, size 14, for ₦28,000 plus ₦2,500 delivery — a total of ₦30,500. Your delivery address is Number 12 Admiralty Way, Lekki Phase 1. Is that correct? And will you be available to receive it tomorrow between 10 a.m. and 2 p.m.?"

Listen to the answers. If the customer hesitates, sounds unsure, or says they need to check with someone, do not ship yet. Say: "No problem at all. Please confirm with me by this evening, and I will send it tomorrow once you are ready." A customer who needs time is telling you the order is not firm.

## Step 3: Get Paid Before You Ship

This is the single most effective step in the whole system. An order that has been paid for almost never fails. An order that has not been paid for fails regularly.

### Payment before delivery

The strongest policy is simple: full payment before dispatch. The customer transfers the money, you confirm it has arrived in your account, and then you ship. This is standard practice for many successful Nigerian online sellers, and most genuine customers accept it — especially if you have reviews, a real business presence, and a clear refund policy.

If a customer is nervous about paying first, understand their fear — they are worried you might not send the goods. Reduce that fear by being transparent: share your business address, your reviews, your social media page, and your phone number. Offer to do a video call showing the product before they pay. Trust is built in both directions.

### If you must offer pay on delivery

Some businesses, especially those selling to new customers or in competitive markets, offer pay on delivery. If you do, protect yourself:

- **Collect a delivery commitment fee.** Ask the customer to pay a small amount upfront — for example, ₦1,000 to ₦2,000, or the delivery fee itself — before you dispatch. This is not the full price, but it is a real commitment. A customer who has paid even ₦1,500 is far more likely to receive the order than one who has paid nothing. Explain it clearly: "We ask for the delivery fee upfront so we can book the rider. The rest you pay when the goods arrive."
- **Set a clear rule for failed deliveries.** Tell the customer upfront: "If the rider arrives and you are not available, the delivery fee is not refundable, and a second delivery will attract another fee." Saying this before you ship prevents arguments afterwards.
- **Limit pay on delivery to your local area.** Offering pay on delivery within Lagos or Abuja, where your rider can return quickly, is manageable. Offering it for a delivery to Port Harcourt or Enugu, where a failed delivery costs you far more, is risky. For long-distance orders, insist on payment before dispatch.

### Verify the payment properly

When a customer says they have paid, check your account before you ship — do not take their word for it, and do not ship based on a screenshot alone, because screenshots can be faked. Wait until the money shows in your bank app or your POS record. For bank transfers, this is usually instant or within a few minutes. If a customer claims they transferred money and it has not arrived after thirty minutes, ask them to check with their bank before you ship.

For larger orders — say, above ₦100,000 — consider asking for payment in two parts: a deposit to confirm the order, and the balance before dispatch. This shares the commitment between you and the customer.

## Step 4: Confirm the Delivery Details With the Rider

Your rider is part of your confirmation system. A good rider does not just carry a parcel — they communicate.

### Before the rider leaves

Give the rider everything they need: the customer's full name, both phone numbers, the exact address with the landmark, the total amount to collect if it is pay on delivery, and your own number in case there is a problem. Tell the rider to call the customer before they set out, not when they are already at the gate. A rider who calls ahead catches problems early — the customer is not at home, the address is wrong, the customer says they are no longer interested — before your goods have travelled across town.

### Track the delivery

Ask the rider to confirm when the goods have been delivered and, for pay-on-delivery orders, when the money has been collected. For important orders, ask the customer to send you a short message when they receive the goods: "Please let me know when it arrives so I can confirm with you." This closes the loop and gives you a record that the delivery was completed.

### Use a delivery log

Keep a simple record of every delivery: date, customer, product, rider, delivery fee, and whether it was delivered, failed, or returned. After a month, this log will show you patterns — which areas have the most failed deliveries, which riders are most reliable, and which customers have failed before. A customer who has failed two deliveries should not get a third shipment without full payment upfront.

## Step 5: Handle the Awkward Cases

Even with a good system, you will still face difficult situations. Here is how to handle the most common ones.

### "I am not at home, come tomorrow"

This is the most common failed delivery excuse. Your response should be warm but firm: "No problem, the rider can come tomorrow between 10 a.m. and 1 p.m. Please confirm you will be available, because a second delivery will attract another delivery fee." If the customer fails a second time, ask for full payment before a third attempt, or ask them to pick up from your shop or a pickup point.

### "I have changed my mind"

If the customer changes their mind before you ship, that is fine — cancel the order politely and keep the relationship. If they change their mind after you have shipped, and they have not paid, you lose the delivery fee. This is why payment before dispatch matters. If they paid and then changed their mind before delivery, offer a refund minus the delivery cost, or offer to exchange the product. A fair policy keeps the customer, even if this order is lost.

### "The price is too high, I thought it was less"

This usually means the total — product plus delivery — was not made clear at the start. Go back to your order template. If you quoted the product price only, and the delivery fee was a surprise, the customer has a point. Apologise, explain the delivery cost, and offer a solution — a small discount, free delivery on their next order, or a pickup option. Then fix your process so the total is always stated upfront.

### "I never ordered that"

Check your records. If you have the customer's written confirmation — the "Confirmed" message with their name, address and the product details — you can politely show it to them. If you do not have that record, you have no way to prove the order was real, and you will have to absorb the loss. This is exactly why the written confirmation matters.

### The customer who keeps failing

Some customers fail deliveries repeatedly. After the second failed delivery, change the terms: full payment upfront, no exceptions, and the customer pays for any re-delivery. If they refuse, politely decline future orders. You are running a business, not a charity for indecisive buyers.

## The Confirmation Checklist

Use this checklist before any parcel leaves your shop:

- [ ] Customer's full name recorded
- [ ] Two phone numbers collected, main number tested
- [ ] Product, size, colour and quantity repeated back and confirmed
- [ ] Full delivery address with landmark recorded
- [ ] Total price including delivery fee told to the customer
- [ ] Customer replied "Confirmed" in writing
- [ ] Confirmation call made, customer available at delivery time
- [ ] Payment received and verified in your account (or commitment fee paid for pay on delivery)
- [ ] Rider briefed with full details and told to call the customer first
- [ ] Order and delivery logged in your records

If any box is unchecked, do not ship yet. Fix it first. Five minutes of checking is cheaper than a failed delivery.

## What Confirming Orders Really Buys You

Confirming orders is not about distrusting your customers. Most of your customers are honest people who want their goods and will pay for them gladly. Confirmation is about respecting your own business — your time, your money, and your stock — enough to protect them with a simple process.

The sellers who last in Nigerian online business are not always the ones with the best products or the lowest prices. They are the ones with the best systems: they take orders properly, they confirm before they ship, they get paid before they dispatch, and they learn from every failed delivery instead of repeating it.

Start tomorrow. Send the order template to your next customer, make the confirmation call, and do not ship until the payment is in your account. Do it for every order for two weeks, and count how many deliveries fail compared to before. The numbers will convince you faster than any advice can.
