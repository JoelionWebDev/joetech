Before a new customer in Lagos or Abuja spends money with a business they have never used, they do one thing first: they search for it on Google. What they find in that moment often decides whether they call you or call your competitor. If they see a business with recent five-star reviews and thoughtful replies from the owner, they feel safe. If they see no reviews at all, or a profile that looks abandoned, they move on without a second thought. Reviews are not vanity. They are the first conversation a stranger has with your business.

For Nigerian small businesses, Google reviews are one of the cheapest forms of marketing available. They cost nothing to collect, they work while you sleep, and they carry more weight than any advert you could pay for, because they come from real customers. A tailor in Surulere, a restaurant in Lekki, a pharmacy in Ikeja, or a cleaning service in Abuja can all win new customers simply because past customers said good things in public. The problem is that most happy customers never leave a review unless someone asks them. They are busy, they forget, or they do not know how.

This guide shows you how to fix that, step by step. You will learn how to set up and claim your Google Business Profile, how to make leaving a review so easy that customers can do it in under a minute, when and how to ask without being pushy, and how to reply to reviews — good and bad — in a way that builds trust. You will also learn what to do about unfair reviews and how to keep new reviews coming week after week.

## Why Google Reviews Matter So Much in Nigeria

Nigerians are careful with their money, and for good reason. Online scams, poor service, and businesses that disappear after payment have made people cautious. When someone finds your business on Google, your reviews are the proof that you are real, that you deliver what you promise, and that other people have trusted you before. A business with twenty honest reviews will beat a business with none, even if the second business is cheaper.

Reviews also help people find you. When someone in Lekki searches for "best hair vendor near me" or "plumber in Ikeja", Google shows businesses with strong, recent reviews more prominently. That means reviews do two jobs at once: they convince the people who already found you, and they help new people find you in the first place. This is especially powerful for local businesses, because most customers search for services close to where they live or work.

There is a third benefit that owners often miss. Reviews tell you what your business is doing well and where it needs to improve. If three customers mention that your delivery was slow, that is not an attack — it is free business advice. If five customers praise your packaging, you have learned what to keep doing. Businesses that read their reviews and act on them get better, and customers notice. In a crowded market, that steady improvement is a real advantage.

## Set Up Your Google Business Profile First

You cannot collect Google reviews until your business exists properly on Google. That means setting up a Google Business Profile, which is free. Search for "Google Business Profile" on your phone or laptop, sign in with the Google account you use for your business, and add your business name, category, address, phone number, and opening hours. Choose a category that matches what you actually do — "Hair salon", "Restaurant", "Pharmacy", or "Cleaning service" — because the category helps Google show you to the right searchers.

Google will ask you to verify the business, usually by sending a postcard with a code to your address, or by a phone call or video check. Verification can take days, so start it early and do not delay. While you wait, fill in everything else: add photos of your shop, your products, and your team, write a short description of what you do, and list your services. A profile with photos gets far more attention than an empty one, and customers trust a business they can see.

Once your profile is live, check how it looks by searching for your business name on Google, the way a customer would. Make sure the phone number is correct, the hours are right, and the address pin sits in the right place on the map. A wrong pin that sends customers to the wrong street in Lekki is worse than no pin at all. Ask a friend to search for you and tell you what they see. Fix anything that looks wrong before you start asking for reviews, because every review will send more people to this profile.

## Make Leaving a Review Easy — Under One Minute

The main reason customers do not leave reviews is that it feels like work. Your job is to remove every obstacle. Google gives every verified business a direct review link. Find it in your Business Profile dashboard, under the option to share your review form. Copy that link and save it somewhere you can reach it quickly — your notes app, your WhatsApp Business shortcuts, or a pinned message to yourself.

Shorten the link if it is long and ugly. You can use a free link shortener, or simply save it as a link in your WhatsApp Business catalogue and your Instagram bio. Better still, turn it into a QR code and print it. Put the code on your shop counter, on your receipts, on your packaging, and on the table where customers sit. A customer who has just enjoyed your food or picked up a beautiful dress can scan the code and leave a review before they reach their car. That is the easiest review you will ever get.

Test the whole process yourself first. Open the link on your own phone, with a different Google account if you can, and count how many taps it takes to leave a review. If it takes more than a minute, or if the link opens the wrong page, fix it before you share it with anyone. Also make sure the link works on both Android and iPhone, since your customers use both. A broken review link is a missed review, and you will never know how many you lost.

## Ask at the Right Moment, in the Right Words

Timing is everything. The best moment to ask for a review is right after a customer has smiled — when they have just received their order, tried on the dress, eaten the food, or told you they love the result. That is when their good feeling is strongest, and a simple ask feels natural. Asking a week later, when the excitement has faded, is much harder.

Keep your ask short, warm, and specific. In person, you can say: "Thank you so much. If you have a minute, a Google review would really help our small business. I can send you the link on WhatsApp." On WhatsApp, after a successful delivery, send a message like: "Hi Ada, thank you for your order. We are glad you loved the cake. If you have a minute, please leave us a quick review on Google — it helps other customers find us. Here is the link." Notice what the message does: it thanks the customer first, explains why the review matters, and gives the link immediately, so the customer does not have to ask for it.

Ask every happy customer, not just your favourites. If you only ask the customers you know well, your reviews will grow slowly and look one-sided. Build the ask into your routine: after every delivery, after every completed service, after every compliment. Your staff should know the script too. A salon assistant who says "If you loved your hair, a Google review would mean a lot to us" after every appointment will collect more reviews in a month than an owner who asks occasionally. Never pay for reviews, and never offer discounts in exchange for a five-star rating. Google can remove fake or paid reviews, and customers can tell when reviews are not genuine. Ask honestly, and let the stars take care of themselves.

## Reply to Every Review — Good and Bad

Collecting reviews is only half the job. Replying to them is what turns reviews into trust. When a customer leaves a good review, reply within a day or two. Thank them by name, mention something specific from their review, and invite them back. "Thank you, Chidinma. We are so glad you loved the braids, and we look forward to seeing you again next month" is far better than a copied "Thanks for your review." Personal replies show future customers that a real human runs this business and actually reads what people write.

Bad reviews need replies too — calm ones. A one-star review feels personal, especially when you know you tried your best, but an angry public reply will hurt you more than the review itself. Take a breath, thank the customer for the feedback, apologise for their experience without arguing about who was right, and invite them to contact you directly to fix it. "Thank you for letting us know, Tunde. We are sorry the delivery arrived late, and we have spoken to our rider about it. Please send us a message so we can make it right" shows everyone reading that you take complaints seriously.

Never argue with a customer in a public reply, and never reveal private details about their order. If the complaint involves a payment dispute or a personal matter, keep the public reply short and move the conversation to WhatsApp or a phone call. And remember: a business with a few bad reviews and gracious replies often looks more trustworthy than a business with only perfect reviews. People know that no business is perfect. What they want to know is how you behave when something goes wrong.

## Handle Unfair Reviews and Keep New Ones Coming

Sometimes a review is unfair — from someone who was never your customer, from a competitor, or from a customer whose demands were impossible. Do not panic. First, check whether the review breaks Google's rules. Reviews that contain insults, false claims about a business, or content from someone who never used your service can be reported. In your Business Profile dashboard, find the review, tap the three dots, and report it, explaining briefly why it violates the policy. Google will review it, though it may take time, and not every report succeeds.

If the review stays up, reply to it calmly anyway. A short, factual reply — "We have no record of this order, and we would like to understand what happened. Please contact us on 0803 123 4567 so we can look into it" — tells future readers that the review may not be genuine, without you sounding defensive. Then move on. One unfair review among many good ones does very little damage. Twenty good reviews will always speak louder than one strange one.

Keeping reviews coming is about routine, not luck. Set a small weekly target, such as five new reviews a week, and track it. Remind your team, put the QR code where customers can see it, and follow up once — never more than once — with customers who said they would leave a review but have not yet. A gentle reminder is fine; pestering is not. Over time, your review count becomes a quiet asset that grows on its own, because each new review makes the next customer more likely to choose you, and more likely to leave a review of their own.

Do not let your good reviews sit quietly on Google. Share them. Screenshot a kind review and post it on your WhatsApp status or Instagram story, with a thank-you to the customer. Put your best two or three reviews on your website or in your WhatsApp Business catalogue. When new customers see real words from real people, they trust you faster. Just share reviews honestly: use the customer's first name only, do not edit their words to make them sound better, and never invent a review. A genuine review, shared well, is one of the strongest adverts your business will ever have.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need a physical shop to get Google reviews?

No. You can run a Google Business Profile as a service-area business if you deliver or visit customers, such as a home cleaning service or a dispatch rider. You will still need to verify the business, and you can choose to hide your exact home address while showing the areas you serve. What matters is that Google can verify you are a real business.

### How many reviews do I need before they start helping?

Even five to ten honest reviews make a difference, because they show that real people have used your business. The benefit grows as the number grows, but there is no magic number. What matters more than the total is that reviews are recent and that you reply to them. A business with ten reviews from this year looks better than one with thirty reviews from three years ago.

### Can I ask customers for a review on WhatsApp?

Yes, and WhatsApp is one of the best places to ask, because that is where you already talk to your customers. Send the review link right after a successful delivery or service, while the customer is still happy. Keep the message short and grateful, and ask only once. If they do not leave a review, thank them anyway and move on.

### What should I do if a customer leaves a one-star review?

Reply calmly and quickly. Thank them for the feedback, apologise for their experience, and invite them to contact you directly so you can fix it. Do not argue in public and do not reveal private details. If the review breaks Google's rules — for example, it is from someone who was never a customer — report it through your Business Profile dashboard.

### Is it against the rules to offer a discount for a five-star review?

Yes. Google does not allow businesses to pay for reviews or offer rewards in exchange for positive ratings. It also damages trust: customers can often tell when reviews were bought. Ask every happy customer for an honest review, and let the quality of your work earn the stars.

## What to Do Next

First, claim or create your Google Business Profile today, complete every section, add at least five good photos, and start the verification process if you have not done it already. A complete profile is the foundation everything else depends on.

Second, find your review link in the dashboard, test it on your own phone, and turn it into a QR code. Print the code and place it where customers will see it — on your counter, your receipts, and your packaging — and save the link in your WhatsApp Business shortcuts so you can send it in seconds.

Third, write one short message you will send after every successful order or service, and use it for the next ten customers. Reply to every review you receive, good or bad, within two days. In a month, count your new reviews and watch how many new customers mention that they found you on Google.
