A mobile car wash business is one of the most practical small businesses you can start in Nigeria today. You do not need to rent a shop, you do not need millions of naira to begin, and demand is everywhere — from Lekki to Ikeja, from Abuja to Port Harcourt, car owners want their vehicles cleaned without driving to a wash bay and waiting in a queue.

What makes this business attractive is the low barrier to entry combined with repeat customers. A car that gets washed this week will need washing again next week. If you serve the same customers well, you build a weekly income that grows steadily. This guide walks you through every step, from startup costs to pricing, equipment, marketing and the mistakes to avoid.

## Why a Mobile Car Wash Works in Nigeria

Nigeria has millions of private cars, and the number grows every year. In Lagos alone, traffic congestion means many car owners would rather have someone wash their car at home or at the office than spend an hour at a car wash. Estates, offices, hotels and residential areas are full of people who value convenience and are willing to pay for it.

The mobile model also removes the biggest cost of a fixed car wash: rent. A good car wash location in Lekki or Ikeja can cost ₦1,500,000 to ₦3,000,000 a year in rent. A mobile operation skips that entirely, so your money goes into equipment, transport and marketing instead. You can even run the business part-time while keeping a job, then scale up when the customer base grows.

## Step 1: Understand the Business Model

Before spending any money, understand exactly how the business makes money. You earn per car washed. Your costs are water, soap, fuel for your generator or pressure washer, transport to the customer, and your time. The difference between what you charge and what you spend per car is your profit.

There are two main service levels to offer. A basic exterior wash covers the body, tyres, windows and mirrors. A full wash adds the interior — vacuuming seats and carpets, wiping the dashboard, and cleaning door panels. Most mobile operators charge more for the full wash, and many customers prefer it because it saves them a trip to a detailing shop.

You can also offer add-ons: engine cleaning, leather seat treatment, tyre shine, air freshener, and headlight restoration. Add-ons are where the real profit sits, because the extra cost to you is small while the price you charge is meaningful. A bottle of tyre shine that costs ₦2,500 can serve dozens of cars. Many operators start alone and hire one assistant once bookings become regular.

## Step 2: Plan Your Startup Costs

You can start a mobile car wash with as little as ₦150,000 if you already own a motorcycle or have access to one, and a comfortable start sits around ₦350,000 to ₦500,000. Here is a realistic breakdown for a solid beginner setup in Lagos or Abuja.

A pressure washer is your most important purchase. A good petrol-powered pressure washer costs between ₦120,000 and ₦250,000, depending on brand and power. Electric models are cheaper, from ₦80,000, but they need a power source at the customer's location, which is not always available. A petrol washer gives you independence, and that matters in Nigeria where power supply is unreliable.

A small generator, if you choose an electric washer or need a vacuum cleaner, costs between ₦90,000 and ₦180,000 for a 1kVA to 2.5kVA unit. A wet and dry vacuum cleaner for interiors costs ₦60,000 to ₦120,000. Water storage is essential: a 100-litre or 200-litre water tank or several large jerry cans cost ₦15,000 to ₦40,000. Hoses, buckets, wash mitts, microfibre towels, brushes and a spray bottle will cost another ₦25,000 to ₦40,000 in total.

Cleaning supplies are your running cost. Car shampoo, all-purpose cleaner, glass cleaner, tyre shine, dashboard polish and air freshener will cost roughly ₦30,000 to ₦50,000 to stock up initially, and that stock can last for dozens of cars. A branded apron or polo shirt costs ₦5,000 to ₦10,000 and makes you look professional from day one.

Transport is the other big item. If you already have a motorcycle, you can strap a small tank and your equipment to it. A used motorcycle costs ₦350,000 to ₦700,000 if you need to buy one. A small van or keke is better for carrying water and equipment, but it is a bigger investment, so most beginners start with a motorcycle and upgrade later.

Add a small budget for printing flyers and business cards, about ₦10,000 to ₦20,000, and keep at least ₦30,000 as working capital for fuel and supplies in your first month. Your total realistic startup budget is therefore between ₦350,000 and ₦600,000 for a professional setup, or under ₦200,000 if you already own transport and buy a basic washer.

## Step 3: Choose Your Equipment Wisely

Do not buy the cheapest pressure washer you can find. A weak washer takes longer per car and breaks down faster, which costs you customers. Look for a washer with at least 120 bar of pressure and a reliable engine. Ask sellers about spare parts and servicing before you buy, because a washer you cannot repair is money wasted.

Your water supply plan matters as much as the washer. Many customers will let you use their tap, but you cannot depend on that. Carry your own water in a tank or jerry cans so you can work anywhere, including estates where water supply is rationed. A 100-litre tank is enough for two to three cars, depending on how dirty they are.

Invest in good microfibre towels. Cheap rags scratch paint, and a customer who finds swirl marks on their car will never call you again. Buy a set of at least ten microfibre cloths and keep separate ones for the body, glass and interior. Wash them regularly and replace them when they wear out.

A vacuum cleaner changes the quality of your interior service completely. Customers notice the difference between a car that has been wiped and one that has been properly vacuumed. If a vacuum cleaner is beyond your starting budget, begin with exterior-only services and add interior cleaning once you have earned enough to buy one.

## Step 4: Set Prices That Make You Money

Pricing is where many beginners fail. Charge too little and you work all day for nothing. Charge too much and nobody books. Research what fixed car washes in your area charge, then price your mobile service slightly above that, because you are offering convenience.

In Lagos, a reasonable starting price is ₦5,000 to ₦7,000 for a basic exterior wash of a saloon car, and ₦8,000 to ₦12,000 for a full wash including interior. SUVs and larger vehicles cost more because they take longer and use more water and soap — add ₦2,000 to ₦3,000 for SUVs. In Abuja, prices are similar, sometimes slightly higher in areas like Maitama and Asokoro. In smaller cities, prices may be lower, but so are your costs.

Offer weekly and monthly packages to lock in repeat customers. A weekly wash package of four washes a month at ₦18,000 to ₦25,000 for a saloon car gives the customer a discount and gives you guaranteed income. Corporate packages are even better: an office with ten staff cars washed every Friday is steady money you can plan around.

Be clear about your prices from the start. Put them on your flyer, your WhatsApp status and your business card. Customers who know the price before they book are less likely to argue after the job is done. Never wash a car without agreeing the price first.

Always collect payment immediately after the wash. Cash, bank transfer or a Paystack payment link all work, but do not leave without being paid. Mobile money and transfers are now normal in Nigeria, and most customers are happy to transfer once they see the result.

## Step 5: Register the Business and Stay Legal

You do not need a CAC registration to wash your first car, but registering your business makes you look serious and opens doors to corporate clients. A business name registration with the Corporate Affairs Commission costs around ₦10,000 to ₦15,000, and it is worth doing once you have steady customers.

If you plan to serve estates and corporate offices, you will need an ID card and possibly a letter of introduction. Some estates require service providers to register at the gate. Prepare a simple letter on your business letterhead and carry your ID always. Be extremely careful around mirrors, antennas and expensive paintwork, and never use harsh chemicals on a car you do not know.

## Step 6: Find Your First Customers

Your first customers will come from people who already know you. Tell your neighbours, your church or mosque community, your colleagues and your estate WhatsApp group that you have started a mobile car wash. Offer the first wash at a discount or free for a few people in exchange for honest feedback and a referral.

Estates are the best hunting ground for a mobile car wash. One estate can give you twenty or thirty regular customers if you do good work and show up on time. Visit estate management, introduce yourself politely, and ask for permission to serve residents. Some estates will let you post on their notice board or WhatsApp group.

Offices are the second-best market. Workers park their cars for eight hours, which is plenty of time for a wash. Approach office managers or facility officers and offer a Friday wash service for staff cars. A single office with fifteen cars washed weekly at ₦6,000 each is ₦90,000 a week.

Use WhatsApp Business as your main tool. Set up a business profile with your services, prices and photos of your work. Post before-and-after photos on your status every day. Ask happy customers to share your number in their own groups. Word of mouth is the strongest marketing a car wash can have, because a clean car is visible proof of your work.

Print simple flyers and distribute them in estates, churches, mosques, gyms and offices. A flyer with your phone number, prices and a photo of a clean car costs little and keeps working for weeks. Include your WhatsApp number in large print.

## Step 7: Deliver a Wash Customers Remember

The difference between a one-time customer and a weekly customer is the quality of the wash. Follow a consistent routine every time so no step is skipped.

Start by rinsing the car to remove loose dirt and sand. Washing a dirty car without rinsing first drags grit across the paint and causes scratches. Use your pressure washer on a wide spray setting for the rinse, not the narrow jet.

Wash from the top down with car shampoo and a wash mitt, never with a sponge that traps dirt. Rinse each section as you go so soap does not dry on the paint, especially under the Nigerian sun. Dry the car with a clean microfibre towel immediately after rinsing to prevent water spots.

Clean the wheels and tyres properly. Dirty tyres make a clean car look unfinished. Use a brush on the tyres and apply tyre shine for a finished look. Clean the windows inside and out with glass cleaner and a separate cloth, because streaky windows are the first thing a customer notices.

For the interior, remove floor mats and shake them out, vacuum the seats and carpets, wipe the dashboard and door panels with a damp microfibre cloth, and finish with a light air freshener. Never spray strong chemicals directly on a dashboard. Always test a new product on a small hidden area first.

Finish by walking around the car with the customer. Let them inspect your work and point out anything they want touched up. This takes two minutes and prevents complaints later. A customer who inspects and approves the work is far more likely to book again and recommend you.

## Step 8: Manage Water, Power and Logistics

Water is your biggest logistical challenge in Nigeria. Plan your routes so you can refill easily, and always carry more water than you think you need. A car that is very dirty can use 60 to 80 litres. Running out of water halfway through a job is embarrassing and unprofessional.

Fuel your generator and pressure washer the night before, not on the morning of a job. Keep a small jerry can of spare fuel in your kit. In areas where fuel stations have long queues, this habit saves you from missing appointments.

Schedule your jobs with travel time between them. Lagos traffic can turn a 20-minute trip into an hour. Book jobs in the same area on the same day, and confirm each appointment by WhatsApp the evening before. A simple message saying "Good evening, confirming your car wash tomorrow at 10am" reduces no-shows dramatically. Keep a simple record of each customer, their car type and their preferred day so you can plan your week and remind them when they forget.

## Step 9: Grow Beyond One Person

Once you have more bookings than you can handle alone, hire one assistant and train them to your standard. Pay them daily or weekly, and make it clear that quality matters more than speed. A helper who rushes and scratches a car costs you more than the extra jobs they help you finish.

As demand grows, add a second pressure washer and a second team. You can cover two estates at the same time, or take on a corporate contract that needs ten cars washed in one morning. Growth should follow demand, not the other way round. Do not buy a second washer until the first one is fully booked most days.

Consider adding services that use the same customers and equipment. Detailing, headlight restoration and engine cleaning all earn more per job than a basic wash. A simple interior detailing service can earn ₦20,000 to ₦35,000 per car, and the products cost a fraction of that.

Build a simple online presence as you grow. A one-page website with your services, prices, service areas and a WhatsApp button makes you look established and helps corporate clients find you. You can also list your business on Google so people searching for a car wash near them can find your number.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

The most common mistake is undercharging. Many beginners charge ₦2,000 or ₦3,000 per car to attract customers, then discover that fuel, soap and transport eat the whole fee. Price for profit from day one, and let quality justify the price.

The second mistake is accepting too many bookings in one day. A tired washer cuts corners, and a customer who waits two hours past the agreed time will not book again. It is better to wash six cars well than ten cars badly.

The third mistake is neglecting your equipment. Clean your pressure washer filter regularly, service the engine on schedule, and store your towels and chemicals properly. Equipment that breaks down in the middle of a job costs you the job, the customer and the repair bill. Never wash a car with dirty water or a dirty cloth — it is the fastest way to damage paint and lose a customer. Change your wash water regularly and keep your towels clean.

## How Much Can You Earn?

A realistic picture helps you plan. If you wash five cars a day at an average of ₦7,000, that is ₦35,000 a day, or ₦210,000 a week over six working days. After fuel, soap, water, transport and a helper's pay of roughly ₦60,000 to ₦80,000 a week, you could keep ₦130,000 to ₦150,000 a week as profit in a good week. Real life will be slower at the start — two or three cars a day is normal in your first month — but the maths shows why this business works when customers return weekly.

Rainy season slows the business, because fewer people wash cars when rain is expected. Plan for it by offering interior-only services during heavy rain weeks and saving during dry months. December is usually strong, as people want clean cars for Christmas travel and events.

## Conclusion

Starting a mobile car wash business in Nigeria is one of the most accessible ways to build a steady income. You need a pressure washer, water, cleaning supplies, transport and the willingness to do excellent work every time. Price your services for profit, find customers in estates, use WhatsApp to stay in touch, and let the quality of each wash bring the next booking. Start small, keep good records, reinvest your earnings, and grow one loyal customer at a time.
