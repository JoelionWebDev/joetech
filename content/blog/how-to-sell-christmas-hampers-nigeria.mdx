Every December in Nigeria, the same thing happens. Companies look for gifts for their staff and clients. Families look for gifts for loved ones. Churches, mosques, estates and associations look for gifts for their members. And almost all of them buy the same thing: a Christmas hamper.

A hamper is a basket or box filled with food, drinks and useful items, wrapped beautifully and given as a gift. It is simple, it is festive, and it is one of the best seasonal businesses a small seller can run. You do not need a shop. You do not need a large team. You need good products, beautiful packaging, clear prices, and a plan that starts early.

This guide walks you through the whole Christmas hamper business in Nigeria: who buys hampers, what to put in them, how to price them, where to buy your stock, how to package them, how to find corporate and family customers, and how to deliver them on time in December, when Lagos traffic is at its worst.

## Why Christmas Hampers Sell So Well in Nigeria

Christmas is the biggest gifting season in Nigeria. It is a time for appreciation, celebration and generosity. A company that has worked with a client all year wants to say thank you. A boss wants to reward staff. A child living abroad wants to send a gift to parents in Lagos or Enugu. A church wants to bless its members. A hamper says all of that in one beautiful package.

Hampers also solve a problem for the buyer. Many people do not have time to shop for ten different gifts, wrap them, and deliver them. A ready-made hamper saves them time. They pay once, and the gift is ready. That convenience is what you are selling, as much as the items inside.

The season is short, but it is intense. Most hamper sales happen between late November and 23 December. Companies often order in November, so their gifts arrive before staff travel for Christmas. Families order in early December, and last-minute buyers order in the final week. If you start early, you can serve all three groups. If you start late, you will miss the corporate orders, which are usually the biggest and most profitable.

## Who Buys Christmas Hampers?

Understanding your buyers helps you build the right hampers at the right prices. There are four main groups.

**Companies and organisations** buy the most hampers, and they buy in bulk. A company may order 20, 50 or 200 hampers for staff, clients and partners. They need an invoice, a receipt, and often branding — a card with their logo, or a ribbon in their company colour. They pay by bank transfer, and they expect delivery to their office on an agreed date. Corporate orders are the backbone of a profitable hamper business.

**Families and individuals** buy one or a few hampers for parents, in-laws, friends, pastors, and loved ones. They care about the look of the hamper, the quality of the items, and the price. They often order on WhatsApp or Instagram after seeing a photo.

**Churches, mosques and associations** buy hampers for members, widows, and community outreach. They may order in bulk, but their budgets are often modest. A well-priced, respectful hamper for this group can bring repeat orders every year.

**Nigerians abroad** buy hampers for family back home. A son in London or a daughter in Canada will pay online and ask you to deliver a hamper to their mother in Lekki or their father in Onitsha. This is a growing market, and it pays well, because the buyer is paying in pounds or dollars and wants a reliable seller they can trust.

Decide which group you will focus on first. A new seller should start with families and small companies, learn the process, and then approach bigger companies the following year with photos and testimonials from the first year.

## What to Put in a Christmas Hamper

A good hamper is a mix of useful, festive and tasty items. It should feel generous when opened, but every item should be something the receiver will actually use. Avoid filling a hamper with cheap items nobody wants, just to make it look full.

Here are items that work well in Nigerian hampers:

- **Food staples:** Rice, vegetable oil, pasta, noodles, tomato paste, seasoning cubes, and canned foods. These are practical and always welcome.
- **Drinks:** Non-alcoholic wine, fruit juice, malt drinks, and soft drinks. Add real wine or champagne only for a premium hamper, and only if you are sure the receiver drinks alcohol.
- **Snacks and treats:** Biscuits, chocolates, cookies, chin chin, groundnuts, and dried fruits. These add colour and festive feeling.
- **Breakfast items:** Tea, coffee, milk, sugar, oats, and cereal. A breakfast hamper is a thoughtful idea for families.
- **Personal care:** Good soap, body cream, perfume, or a scented candle, for a premium or ladies' hamper.
- **Useful extras:** A mug, a small towel, a notebook, or a kitchen item, for a hamper that feels personal.

Build three or four hamper sizes, not twenty. Too many options confuse buyers. A simple range works best:

- **Mini hamper — ₦25,000 to ₦35,000:** A small basket with rice, oil, noodles, biscuits, juice and a card. Good for churches, staff gifts, and modest family gifts.
- **Classic hamper — ₦50,000 to ₦75,000:** A medium basket with more food, drinks, snacks, tea and a personal care item. This is your bestseller for families and small companies.
- **Premium hamper — ₦100,000 to ₦150,000:** A large basket or box with premium drinks, chocolates, imported biscuits, a good wine, and a gift item. Good for valued clients and senior staff.
- **Luxury hamper — ₦200,000 and above:** A large, beautifully packed hamper with top-quality items, for executives, key clients, and special gifts.

You can also build themed hampers: a food-only hamper, a breakfast hamper, a drinks hamper, a kids' hamper with snacks and toys, or a hamper for a new mother. Themed hampers help you stand out, because a buyer who wants a specific gift will choose you over a seller with only generic baskets.

Buy your stock early, in November, before prices rise in December. Buy from wholesale markets like Mile 12, Oyingbo, Balogun, or Onitsha Main Market, or from trusted distributors who can give you a bulk discount. Check expiry dates carefully. A hamper with an item that expires in January is a complaint waiting to happen. Buy items with at least six months before expiry.

## How to Price Your Hampers So You Make a Profit

Pricing is where many hamper sellers fail. They add up the cost of the items, add a small amount, and forget the basket, the wrapping, the ribbon, the card, the delivery, and their own time. At the end of December, they have sold many hampers and made almost nothing.

Work out the full cost of one hamper. Add the cost of every item, plus the basket or box, the cellophane wrap, the ribbon, the bow, the gift card, the sticker, and the delivery. Then add your time. If it takes you 30 minutes to pack one hamper, that time has a value.

Use this formula: **Selling price = total cost + your profit margin.**

Aim for a profit margin of 30 to 40 percent on each hamper. For example, if a classic hamper costs you ₦38,000 in total — items, basket, wrapping and delivery — and you sell it for ₦55,000, your profit is ₦17,000 per hamper. Sell 50 of those, and your profit is ₦850,000 before other costs like data and transport. Sell 50 at a price that leaves only ₦3,000 profit each, and you earn only ₦150,000 for the same work.

Offer a small bulk discount for corporate orders, but keep it planned. For example, 5 percent off for 20 hampers or more, and 10 percent off for 50 or more. A clear bulk rule makes negotiation short and protects your margin. Do not agree to a 30 percent discount for a corporate client just because they are a big name. A big name that pays you at a loss is not a good client.

State your prices clearly in your catalogue, on WhatsApp, and on Instagram. A customer who sees "Classic Hamper — ₦55,000" can decide quickly. A customer who has to ask "How much?" for every hamper will ask three sellers and buy from the one who answers first with a clear price.

Collect a deposit for corporate orders. A 50 percent deposit before you buy stock, and the balance before delivery, is standard and fair. It protects you from a company that orders 100 hampers and cancels in December, leaving you with stock you cannot sell. Put the deposit rule in writing, in your quotation, so there is no argument later.

## Packaging: How Your Hamper Looks Is Half the Sale

People buy hampers with their eyes first. A hamper that looks beautiful in a photo will sell. A hamper that looks messy will not, even if the items inside are good.

Choose a good basket or box. Wicker baskets are classic and reusable, which customers love. Sturdy cardboard boxes with a festive design are cheaper, easier to stack, and easier to deliver. For a premium hamper, a wooden crate or a fabric-covered box feels luxurious. Buy your baskets in bulk in November, before the price rises.

Pack the heaviest items at the bottom and the lightest, most colourful items on top. Place drinks upright and secure them so they do not fall. Fill gaps with tissue paper, shredded paper, or small snacks, so nothing moves when the hamper is carried. A hamper that rattles or looks empty at the top feels cheap.

Wrap it in clear cellophane, pull it tight, and tie it with a wide ribbon and a bow. Add a gift tag or card with a short message: "Merry Christmas from the team at [Company Name]" or "With love, from the Ade family." A personal card makes a hamper feel like a gift, not a shopping bag.

Take good photos in daylight, against a clean background. Photograph each hamper size from the front, from the top, and with the wrapping off so buyers can see what is inside. A photo that shows the real items, with a clear price, will sell far more than a photo of a wrapped basket with no details.

## How to Find Customers for Your Hampers

Start marketing in early November, not in December. By December, many corporate buyers have already chosen their supplier. Early marketing gives you time to take orders, buy stock, and pack without panic.

**WhatsApp is your best channel.** Post your hamper photos on your Status every day, with prices. Send your catalogue to your broadcast list. Message past customers personally: "Hello Mrs Ade, our Christmas hampers are ready. The classic hamper is ₦55,000. Would you like to order for your family or your office?" Personal messages work far better than a generic broadcast.

**Instagram and Facebook** are good for discovery. Post a Reel showing you packing a hamper, with the items laid out beautifully. Use a short caption with the price and how to order. Run a simple giveaway: "Tag two friends and follow us to win a mini hamper." That can bring you new followers who may become buyers.

**Approach companies directly.** Make a list of 20 small and medium companies in Lekki, Ikeja, Victoria Island, Wuse and GRA Port Harcourt. Send a short, professional message or email to their HR or admin team in early November, with a one-page PDF showing your hamper range, prices, and delivery dates. Follow up once, politely, a week later. Companies buy from sellers who are organised and early, not from sellers who message them on 20 December.

**Use your network.** Tell your church, your estate group, your alumni group, and your family that you sell hampers. Ask them to share your catalogue. Word of mouth is powerful in December, because one happy customer will tell five friends.

**Offer a referral reward.** "Refer a company that orders 10 hampers, and get a mini hamper free." A small reward can bring you a corporate order worth far more than the cost of the gift.

## Delivery: The Part That Makes or Breaks Christmas

Delivery in December is hard. Traffic in Lagos is heavy, dispatch riders are busy, and customers are travelling. Plan delivery like a professional, or your beautiful hampers will arrive late and your reputation will suffer.

Set a clear order deadline. For example, "Order by 15 December for delivery by 20 December." After that date, accept only orders you can definitely fulfil. A refused order is better than a failed delivery on Christmas Eve.

Agree delivery fees in advance. Charge a delivery fee based on location: ₦2,000 to ₦3,500 within Lekki, more for Ikeja, Surulere, or Abuja, and a separate fee for interstate delivery. Tell the customer the fee before they pay. A surprise delivery charge after payment will cause an argument.

Use reliable dispatch riders. Book them early, especially for corporate orders of 20 or more hampers. Give the rider a list with names, phone numbers, addresses and landmarks. Call the customer before delivery, so they know the rider is coming. For large orders, deliver in batches over two days, instead of trying to deliver 100 hampers in one day.

For orders outside Lagos, use a trusted interstate logistics company, and pack the hamper carefully so items do not break. Wrap drinks in bubble wrap, secure the basket, and label it "Fragile — This Side Up." Send the customer a photo of the packed hamper before dispatch, so they know what to expect.

Keep a delivery sheet. For every order, write the customer name, hamper type, quantity, amount paid, delivery address, phone number, and delivery date. Tick it off when delivered. On a busy December day with 30 deliveries, a sheet is the difference between a smooth day and chaos.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Starting too late.** If you start in mid-December, you will miss corporate orders and pay higher prices for stock.
- **Buying stock without orders.** Buy a small amount first, take deposits for corporate orders, and buy more as orders come in.
- **Forgetting the cost of packaging and delivery.** These costs add up, and forgetting them wipes out your profit.
- **Using items close to expiry.** Check every expiry date. One expired item can damage your reputation for a year.
- **Promising delivery you cannot keep.** Be honest about your delivery dates, and do not accept an order you cannot fulfil.
- **Posting hampers without prices.** "DM for price" slows down sales in a busy season when buyers want to decide fast.
- **Packing hampers carelessly.** A messy hamper, a broken bottle, or a missing item will lead to complaints and refunds.
- **Taking full payment from a stranger with no deposit rule.** For large orders, a deposit protects you. For new customers, payment before delivery is standard.

## Your Christmas Hamper Timeline

Use this timeline to stay on track:

- **Early November:** Decide your hamper range and prices. Buy baskets, wrapping and ribbons. Take photos of sample hampers.
- **Mid-November:** Start marketing on WhatsApp, Instagram and Facebook. Send your catalogue to companies. Take deposits for corporate orders.
- **Late November:** Buy your stock in bulk. Start packing hampers for early orders. Confirm delivery dates with corporate clients.
- **Early December:** Deliver the first corporate orders. Post customer photos and testimonials. Keep marketing to families.
- **Mid-December:** Peak packing and delivery. Stick to your order deadline. Do not accept more orders than you can deliver.
- **20–23 December:** Final deliveries. Handle last-minute orders only if you have stock ready. Thank every customer.
- **After Christmas:** Count your profit. Write down what sold best, what did not sell, and which customers to contact first next November.

Christmas hampers are a seasonal business, but the customers you win in December can become customers for the whole year. A company that buys 50 hampers from you this Christmas may buy branded gifts, staff souvenirs, or event packages from you next year. A family that loved your hamper may order food, gifts, or party packs from you in January. Treat every hamper customer like a long-term customer, not a one-time sale.

Start early, price fairly, pack beautifully, and deliver on time. Do that, and Christmas will not just be a season of giving. It will be a season of real profit for your small business.
