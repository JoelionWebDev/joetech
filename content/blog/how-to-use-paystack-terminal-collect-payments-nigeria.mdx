# How to Use a Paystack Terminal to Collect Payments in Nigeria

A customer walks into your shop in Ikeja, picks goods worth ₦85,000, and reaches for her card. You have no card machine, so you ask her to transfer. She opens her bank app, and the transfer fails. She tries again. It fails again. She sighs, says she will come back later, and leaves. You have just lost a sale — not because of your price or your product, but because you could not take her money in the way she wanted to pay.

This happens every day in Nigerian shops, restaurants, salons, and pharmacies. Bank transfers fail, customers run out of cash, and "I will transfer when I get home" often means the money never comes. A card terminal solves this problem. It lets you accept payment on the spot, from any Nigerian debit or credit card, and the money lands in your account.

Paystack Terminal is one of the most popular card machines for Nigerian businesses. It is a small handheld device, about the size of a phone, that connects to the internet and processes card payments through your Paystack account. This guide explains how to get one, how to set it up, how to use it every day, and how to handle the problems that come up — so you never lose another sale to a failed transfer.

## What a Paystack Terminal Is

A Paystack Terminal is a physical point-of-sale device provided by Paystack, the Nigerian payments company. When a customer pays with their card, the terminal reads the card, contacts the customer's bank through Paystack, and confirms the payment in seconds. The money goes into your Paystack balance, and from there you can settle it into your Nigerian bank account.

The terminal works with the cards Nigerians actually carry: Verve, Mastercard, and Visa debit and credit cards from banks like GTBank, Zenith, Access, First Bank, UBA, and Kuda. It also supports contactless payments, where the customer simply taps the card on the device, and it can accept payments through transfer as a backup when a card fails.

A Paystack Terminal is tied to your Paystack business account, which means your in-person sales sit next to your online payments in one dashboard, and you can see all your money in one place.

## Why Your Business Needs One

There are several practical reasons to get a terminal, and they all come down to one thing: you get paid more often, and you get paid faster.

First, customers prefer cards. Many Nigerians now carry little or no cash, especially in Lagos and Abuja. When a customer has to transfer and the transfer fails, the sale is at risk. A card payment does not depend on the customer's bank app working — it goes through the card network, which is often more reliable than a mobile transfer.

Second, you get paid instantly. When a card payment succeeds, the money is in your Paystack balance immediately. You do not have to wait for a customer to remember to transfer, and you do not have to chase anyone for payment after they have left your shop.

Third, it builds trust. A business with a card machine looks established and professional. Customers trust a shop that gives them a printed or digital receipt more than a shop that writes amounts in a notebook. For new customers who do not know you yet, that trust matters.

Fourth, it reduces cash problems. Cash gets lost, miscounted, or stolen. It also attracts fake notes. A terminal means less cash in your till at the end of the day, which means less risk and easier accounting.

Fifth, it gives you records. Every payment on the terminal is logged in your Paystack dashboard with the amount, the time, and the card type. At the end of the month, you can see exactly how much you took in, which days were busiest, and which staff member handled which sale. That record is useful for your own planning, and it is useful if you ever need to show a bank or an investor how your business is doing.

## What It Costs

Before you order a terminal, understand the costs so there are no surprises.

The device itself has a one-time cost. Paystack charges for the terminal hardware, and the price has changed over time, so check the current price on the Paystack website or in your dashboard before ordering. Some businesses recover the cost of the device within the first few weeks simply from sales they would otherwise have lost.

Each transaction carries a fee. Paystack charges a percentage of each card payment, with a cap per transaction, and the exact rate depends on whether the card is local or international. Local Verve and Mastercard transactions are the cheapest; international cards cost more. Check the current rates in your Paystack dashboard under Terminal, because rates change and you should always work from the current figure, not from something you read a year ago.

There may also be a fee when you move money from your Paystack balance to your bank account, depending on your settlement settings. Many businesses settle daily or weekly into their GTBank, Zenith, or Access account, and the settlement fee is small compared with the value of getting paid reliably.

## Step 1: Get a Paystack Account

You cannot use a Paystack Terminal without a Paystack business account, so set that up first if you do not have one.

Go to paystack.com and sign up with your business email address. You will be asked for your business name, your business type — sole trader, registered company, or other — and your contact details. If your business is registered with the Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC), you will be asked for your registration number and documents. If you are a sole trader without CAC registration, you can still sign up, but some features may be limited until you complete verification.

Paystack will ask you to verify your identity with a government ID, such as a National ID, driver's licence, or international passport, and to add your Nigerian bank account details for settlement. This is the account your money will be paid into. Double-check the account number and bank name before submitting — a wrong digit means your money goes to the wrong place or gets stuck.

Verification usually takes one to three working days. Do not order a terminal until your account is verified, because the terminal is linked to that account.

## Step 2: Order Your Terminal

Once your account is verified, ordering the terminal is straightforward.

Log in to your Paystack dashboard, go to the Terminal section, and click the option to request a terminal. You will be asked to confirm your business details and your delivery address — the address where the device will be sent. Make sure someone will be available to receive it, because delivery companies in Lagos and Abuja sometimes need a phone call to find the address.

Pay for the device using the payment method Paystack shows you. Keep the receipt and the order confirmation email. Delivery usually takes a few working days within Lagos and Abuja, and a little longer to other states.

When the terminal arrives, check the box before the delivery person leaves. Inside should be the terminal itself, a charging cable, and a short guide. If anything is missing or the device looks damaged, contact Paystack support immediately with photos — do not try to use a damaged device.

## Step 3: Set Up the Terminal

Setting up the terminal takes about ten minutes. Do it in your shop, on a good connection, before your first customer needs it.

First, charge the device fully — a terminal with a low battery can switch off in the middle of a transaction. Charge it overnight before the first use, and make charging part of your closing routine every evening.

Switch the terminal on and connect it to the internet. It can connect through Wi-Fi or through a SIM card with mobile data. Wi-Fi is usually more stable and cheaper, so connect it to your shop's Wi-Fi if you have one. If you rely on mobile data, use a network with good coverage in your area — test MTN and Airtel and use whichever is stronger at your location — and keep data on the SIM at all times.

Next, sign in or link the terminal to your Paystack account. The device will show a code or ask you to confirm the link from your dashboard. Follow the on-screen instructions, and confirm in your dashboard that the terminal appears as connected. Give the terminal a name you will recognise, such as "Main Shop" or "Lekki Branch," especially if you plan to have more than one device.

Finally, run a test transaction. Ask a staff member or a friend to pay a small amount, such as ₦100, with their card, then refund it or treat it as a test sale. This confirms that the device, the connection, and your account all work together. It is far better to discover a problem during a test than during a real sale with a customer waiting.

## Step 4: Taking a Payment, Step by Step

Using the terminal during a sale is simple, and your staff should be able to do it after one demonstration.

Enter the amount on the terminal and double-check it before proceeding. A wrong amount is the most common error — typing ₦15,000 instead of ₦150,000, or missing a zero. Read the amount back to the customer: "That is ₦25,000, please." This protects both of you.

Hand the terminal to the customer or place it where they can reach it. The customer will insert their card into the slot, tap it on the device for a contactless payment, or swipe it if the chip fails. Then they enter their PIN on the terminal keypad. Never ask a customer for their PIN, and never enter it for them. The PIN is private, and handling it yourself creates risk for you and for the customer.

Wait for the result. The terminal will show "Approved" or "Successful" within a few seconds, and it will print a receipt if it has a printer, or offer to send a digital receipt. Do not hand over the goods until you see the approval on the screen. A customer saying "it has gone" is not proof — the terminal screen is the proof.

Give the customer their receipt, and keep your copy. If the terminal has no printer, you can send the receipt by SMS or email from the device, or simply show the customer the approval screen. ## Step 5: Handling Failed and Declined Payments

Failed payments happen, and knowing what to do keeps a small problem from becoming a lost sale.

If the terminal says "Declined," the problem is usually with the customer's card or bank — insufficient funds, a daily limit, or a card that has been blocked. Politely ask the customer to try another card, or to use a transfer as a backup. Do not keep retrying the same card again and again; repeated declines can lock the card temporarily.

If the terminal says "Network error" or the payment times out, the problem is usually your connection. Check whether the Wi-Fi is working, or switch the terminal to mobile data. Wait a minute and try again. If the connection is poor, move the terminal closer to the router or near a window for a better signal.

If the customer was debited but the terminal shows the payment failed, stay calm. This is the situation customers fear most, and how you handle it decides whether they ever come back. First, check your Paystack dashboard — the payment may have succeeded even though the terminal showed an error. If the dashboard shows the payment, the sale is complete and you can reassure the customer. If the dashboard does not show it, the money will normally be reversed to the customer's account automatically by their bank, usually within 24 hours. Give the customer your number and your business name so they can follow up, and offer to check with them the next day. Never ask the customer to pay a second time until you have checked the dashboard.

If the terminal itself freezes or will not switch on, restart it by holding the power button. If it still does not work, charge it and try again. If the problem continues, contact Paystack support with the terminal's serial number and a description of the fault.

## Step 6: End-of-Day Routine and Reconciliation

A terminal saves you money only if you track what it collects. Build a short end-of-day routine and follow it every evening.

First, check the terminal's daily total against your Paystack dashboard. The two numbers should match. If they do not, look for a payment that was reversed, a test transaction you forgot, or a sale that was recorded twice.

Second, compare the terminal total with your sales record. If you sold ₦200,000 worth of goods during the day and the terminal shows ₦120,000 in card payments, the remaining ₦80,000 should be accounted for by transfers and cash. If money is missing, you will catch it the same day instead of discovering it at the end of the month.

Third, settle your funds. Paystack can settle your balance into your bank account automatically on a schedule you choose — daily, weekly, or on specific days. Daily settlement is best for cash flow if you restock often, but check whether your plan charges per settlement. Pick the schedule that fits how you buy stock.

Fourth, charge the terminal, wipe the screen and keypad clean, and store the device somewhere safe. A terminal left on the counter overnight is an easy target for theft. Treat it like cash: lock it away at closing time.

## Step 7: Train Your Staff and Protect Your Money

A terminal is only as reliable as the people who use it. If you have staff, train each person who will handle payments, and write the steps on a small card next to the till: enter the amount, confirm it with the customer, wait for approval, give a receipt.

Give each staff member their own login or PIN on the terminal if the device supports it. This lets you see who handled each sale, which makes reconciliation easier and discourages theft. If the terminal does not support separate logins, keep a simple paper log: date, amount, staff name, and receipt number.

Set rules for refunds. A refund should need your approval, not just a staff member's decision. Decide in advance when you offer refunds — faulty goods, wrong items, or within a set number of days — and make sure your staff know the policy. A clear refund policy, displayed in the shop, prevents arguments and protects your money.

Watch out for the common tricks used against businesses with card machines. Never let a customer take the terminal out of your sight. Never accept a payment where the customer insists the money "will show later" without an approval on the screen. And never let someone "test" your terminal with a card that is not theirs. These are simple rules, but they prevent most of the fraud small businesses face.

## Your Paystack Terminal Checklist

Run through this list before and after you start using your terminal:

- Your Paystack business account is verified, with the correct bank account for settlement.
- Your terminal is ordered, delivered, charged, and linked to your account.
- The terminal is connected to stable Wi-Fi or mobile data with a strong signal.
- You have run a small test transaction and confirmed the money arrived in your dashboard.
- You and your staff know the payment steps: enter the amount, confirm it, wait for approval, give a receipt.
- You know what to do when a payment is declined, times out, or debits the customer without approval.
- You check the terminal total against your dashboard and your sales record every evening.
- Your settlement schedule is set, and you know when money reaches your bank account.
- The terminal is charged every night and locked away safely at closing.
- Refunds need your approval, and your refund policy is clear to staff and customers.

A Paystack Terminal is not a complicated piece of technology. It is a simple tool that does one job well: it takes your customer's money when they are ready to pay. Set it up properly, train your staff, check your totals every day, and you will stop losing sales to failed transfers — and you will know, at the end of every day, exactly how much your business earned.
