Digital inclusion means ensuring everyone has access to and can effectively use digital technologies. Despite Africa's rapid digital growth, over 50% of the continent's population remains offline. In 2026, with the African Continental Free Trade Area accelerating and digital services becoming essential infrastructure, bridging this gap is more urgent than ever.

Bridging this gap is not just a social issue — it is a business opportunity and a necessity for sustainable development.

## The Scale of the Problem

### Current Stats

- Africa has the lowest internet penetration of any region: ~40%
- Rural areas are disproportionately affected (under 20% connectivity)
- Women are 30% less likely to have internet access than men
- Internet costs in Africa are among the highest globally relative to income

### Why It Matters

- Digital inclusion is linked to GDP growth (10% increase in broadband = 1.4% GDP growth)
- Remote work, education, and healthcare depend on connectivity
- Businesses cannot reach customers who are offline
- The next billion internet users will be largely African

## Barriers to Digital Inclusion

### Infrastructure

- Limited fibre optic coverage in rural areas
- Unreliable electricity (affects internet devices and infrastructure)
- Expensive last-mile connectivity

### Affordability

- Smartphones cost 2-3 months of income for many Africans
- Data costs are high relative to income
- Device financing is limited

### Digital Literacy

- Many potential users lack basic digital skills
- Content is often not available in local languages
- Fear and mistrust of technology

## What Is Being Done

### Infrastructure Initiatives

- **Google Equiano** — Subsea cable connecting Africa to Europe (lowered internet costs by 20% in Nigeria)
- **2Africa** — World's largest subsea cable project circling Africa
- **Starlink** — Satellite internet available in Nigeria, Kenya, Rwanda, and more
- **Liquid Telecom** — Pan-African fibre network

### Affordability Solutions

- **Pay-as-you-go smartphones** — M-Kopa, Safaricom
- **Zero-rated services** — Facebook Free Basics, Wikipedia Zero
- **Community networks** — Local, low-cost internet infrastructure

### Digital Literacy Programs

- Google Digital Skills for Africa (free training)
- Microsoft's Airband Initiative
- Local NGOs and tech hubs offering training

## What African Businesses Can Do

### Build for Inclusion

- Design offline-capable applications
- Optimise for low bandwidth
- Support USSD and SMS interfaces
- Provide content in local languages
- Create text-only versions of your service

### Hire and Train Locally

Invest in digital skills training for your community. Employees with digital skills earn 2-3x more, creating a virtuous cycle of demand and capability.

### Partner With Inclusion Initiatives

- Sponsor community internet access points
- Donate devices to schools and training centres
- Provide internships and apprenticeships

## The Opportunity

Digital inclusion is not just a development challenge — it is a market opportunity. The next billion internet users are African. Businesses that build for inclusion today will serve the customers of tomorrow.

### Key Statistics

- Each new internet user in Africa generates $200-500 in annual digital economic activity
- Bridging the gender digital divide could add $150 billion to Africa's GDP
- Digital inclusion creates jobs, enables entrepreneurship, and improves public services

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How can small businesses contribute to digital inclusion?**

Start with your own practices: ensure your website works on low-end devices and slow connections, offer training to your community, and hire and train locally.

**Is Starlink available in Nigeria?**

Yes. Starlink launched in Nigeria in 2023 and is available in most urban and peri-urban areas. Monthly cost is approximately ₦50,000 for the hardware and ₦40,000 per month for service.

**What is the biggest barrier to digital inclusion in Africa?**

Infrastructure is the primary barrier in rural areas. Affordability is the primary barrier in urban areas. Both must be addressed simultaneously.
