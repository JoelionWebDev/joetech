Hair is big business in Nigeria. Walk through any market in Lagos, Abuja or Port Harcourt and you will see it: bundles, closures, frontals, bone straight, curly, coloured, and ready-to-wear wigs in every length. Women buy hair for birthdays, weddings, church, work, and simply because they want to look good. And more and more of them buy it online, from vendors they found on Instagram, TikTok or WhatsApp.

This guide is for anyone who wants to sell hair and wigs online in Nigeria — whether you are starting with five wigs in your wardrobe or planning a full hair store. It covers sourcing, pricing, setting up your online shop, photographing your products, taking payments, delivering safely, and keeping customers coming back.

## Why Hair Sells So Well Online in Nigeria

Hair is a repeat purchase. A customer who buys a wig today will come back for another one in three months, or for a different style for a wedding next month. That repeat buying is what makes a hair business grow faster than many other online businesses.

The margins are also attractive. A wig you buy for ₦80,000 can sell for ₦120,000 to ₦150,000. But those margins only exist if you buy well, price honestly, and sell hair that matches what you advertised. One bad batch — hair that sheds, tangles or turns out to be synthetic when you promised human hair — can destroy a reputation you spent a year building.

December is the biggest month, because of Christmas parties, weddings and Detty December events, but there is demand all year round. A vendor in Lekki, Ikeja, Wuse or GRA who posts consistently and delivers on time can build a customer base across the whole country.

## Step 1: Decide What Kind of Hair You Will Sell

Do not try to sell everything. A vendor who stocks bone straight, deep wave, curly, coloured, short, long, closure wigs, frontal wigs, bundles, ponytails and attachments all at once will tie up all her money in stock that moves slowly. Start narrow and expand as you learn what your customers actually buy.

Here are the main lanes in the Nigerian hair market:

- **Ready-to-wear wigs:** The most popular lane online. Glueless closure wigs and frontal wigs, factory-made or hand-made, in lengths from 10 to 30 inches. Prices in Lagos typically run from ₦45,000 for a short closure wig to ₦250,000 or more for a long luxury frontal wig.
- **Bundles and closures:** For customers who want to make their own wigs, or who have a stylist. Bundles sell from ₦30,000 to ₦90,000 each depending on length and grade, and closures sell from ₦25,000 to ₦70,000.
- **Budget-friendly wigs:** Synthetic and human-hair-blend wigs for customers who want a good look without spending a fortune. These sell from ₦20,000 to ₦60,000 and move fast, especially among students and young professionals.
- **Luxury raw and virgin hair:** High-end human hair for customers who want the best quality and are willing to pay for it. These sell from ₦150,000 to ₦500,000 and above for long lengths. The margins are high, but so is the risk if you buy from the wrong supplier.
- **Coloured and statement wigs:** Burgundy, blonde, ginger, highlighted and ombre wigs. These photograph beautifully and sell well around festive seasons.

Choose based on three things: how much capital you have, who your customers are, and what you know. If your audience is students and young workers in Yaba or Akoka, budget wigs and short lengths will move fastest. If your audience is working professionals and brides in Lekki or Victoria Island, luxury and frontal wigs will sell better.

## Step 2: Find a Reliable Supplier

Your supplier is the most important business decision you will make. Bad hair ruins your reputation faster than anything else. Never buy a large quantity from a supplier you have not tested.

### Where vendors source hair

- **Wholesale markets in Nigeria:** Balogun market in Lagos, Onitsha main market, and Wuse market in Abuja. You can touch the hair, check the quality, and negotiate face to face. Prices are often better than online, and you can start small.
- **Direct suppliers in China and Vietnam:** Many established vendors import directly through agents. The prices are lower, but you need to factor in shipping, customs, and the risk of a supplier who sends different hair from the sample. Only go this route after you have tested the supplier with a small order.
- **Nigerian wholesale suppliers online:** Several trusted suppliers sell on Instagram and WhatsApp. Ask other vendors for recommendations, and always test with a small order first.

### How to test a supplier

Order one bundle or one wig first. Wash it. Comb it. Straighten it. Curl it. Wear it for two weeks. Check whether it sheds, tangles, smells strange, or loses its curl after washing. Ask the supplier direct questions: Is this raw, virgin or blend? Can it be bleached? What is the weft like? A good supplier answers clearly. A supplier who dodges questions is a warning sign.

Never pay a new supplier in full without a track record. Use a payment method that gives you some protection, and keep records of every transaction. If a deal looks too cheap to be true — a 30-inch raw hair wig for ₦40,000 — it almost certainly is not what it claims to be.

## Step 3: Price Your Hair Honestly

Pricing hair is where many new vendors fail. They price by copying a big vendor on Instagram, or by guessing, and then discover they are selling at a loss after delivery costs and discounts.

### Work out your true cost

Your cost is not just the price you paid the supplier. Add shipping or transport, customs or agent fees, packaging, delivery, data and your time. A wig that costs ₦80,000 from the supplier might cost you ₦95,000 by the time it is in your hands and packaged for a customer.

Use this formula:

**Selling price = supplier cost + shipping and fees + packaging + delivery allowance + your profit**

Aim for at least 30 to 40 percent gross margin. If your total cost is ₦95,000, your price should be ₦130,000 to ₦150,000. Customers will pay for hair that looks good, feels good, and matches what you advertised. They will not come back to a vendor who sold them a wig that tangled in two weeks.

### Publish a clear price list

Publish prices for your main products and pin them in your WhatsApp Business catalogue and Instagram highlights. For example:

- 14-inch closure wig, bone straight — ₦65,000
- 18-inch frontal wig, body wave — ₦120,000
- 22-inch frontal wig, deep wave — ₦165,000
- 3 bundles + closure, 18 inches — ₦110,000
- Short pixie cut wig — ₦35,000

State the length, the style, the cap type and whether the wig is glueless. Be clear about what is included: a wig cap, an elastic band, and the packaging. Charge extra for custom colouring, extra styling, and express delivery. And never advertise a price you cannot honour — a customer who sees ₦60,000 on your page and is told ₦85,000 in the chat will leave and tell her friends.

## Step 4: Set Up Your Online Shopfront

Your customers will find you on Instagram, TikTok and WhatsApp. Each one has a job to do, and you need all three working together.

### Instagram

Your Instagram page is your shop window. Use a business account with a clear name, for example "Hair by Ada — Lagos." Your bio should say what you sell, where you are based, and how to order: "Luxury and budget wigs in Lagos. Nationwide delivery. Order via WhatsApp. Link below." Put your WhatsApp link in your bio so a customer can tap once and start chatting.

Your feed should be full of your actual products. Post every wig you stock, worn by a model or on a mannequin head, with the length, style and price in the caption. Post videos of the hair being combed, straightened, curled and worn. A video of a wig moving naturally sells far better than a flat photo.

### TikTok

TikTok is where many Nigerian hair vendors find new customers today. Short videos of wig installations, hair transformations, and before-and-after looks go viral easily. You do not need expensive equipment — a phone, good light and a willing model are enough. Post at least three times a week and use sounds that are trending in Nigeria.

### WhatsApp Business

Download WhatsApp Business and set up a business profile with your shop name, area, opening hours and a clear profile photo. Your catalogue is your price list: add every wig with a good photo, the exact name, the price and a short description. "22-inch frontal wig, deep wave, glueless, ₦165,000." Set up quick replies for the questions you answer every day: "/prices" sends your price list, "/delivery" sends your delivery areas and fees, "/payment" sends your account details.

## Step 5: Photograph and Film Your Hair Properly

Hair is a visual product. Nobody can touch your wig from a photo, so the photo and video have to do all the selling. Good content will bring you orders; bad content will quietly kill your business.

Film in daylight, near a window, against a clean background. Show the wig on a model or a mannequin head, from the front, the side and the back. Comb it on camera so customers can see that it does not shed. Straighten a section and curl a section, so customers can see that the hair can take heat. Show the lace, the knots and the cap construction in close-up — customers who know hair will check these details.

Always show the real product. Never use another vendor's photos, and never post a photo of a luxury wig when you are selling a budget blend. The customer will open the package, see the difference, and demand a refund — or worse, post about it. Honest photos build trust, and trust is what brings repeat customers.

Take a short video of every new wig you stock. A 20-second clip of the wig being worn and moved around is worth more than ten still photos. Post these videos on your status, your feed and TikTok on the same day the stock arrives.

## Step 6: Take Orders and Payments Safely

Orders will come from Instagram DMs, TikTok comments, WhatsApp chats, status replies and referrals. You need one simple order process that works every time.

Ask every customer for the same details: name, the wig or bundle they want, the length, the colour, the delivery address with a landmark, and their phone number. Confirm the order back to them in one message: "Confirmed: 18-inch frontal wig, body wave, ₦120,000 + delivery to Ikeja (₦2,500) = ₦122,500. Please send payment to confirm your order."

### Payment before dispatch

For new customers, collect full payment before you send the hair. This is standard in the Nigerian hair business. Hair is expensive and cannot be resold easily once it has been worn or styled. Accept transfers to your bank account, and consider a Moniepoint or Opay account for fast confirmation alerts. Always confirm when you have seen the money: "Payment received, thank you! Your wig will be dispatched today."

Be very careful with payment scams. Common tricks include fake transfer alerts and screenshots of payments that never arrived. Check your own account balance before dispatching anything — never trust a screenshot alone.

## Step 7: Deliver Hair Safely Across Nigeria

Hair is light, valuable and fragile. Good packaging and a trusted rider make all the difference.

Pack each wig in a satin bag or a protective bag, then in a sturdy box or a branded carrier bag. Include a small care card with washing and styling instructions — customers appreciate it, and it reduces complaints. If you sell bundles, wrap them neatly and label the lengths.

For deliveries within Lagos, Abuja or Port Harcourt, use a dispatch rider you trust. Agree the fee with the customer before you dispatch, and add it to the total. A typical short delivery within Lekki might cost ₦2,000 to ₦3,500. For deliveries outside your city, use a trusted logistics company: GIG Logistics, DHL, or a reliable inter-state bus service for customers who prefer it. Always share the tracking details with the customer and follow up until the package arrives.

Tell the customer the delivery fee upfront, before they pay. Nothing annoys a customer more than a surprise delivery charge after payment. If delivery to a far area is too expensive, say so honestly.

## Step 8: Market Your Hair Every Day

You do not need paid adverts to start. You need consistency and visibility in the places your customers already are.

Post on your WhatsApp Status at least once a day: a new wig, a customer wearing your hair, a restock announcement, or a short video of a wig being styled. The best times are the morning, around 7 a.m. to 8 a.m., and the evening, when people are scrolling after work.

On Instagram, post a Reel at least three times a week. Wig transformations, installations, and styling videos perform very well. Use stories for polls and questions: "Bone straight or body wave for December?" Customers love to vote, and every vote pushes your page to more people.

Run small promotions around festive seasons: a discount on a bundle deal for Christmas, a free wig cap with every order during a birthday sale, or a giveaway for your followers. Promotions should be planned and time-limited, not a permanent discount that eats your profit.

## Step 9: Keep Customers Coming Back

Getting an order is hard. Keeping a customer is cheaper and more profitable. A customer who buys a wig every three months is worth more than 20 one-time buyers.

After delivery, send a short follow-up message: "Hello Ada, hope you love the wig. Here is a quick care guide so it stays beautiful. Thank you for shopping with us!" That single message makes people feel valued. If there was a problem, they will tell you privately instead of posting about it.

Keep a simple record of your customers: their names, what they bought, their preferred lengths and styles, and when they last ordered. Three months after a purchase, send a friendly message: "Hello! New stock just arrived — 22-inch bone straight in your favourite style. Would you like me to reserve one for you?"

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Buying from an untested supplier in bulk.** Test with one wig first, always.
- **Advertising luxury hair and delivering a blend.** Your reputation will not survive it.
- **Dispatching before confirming payment in your own account.** Never trust a screenshot alone.
- **No price list.** Customers who have to ask for every price will buy from a vendor who shows prices.
- **Poor photos and videos.** Dark, blurry photos of a wig will kill your sales.
- **No records.** If you do not write down what you sold and what you spent, you will not know whether you are making money.
- **Ignoring customer messages.** Hair customers ask many questions before they buy. Slow replies send them to another vendor.

## How Much Can You Realistically Make?

It depends on your capital, your niche and your consistency, but here is a realistic example. If you sell 20 wigs a month at an average price of ₦100,000, that is ₦2,000,000 in monthly sales. If your average cost per wig is ₦65,000, your gross profit is ₦35,000 per wig, or ₦700,000 a month before delivery, data and packaging costs.

## Your First Month Action Plan

Do not wait for everything to be perfect. Start small and start this month.

1. **Week 1:** Choose your hair lane. Find two or three suppliers and test them with small orders.
2. **Week 2:** Set up Instagram, TikTok and WhatsApp Business. Photograph and film your first stock in daylight.
3. **Week 3:** Post daily on your status and at least three Reels. Send your price list to 30 contacts personally.
4. **Week 4:** Take your first paid orders. Confirm payment in your account before dispatching. Follow up with every customer.

If you sell only five wigs in your first month, that is a win. You have proof that people will pay for your hair. Build from there, one happy customer at a time.

Hair is a business built on trust. Source honestly, price fairly, show the real product, confirm payment before dispatch, and deliver what you promised. Do that every week, and the orders will come — from your phone to doorsteps across Nigeria, one wig at a time.
