When was the last time you bought something online in Nigeria without checking what other customers said first? Most of us scroll through the comments, look for photos from real buyers, or ask a friend whether the seller is reliable before sending any money. Your customers do the same. Before they buy from you, they want proof that someone else bought from you and was happy.

That proof is called a testimonial: a customer saying, in their own words, that your product or service was good. It can be a short WhatsApp message, a voice note, a video, a Google review, or an Instagram comment. It does not need to be polished — the most believable testimonials are the rough, honest ones, like "I love it, the dress fits perfectly" with three smiling emojis.

Many Nigerian small businesses have dozens of happy customers but almost no testimonials, because nobody ever asked them to put it in writing. This guide shows you how to ask for testimonials, collect them, and use them to sell more.

## Why Testimonials Work So Well in Nigeria

Nigeria is a trust-first market. Scams are common, and every buyer has either been disappointed or knows someone who has. Your own words — "we sell quality", "we deliver fast" — mean very little, because every seller says the same thing, including the dishonest ones. What buyers trust is the voice of another buyer.

This is called social proof. If ten customers say your food tastes great and arrives hot, a new customer believes it. If nobody says anything, the new customer must take a risk on your word alone, and many will not. Two businesses can sell the same product at the same price, and the one with visible reviews gets far more orders.

A testimonial also answers the questions a buyer is asking in their head: Did it arrive on time? Was the quality as promised? Would this person buy again? One good testimonial answers all of those in two sentences.

## Ask at the Right Moment, Not at Random

The best time to ask is when the customer is happiest — the moment of delight. That is usually right after they have received the product and used it, or right after you solved their problem. For a fashion seller, it is when the customer sends a photo wearing the outfit; for a food vendor, the evening after delivery; for a salon, right after the service.

Do not wait a week — after a week, the excitement has faded and your message feels like a chore. Do not ask before delivery either — the customer has nothing to review yet. The window is short, usually a few hours to two days, so build the ask into your routine: when you confirm a delivery, set a reminder to follow up the next day.

Ask more than once, gently. Many customers mean to reply but forget. If a customer says "yes, I will send it" and goes quiet, a polite reminder after two or three days is fine. One friendly follow-up often brings the testimonial you thought you had lost. Just do not chase the same person more than twice.

## How to Ask: Exact Words That Get a Yes

Most happy customers are glad to help — they simply need to be asked clearly and made to feel it will be quick and easy. Copy and adapt this WhatsApp message:

"Hello [name], thank you so much for buying from us! We are really glad you liked it. Would you mind sharing a few words about your experience? It would mean a lot to us and it helps other customers trust us. You can just reply to this message — even one or two sentences is perfect. Thank you!"

It thanks the customer first, asks a small favour, tells them exactly how to respond, and lowers the bar by saying one or two sentences is perfect. Never send a long, formal request that feels like homework — "kindly furnish us with a detailed testimonial for our marketing purposes" will get you silence. Keep it human.

If the customer replies with something short like "The shoes are nice, thank you", guide them gently: "Thank you! What did you like most — the quality, the fit, or the delivery?" Their answer gives you a testimonial with a specific detail, and specific details convince new buyers. "Good seller" is weak; "the shoes fit perfectly and arrived in two days" is strong, because it answers a real doubt. You can also ask, "If you were telling a friend about us, what would you say?" Save replies exactly as written — a customer's own words beat a polished paragraph.

Always ask for permission before sharing a testimonial publicly: "Can I share your words on our page? I will use only your first name." Most customers will say yes immediately. Never share a customer's full name, phone number, or photo without their clear agreement.

## Make It Easy: Give Customers a Simple Way to Reply

Even a willing customer will not send a testimonial if the process is difficult. The easiest channel is the one they already use to talk to you — usually WhatsApp. Do not ask a WhatsApp customer to create an account and find your business page to write a Google review. Let them reply on WhatsApp, and use their words elsewhere yourself, with permission.

If you do want Google reviews, save a short link to your review page on your phone, or print it as a QR code on a card in every delivery bag. A customer who can point their phone camera at a code and tap "write review" is far more likely to do it. If you have a website, add a short feedback form with one question and a name field — and a QR code made with Joetech's free QR code generator at /tools/qr-code-generator can link straight to it.

Offer options. Some customers prefer to type; others would rather send a voice note. Accept whatever they offer — a voice note can be transcribed, a short video can be posted as a reel, and a photo of the customer using your product is a testimonial in itself. Also collect praise passively: when a customer writes "this cake was the highlight of the party" without being asked, screenshot it, ask permission, and save it in a "Customer Love" folder on your phone.

## Video Testimonials: The Most Convincing Proof You Can Get

A written testimonial is good. A video testimonial is far better. When a buyer sees a real person, hears their voice, and watches them describe their experience, doubt melts away. Video is very hard to fake, and in a market where buyers worry about scams, that matters. A short video of a customer holding your product and smiling is worth more than ten written reviews.

You do not need a studio — a phone camera, good daylight, and a quiet corner are enough. Keep it to thirty seconds or one minute, and ask three simple questions: What did you buy? What did you like about it? Would you recommend us to a friend? Three questions, three short answers, and you have a complete video testimonial.

Many customers are nervous on camera, so make it comfortable. Ask in person when they are already happy, and offer to record it yourself. If they stumble or laugh, keep recording — the natural moments feel real. Trim the video afterwards, but do not over-edit it. If the customer is far away, ask them to record on their own phone and send it on WhatsApp, giving them the three questions in advance. Normal clothes and normal lighting prove this is a real person, not an actor.

## Screenshots as Proof: Turning Chats Into Sales Material

In Nigeria, the WhatsApp screenshot is one of the most trusted forms of proof. A screenshot of a real conversation — the customer's name, the time stamp, and their own words — feels honest in a way that a typed quote on a designed graphic sometimes does not.

Screenshot with care. Capture the customer's actual words, with enough context to show the order, the delivery, and the happy reply. If the chat shows a payment confirmation followed by "I have received it, thank you, it is beautiful", that single screenshot tells a whole story: the customer paid, the goods arrived, and the customer was pleased.

Protect privacy before you post. Hide or crop out the customer's phone number, home address, and personal details. Ask permission, use only their first name, and say so when you post: "Shared with permission from Ada, Lagos." That line builds trust twice over — it shows the review is real, and it shows you treat customers with respect. Crop tightly so the text is readable, and add a short headline like "Another happy customer in Lekki!"

Never fabricate screenshots. Do not write a fake conversation or edit a customer's words to make them sound better. Buyers are sharp, and once one catches you faking a review, they will assume all of them are fake. Real testimonials, even short and imperfect, will always beat invented ones.

## Where to Display Testimonials So Buyers Actually See Them

Collecting testimonials is only half the job. The other half is putting them where buyers see them at the moment they are deciding. A testimonial saved in your phone gallery helps nobody — one placed on your homepage, next to your prices, or in your WhatsApp catalogue can close a sale.

On your website, add a testimonials section to your homepage and one or two short reviews near every product or service, close to the point of decision — beside the price, the "order now" button, or the booking form. A buyer hesitating over the price who then reads "worth every naira, I have already ordered again" is much more likely to complete the purchase.

On Instagram, create a highlights folder called "Reviews" or "Happy Customers" and save testimonial stories there. New visitors check highlights before they follow or buy. Post testimonials on your feed regularly, and pin your best video to the top of your profile.

On WhatsApp, post a testimonial screenshot to your status once or twice a week, and add your best reviews to your WhatsApp Business profile and catalogue descriptions. A customer browsing your catalogue and seeing a review under the product is already halfway to buying. In direct sales chats, send a testimonial that answers the buyer's live doubt — a screenshot confirming quality, or a video of a customer in their city.

## Turning Testimonials Into More Sales

Match the testimonial to the doubt. Every product has a common objection: "too expensive", "might not fit", "delivery might be slow", "might be fake". Collect testimonials that answer each objection and keep them labelled. When a buyer raises the price objection, send the review from the customer who said "I thought it was expensive too, but the quality made it worth it". That reply can save a sale that a discount would not.

Turn testimonials into content. Share one customer story each week on Instagram or Facebook, with a photo of the product and the customer's words in the caption. Over time this builds a public record of happy customers, and it keeps your page active without you inventing new content every day. Use testimonials in your adverts too: an advert that opens with a real customer's face and voice saying "my skin has never looked this good" stops the scroll far better than "buy our cream, it is the best". You can also point adverts at a testimonials page on your website, so people who click arrive at proof instead of promises.

Ask for referrals alongside testimonials. When a customer has just praised you, they are in the perfect mood to recommend you: "If you know anyone who might need this too, please share our number with them." Some businesses offer a small reward for every new customer sent their way, but never make a testimonial conditional on a reward — a paid-for review is an advert, not a testimonial. Finally, track which testimonials sell — the screenshots that get the most replies, the videos shared most. Put those in the most visible places and refresh them every few months.

## Handling Negative Feedback Honestly

No business gets only good reviews, and you should not want to. A page with nothing but perfect praise looks suspicious. A page with mostly happy customers and one or two honest complaints — answered politely — looks real. The way you handle a complaint often matters more than the complaint itself.

When a customer complains, respond quickly and calmly. Thank them, apologise, and ask how you can fix it. Do not argue in public, do not blame the customer, and do not delete the comment unless it is abusive or false. A reply like "We are sorry the delivery was late. We have spoken to our dispatch rider and would like to resend your order free of charge" shows every other buyer that you take responsibility.

A fixed complaint can become one of your most persuasive testimonials: a problem, a fix, a happy ending. The exception is dishonest feedback: if someone who never bought from you leaves a false review, respond once with the facts, then report it to the platform. Never write fake positive reviews to bury a real complaint. Buyers can tell, and the damage to your trust is far worse than the complaint itself.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many testimonials does my small business need?

Start with five to ten good ones. Five genuine, specific testimonials displayed well are enough to make a new buyer trust you. After that, add one or two new ones every month so your proof stays fresh. Quality matters more than quantity: one detailed review mentioning fast delivery and good quality is worth more than twenty that just say "nice".

### What if my customers do not want to be on video?

That is fine. Most customers prefer to write a message, and a written testimonial is still powerful. Never pressure anyone to appear on camera — offer a written message, a voice note, or a photo of the product instead. As customers see others appearing in your videos, some will volunteer on their own.

### Can I use a testimonial sent privately on WhatsApp?

Yes, as long as you ask for permission first. A private message is still the customer's words, but they did not send it expecting it to be published. "Thank you! May I share your words on our page? I will use only your first name" is all it takes. If they say no, respect that and keep it private.

### Should I offer a discount in exchange for a testimonial?

It is better not to pay for testimonials. A small thank-you gift afterwards is fine, but offering a discount in advance for a good review makes it less honest. Ask for honest feedback and let any reward be a surprise thank you afterwards, not a condition.

### What should I do with a testimonial that mentions a problem we fixed?

Share it — it is one of your strongest stories. A customer who says "the first order had an issue, but they replaced it the same day and I have ordered twice since" proves you stand behind your work. That reassurance often decides whether a buyer orders.

## What to Do Next

First, open your WhatsApp and scroll through your recent chats. Find three customers who already said something kind, screenshot their words, and message each of them today to ask if you may share their testimonial. By this evening, you will have your first three testimonials ready to publish.

Second, create a "Customer Love" folder on your phone and a highlights folder called "Reviews" on Instagram. From today, save every kind word the moment it arrives, and post one testimonial to your WhatsApp status and Instagram highlights each week. Within a month, you will have a visible record of happy customers.

Third, write down your three video questions — what did you buy, what did you like, and would you recommend us — and ask your next happy customer to answer them on camera. One short video this week will do more for your sales than any advert. Start small, stay consistent, and let your customers sell for you.
