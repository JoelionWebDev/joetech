A customer finds your website on Google. They like your work. They want a price for a website, a cleaning job, a cake, or a training session. They look for a way to contact you. If they find only a phone number, they may call later and forget. If they find a clear contact form, they can send their enquiry in one minute, even at midnight. That enquiry lands in your email or WhatsApp, ready for you to reply in the morning.

Many Nigerian small business websites fail at this step. The form is hidden at the bottom of the page, it asks for too much information, it shows an error when submitted, or nobody ever replies to the messages it collects. The business loses customers it never knew about.

This guide shows you how to set up a contact form that actually brings you enquiries. You will learn what fields to include, where to place the form, how to connect it to your email or WhatsApp, how to stop spam, and how to reply in a way that turns an enquiry into a sale.

## Why a Contact Form Matters

A contact form gives customers a low-pressure way to reach you. Some people do not like calling a stranger. Others are browsing at night when your shop is closed. A form lets them send their question without waiting for your working hours. It also gives you the details you need in writing, so you do not have to rely on memory from a phone call.

A form also helps you look professional. A business with a working contact form looks more established than one with only a social media link. It tells the customer that you are organised and that you take enquiries seriously.

For you, a form creates a record. Every enquiry is saved with a name, a phone number, a service needed and a date. You can track how many enquiries you receive each week, which services people ask about most, and how many turn into paying customers. That information helps you decide what to promote and what to improve.

A form also works while you sleep. A customer in Port Harcourt can send an enquiry at 11pm. A customer in London can send one at 3am Nigerian time. When you wake up, the enquiries are waiting for you. Without a form, those customers might move on to a competitor whose website makes it easier to get in touch.

## Step 1: Decide What You Want the Form to Do

Before you build anything, decide what a good enquiry looks like for your business. A wedding planner needs different information from a web designer. A tutor needs different information from a cleaning company. Write down the three or four pieces of information you must have before you can reply properly.

For most service businesses, the essential details are the customer's name, phone number or WhatsApp number, the service they need, and a short message describing what they want. A web designer might also need to know the type of website and the customer's budget range. A caterer might need the event date, the number of guests and the location. A cleaner might need the type of property and the number of rooms.

Do not collect information you will not use. If you never call customers on the phone, do not ask for a phone number and an email address and a WhatsApp number. One contact method is enough. If you do not need the customer's company name, do not ask for it. Every extra field makes the form longer and reduces the number of people who complete it.

Also decide where the enquiries should go. Should they arrive in your email inbox? Should they go to WhatsApp? Should they be saved in a spreadsheet? For most small businesses, email plus WhatsApp is the best combination. Email gives you a permanent record, and WhatsApp lets you reply quickly from your phone.

Finally, decide who will reply to enquiries and how fast. A form is useless if nobody checks it. Choose one person to own the inbox, and set a reply target, such as within four working hours. Put that promise on your website if you can keep it: "We reply to all enquiries within four working hours."

## Step 2: Choose the Right Fields

A good contact form is short. Aim for four to six fields. More than that, and many visitors will leave without submitting. Here is a simple structure that works for most Nigerian small businesses.

Name is first. Use one field for full name, not separate fields for first name and surname. One field is faster to fill in on a phone.

Phone or WhatsApp number is second. This is the most important field, because it is how you will reply. Make it clear that you need a WhatsApp number if possible. Add a small note under the field: "Please enter your WhatsApp number so we can reply quickly."

Service needed is third. Use a dropdown list instead of a blank box. A dropdown is faster for the customer and easier for you to sort later. List your main services, for example: Website Design, Logo Design, Social Media Management, Training, Other. A customer can choose in one tap instead of typing a long description.

Message is fourth. This is a larger box where the customer can explain what they need in their own words. Add a helpful prompt inside the box, such as "Tell us what you need, your preferred date, and your budget if you have one." This prompt helps customers write a useful message instead of just writing "Hello" or "I need your service."

Email address is optional. Add it only if you need it. Many Nigerian customers check WhatsApp far more often than email, so a phone number is more useful. If you do ask for email, make it optional, not required, so a customer without easy access to email can still submit the form.

Budget is useful for some businesses and not for others. If you sell high-value services, a budget dropdown can save time. Options such as "Under ₦50,000", "₦50,000 to ₦150,000", "₦150,000 to ₦500,000" and "Above ₦500,000" help you know whether the customer can afford your service before you spend an hour on a call. Do not ask for a budget if your prices are fixed and shown on your website already.

Location is useful if you serve specific areas. A cleaning company in Lekki may need to know whether the customer is in Lekki, Ajah, Victoria Island or Ikeja. A dropdown with your service areas is quick to fill in and helps you plan. Do not ask for a full home address in a contact form. That is private information, and customers may not want to share it before they trust you.

## Step 3: Place the Form Where Customers Can Find It

A form hidden on a page nobody visits will not bring enquiries. Place it where customers naturally look for it.

Create a dedicated Contact page. This is the page customers expect to find when they want to reach you. Put the form at the top of the page, not at the bottom after a long block of text. Add your phone number, WhatsApp link, email address and business address on the same page, so customers who prefer to call can do so.

Add a shorter version of the form, or a clear button linking to it, on your homepage. A button that says "Get a Free Quote" or "Send an Enquiry" near the top of your homepage will catch visitors who are ready to act. Do not make them search for it.

Add the form link to your service pages too. A customer reading about your website design service is more likely to enquire if there is a form or button right there on that page. A customer who has to go back to the homepage and then find the Contact page may give up.

Make sure the form works well on a phone. Most Nigerian website visitors browse on a mobile phone, often on mobile data. The form should be easy to tap, the text should be large enough to read, and the submit button should be easy to find. Test the form on your own phone before you launch it. If you struggle to fill it in, your customers will struggle too.

## Step 4: Build the Form on Your Website

How you build the form depends on what your website is built with. The steps are different for WordPress, Wix, Squarespace and a custom website, but the principles are the same.

If your website is on WordPress, you can use a form plugin such as WPForms, Contact Form 7, or Gravity Forms. WPForms is a good choice for beginners because you can build a form by dragging fields into place. Contact Form 7 is free and works well, but it needs a little more setup. Install the plugin, create a new form, add the fields from Step 2, set the email address where enquiries should go, and add the form to your Contact page. Test it by submitting a test enquiry yourself.

If your website is on Wix or Squarespace, both have built-in form tools. Add a form block to your Contact page, choose the fields you need, and set the email address for notifications. These platforms usually store the enquiries in your website dashboard as well as sending them by email, so you have a backup record.

If your website was built by a developer, ask them to build a form that sends enquiries to your email and, if possible, to WhatsApp. A simple form can send an email with the customer's details and a link that opens WhatsApp with a pre-filled message. This gives you two ways to receive the same enquiry, so you are less likely to miss it.

Whichever platform you use, check three things after you build the form. First, does the form submit without an error? Second, does the enquiry arrive in your email? Third, does the customer see a clear success message after submitting? If any of these fail, fix it before you tell anyone about the form.

## Step 5: Write a Good Success Message

What happens after a customer clicks submit matters as much as the form itself. If the page goes blank or shows a technical error, the customer will think the enquiry failed and may not try again. If they see a clear success message, they will feel confident and wait for your reply.

Write a success message that confirms the enquiry and tells the customer what happens next. For example: "Thank you, Ada. We have received your enquiry and we will reply on WhatsApp within four working hours. If your matter is urgent, you can call us on 0803 000 0000." This message is short, friendly and useful.

Add the success message on the same page if you can, rather than sending the customer to a new page that looks different from your website. If you do send them to a thank-you page, make sure it matches your website design and includes a link back to your homepage.

## Step 6: Stop Spam Without Blocking Real Customers

Once your form is live, you may receive spam messages. These are often sent by bots, not real people. They may advertise other services, include strange links, or send the same message many times. Spam is annoying, but you can reduce it without making your form hard for real customers to use.

The simplest protection is a spam filter built into your form plugin. Many WordPress form plugins include a honeypot field, which is invisible to humans but traps bots. Others connect to a service like reCAPTCHA, which asks the user to confirm they are not a robot. Use the simplest version of reCAPTCHA if you use it, because a difficult puzzle will frustrate real customers.

Another simple step is to add a basic question that a human can answer easily, such as "What is 3 + 4?" This stops many bots but is easy for a real customer. Do not use a question that is hard or confusing.

Check your spam folder regularly. Sometimes a real enquiry is wrongly marked as spam. If you only check your main inbox, you may miss a genuine customer. Set a reminder to check the spam folder once a day, and move any real enquiries to your inbox.

## Step 7: Reply Fast and Follow Up

A form only brings you enquiries. Your reply turns those enquiries into customers. Speed matters. A customer who sends an enquiry to three businesses will usually buy from the one that replies first with a clear, helpful answer.

Set up email notifications on your phone so you see new enquiries quickly. When an enquiry arrives, reply within four working hours, and faster if you can. Your first reply should thank the customer, answer their question if you can, and ask for any missing information you need. For example: "Thank you for your enquiry, Tunde. Yes, we can design a website for your fashion business. Our basic package is ₦150,000 and takes two weeks. Can you tell me how many pages you need and whether you already have a logo?"

If the customer does not reply to your first message, follow up once after two days. A polite follow-up can bring back a customer who was busy or forgot. "Hello Tunde, I hope you are well. I wanted to check if you still need a website for your fashion business. I am happy to answer any questions." Do not follow up more than twice. After that, you risk annoying the customer.

Keep a record of enquiries and outcomes. A simple spreadsheet with the date, name, service, and result — replied, quoted, booked, or lost — will show you which services bring the most enquiries and where you lose customers. If many people enquire but few buy, your price may be too high, your reply may be too slow, or your first message may not answer their question clearly.

## How to Test Your Form Every Month

A form can break without you noticing. A plugin update, a change in your email settings, or a problem with your website host can stop enquiries from arriving. Test your form once a month to make sure it still works.

Submit a test enquiry yourself using a different email address and phone number. Check that the enquiry arrives in your inbox within a few minutes. Check that the success message appears. Check that any automatic reply reaches the customer. If anything fails, fix it immediately and test again.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

The first mistake is asking for too much information. A form with twelve fields will scare away most visitors. Keep it to four to six fields and collect the rest later in the conversation.

The second mistake is not replying. A form that collects enquiries nobody reads is worse than no form at all, because the customer thinks you have ignored them. Assign one person to check the inbox every working day.

The third mistake is using a personal email address that you rarely check. Use a business email address that you check daily, such as info@yourbusiness.com, and make sure notifications are turned on.

The fourth mistake is hiding the form. If your Contact page is hard to find, or the form is at the bottom of a long page, visitors will not use it. Put the form near the top and link to it from your homepage and service pages.

The fifth mistake is forgetting to check your website after an update. A WordPress update or a plugin update can break a form. Test the form after every major update, not just once a month.

## Final Thoughts

A website contact form is a small part of your website, but it can make a big difference to your business. It turns anonymous visitors into named enquiries, it works while you sleep, and it gives you a record you can follow up on. Set it up properly, keep it short, place it where customers can find it, reply fast, and test it regularly.

Start with the basics. Choose four to six fields, build the form, write a clear success message, protect it from spam, and assign someone to reply to every enquiry. Then improve it step by step, adding a form to your service pages, a WhatsApp button, and a clear price range. Over time, you will see more enquiries, faster replies, and more customers who found you through your website and chose you because it was easy to get in touch.
