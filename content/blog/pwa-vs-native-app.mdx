Every business eventually faces the question: should we build a mobile app? And if so, should it be a Progressive Web App (PWA) or a native app?

The answer depends on your budget, audience, and feature requirements. Here is a clear comparison to help you decide.

## What Is a PWA?

A PWA is a website that behaves like a mobile app. Users can install it on their home screen, receive push notifications, and access it offline. PWAs are built with web technologies (HTML, CSS, JavaScript) and do not require app store approval.

## What Is a Native App?

A native app is built specifically for iOS or Android using platform-specific languages (Swift, Kotlin, or cross-platform tools like Flutter). It is distributed through the Apple App Store and Google Play Store.

- Your app is content-heavy (news, blog, e-commerce)
- You want search engine discoverability
- Your users are in areas with limited storage (PWAs are small)

## When to Choose a Native App

**Native apps are ideal when:**

- You need access to device hardware (camera, GPS, Bluetooth)
- You need high-end performance (games, AR, video editing)
- Your app requires complex animations
- You want to monetize through app stores
- You need offline-first functionality with local data storage

**Example:** A delivery tracking app that uses GPS for real-time driver location, camera for photo proof of delivery, and push notifications for order updates.

## The Hybrid Approach

Many successful businesses use both: a PWA for broad reach and low-friction access, plus a native app for power users who need advanced features.

**Strategy:** Launch with a PWA to validate demand, then build a native app once you have proven product-market fit and budget.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can a PWA be as good as a native app?**

PWAs have closed the gap significantly but still cannot match native apps for performance, hardware access, and complex interactions. For 80% of use cases, a well-built PWA is sufficient.

**Do PWAs work on iPhones?**

Yes, but with limitations. Safari supports PWAs but restricts some features (push notifications, background sync, long-term storage). iOS support has improved with recent versions.

**How much does it cost to maintain a PWA vs native app?**

PWAs cost 30-50% less to maintain because you have one codebase instead of two (iOS + Android). Updates are instant without app store review delays.
