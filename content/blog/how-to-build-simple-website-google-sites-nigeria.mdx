A woman in Abuja sells handmade soaps. Customers find her on Instagram, but every day she answers the same questions: where is your shop, what do you sell, how much is delivery to Lagos, how do I order? She types the same answers again and again, and sometimes she is busy and replies late, and the customer buys from someone else.

A tailor in Surulere has the same problem. A food vendor in Enugu too. Their businesses are real, their products are good, but they have no single place where a stranger can see everything at once and trust them enough to order.

A simple website solves that problem. It is your shop front on the internet, open day and night. It tells people who you are, what you sell, what it costs, and how to reach you. And you do not need to pay a developer or learn coding to build one. Google Sites is a free tool from Google that lets you build a simple website by dragging and dropping. If you can use WhatsApp and Google Docs, you can use Google Sites.

This guide shows you how to build a simple business website with Google Sites in Nigeria, step by step. You will learn how to set it up, what pages to include, how to add your products and photos, how to connect WhatsApp so customers can reach you in one tap, and how to publish and share your new website.

## Why Google Sites Works Well for Nigerian Small Businesses

Google Sites is not the most powerful website tool in the world. It cannot run an online shop with a shopping cart and automatic payments. It cannot do everything a custom website can do. But for a small business that needs a simple, clean website fast, it is an excellent choice.

First, it is free. You only need a Google account, which most Nigerians already have for Gmail. There is no hosting fee, no monthly payment, and no trial that expires.

Second, it needs no coding. You build your site by choosing a layout, typing your text, and adding photos. Everything is done with a mouse or your finger on a phone. You do not need to learn HTML, and you do not need to hire anyone.

Third, it works well on phones. Most of your customers will visit your website on a phone, using mobile data. Google Sites automatically makes your site look good on a small screen, without you doing anything extra.

Fourth, it is reliable. Your website is hosted by Google, so it loads fast and rarely goes down. You do not need to worry about renewing hosting or fixing a broken server.

## Before You Start: What You Need

Before you open Google Sites, gather four things. Doing this first will save you hours.

First, your business information. Write down your business name, a one-sentence description of what you do, your location, your phone number, your WhatsApp number, your email address, and your opening hours. Keep this in a note on your phone.

Second, your products or services and their prices. List your main products or services, with a short description and a price for each one. Start with five to ten items. You can add more later. Use real prices in naira.

Third, good photos. Take clear photos of your products, your shop, or your work, in daylight, with a clean background. Use your phone camera. You need at least five good photos: one for the top of your homepage, and one for each main product or service. Blurry, dark photos will make your business look careless, so take your time.

You also need a Google account. If you have Gmail, you already have one. If not, create one for your business, for example with your business name, so your website and your email match.

## Step 1: Create Your Site

Open your browser and go to sites.google.com. Sign in with your Google account. Click the plus button or the blank site option to create a new site. Google will open a blank website with a simple editor.

First, give your site a name. Click where it says "Untitled site" at the top left and type your business name, for example "Adaeze Catering" or "Bright Path Tailoring". This name will appear in the browser tab when people visit your site.

Next, choose a theme. On the right side of the editor, you will see the Themes tab. Pick a simple theme with colours that suit your business. A food business might choose warm colours. A fashion business might choose black and white. Do not spend too long on this. You can change the theme later. Pick one that looks clean and easy to read.

Then, look at the editor. The page has a header at the top, where you can add a title and a background photo, and a body below, where you add your content. The right-hand panel has three tabs: Insert, Pages, and Themes. Insert lets you add text, images, buttons, and more. Pages lets you add new pages. Themes lets you change colours and fonts.

Take five minutes to click around and get comfortable. You cannot break anything. Every change can be undone, and nothing goes live until you press Publish.

## Step 2: Plan Your Pages — Keep It Simple

A simple business website needs four or five pages. More pages confuse visitors. Fewer pages leave them with questions. Plan these pages before you build them.

Your homepage is the most important page. It should tell a visitor in ten seconds what you do, where you are, and how to order. It should have a clear headline, a few photos, your main products, and a WhatsApp button.

Your About page tells your story. Who are you, when did you start, why should people trust you? Keep it short and honest. Customers buy from people they trust.

Your Products or Services page lists what you sell, with photos and prices. This is the page customers will check most often.

Your Contact page has your phone number, WhatsApp number, email, address, and opening hours. If you deliver, say where you deliver and what delivery costs.

A Testimonials or Reviews page is useful if you have happy customers. Even three short reviews, with the customers' first names, will help a new visitor trust you.

## Step 3: Build a Homepage That Sells

Your homepage is your shop window. A visitor decides in a few seconds whether to stay or leave. Make those seconds count.

Start with the header. Click on the header area and add a title — your business name — and a short line under it that says what you do. For example: "Adaeze Catering — Fresh food for events, offices, and families in Lekki." Then add a background photo of your best product or your shop. Choose a bright, clear photo.

Below the header, add a short welcome paragraph. Two or three sentences are enough. Say what you do, who you serve, and what makes you different. "We cook fresh Nigerian food for weddings, birthdays, and office trainings in Lagos. Every order is cooked on the day of delivery, and we deliver hot to your door."

Next, add your main products or services. Use the Insert panel to add a section with three or four items. For each one, add a photo, a name, a short description, and a price. For example, a caterer might show small chops, lunch packs, and party jollof. A tailor might show senator wear, dresses, and alterations.

Finally, add a short section about trust: how long you have been in business, how many customers you have served, or a short review from a happy customer. Even one line helps: "Trusted by families and offices across Lagos since 2022."

Keep your homepage short enough to scroll through in one minute. If it takes five minutes to read, most visitors will leave before they reach the order button.

## Step 4: Add Your Products and Prices

Your products page is where visitors decide to buy, so make it clear and honest.

For each product, add a photo, the name, a short description, and the price. Use real photos of your own products, not photos downloaded from the internet. Customers in Nigeria have learned to distrust businesses that use other people's photos. Your own photos, even if they are simple, build trust.

Write descriptions in plain language. Say what the product is, what it is made of, what size it comes in, and how long it takes to prepare or deliver. A soap seller might write: "Shea butter soap, 150g. Made with shea butter and honey. Gentle on sensitive skin. Price: N3,500. Delivery in Lagos: N1,500."

Use the same layout for every product so the page is easy to scan. Google Sites has ready-made layouts for this. Click Insert, choose the layout you like, and fill it in. Do not use a different style for each product. Consistency looks professional.

Add a note at the bottom of the page about payment and delivery: what payment methods you accept, whether you accept transfers, and how delivery works. Clear information prevents arguments later.

## Step 5: Write Your About and Contact Pages

Your About page does not need to be long. Three short paragraphs are enough.

The first paragraph says who you are and what you do. The second says when and why you started, and what you care about. The third says what customers can expect from you. Write it the way you would speak to a customer in your shop. Avoid big words and empty claims. Honest, simple words build trust.

Add a photo of yourself, your team, or your shop if you are comfortable. Customers like to see the person behind the business. If you are not comfortable showing your face, a photo of your workspace or your products is fine.

Your Contact page should have everything a customer needs to reach you: your WhatsApp number, your phone number, your email address, your address, and your opening hours. Add a simple map if you have a shop people can visit. Google Sites lets you insert a Google Map easily from the Insert panel.

## Step 6: Add a WhatsApp Button and Other Links

Most Nigerian customers want to order on WhatsApp. Your website should make that as easy as possible. Every page should have a clear way to reach you on WhatsApp.

To add a WhatsApp button, first create your WhatsApp link. Your link looks like this: https://wa.me/2348012345678, where the number is your WhatsApp number with the country code 234 and without the first zero. Test the link in your browser before you add it to your site. It should open a chat with your number.

In Google Sites, click where you want the button, choose Insert, then Button. Type your button text, for example "Order on WhatsApp", and paste your WhatsApp link as the button link. Place this button on your homepage, your products page, and your contact page. A visitor should never have to search for how to order.

Also add links to your Instagram, Facebook, or TikTok pages. Use the social links option or simple text links. Do not add every social account you have. Add the one or two where you post regularly and reply to messages.

## Step 7: Check Your Site on a Phone, Then Publish

Before you publish, check your site the way your customers will see it. Most of them will use a phone, so check it on a phone first.

Click the Preview button at the top of the editor. Google Sites will show you how your site looks on a computer, a tablet, and a phone. Switch to the phone view. Check that your photos look good, your text is easy to read, your buttons are easy to tap, and nothing is cut off or overlapping.

Read every word on your site. Check your phone number, your prices, and your spelling. Ask a friend or a family member to read it too. A wrong phone number on a published website is a costly mistake, because customers will try to call it and give up.

When you are happy with the site, click Publish at the top right. Google Sites will ask you to choose a web address. Your free address will look like sites.google.com/view/yourbusinessname. Choose a name that is short and easy to remember, using your business name. Click Publish again, and your site is live.

Share the link immediately. Put it in your WhatsApp bio, your Instagram bio, and your Facebook page. Send it to your regular customers and ask them what they think. Their feedback will show you what to fix first.

## Step 8: Keep Your Site Updated and Help People Find It

A website that is never updated slowly becomes useless. Prices go out of date, old products stay listed, and customers lose trust. Set a simple routine: once a month, open your site and check your prices, your photos, and your contact details. Remove products you no longer sell. Add new products. Update your opening hours if they change.

To help people find your site on Google, do four simple things. First, use clear titles on every page, with words customers actually search for, like "Catering in Lekki" or "Tailor in Surulere". Second, write a short description for each page. Third, add your business name, location, and phone number on every page. Fourth, share your link wherever you can — on your social media profiles, on your WhatsApp status, and on your Google Business Profile.

If you want your own domain name, like yourbusiness.com.ng, you can buy one later and connect it to your Google Site. You do not need to do that now. Start with the free address, earn money from the customers it brings, and upgrade when you are ready.

## A Real Example: A Soap Seller Goes Online in One Day

Consider a soap seller in Abuja. She sells on Instagram and WhatsApp, but she spends hours answering the same questions every day. One Saturday morning, she decides to build a Google Site.

She gathers her photos, writes down her six main products with prices, and writes a short story about why she started making soap. She creates the site, adds a homepage with a photo of her best-selling soap, a products page with prices, an About page, and a Contact page with her WhatsApp button. She checks it on her phone, fixes a wrong price, and publishes it before lunch.

She adds the link to her Instagram bio and her WhatsApp status. Within a week, new customers start saying they found her website first. They already know the prices before they message her. They ask fewer questions, and they order faster. She still takes orders on WhatsApp, but now the website does the explaining for her, and she spends her time making soap instead of typing the same answers.

That is what a simple website does. It does not replace WhatsApp or Instagram. It supports them, by answering questions before they are asked and making your business look trustworthy to strangers.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

First, do not copy another business's text or photos. Write your own words and use your own photos. Customers can tell when a website is copied, and Google can too.

Third, do not forget your phone number. Your website exists to bring customers to you. If your number is hard to find, you will lose orders.

Fourth, do not make your text too long. Visitors scan websites. They do not read long essays. Keep your paragraphs short and your sentences simple.

Fifth, do not publish and forget. Check your site every month. A website with last year's prices tells customers you are no longer serious.

## Your One-Day Plan

Here is a plan to build your website in one day. In the morning, gather your business information, your product list with prices, and your photos. Before noon, create your site, choose a theme, and build your homepage. In the afternoon, add your products page, your About page, and your Contact page, and add your WhatsApp button. In the evening, preview the site on your phone, fix any mistakes, publish it, and share the link on your social media pages and WhatsApp status.

You do not need a developer, and you do not need a budget. You need one day, a Google account, and honest information about your business. Build the site today, share it tonight, and let it work for you while you sleep. Your customers are already searching online. Make sure they find you.
