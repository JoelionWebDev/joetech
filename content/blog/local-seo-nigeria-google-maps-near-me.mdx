Your customer picks up their phone, types "car wash near me," "barbing salon in Ikeja," or "event planner Lekki" — and Google shows them a map with five businesses. You are either on that map, or you might as well not exist.

The painful part? Most Nigerian businesses do not even have a Google Business Profile claimed, much less optimized. The good news: ranking locally is free, the competition profile that matters is usually thin, and anyone can do the work in a weekend.

This guide is the complete, no-ad-budget local SEO system — Google Business Profile, NAP consistency, a review machine, local keywords, photos, citations, and measurement.

> **Local SEO = showing up where people already search, at the moment they're ready to buy.** It is the closest thing online to a walk-in customer.

---

## Step 1: Claim and verify your Google Business Profile (the foundation)

Google decides local rankings mostly with three signals: **relevance** (do you match the search), **distance**, and **prominence** (reviews, links, citations).

1. Go to **google.com/business** → **Manage now**.
2. Enter your business name. Pick the **category** that best describes you (category is a strong signal — "African restaurant" beats "restaurant").
3. Add your **physical address** (if you serve customers at a location) or select **"service area"** if you travel (e.g. a mobile mechanic in Ikeja who serves Lekki/Ajao). For service-area businesses, list town + area, shown in the map card.
4. Add your **phone number in NAP** (more on NAP below) and website URL.
5. **Verify** via the phone/SMS, video, or postcard, then mark the listing as verified.

> **Never create a fake third party listing for the same business — that gets you flagged.** One clean, fully completed profile wins.

---

## Step 2: Optimize the profile like a title tag (every field counts)

Google reads the profile as a mini SEO page. Fill every field:

- **Category**: if you offer two goods, use both (Google allows several).
- **Operating hours** — always updated, especially "open now."
- **Services list** with descriptions.
- **Products** categories.
- **Description** ~750 characters: who you are, what you do, where you operate, keywords naturally.
- **Attributes** (e.g. "women-led," "free Wi-Fi," "wheelchair accessible" if true).
- **Bookings/Action button** — add "Book", "Order", "Call".
- **Your profile name** stays 100% consistent: *"Joetech — Web Design in Lagos"* is fine if appended consistently.

> Completed profiles with a description outrank empty "claimed" profiles on near me results.

## Step 3: Reviews — the #1 local ranking + conversion lever

Prominence is heavily review-based. More importantly, real buyers read every 4-5 lines.

**The review system that works without harassing anyone:**

1. **Ask at the right moment** — right after delivery, when the customer's smiling (not 3 weeks later).
2. **Make the link a 1-click** — create a short review link from your profile via Google's official "Get more reviews" feature (or use the profile direct link and share as short link). A link that opens the app directly = 3x more reviews.
3. **Give a prompt template**: *"Hi [Name], thanks for choosing us! Would you mind leaving a quick 2-line review here — it really helps a small business: [link]. No pressure either way!"*
4. **Print it on physical receipts / WhatsApp business card after service** — small print, big ROI.
5. **Ask via monthly broadcast** — a WhatsApp broadcast to past customers (opt-in only) once a month asking for feedback and reviews.

**What to do when a customer says "no" or "no thanks":** send a short feedback form instead. Address the complaint quickly, then if they're happy after you fix it, they may upgrade to a review.

**Types of negative reviews:**
- Respond **calm, fast, publicly**: "Thank you for your feedback [name]. We've fixed [thing] — please contact us on [line]."
- Never argue publicly. It protects your reputation far more than the review.

> **Volume + recency matters**: a review last 30 days is far more powerful than one from 2019.

## 4: Local keyword research (the free "near me" map)

Searching your business name won't work. You need to match **how people actually talk**:

1. Google **Trends** → filter Nigeria / Lagos / your city → see rising "near me" patterns.
2. **Google Autocomplete**: type "barbecue near …" let it finish "*… near me*", "*near Ikoyi*".
3. **Look at your competitor's Q&A** — actual questions customers ask are keyword gold.
4. Ask your own customers: *"What would you search to find us?"* Take the exact phrase into your:

- Profile description + category
- Your website's location pages (**"Pest control in Magodo"** page style)
- Every location-specific web page & blog you write

**Back to basics reminder from our [SEO basics guide](/blog/seo-basics-ranking-google):** for local intent, the content of your site must mention your city and your service.

## 5: Photos — the free & instant trust lever

Businesses with high-quality photos get far more clicks. Here's the fix:

- **Logo** (square, crisp) + **cover**.
- **10+ quality service/product photos** — can show your work, your team with products and your space.
- **Geo photos**: an exterior + interior + nearby landmarks, so the business feels real to the map.
- **Add photos to the Q&A** answer with a photo.
- Update your photo set **monthly** — Google loves freshness.

> Amateur tip: a photo of your **service in action** (the cake, the makeup app, the install) always outperforms a logo-only profile.

## 6: NAP consistency + citations (the boring ranks that quietly boost you)

**NAP = Name + Address + Phone.**

If the phone number is different on Facebook, and different on your website and business directories, Google's algorithms hesitate to trust you.

- **Audit**: search your own business name in quotes and note every place you appear.
- **Standardise to one format** you use everywhere (e.g. `+234 803 123 4567`, the same on your website, profile, and social media).
- **Claim systematically these free citation sources for Nigeria:**
  - Google Business Profile
  - Bing Places (yes, Bing matters — some of your customers use it)
  - Facebook Business Page (same name+address+phone)
  - Instagram (same desk NAP)
  - Nairaland / Google map directories as relevant
  - Trusted local directories where your category lists
- **Keep the NAP identical** character-by-character — this is the part that moves in Maps.

## 7: Measure it (do metrics, don't just do steps)

Track monthly with Google's **Insights** (built into GBP):

- **Search queries** — how were customers finding you? (brand vs service vs "near me")
- **Calls from directions/website clicks**
- **Photo views**

Plus: **Google Search Console** (free) → Performance → your "near me" group report. Our [guide](/blog/google-analytics-beginners-guide-nigeria) will set that up.

**60-day diagnostic:**

- Do you have 25+ reviews (including at least one in the past 30 days)?
- Is NAP identical across the 5 core places?
- Do your 2 top "near me" searches show you in the top 3?
- Were 3+ photos updated this month?

If the map doesn't change, focus on the levers in order: reviews first, consistency second, photos third.

## Your free local SEO 5-day plan

- **Day 1:** Claim, verify, and completely fill the Google Business Profile.
- **Day 2:** Fix NAP everywhere; add cover photo + 10 photos.
- **Day 3:** Set up the review link; send polite review requests to 10 happy past customers.
- **Day 4:** Write 2 service/location pages on your site (e.g. "Barbing salon in Magodo"), and list your services.
- **Day 5:** Run a "near me" test: you vs the competitor in Maps, and note the gap.
