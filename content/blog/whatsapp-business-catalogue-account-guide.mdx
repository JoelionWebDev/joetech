Your customers are already on WhatsApp. In fact, for most Nigerian service businesses, WhatsApp is where the customer says "hello," asks three questions, and then decides whether to book you. The only problem? Most business profiles look like a blank number with a dp.

The fix is free. A well-set-up **WhatsApp Business catalogue** turns your profile into a 24/7 salesperson that answers the "what, how, how much" part of the sale before you even open a conversation — and automated follow-ups make sure the person who asked you a question at 9pm doesn't forget about you by 9am.

This is the exact system we've built with service providers across Lekki, Ikeja, and Abuja — plumbers, makeup artists, photographers, clinic owners, event planners, and consultants.

## Step 1: Switch to WhatsApp Business (free) and set up your identity

If you still use personal WhatsApp to run your hustle, you're carrying a huge disadvantage. The Business app gives you a catalogue, labels, quick replies, away messages, and business hours — all free.

1. Download **WhatsApp Business** from Play Store / App Store.
2. Verify your account with the business line. Use a dedicated line if you can afford it (a separate SIM for work is worth it).
3. Fill the profile completely: **business name** (not a nickname — the name people search for), **category** (choose the most precise one), **description** (who you are + what problem you solve + what to do), **address**, **business hours**, **website** (even /services link).
4. Set your **Business Description** like a storefront sign: *"Lagos-based events photographer with 400+ weddings covered. Send a message for same-week availability."* — short, specific, and persuasive.
5. Add links to your **Business Website** (if you have one), your Instagram, and your booking page.

> **The golden rule:** A customer screenshots your profile within 5 seconds and shows it to a friend. Make sure that screenshot says what you do — not just your name.

## Step 2 — Build a catalogue that converts before a single word is typed

The catalogue is a visual price board. It must answer: *what, why, how much, how to book.* 

### Catalogue item structure (copy this)

**Title:** Clear & benefit-led — "Full Makeup (Bridal)" not "Makeup."
**Price:** give a number or a range. "₦80,000–₦120,000" is stronger than "Custom quote" because it filters customers already.
**Category:** put every selling item under ONE folder — e.g. "Sessions," "Packages," "Add-Ons."
**Description (write these 3 bullets):**
- What exactly is included (time, deliverables)
- Who it's for (1 line)
- How to book (call / click-to-chat / reply "BOOK")

**Photo:** one clear image of the deliverable result (not a screenshot of text). Vertical or square works better on mobile.

**Tip for a clean catalogue:** refresh photos seasonally, put your **best-selling item first**, and use the **"Bestseller"** tag where available.

### Written checklist

- [ ] A single collection named after what you do (e.g. "Web Design Packages")
- [ ] 3–6 items, each with number & category
- [ ] Each item ends with a clear next action ("Reply BOOK to order")
- [ ] Each item links to a service detail or full portfolio where needed
- [ ] Prices updated within 48h of changing rates

## Step 3 — Use Quick Replies to answer the questions you always get

The repeated question — "How much?" "What's included?" "Are you available Saturday?" — should not cost you 5 minutes every time. **Quick Replies** (Settings → Chat settings → Quick replies) insert pre-written text with one tap.

**Templates to copy (bold = value, direct):**

- **/price** → *"My packages start at ₦80,000. To suggest a plan, message me and tell me your date/guest count/service. Reply BOOK for the full breakdown + availability."*
- **/how** → *"Thanks for reaching out! Next step: I'll confirm your date, send a simple package selection, and hold it with a 30% deposit via bank transfer/USSD."*
- **/hours** → *"I'm live Mon–Sat 9am–6pm. Send a message and I'll reply right away."*
- **/catalogue** → *"Check everything I offer here: [link] — tap Catalogue to view items & prices."*

Use `*bold*` on prices to make WhatsApp render the important part.

## 4 — Automate follow-ups without buying expensive tools

You don't need ManyChat or a paid API subscription to start closing more chats. These methods work today:

### 4.1 Away message + trigger (the free safe bet)

Set an **Away Message** for new chats — "Thanks for messaging! I'll be with you before 6pm today. To speed things up, reply BOOK with your event date." This is your automatic first reply to every new contact outside business hours.

### 4.2 The "note + 3 taps" follow-up system (for everyone)

The problem isn't tools — it's memory. Keep a **Notion/Google Sheets follow-up board** with columns:

> Contact name | What they asked | Status (new/sent quote/followup bought) | Next follow-up date | Channel (WhatsApp/dm)

- **T+1 (24h)** — "Hi [name], just following up — did you find what you needed? If not, I'm ready to help."
- **T+5 (day 5)** — "Bringing it back up — I still have [date] open for your event. No rush, but it's filling up… Reply and I'll hold it for you."
- **T+14 (2 weeks)** — "Hi [name], one last nudge — my diary for [month] is almost full. If you'd still like to book, here's the link: [pay]. Either way, best wishes!"

The T+5 and T+14 touches start manual: spend 20 minutes a week on the board. You can later automate follow-ups with a WhatsApp Business API tool (or with Joetech integration) when volume justifies it.

> Make each follow-up a value-add, not a nudge: a testimonial, a mini tip, or genuinely updated availability — not just a "ping."

## 5 — Track what converts (and kill what doesn't)

Free metrics that matter:

- **Catalogue views** vs **enquiry chats** → is your catalogue useful? If views are rare, fix photos/descriptions.
- **Chat → booking conversion** — where do you lose them? (Track it so you know which step to fix.)
- **Speed of first reply** is a free trust-builder in itself.

Use WhatsApp **Business labels** (New, Customer, Follow-up — the six built-in labels) or a **Google Sheet** to log the funnel.

> The easiest wins are almost always in follow-up speed + follow-up frequency. A customer is far more likely to say "yes" when they got a well-timed follow-up — it makes you *look* like the professional with systems.

## 6 — Compliance & comms etiquette for Nigerian service providers

- **Never spam** people who never met you in bulk with unsolicited ads — it gets your number report-blocked and damages trust.
- **Use opt-in**: "Would you like me to update you when I have slots?" → then add them to your broadcast list.
- **Respect privacy** — don't put customers' names/profiles in public broadcast lists.
- **Being "online" everywhere**: run a morning "available now" status but keep it real (don't claim to be free when you're not).

## The 3-day setup action plan

**Day 1:** Switch to WhatsApp Business; fill name/category/description/hours; add profile pic; set **business hours**.
**Day 2:** Create your 3–6 catalogue items with the structure above; add quick replies for price/service/hours/booking.
**Day 3:** Build your Notion/Sheets follow-up board; load it with contact info from your last 15 chats; schedule T+1 and T+5 touches.

Then, every morning: "20 minutes of follow-up." That single habit is worth more than any tool subscription.
