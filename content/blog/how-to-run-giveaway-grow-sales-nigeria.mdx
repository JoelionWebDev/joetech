Every week on Instagram and WhatsApp, a Nigerian business announces a giveaway. Sometimes it works. Followers grow, orders come in, and new customers keep buying after the prize is gone. More often, it does not. The post gets likes from people who never buy, the winner disappears, and the business owner is left with less money and the same slow sales.

The difference is not luck. It is planning. A good giveaway is not about giving something away for free. It is about getting the right people to notice you, trust you, and buy from you — during the giveaway and after it ends.

This guide shows you how to run a giveaway that grows sales in Nigeria: pick a goal, choose a prize people want, set simple rules, promote it on WhatsApp and Instagram, pick a winner fairly, and turn entrants into customers. It works for a fashion shop in Lekki, a food vendor in Surulere, a skincare brand in Abuja, or a phone seller in Computer Village.

## Why Most Giveaways Fail in Nigeria

Most giveaways fail for the same three reasons. First, the prize is wrong. A business that sells wigs gives away a phone. A food vendor gives away cash. People enter for the phone or the cash, not because they want wigs or food. When the giveaway ends, they unfollow and never buy.

Second, the rules are too hard or too vague. "Follow, like, tag ten people, share to your story, join our WhatsApp group, and subscribe to our YouTube" will put off real customers. People in Nigeria are careful with data and time. If entering costs too much effort, only professional giveaway hunters will bother.

Third, there is no plan after the giveaway. The owner announces a winner, sends the prize, and goes quiet. The new followers never hear about the products again, never get an offer, and never become customers. A giveaway without follow-up is just an expensive advert for one day.

A successful giveaway avoids all three mistakes. The prize attracts your ideal buyer. The rules are simple enough to enter in one minute on a phone. And you have a clear plan to sell to entrants the day the giveaway ends.

## Start With One Clear Goal

Before you choose a prize or write a caption, decide what you want the giveaway to do. Pick one main goal. Trying to do everything at once makes the rules messy and the results hard to measure.

Here are the goals that work best for small businesses in Nigeria:

- **Get new customers:** you want people who have never bought from you to try your product. Your prize should be your product, and your follow-up should be a first-order discount.
- **Grow your WhatsApp list:** you want more people on your broadcast list or WhatsApp channel, where you can sell every day. Your entry step should send people to WhatsApp.
- **Clear old stock:** you have sizes, colours, or products that are not selling. Give away one as the prize and offer the rest at a discount to everyone who entered.
- **Launch a new product:** you want attention for something new. Give away the new product so people talk about it and share photos when they use it.
- **Get reviews and photos:** you want customers to post about you. Make sharing a photo or video part of the entry, and use those posts as proof for future buyers.

Write your goal in one sentence, for example: "Get 200 new WhatsApp contacts from Lagos who buy skincare, and turn at least 20 into customers in two weeks." That sentence will guide every decision that follows.

## Choose a Prize Your Ideal Customer Wants

Your prize should make your ideal customer stop scrolling, not just anyone. The best prize is usually your own product or service, because it attracts people who already want what you sell.

A hair vendor should give away a wig or a hair bundle, not a phone. A baker should give away a cake or a box of cupcakes, not cash. A fashion designer should give away a dress or a two-piece set. A cleaning service should give away a free deep clean for a two-bedroom flat. A tutor should give away a free month of lessons. When the winner uses your product, they become your advert. Their friends see it and want it too.

If your product is expensive, give away a smaller version. A furniture maker can give away a side stool instead of a full sofa set. A skincare brand can give away a starter kit worth ₦25,000 instead of a full set worth ₦80,000. A restaurant can give away dinner for two worth ₦30,000. The prize must feel valuable, but it must not wipe out your profit.

Set a prize budget before you start. Add the cost of the product, delivery within Lagos or Abuja, and any small ad spend. For many small businesses, a total budget of ₦30,000 to ₦100,000 is realistic. If you cannot afford that yet, start smaller: a ₦15,000 hamper, a free service, or a product bundle. A small, well-run giveaway beats a big, messy one.

Avoid cash as the main prize unless you sell a money-related service. Cash attracts everyone, including people who will never buy from you. It also creates arguments about transfer fees and fake winners. If you do offer a shopping voucher, make it a voucher to spend in your shop, not cash they can take elsewhere.

## Pick the Right Platform: Instagram, WhatsApp, or Both

Most Nigerian customers find businesses on Instagram and buy on WhatsApp. Your giveaway should use both, but keep one as the main entry point so you can track entries easily.

Instagram is best for reach. A good Reel or carousel can travel beyond your followers through shares, tags, and the Explore page. Use Instagram if your goal is new followers and brand awareness. WhatsApp is best for sales. People on your WhatsApp list see your status every day and can order in one chat. Use WhatsApp if your goal is orders and repeat buyers.

A simple combination works well: post the giveaway on Instagram, and ask people to send a keyword like "GIVEAWAY" to your WhatsApp number to enter. That way you grow Instagram reach and build a WhatsApp list at the same time. You can also ask entrants to join your WhatsApp channel, where you will announce the winner live.

Facebook still works for older customers and for businesses where Facebook groups are strong. If most of your buyers are there, run it there. Pick one main platform, add WhatsApp for entries, and do it well.

Make sure your phone can handle it. A giveaway can bring hundreds of messages in a day. Charge your phone and set up a quick reply that explains the rules.

## Write Simple Rules People Can Follow in One Minute

Complicated rules kill giveaways. Your rules should fit in one short caption and take less than a minute to complete on a phone. A good set of rules looks like this:

1. Follow our page.
2. Like this post and tag two friends who would love this prize in the comments.
3. Send "GIVEAWAY" to our WhatsApp number to confirm your entry.
4. Share this post to your story and tag us for a bonus entry.

That is enough. Each step has a purpose. Following grows your audience. Tagging brings new people. WhatsApp gives you a direct line to sell later. Sharing spreads the post. Do not ask people to tag ten friends, follow five other pages, or join three groups. Real customers will not do it, and Instagram may flag the post as spam.

Write the rules in plain English and, if your customers speak Pidgin, add a Pidgin line too. For example: "No long thing. Follow us, tag two friends, send GIVEAWAY to our WhatsApp. Na so e be." People understand you faster when you speak like them.

Include the key details in every post: what the prize is, who can enter, the closing date and time, how the winner will be picked, and when delivery will happen. For example: "Open to Lagos residents only. Ends Sunday 30 November by 6pm. Winner picked live on Instagram on Monday 1 December. Delivery within Lagos is free." Clear details prevent arguments later.

## Set a Realistic Timeline and Budget

A giveaway needs enough time to spread, but not so long that people forget it. For most small businesses, seven to ten days is ideal. Shorter than five days, and not enough people will see it. Longer than fourteen days, and excitement drops. Announce the start date, run daily reminders on your status, and count down in the last two days.

Plan your posts in advance: launch on day one, a reminder on day three, a video of the prize on day five, and a last-chance post on day seven. A clean phone video in daylight works better than a stiff graphic.

Budget for promotion. You do not need a big ad budget to start. ₦5,000 to ₦15,000 on Instagram ads targeted to Lagos or Abuja, women aged 22 to 40, or your specific audience, can bring in many more entries than posting alone. If you cannot afford ads, ask two or three loyal customers or friends with good followings to share the post. Offer them a small discount for sharing, not cash.

Also budget for delivery. If the winner is in Lekki and you are in Ikeja, delivery by dispatch rider may cost ₦3,000 to ₦7,000. State clearly whether delivery is free or the winner pays. Surprise delivery fees cause complaints and damage trust.

## Promote Without Annoying People

Promotion is where many giveaways fail. Owners post once and expect magic, or they post ten times a day and annoy their followers. Find the middle.

Post the launch on your feed and pin it. Share it to your story three times in the first day, then once a day after that. Put it on your WhatsApp status morning and evening, because that is when most people check. Send one broadcast message to your existing customers — not five. In the broadcast, explain the prize in one line and add the link to enter. People who already buy from you are your best promoters. Ask them to tag a friend who would love the prize.

Use a short video to show the prize. Hold the dress up, open the box, or wear the wig. Add text with the prize value and closing date, because many people watch without sound.


## Pick a Winner Fairly and Publicly

Nothing damages a giveaway faster than a winner announcement that looks rigged. People will assume you gave the prize to your friend or sister if you pick quietly and post a single screenshot.

Pick the winner live. Go live on Instagram or WhatsApp video at the time you promised, show the list of entries, and use a free random picker tool or a transparent draw. Call out the name, show the entry, and ask the winner to confirm in the comments. Save the live video and post it as a Reel so people who missed it can watch later.

Check that the winner followed all the rules before you announce them. Did they follow you? Did they tag friends? Did they send the WhatsApp message? If not, pick another winner on the same live session and explain why. It is better to be strict and fair than to reward someone who skipped steps while honest entrants watch.

Contact the winner within 24 hours for their name, phone, and delivery address. Deliver within the time you promised, usually three to seven days in Lagos. Post a photo of the delivery, with permission. That proof makes your next giveaway easier.

If the winner does not respond within 48 hours, announce that you will pick another winner. State this rule in advance so nobody feels cheated. Keep a record of all entries in a simple sheet: name, Instagram handle, WhatsApp number, and date entered. That sheet becomes your customer list for follow-up.

## Turn Entrants Into Paying Customers

The giveaway ends when the winner is announced. Sales begin the next day. This is the step most businesses skip, and it is where the money is.

On the day after the announcement, send a message to everyone who entered but did not win. Thank them for entering, and offer them something small and time-limited. For example: "Thank you for entering our giveaway. As a thank you, you get 10% off your first order this week only. Use code THANKYOU10 before Friday." Or: "You did not win the wig, but you can get the same style for ₦5,000 off if you order before Sunday."

Make the offer easy to use. Add a direct WhatsApp link or a payment link through Paystack or Flutterwave so they can order in two taps. Do not send a long catalogue with no clear next step. One product, one price, one link, one deadline.

Follow up once more after three days, then stop. Do not spam entrants. If they do not buy after two messages, leave them on your status list. Some will buy a month later when they need your product.

Track your results: entries, WhatsApp joins, discount uses, and sales in the two weeks after. Compare that to what you spent. If you spent ₦50,000 and made ₦150,000 from entrants, it worked. If you made only ₦10,000, change the prize or rules next time.

## Mistakes to Avoid

Do not buy fake followers or entries. It makes your numbers look big, but those accounts will never buy. Instagram also reduces the reach of pages with fake engagement.

Do not change the rules halfway through. If you said the giveaway ends on Sunday, do not extend it to Wednesday because entries are low. People will lose trust. Instead, promote more in the final days.

Do not pick a winner in secret or give the prize to family. Do not delay delivery for weeks without explaining why.

Do not run a giveaway with no stock to sell after it. If 30 people want to buy, you need products ready. Prepare stock before you launch.

Do not forget to keep your business details clear. Your WhatsApp number, delivery areas, prices, and payment account should be easy to find. If entrants cannot figure out how to order, they will not try twice. A simple link in your bio that leads to your WhatsApp and your website helps. ## A Simple Giveaway Plan You Can Copy

Here is a one-week plan you can use for your first giveaway.

Day 1: Announce the prize with a Reel and a feed post. Share to WhatsApp status. Send one broadcast to past customers.
Day 2: Post a photo of the prize with the rules in the caption. Reply to every comment.
Day 3: Share a customer review or a video of the prize in use. Remind people of the closing date.
Day 4: Go live for five minutes to answer questions about the prize and delivery.
Day 5: Post a countdown story. Ask followers to tag a friend who needs the prize.
Day 6: Last chance post. Share entries and excitement in stories.
Day 7: Close entries at 6pm. Pick the winner live. Post the winner and thank everyone.
Day 8: Send a thank-you discount to all entrants on WhatsApp. Start taking orders.

Keep the whole process simple and honest. You do not need a big team or a big budget. You need a prize your customers want, rules they can follow, a fair draw, and a follow-up offer that turns attention into sales.

## Conclusion: Give to Get Customers, Not Just Likes

A giveaway is a marketing tool, not a charity event. Its job is to bring the right people to your business and give them a reason to buy. When you choose a prize that matches your product, keep the rules simple, promote it well on Instagram and WhatsApp, pick a winner in public, and follow up with a clear offer, you turn a free prize into paying customers and repeat sales.

Start small, measure what happens, and improve next time. What matters is not likes, but how many people buy, return, and tell friends about you. That is real growth.
