Picture this: it is 8pm, your shop in Surulere has just closed, and somewhere nearby someone types "auto mechanic near me" into Google. The businesses at the top of that map get the call tomorrow morning. If your business is not on the map at all — or is buried on page three — that customer goes to your competitor.

This is local SEO, and it is the single highest-return marketing a Nigerian SME can do. No paid ads, no hype, just showing up exactly when someone in your area is ready to buy. Here is your complete, step-by-step playbook.

---

## What "local SEO" actually means

Local SEO is the practice of making your business visible to **people searching near you**, especially on Google Maps and the "local pack" (the map + three businesses that appear above regular results when you search "near me").

Two things set local SEO apart from regular SEO:

1. **The search is urgent.** Someone searching "tyre repair Ikeja" usually needs it *now* — not "someday." That means they click fast, and they click the top results.
2. **The results depend on proximity + trust + relevance.** Google decides who to show based on how close you are, how well your business matches the search, and how much evidence (reviews, info) it has about you.

The great news for Nigerian businesses: most of your local competitors are still ignoring this. So with consistent effort, you can rank ahead of businesses much bigger than yours.

---

## Step 1: Claim and nail your Google Business Profile

Your Google Business Profile is the foundation — it literally feeds Google the map data. If you do nothing else, do this properly.

**The exact setup:**

1. Go to Google Business Profile (formerly Google My Business) and search for your business. If it exists, claim it; if not, create it.
2. Enter your **exact business name** — no extra keywords stuffed in. Google does not reward "Ade & Sons Fashion — Best Boutique in Ikeja". It does reward clarity.
3. Choose your **category precisely**. "Boutique" beats "Business establishment" because it describes what you do.
4. Add your **address** exactly as a customer would find it, and your **service area** (if you're a mobile service like an electrician).
5. Add your **phone number and WhatsApp** — in Nigeria, WhatsApp is where the conversion happens, so make it prominent.
6. Upload photos of your storefront, products, and team. Businesses with photos get significantly more clicks. Show real corners of your shop, not stock photos.

**Verification:** Google will want to verify the business — usually a postcard, phone call, or video. Do it. An unverified profile is invisible.

## Step 2: Choose and use the right local keywords

When you build your website and write your profile, think like your customer searching from a phone:

- "best *laundry service* in **Yaba**"
- "*hair salon* near **Lekki**"
- "*generator repair* in **Benin City**"

**Your local keyword formula:** service + location + trust word. For example: *"barbing salon in Ikorodu"* or *"affordable web designer in Lagos"*.

**Where to place these keywords:**
- Your homepage title and headings
- Your "About" page
- Photo captions and descriptions
- Your Google Business Profile description
- Any page titles in your blog or service pages

But here is the pro move: do not stuff the same phrase everywhere. Create one solid page per location or service (for example, "Bridal Makeup in Abuja" and "Bridal Makeup in Kano") so each page is an expert on its own keyword.

> Think of it this way: a customer rarely types "business services Nigeria." They type "PRINTING WORKS near me." Win the specific search and you win the customer.

## Step 3: Turn reviews into your ranking engine

Reviews are the strongest local signal you have. Google uses quantity, recency, and relevance — a steady flow of new reviews beats a burst of old ones.

**The realistic Nigerian review strategy:**

- **Ask at the peak moment.** Immediately after delivery — payment made, service done, food eaten — ask satisfied customers on the spot. In-person, that means holding the phone open to your review link. Online, it means a WhatsApp follow-up with the direct link.
- **Make it one tap.** Create a short custom link to your Google review page and share it after every good transaction.
- **Reply to every review**, positive or negative. A polite reply to a complaint rebuilds trust far better than deleting it (and you can't delete them anyway). Encourage happy customers to mention the specific thing they loved — "fixed my generator in 30 minutes" is a better review than "excellent service."
- **Never buy fake reviews.** Google's spam filters are frighteningly good, and a cluster of fake five-star reviews from suspicious accounts can get your profile suspended. One honest review a week beats fifty fake ones that get you banned.

---

## Step 4: Keep your business information consistent everywhere

Google cross-checks your name, address, and phone number (NAP) across the whole internet. If your profile says "14 Marina Road, Lagos Island" but your website says "Marina Road, VI", Google gets confused and trusts you less.

**The consistency checklist:**

- Same business name spelling everywhere (Twitter, Facebook, website, directories — no "Ade & Sons" on one, "Ade and Sons" on another).
- Same phone/WhatsApp number everywhere.
- Same address format everywhere.
- If you are listed on Nigerian directories (like business directories or local association listings), make the details match exactly.

Even small mismatches — a carrying case full of abbreviations — quietly drag your ranking down.

---

## Step 5: Localise your website content

Your Google Business Profile gets you into the race; your website helps you win it and converts the visitor.

**Local website wins:**

1. **A dedicated contact/location section** with your map, address, and opening hours on every page footer.
2. **An embedded Google Map** on your contact page.
3. **Local content** — write about your area: "Third Mainland Bridge traffic hack for customers" or "Why Yaba's startups need a local web designer." This builds authority on local topics and attracts links.
4. **Fast mobile site** — most local searches happen on phones. If your site is a struggle on 4G, you lose to the slower-but-smoother competitor.
5. **Local maps schema** — technical markup that tells Google exactly where and what you are (a developer can add this).

---

## Step 6: Measure and adjust (the dashboard habit)

Local SEO is an engine, not a light switch. Check your Google Business Profile insights monthly:

- Where are profile views coming from — Maps or Search?
- Which search terms pulled people to you? Double down on those keywords.
- How many customers called or navigated through the map? That is your conversion.

Then adjust: if "near me" for "plumber" is driving calls but "24-hour plumber" is not, focus your content and reviews on the phrase that actually works.

## Your quick 30-day local SEO plan

- **Week 1:** Claim and complete your Google Business Profile. Verify it. Add 10 photos.
- **Week 2:** Write your local keyword pages on your website (one per service or location).
- **Week 3:** Set up the review habit — put your review link in your WhatsApp auto-reply and ask after every good order.
- **Week 4:** Reply to all existing reviews, get consistency check on NAP, and review your first month of insights.
