A customer in Lekki searches for a service on their phone. Your business appears on Google with a map pin, your opening hours, and a few photos. The customer taps through, finds your number, and calls you. Job done, right?

Not quite. That same customer, or the next one, may want to see your prices, read about your services in detail, or check whether you serve their area before they call. A Google listing cannot carry all of that. Your website can. And a website on its own, without a Google listing, can be hard for nearby customers to find in the first place.

This is not a contest between two tools. It is a partnership, and Nigerian businesses that understand the difference get more enquiries than businesses that rely on only one side.

## What a Google Business Profile actually does

A Google Business Profile is your listing on Google Search and Google Maps. When someone searches for a service near them, or searches for your business name, the profile can show your name, address, phone number, opening hours, photos, and customer reviews.

Its strength is discovery. It puts you in front of people at the exact moment they are looking for what you offer, especially on a phone, where the map results often appear before ordinary website results. For a restaurant, salon, pharmacy, hotel, or repair service, that visibility matters enormously, because many customers choose from the first few options they see.

The profile also carries social proof. Reviews from real customers sit right there next to your name, and a steady stream of honest reviews builds trust before a customer ever visits your website or your premises.

But a profile has hard limits. You do not control the layout. You cannot tell your full story, explain a service in depth, or guide a visitor step by step towards an enquiry. The information is presented in Google's format, alongside your competitors, and a customer can tap away to a rival listing in one second.

## What your website actually does

Your website is the one place online that belongs entirely to you. You control the message, the order of information, the design, and the next step you want a visitor to take.

A good website answers questions a listing cannot. What exactly do you offer? How does it work? What does it cost, or how is pricing worked out? What areas do you serve? What makes you different from the business down the road? It can carry detailed service pages, a gallery of your work, answers to common questions, and a clear enquiry form or WhatsApp button.

Your website also builds trust in a deeper way. A listing proves you exist. A website proves you are serious. When a customer lands on a clean, well-written site with real photos, clear services, and easy contact options, they feel safer spending money with you, especially for bigger purchases like a wedding venue, a renovation, or a school admission.

The weakness of a website on its own is that people have to find it. Search engines need time and content to rank it, and a brand-new site will not automatically appear when someone searches for a service in your area. That is where the profile comes back in.

## How the two work together

Think of it this way. Your Google Business Profile is the signpost, and your website is the shop. The signpost catches attention and points people in the right direction. The shop is where the real conversation happens.

Here is a practical setup that works for most Nigerian small businesses:

- Claim and complete your Google Business Profile with your correct name, address, phone number, opening hours, category, and real photos of your premises and work.
- Add your website link to the profile, so anyone who wants more detail can reach you in one tap.
- Make sure your website carries the same name, address, and phone number as the profile, so Google and customers see consistent information.
- Use your website to answer the questions your profile cannot: services in detail, pricing approach, service areas, and a simple way to enquire.
- Ask happy customers to leave a review on your profile, and show your best feedback on your website too, with the customer's permission.

When the two agree with each other, customers trust you more, and search engines have an easier time connecting your listing to your site. ## Common mistakes to avoid

The first mistake is choosing only one. A business with a great website but no Google listing is invisible to the many customers who search on Maps. A business with a listing but no website has nowhere to send customers who want detail, and risks losing them to a competitor with a fuller online presence.

The second mistake is inconsistent information. If your profile says you close at 6pm and your website says 8pm, or your profile lists one phone number and your website lists another, customers get confused and may simply move on. Keep the details identical everywhere.

The third mistake is letting either one go stale. A profile with old photos and unanswered reviews looks neglected. A website with outdated services or a broken contact form does the same. Set a simple routine: check your profile monthly, and review your website content every few months.

The fourth mistake is copying your competitor's listing and hoping for the best. Your profile and website should sound like your business, not a generic version of everyone else in your area. Use your own photos, describe your own services, and write in your own voice.

## A simple action plan for this week

You do not need to rebuild everything at once. Start with these steps:

1. Search for your business on Google and check what your profile shows. Correct anything wrong.
2. Add at least five real photos to your profile, and make sure your opening hours are accurate.
3. Open your website and check that your phone number, address, and main services are easy to find on the homepage.
4. Add a link from your profile to your website, and a link from your website back to your Google reviews, if your site design allows it.
5. Ask two recent happy customers to leave an honest review on your profile.

That is enough to start. Over time, add more detail to your website, publish useful content, and keep your profile fresh with new photos and replies to reviews.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a Google Business Profile replace a website?

No. A profile is excellent for discovery and quick contact, but it cannot present your full range of services, explain your process, or guide a visitor through a proper enquiry. A website gives you that control, and customers expect serious businesses to have one.

### Can a website replace a Google Business Profile?

Not for local discovery. Many customers search on Google Maps or look at map results first, especially on a phone. Without a profile, you miss those customers entirely, even if your website is excellent.

### Do I need to pay for a Google Business Profile?

Creating and managing a standard Google Business Profile does not require payment. Be careful of anyone charging you simply to create one. You may choose to run paid advertising separately, but the listing itself is free to set up and maintain.

### What if my business has no walk-in address?

Some businesses serve customers at their location or work from home. Google offers options for service-area businesses, where you list the areas you serve instead of displaying a shop address. The key point is to set it up honestly, so customers are not sent to the wrong place.

## The bottom line

Your Google Business Profile helps customers find you. Your website helps customers choose you. One without the other leaves money on the table.

Set both up properly, keep their details consistent, and let each one do the job it does best. That combination is how a small Nigerian business looks professional, trustworthy, and easy to reach, whether a customer finds you on a map or on your own website.
