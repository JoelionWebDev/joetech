When a customer in Lekki searches for "salon near me" or a trader in Onitsha searches for "phone repair shop open now", Google shows a short list of nearby businesses before it shows websites. That list comes from Google Business Profile, and the businesses that post regularly stand out from the ones that do not. A post is a short update, photo or offer that appears right on your business listing on Google Search and Google Maps. It costs nothing, it takes a few minutes, and it can be the difference between a customer calling you and calling the shop down the road.

Many Nigerian small businesses have a Google Business Profile but never touch it after setting it up. The profile sits there with an old photo and no updates, while competitors who post weekly look active, current and trustworthy. Customers notice. If your last post was six months ago, a searcher may wonder whether you are still in business. If you posted this week about a new product, a price slash or a festive offer, that same searcher has a reason to visit today.

This guide explains how to use Google Business Profile posts and offers step by step, in a way that fits how Nigerians actually search, buy and chat. You will learn the types of posts available, how to write ones that get calls and WhatsApp messages, how to create offers that bring people through the door, and the simple routine that keeps your listing fresh without eating your whole day.

## What Google Business Profile Posts Actually Do

A Google Business Profile post is a short message attached to your listing. When someone finds your business on Google Search or Maps, your recent posts appear under your business name, photos and reviews. They are not adverts in the paid sense. You do not pay per click. They are part of your free listing, and they give searchers a reason to choose you right now.

Posts work because they answer the question every searcher is asking: why should I pick this business today? Your address and phone number tell them where you are and how to reach you. Your reviews tell them what other customers thought. Your posts tell them what is happening now. A restaurant can post today's lunch menu. A fashion shop can post new arrivals. A pharmacy can post that a popular drug is back in stock. A hotel can post a weekend room discount. Each post is a small, timely reason to act.

There is one important limit to know. Most posts expire after seven days, except offers, which run until their end date. That means posting is not a one-time job. It is a small weekly habit, like sweeping the front of your shop. The good news is that a single post takes five to ten minutes once you know what to write.

## Set Up Your Profile Properly Before You Post

Posts only work well when the profile underneath them is complete and correct. Before you write your first post, check the basics. Open your profile on Google Search by typing your business name, or open the Google Maps app and find your listing. Confirm that your business name is spelled exactly as customers know it, your address is correct, your phone number works, and your opening hours match reality. Wrong hours are a common problem in Nigeria. If your profile says you close at 8 pm but you actually close at 6 pm, customers will arrive to a locked shop and leave a bad review.

Choose the right primary category. A hair salon should be listed as a hair salon, not a beauty salon, if hair is the main service. A restaurant that also does catering should pick restaurant as the primary category and add caterer as an extra. The category helps Google show you for the right searches. Add a clear description of what you do, the areas you serve, and what makes you different. Keep it plain and honest. Avoid stuffing it with keywords that do not read naturally.

Add good photos. Your profile photo should show your shopfront, your logo or your best work, clearly and in good light. Add photos of the inside of your shop, your products, your team at work and happy customers where you have permission. Photos taken with a decent phone camera in daylight are fine. Avoid dark, blurry pictures. Customers in Nigeria often judge a business by its photos before they ever call. A clean, bright photo builds trust instantly.

## The Four Types of Posts and When to Use Each

Google Business Profile offers different post types, and each one suits a different goal. Knowing when to use each type keeps your posts useful instead of repetitive.

The first type is the update, sometimes called a "What's new" post. Use it for news: new stock has arrived, you have extended your opening hours for December, you now deliver to a new area, or you have added a new service. Updates are the workhorse of your posting routine. They keep your listing fresh and give regular customers a reason to check back.

The second type is the offer post. This is for a discount, a bundle or a limited-time deal. Offer posts are powerful because they carry a clear incentive and an end date. They are ideal for festive seasons, clearance sales, launch promotions and slow days when you want to pull customers in. You set the offer title, the details, the start and end dates, and optionally a coupon code or a link.

The third type is the event post. Use it when something happens on a specific date: a grand opening, a training, a product launch, a live sale on Instagram, or a Christmas party at your restaurant. Event posts show the date and time prominently, so customers can plan around them.

The fourth type is the product post, available to many business types. It lets you showcase a specific product with a photo, a price in naira, and a short description. A phone shop can post a particular phone model with its price. A bakery can post a cake design with its price. Product posts help customers who are comparing prices before they visit.

A healthy routine mixes these types. You do not need to post an offer every week, or customers will learn to wait for discounts and never pay full price. A good balance is one or two updates a week, an offer once or twice a month, and events or product posts whenever there is something real to announce.

## How to Write a Post That Gets Calls and Messages

A good Google post is short, specific and local. Aim for roughly 100 to 300 words at most, and often much less. The first sentence matters most, because Google may show only the beginning before a searcher taps to read more. Put the most important information first: what is new, what it costs, and where to get it.

Start with a clear headline in your own words. Instead of "Good morning our dear customers", try "New Ankara fabrics just arrived at our Yaba shop". Instead of "We sell the best phones", try "iPhone 13 now available, from 420,000 naira, at our Computer Village shop". Specific beats generic every time. Customers searching on Google are already looking for something. Tell them you have it.

Include a price where it helps. Many Nigerian customers will not call if they do not know the price range. You do not have to list every price, but a starting price or a price range removes doubt. "Hair braiding from 8,000 naira" is more useful than "affordable prices". If your prices change often, give a range and invite customers to confirm on WhatsApp.

Add a clear call to action. Every post should tell the reader what to do next. "Call us now", "Send us a WhatsApp message", "Visit our shop at 14 Allen Avenue, Ikeja" or "Tap to see the full menu" are all clear. If you use WhatsApp for orders, say so plainly and make sure the phone number on your profile is a WhatsApp number. Many Nigerian customers prefer WhatsApp to a phone call, especially for price enquiries and orders.

Write in plain English, and use the words your customers use. If your customers search for "POS machine repair" rather than "point of sale terminal servicing", use their words. You can mix in a little Pidgin where it fits your brand, but keep the main message clear for anyone reading. Avoid shouting in all capital letters and avoid filling the post with too many emojis. One or two are fine if they match your brand, but a wall of emojis looks unprofessional.

## How to Create an Offer That Actually Brings People In

An offer post needs more care than a simple update, because it makes a promise. Follow these steps to create one that works.

First, pick a single clear benefit. "20 percent off all Ankara dresses this weekend" is clear. "Big discount on many items" is not. Customers need to know exactly what they get and what they must do to get it. If the offer applies only to certain items, say so. If there is a minimum spend, say so. Hidden conditions create arguments at the till and bad reviews afterwards.

Second, set a real deadline. Offers work because they create urgency. "Valid until Sunday 23 November" is stronger than "limited time only". A short window, such as a weekend or one week, pushes customers to act now. A very long offer, such as three months, loses its urgency and trains customers to ignore it. Match the length to the goal. A weekend flash sale can run for two or three days. A festive promotion can run for two weeks. A clearance can run until stock finishes, but say so honestly.

Third, add a simple way to claim the offer. A coupon code works well for online orders: "Use code DEC20 on WhatsApp". For walk-in customers, a simple instruction is enough: "Mention this Google offer at the till" or "Show this post when you visit". Keep the claiming step easy. If customers must jump through hoops, they will not bother.

Fourth, use the terms field to state the conditions plainly. Write the rules in one or two sentences: "One discount per customer. Not valid with other offers. Valid at our Lekki branch only." Clear terms protect you and protect the customer. They also show that you run your business properly.

Fifth, choose a strong photo. The photo should show the product or service on offer, not a generic sale banner. A photo of the actual dress, plate of food or phone on offer tells the customer what they are getting. If you use a banner, keep the text on it short and readable on a small phone screen.

Finally, honour the offer exactly as written. Nothing damages trust faster than a customer arriving for a advertised discount and being told it has finished, it does not apply to them, or the price has changed. If stock runs out, update or remove the post. If you extend the offer, update the end date. Your Google listing is a public promise, and customers will hold you to it.

## A Simple Weekly Posting Routine for Busy Owners

You do not need a marketing team to keep your profile active. You need a small routine and a phone. Here is one that works for many Nigerian small businesses.

On Monday morning, plan one post for the week. Look at what is happening in your business: new stock, a service you want to push, a slow day you want to fill, or a question customers keep asking. Pick one topic and take one good photo. Write the post in five minutes and publish it. That single post keeps your listing fresh for the week.

On Wednesday or Thursday, add a second post if you have something worth saying. This could be a customer review you are proud of, a behind-the-scenes photo of your team preparing an order, or a reminder about your weekend opening hours. Do not force a second post if there is nothing real to say. A quiet week with one good post is better than a week with three weak ones.

Once a month, plan one offer. Tie it to something real: a festive season, your business anniversary, a new product launch, or a quiet period when you need more sales. Announce it a few days early with an update post, then publish the offer itself. After the offer ends, post a short thank-you to customers who took part. That closes the loop and shows that real people responded.

Set a reminder on your phone for the same time each week, for example Monday at 9 am. Treat it like opening the shop. Ten minutes, one photo, one post, done. Over a month, that is four or five fresh posts on your listing. Over a year, it is a visible record of a business that is alive, growing and serving customers.

## Common Mistakes Nigerian Businesses Make With Posts

The first mistake is posting the same message everywhere without adapting it. A post written for Instagram, full of hashtags and "link in bio", reads badly on Google. Google posts are for people searching for a business, not scrolling a feed. Write for the searcher: what you offer, what it costs, where you are, and how to reach you.

The second mistake is using photos that do not match the words. A post about new shoes with a photo of an old shopfront confuses the reader. A post about a food discount with a blurry photo of a kitchen puts people off. Take a fresh photo for each important post. It takes one minute and it makes the post believable.

The third mistake is forgetting the location. Many Nigerian businesses serve a specific area, but their posts never mention it. "Available now" means little without "at our Surulere branch" or "we deliver across Abuja". Customers search with location in mind. Mention your area, your street or your landmark in the post where it helps. "Opposite the Chevron roundabout" is more useful than a bare street number for many Lekki customers.

The fourth mistake is posting offers that are too complicated. "Buy two get one free on selected items excluding new arrivals, only on Tuesdays, for the first ten customers who spend above 50,000 naira" is a puzzle, not a promotion. Keep offers simple enough to understand in five seconds. If you cannot explain the offer in one sentence, simplify it.

The fifth mistake is ignoring the insights. Google shows you how many people viewed your posts and how many tapped to call, get directions or visit your website. Check these numbers once a month. If posts with prices get more calls than posts without prices, post more prices. If food photos get more views than text banners, use more food photos. The data is free, and it tells you what your own customers respond to.


## Frequently Asked Questions

### How often should I post on Google Business Profile?
Post at least once a week. One good post each Monday keeps your listing fresh, and a second post midweek helps when you have real news. Offers can run once or twice a month.

### Do Google Business Profile posts cost money?
No. Posts and offers are free on your Google Business Profile. You only pay if you choose to run separate Google Ads, which is different from posting.

### How long do Google posts stay visible?
Most posts expire after seven days. Offer posts stay visible until the end date you set. Plan a weekly post so your listing never looks abandoned.

### Can I add my WhatsApp number to a post?
Yes. Make sure the phone number on your profile is your WhatsApp number, then tell readers to send you a WhatsApp message in the post text and in your call to action.

4. Plan one simple offer for the coming weekend, with a clear deadline and terms.
5. Set a weekly Monday reminder to post again, and check your insights monthly to see what works.
