December in Nigeria is unlike any other month for a small business. Salaries and bonuses land. People travel home for Christmas. Weddings, church programmes, family gatherings, and end-of-year parties fill the calendar. Everyone is buying: new clothes, food for the season, gifts for loved ones, decorations for the house, and treats they denied themselves all year.

For a small business owner, December is the biggest opportunity of the year — and also the biggest risk. A well-planned end-of-year sale can clear old stock, bring in new customers, and produce enough profit to carry the business through the slow January that follows. A badly planned one can leave you with discounted prices, empty shelves, and less money than if you had done nothing at all.

The difference between the two is planning. A sale is not simply cutting your prices and hoping for the best. It is a short campaign with a goal, a budget, a start and end date, and a plan to turn December buyers into customers who return in February and March.

This guide walks you through planning an end-of-year sale for your Nigerian small business, step by step — from choosing what to discount, to setting prices that still make you money, to promoting the sale where your customers actually are.

## Step 1: Set a Clear Goal for the Sale

Before you choose a single discount, decide what the sale is for. Different goals lead to very different sales, and trying to achieve all of them at once usually achieves none of them.

Common goals for a December sale include:

- **Clear old stock.** You have items that have sat on the shelf for months — last season's designs, slow-moving sizes, or products you are replacing. The goal is to turn them into cash, even at a small profit or break-even, rather than carrying them into the new year.
- **Increase cash before January.** January is famously slow in Nigeria — school fees, rent, and post-Christmas caution mean customers spend less. A strong December can build a cash cushion that keeps the business steady through January and February.
- **Win new customers.** A sale is a reason for people who have never bought from you to try you for the first time. If they have a good experience, some of them will become regulars.
- **Reward loyal customers.** A sale can also be a thank-you to the people who supported you all year — early access, a special discount, or a free gift with their December order.

Pick one main goal and one secondary goal, and write them down. Every decision that follows — which products to discount, how deep the discount should be, how you promote it — should serve those goals. If a decision does not serve the goal, skip it.

Set a simple target too. It does not need to be fancy: "Sell 80 percent of my old stock" or "Make ₦500,000 in December sales" or "Get 30 new customers". A target gives you something to measure against, and it stops the sale from drifting on without end.

## Step 2: Choose What Goes on Sale — and What Does Not

Not everything in your shop should be discounted. In fact, the most profitable sales are the ones where only some items are discounted, and the rest sell at full price alongside them.

Start with your stock. Go through everything you have and sort it into three groups:

**Group 1: Must go.** Slow-moving stock, last season's items, sizes or colours that rarely sell, products nearing the end of their shelf life. These are your sale items. Discount them properly — a small discount will not move stock that has not moved all year. This group is where the sale does its real work.

**Group 2: Popular items.** Your best sellers, the products customers ask for by name. These do not need a big discount. A small one — or a bundle deal, like "buy two, get a small free gift" — is enough to make them part of the sale without giving away your margin. Many sellers make the mistake of discounting their best sellers deeply, and then wonder why a busy December produced so little profit.

**Group 3: Do not discount.** New arrivals, limited items, or products with very thin margins. These stay at full price. You can still feature them in your sale promotion — "New collection also available" — but there is no reason to cut the price of something that sells well on its own.

If you sell services rather than products — hair styling, tailoring, catering, photography — the same logic applies. Discount the packages or time slots you want to fill, not your most in-demand service. A caterer might discount weekday bookings while keeping weekend prices full, because weekends sell themselves in December.

One more rule: do not buy new stock specifically to sell at a discount. That is how sales lose money. A sale should move what you already have, not create new costs. If you do bring in new stock for December, buy the items you know will sell, and price them to make a proper profit.

## Step 3: Set Discounts That Still Make You Money

This is where many December sales go wrong. A seller announces "50 percent off everything", gets a flood of orders, works through the whole holiday without rest — and discovers in January that the sale barely made a profit, or actually lost money.

Before you announce any discount, do the maths for each sale item. Take the cost of the item, add your delivery and packaging costs, and then apply the discount to the selling price. What is left must still be a profit — or, for stock you desperately need to clear, at least enough to cover your costs.

Here is a simple way to think about it. If an item costs you ₦10,000 and you normally sell it for ₦15,000, your profit is ₦5,000. A 20 percent discount takes the price to ₦12,000, leaving ₦2,000 profit — still positive, and worth it if the discount doubles how many you sell. A 50 percent discount takes the price to ₦7,500 — below your cost. You would lose ₦2,500 on every sale. No amount of volume fixes that.

Use different discount levels for different groups. Old stock that must go can take a deep discount — 30 to 40 percent, or even more if the alternative is carrying it into next year. Popular items should take a small discount — 5 to 15 percent — or a bundle deal instead. A bundle is often smarter than a straight discount: "Buy two, get 10 percent off the second" or "Spend ₦50,000 and get a free gift" increases the size of each order while protecting your margin.

Consider non-price offers too. Free delivery within Lagos for orders above a certain amount. A free gift with every order over a threshold. A small extra item for the first twenty buyers. These offers feel generous to customers but cost you far less than a deep discount — and they are easier to control.

Whatever discounts you choose, write them down in a simple price list before the sale starts, and stick to it. Changing prices in the middle of a sale confuses customers and creates arguments. Your December price list should be final before the first promotional post goes out.

## Step 4: Pick Your Dates and Prepare Your Stock

Timing is everything with a December sale. Start too early and customers are not yet in the buying mood; start too late and they have already spent their money elsewhere.

For most Nigerian small businesses, a good window is the first or second week of December through to just before Christmas — roughly the 5th to the 23rd of December. This catches the period when salaries and bonuses have landed and people are actively shopping for the season. If you sell food items or hampers, your peak may be even later — the week before Christmas itself.

Decide your dates in advance and announce them clearly: "Our End-of-Year Sale runs from 6th December to 20th December." A sale with a clear end date creates urgency — customers know they cannot keep postponing. A sale with no end date is just a price cut that never ends.

Before the start date, prepare:

- **Count your stock.** Know exactly how many of each sale item you have. There is nothing worse than advertising an item and discovering you only have two of them.
- **Prepare your packaging.** December orders come fast. Have bags, boxes, wrapping, and labels ready before the rush, not during it.
- **Sort out delivery.** Talk to your dispatch rider or logistics partner in advance. December is their busiest month too, and prices can rise. Agree on rates, confirm availability, and consider setting delivery cut-off dates for different locations — for example, orders to Abuja and Port Harcourt must be placed by a certain date to arrive before Christmas.
- **Charge your phone and secure your data.** This sounds small, but a dead phone or an exhausted data bundle in the middle of a sale rush is a real problem. Keep a power bank charged, and make sure you have enough data for a heavy week of chats, calls, and status posts.

If you take pre-orders or deposits for December items, record them carefully — who paid, how much, and what they ordered — so that the rush does not turn into confusion.

## Step 5: Promote the Sale Where Your Customers Are

A sale nobody hears about is not a sale. Promotion for a December sale does not need a big budget, but it does need to be consistent and to start before the sale itself.

**WhatsApp is your strongest channel.** Most Nigerian small businesses sell primarily through WhatsApp, and that is where your promotion should be heaviest. Post the sale on your status every day during the sale period — different products each day, with clear prices and the end date. Send a broadcast to your customers, ideally in two waves: one announcement a few days before the sale starts, and one reminder midway through. Keep messages short, include the key details (what is on sale, the discount, the dates, how to order), and include a photo or short video of the actual products.

**Instagram and Facebook matter too.** If you have a page, post the sale there with good photos and clear prices. A short video — even one filmed on your phone showing the products — usually performs better than a plain graphic. Use simple captions and the end date. If you have a small advertising budget, even a modest sponsored post targeted at Lagos or your city can bring in new customers during December, when people are actively looking to buy.

**Tell your existing customers first.** Your loyal customers should hear about the sale before the general public. A short personal message — "Our end-of-year sale starts on Saturday, and I wanted you to know first" — makes them feel valued and gives them first pick of the best items. These are also the customers most likely to share your sale with their friends and family.

**Use word of mouth deliberately.** Ask happy customers to share your sale post or forward your broadcast to someone who might need what you sell. December is a social month — people are already recommending sellers to each other for gifts, food, and outfits. A simple "please share with anyone who might need this" at the end of your message goes a long way.

**Keep the message consistent.** Whatever you say on WhatsApp should match what you say on Instagram, Facebook, and in person. Same dates, same prices, same products. Inconsistent information makes customers suspicious and creates unnecessary questions.

## Step 6: Run the Sale Well — and Know When to Stop

Once the sale starts, your job is to keep it running smoothly. That means replying fast, confirming orders clearly, and delivering on time.

December customers are impatient — they have many options, and a slow reply sends them to the next seller. During the sale period, check your phone more often than usual, and if you have an assistant or family member who can help with replies, bring them in. Use quick replies for your most common questions: the price list, delivery fees, and account details.

Keep a simple daily record during the sale: how many orders came in, how much money came in, and what is left in stock. This takes ten minutes a day and tells you whether the sale is working. If one item is selling out fast, you can feature it more in your promotion. If another is not moving at all, you may need a deeper discount or a different photo.

Know when to stop. When your sale end date arrives, end the sale. Do not extend it by a week, then another week, because customers learn that your deadlines mean nothing — and next year, nobody will hurry. Ending on time also protects your margins: the days after a sale ends are when your full-price sales resume.

The day after the sale ends, do a proper review. How much did you sell? How much profit did you make? Which items sold out, and which did not move? How many new customers did you gain, and how many of them have bought again since? Write the answers down. They are the plan for next December.

## What to Do Next

1. **Set your goal and your dates this week.** Decide what the sale is for, pick your start and end dates in December, and write down a simple sales target. A sale with a goal and a deadline is a campaign; a sale without them is just cheaper prices.

2. **Sort your stock and set your prices.** Divide your stock into must-go, popular, and do-not-discount groups. Work out the discount on each sale item and confirm it still leaves you a profit. Write the final sale price list down before you promote anything.

3. **Announce the sale before it starts.** Tell your loyal customers first, then post it on your WhatsApp status, Instagram, and Facebook a few days before the start date. Include the products, the discounts, the dates, and how to order — and make sure your packaging, delivery, and data are ready for the rush.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### When should I start my end-of-year sale in Nigeria?

For most small businesses, the first or second week of December through to just before Christmas works best — roughly the 5th to the 23rd of December. This catches the period when salaries and bonuses have been paid and people are actively shopping for Christmas. Food and hamper sellers may peak even later, in the week before Christmas itself.

### How much discount should I offer in a December sale?

It depends on the product. Old or slow-moving stock can take a deeper discount of 30 to 40 percent, because the alternative is carrying it into the new year. Popular items should take only a small discount of 5 to 15 percent, or a bundle deal instead. Always check that the discounted price still covers your costs and leaves a profit.

### How do I promote my end-of-year sale without spending a lot of money?

WhatsApp is your strongest free channel — daily status posts with clear prices and dates, plus a broadcast to your customers. Instagram and Facebook posts with good photos help too, and asking happy customers to share your sale costs nothing. Tell your loyal customers first, before the general announcement, so they feel valued and get first pick.

### What if I run out of stock during the sale?

That is a good problem, but handle it honestly. Tell customers immediately when an item is sold out, offer them a similar item or the chance to pre-order, and never take payment for something you do not have. Count your stock before the sale starts so you know your limits, and feature items you have plenty of most heavily in your promotion.

### Should I extend my sale if it is going well?

It is usually better to end on the date you announced. Extending a sale teaches customers that your deadlines mean nothing, so next year nobody will hurry. If the sale went well, use that energy to plan a smaller January offer for your new customers instead — and keep your full prices in place.
