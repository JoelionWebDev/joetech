You had a good meeting. The client nodded, asked questions, and said they would think about it. You sent a quotation. They said it looked good and they would get back to you. Then: silence. A day passes. A week passes. You wonder whether to write again, and if you do, what to say that does not sound desperate.

This guide shows you how to write a follow-up email that gets a reply after a meeting or a quote: when to send it, what to say, and how to follow up again without being annoying.

## Why Most Follow-Up Emails Get Ignored

Before you write a better one, understand why the usual ones fail.

The first reason is that they say nothing new. "Just following up" and "Just checking in" tell the client nothing. There is no information, no value, and no reason to reply. The client reads it, thinks "I will reply later", and forgets again.

The second reason is that they make replying hard. A follow-up that asks the client to re-read the whole quotation, remember the whole meeting, and make a decision from scratch is work. Busy people avoid work. A good follow-up does the work for them: it reminds them of the key points, and it asks one small, easy question.

The third reason is the subject line. If your subject line is "Follow up" or "Re: Quotation", it looks like every other email in the inbox. The client has no reason to open yours first. A subject line that names the project and the next step gets opened.

## When to Follow Up

Timing matters more than most people think. Here is a practical schedule that works for most situations:

**After a meeting:** Send a thank-you and summary email within 24 hours, while the meeting is still fresh in the client's mind. This is not the follow-up — it is the opening move. Summarise what was agreed, attach anything you promised, and state the next step clearly.

**After a quote:** If the client has not replied in two to three working days, send your first follow-up. Two days is not pushy — it is professional. The client expected to hear from you, and a short, helpful email is welcome.

**Second follow-up:** If there is still no reply after another four to five working days, send a second follow-up. This one should add something new: a useful piece of information, a deadline, or a simpler question.

**Third and final follow-up:** If there is still silence after another week, send one last email. This is the "closing the loop" email. It is polite, it is final, and it often produces a reply precisely because it gives the client permission to say no — and many clients, faced with a polite close, finally say yes instead.

After three follow-ups with no reply, stop emailing and move on. You can check in again in a month or two with something genuinely useful, but daily or weekly chasing damages your reputation and wastes your time. Silence after three good follow-ups is an answer, even if it is not the one you wanted.

## The Structure That Gets Replies

A follow-up email that works follows a simple structure. Keep it short — five short paragraphs at most, and often three is enough.

**1. A subject line that names the project.** Not "Follow up", but "Next step on the Lekki office branding quote" or "Your website proposal — one question". The client should know what the email is about before opening it.

**2. One line of context.** Remind the client who you are and what you are writing about, in one sentence. "It was good meeting you on Tuesday about the branding for your Lekki office." Do not assume the client remembers you. They met several people that week.

**3. One useful thing.** Give the client something they did not have before: a link, a sample, an answer to a question they asked, a short case note, or a simpler option. This is what separates a follow-up from a nag. You are not just asking for a decision — you are helping them make it.

**4. One clear, easy question.** Ask one thing, and make it easy to answer. Not "Please let me know your thoughts", which is vague and hard. Instead: "Would you like me to start with the logo, or the full branding package?" or "Is Thursday a good day for a 15-minute call to finalise this?" A question with two options is easier to answer than an open question.

**5. A short, warm close.** Thank them for their time, sign off with your name and your business, and include your phone number. Make it easy to reply by email or to call you directly.

## Templates You Can Adapt

Use these as starting points, not as scripts to copy word for word. Change the details to fit your situation, and write in your own voice.

**Template 1: After a meeting**

Subject: Great meeting you — next step on [project]

Hi [Name],

It was good meeting you yesterday about [project]. Thank you for your time.

As discussed, [one-line summary of what was agreed]. I have attached [what you promised].

Would [specific next step] work for you? I am available on [two options].

Best regards,
[Your name]
[Business name] | [Phone number]

**Template 2: After a quote, first follow-up**

Subject: Your [project] quote — a quick question

Hi [Name],

I sent the quotation for [project] on [day], and I wanted to check you received it.

The quote covers [one-line summary: what is included and the total]. If the price is above your budget, I can adjust the scope — for example, [one simpler option] — without changing the quality of the core work.

Would you like me to go ahead with [option A], or would [option B] suit you better?

Best regards,
[Your name]
[Business name] | [Phone number]

**Template 3: Second follow-up, adding value**

Subject: A quick idea for [project]

Hi [Name],

I was thinking about your [project] and wanted to share [one useful thing: a sample, a link, a tip, a relevant example].

No pressure at all — I know you are busy. If you would like to go ahead, I can start [when]. If the timing is not right, just let me know and I will check in again later.

Best regards,
[Your name]
[Business name] | [Phone number]

**Template 4: The final follow-up, closing the loop**

Subject: Closing the loop on [project]

Hi [Name],

I have not heard back about [project], so I will assume the timing is not right and close this for now.

If things change, or if you would like to revisit this later, just reply to this email — I will be glad to pick it up again. Thank you for considering us.

Best regards,
[Your name]
[Business name] | [Phone number]

The fourth template is the most powerful of the four, because it does two things at once: it respects the client's time, and it creates a small sense of urgency. Many clients reply to this email precisely because it is the last one. Do not be afraid to send it.

## Small Details That Make a Big Difference

**Keep it short.** If your follow-up is longer than a phone screen, it is too long. The client should be able to read it in under a minute. If you need to explain something in detail, attach a document or offer a call — do not put it all in the email.

**Check your email address.** A follow-up sent from an unprofessional address — a nickname, a string of numbers, an old account — undermines everything you wrote. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many times should I follow up before I stop?

Three times is the right number for most situations: once after two to three days, once more after another four to five days, and once more after another week. After that, stop and move on. You can reconnect in a month or two with something genuinely useful, but do not keep chasing the same email.

### What if the client says the price is too high?

Do not cut your price straight away. Offer to adjust the scope instead: remove an item, use a simpler option, or split the work into phases. That way you protect your margin and the client still gets a price they can afford. Our guide on [how to send a quotation that wins the job](/blog/how-to-send-quotation-that-wins-the-job) explains this in detail.

### Should I follow up by WhatsApp instead of email?

It depends on how the conversation started. If the client contacted you on WhatsApp and the whole conversation is there, follow up on WhatsApp. If the conversation is by email, follow up by email. Match the channel the client chose. If you switch channels without a reason, it can feel intrusive.

## The Follow-Up Is Part of the Job

Most freelancers treat the meeting or the quote as the work, and the follow-up as an afterthought. Turn that around. The follow-up is part of the work. The client who gets a clear, helpful follow-up within two days thinks: this person is organised, this person cares, this person will deliver. The client who hears nothing thinks the opposite, even if your meeting was excellent.

So write the follow-up before you forget the meeting. Send it on time. Add something useful. Ask one easy question. And if the answer is no, close the loop gracefully and move on to the next client — there will be one, and your follow-up habit is what will win them.
