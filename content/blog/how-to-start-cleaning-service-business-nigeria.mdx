A cleaning service is one of the most practical businesses you can start in Nigeria today. Offices in Lagos, Abuja and Port Harcourt need daily cleaning. Short-let apartments in Lekki, Ikeja and Wuse need turnover cleaning between guests. Busy families need help with deep cleaning before Christmas, Sallah or a housewarming. New estates, hotels, schools, churches and restaurants all need someone reliable to keep their spaces clean.

This guide walks you through the whole process, step by step. You will learn how to choose your niche, calculate realistic startup costs in naira, register your business, set prices that leave you a profit, hire and train cleaners, and win repeat clients. If you follow it carefully, you can move from idea to first paying job within a few weeks.

## 1. Understand the Cleaning Business in Nigeria

Before you buy a single mop, understand what you are getting into. Cleaning in Nigeria is a service business, which means your reputation is your main asset. One bad job can lose you a client forever, while one excellent job can bring you three referrals.

There are two broad markets. Residential cleaning serves homes, flats and short-let apartments. Jobs are smaller but frequent, and clients often pay in cash or by bank transfer on the day. Commercial cleaning serves offices, schools, hotels, banks, churches and estates. Jobs are larger, contracts can run for months, and payment is usually by transfer after an invoice, sometimes 7 to 14 days later.

Demand is strong in fast-growing areas. Lekki, Ajah, Ikeja, Surulere, Abuja Municipal, Gwarinpa, Wuse, and GRA Port Harcourt all have offices and homes that need regular cleaning. Short-let hosts on Airbnb and Booking.com are especially good clients, because they need reliable cleaning every time a guest checks out, sometimes three or four times a week.

## 2. Choose Your Niche and Services

Do not try to clean everything for everybody at the start. Pick a niche you can serve well, then expand later. A clear niche also makes your marketing easier, because people remember a specialist.

Here are proven niches in Nigeria:

Residential cleaning covers regular home cleaning, deep cleaning, post-renovation cleaning and move-in or move-out cleaning. A deep clean of a three-bedroom flat in Lagos typically takes three to four cleaners about four to five hours. Post-renovation cleaning is messy work involving cement dust, paint stains and debris, so charge more for it.

Office cleaning covers daily or weekly cleaning of offices, including sweeping, mopping, dusting desks, cleaning toilets and taking out refuse. Many offices prefer a contract, for example ₦80,000 to ₦250,000 per month depending on size and frequency.

Short-let and Airbnb turnover cleaning is a growing niche. Hosts need fast, reliable cleaning between guests, including changing bed linen, cleaning bathrooms and kitchens, and restocking basics. A one-bedroom short-let in Lekki might pay ₦15,000 to ₦25,000 per turnover, and a busy host may need you four times a week.

Specialised services pay more but need training and equipment. These include sofa and mattress cleaning, carpet cleaning, fumigation, janitorial services for estates, and post-event cleaning for halls and event centres. A sofa set cleaning job can pay ₦30,000 to ₦60,000, while carpet cleaning for a small office can pay ₦50,000 or more.

## 3. Write a Simple Business Plan

You do not need a 40-page document. A simple one-page plan is enough to guide you and help you avoid costly mistakes. Write down your answers to these questions before you spend money.

Who are your customers? Be specific. Are you targeting busy professionals in Lekki Phase 1, short-let hosts in Ikeja GRA, or small offices in Abuja? Each group needs a different price and message.

What services will you offer, and at what price? List each service and its price range. Include your costs for transport, chemicals, labour and your own time, so you can see your profit per job.

How much money do you need to start, and where will it come from? Add up equipment, chemicals, registration, branding and one month of running costs. Many people start with ₦150,000 to ₦500,000, depending on scale.

What is your monthly target? For example, aim for 20 jobs in your first month at an average of ₦30,000, which is ₦600,000 in revenue. After costs, you might keep ₦200,000 to ₦250,000. That target keeps you honest about whether the business is working.

## 4. Calculate Your Startup Costs in Naira

Startup costs vary, but here is a realistic budget for a small cleaning business in Lagos or Abuja in 2026. Prices change, so confirm current prices in your local market before you buy.

Basic equipment for a two-person team can cost ₦80,000 to ₦180,000. This includes mops, buckets, brooms, dustpans, microfibre cloths, scrubbing brushes, a vacuum cleaner, spray bottles, gloves, nose masks and a ladder for high windows. A decent wet-and-dry vacuum cleaner alone can cost ₦70,000 to ₦150,000, but it saves time and improves quality.

Cleaning chemicals and consumables for your first month can cost ₦40,000 to ₦80,000. Buy multipurpose cleaner, disinfectant, toilet cleaner, glass cleaner, floor polish, air freshener, bleach, liquid soap and bin bags. Buy in bulk from markets like Oyingbo, Mile 12, Wuse Market or Onitsha Main Market to save money. Always label your chemicals and keep them away from children.

Business registration with the Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC) costs from about ₦15,000 to ₦50,000 depending on whether you register a business name or a limited company and whether you use an agent. A business name is cheaper and fine for starting out.

Branding and marketing can cost ₦30,000 to ₦80,000. This includes a simple logo, branded polo shirts or aprons for your team, flyers, a WhatsApp Business profile, and a Google Business Profile so people can find you on Google Maps. A basic website or landing page helps you look professional, but you can start with WhatsApp and Instagram.

In total, many beginners start with ₦200,000 to ₦400,000 for a lean operation, while a more equipped setup with a vacuum cleaner, carpet cleaner and branded uniforms can cost ₦500,000 to ₦900,000. Start lean, earn money, then reinvest in better equipment.

## 5. Register Your Business and Get the Basics Right

Registration builds trust. A client is more likely to hire a registered business than an unnamed person, especially for office contracts and estate jobs.

Register a business name with the CAC. Choose a simple, memorable name like CleanNest Services, FreshSpace Cleaning or Lagos Sparkle Cleaning. Check that the name is available, register it, and keep your certificate safe. You will need it to open a business bank account.

Open a separate bank account for the business. Do not mix business money with personal money, or you will never know if you are making a profit. Use the account for all client payments and business expenses. Mobile banking apps from GTBank, Access Bank, Zenith Bank and Moniepoint make this easy.

Get a Tax Identification Number (TIN) from the Federal Inland Revenue Service. It is free and useful when corporate clients ask for it before paying you. Some companies will not pay a vendor without a TIN and an invoice.

## 6. Buy the Right Equipment and Chemicals

Quality equipment saves time and gives better results. Clients notice the difference between a home cleaned with a proper vacuum and one swept with a worn broom.

Start with the essentials: industrial mops, flat mops, buckets with wringers, brooms, dustpans, microfibre cloths in different colours for different areas, scrubbing brushes, toilet brushes, spray bottles, gloves, nose masks, aprons, a step ladder, and a good vacuum cleaner. Colour-code your cloths: one colour for toilets, another for kitchens, another for living areas. This is hygienic and impresses clients who know about cleaning standards.

As you grow, invest in a carpet extractor, a floor scrubber and a pressure washer. These machines let you charge for higher-value jobs like carpet cleaning and post-construction cleaning. You can also rent them per job at first, for ₦15,000 to ₦30,000 per day in Lagos, and buy your own once demand is steady.

## 7. Set Prices That Leave You a Profit

Pricing is where many new cleaning businesses fail. Price too low and you cannot pay staff or replace equipment. Price too high and clients will choose a cheaper competitor. The answer is to price based on your real costs, not on what your neighbour charges.

Start with cost-based pricing. For each job, add up labour, chemicals, transport, and a share of your monthly overheads like phone data, marketing and equipment wear. Then add your profit margin, usually 20 to 30 percent. For example, if a deep clean costs you ₦18,000 in labour, chemicals and transport, charging ₦25,000 leaves you about ₦7,000 profit. Charging ₦20,000 leaves only ₦2,000, which is too thin.

Here are realistic price guides for Lagos and Abuja in 2026. A one-bedroom flat regular clean might cost ₦15,000 to ₦25,000. A two-bedroom flat deep clean might cost ₦30,000 to ₦50,000. A three-bedroom duplex deep clean might cost ₦50,000 to ₦90,000. A small office of 100 square metres on a monthly contract might cost ₦100,000 to ₦180,000 per month for three visits a week. Always inspect the property or ask for photos and a video before you quote, because a dirty post-renovation flat takes twice as long as a well-kept one.

Offer clear packages. For example, a Basic Clean covers sweeping, mopping, dusting, toilet cleaning and refuse disposal. A Deep Clean adds window cleaning, wall wiping, kitchen degreasing, fridge and oven cleaning, and balcony washing. A Premium Clean adds sofa vacuuming, mattress cleaning and air freshener. Packages make it easy for clients to choose and easy for you to train staff.

Charge extra for difficult jobs. Post-renovation cleaning, homes with pets, hoarding situations, and jobs outside your area should attract a higher price or a transport surcharge of ₦5,000 to ₦10,000. State this in your quotation so there is no argument on the day.

Decide your payment terms and stick to them. For residential jobs, collect at least 50 percent upfront to confirm the booking, and the balance on completion. For corporate contracts, invoice monthly and follow up politely but firmly. Accept bank transfer, Paystack or Flutterwave payment links, and POS where possible. Avoid doing a job on credit for a new client you do not know.

## 8. Hire, Train and Manage Your Cleaners

Your cleaners are your business. Clients may never meet you, but they will meet your staff in their homes. Hire slowly and train properly.

Look for cleaners who are honest, punctual, neat and willing to learn. Experience helps, but attitude matters more. You can teach someone to mop correctly in a day, but you cannot easily teach honesty. Ask for a guarantor, a valid ID, and a reference from a previous employer, church or community leader. Keep copies on file.

Start with two to four cleaners on a part-time or per-job basis. Pay per job or per day, for example ₦5,000 to ₦8,000 per cleaner per day in Lagos, depending on the job and hours. As you get steady contracts, move your best cleaners to a monthly salary of ₦60,000 to ₦90,000. Be clear about pay, working hours, transport, and what happens if a job is cancelled.

Train every cleaner before their first job. Teach them your cleaning checklist, how to use each chemical safely, how to handle client property with care, and how to behave in a client's home. Rules should include: no going through drawers or wardrobes, no using a client's phone charger or food without permission, no bringing friends to a job, and always report damage immediately.

Supervise the first jobs yourself. Go with your team, check their work room by room, and correct mistakes on the spot. Use a printed checklist and tick off each task. After the job, ask the client for feedback before you leave, so you can fix any missed spot immediately.

## 9. Find Your First Clients

You do not need a big advertising budget to find clients. You need to be visible where your customers already are, and you need to make it easy for them to book you.

Start with your own network. Tell friends, family, church members, colleagues and neighbours that you now run a cleaning service. Offer a discount for their first job in exchange for a review and a referral. Many cleaning businesses get their first ten clients this way.

Set up WhatsApp Business properly. Use a clear business name, a professional profile photo or logo, your service list, your prices, your working hours, and your location. Use the catalogue feature to show your packages. Use quick replies for common questions like prices and availability. Ask happy clients to share your number in their estate or office WhatsApp groups.

Use Instagram and Facebook to show before-and-after photos and short videos of your work. A 30-second video of a dirty kitchen becoming spotless is more convincing than any flyer. Post two or three times a week, use local hashtags like #LagosCleaning, #AbujaCleaning and #CleaningServicesNigeria, and reply to every comment and DM quickly.

List your business on Google Business Profile. It is free, and it helps people searching for "cleaning service near me" in Lekki, Ikeja or Wuse to find you. Add photos, your services, your prices and your phone number. Ask clients to leave a Google review after a good job.

Partner with people who serve the same customers. Estate agents, short-let managers, facility managers, interior designers, fumigation companies and event planners can all refer clients to you. Offer them a small referral fee, for example 5 to 10 percent of the first job, or agree to refer clients back to them.

## 10. Deliver Excellent Service Every Time

In cleaning, quality is your marketing. A client who is delighted will tell their neighbours, their colleagues and their estate group. A client who is disappointed will do the same, only louder.

Arrive on time. If you promise 9 a.m., be there at 8:45 a.m. If traffic on the Lekki-Epe Expressway or Third Mainland Bridge will delay you, call the client early and give a new time. Punctuality is rare enough in Nigeria that clients notice it and reward it.

Follow your checklist on every job. Clean from top to bottom and from the farthest room towards the door, so you do not dirty a room you have already cleaned. Pay attention to details clients notice: clean light switches, door handles, taps, mirrors, toilet seats and kitchen sinks. Leave the place smelling fresh, not strongly of bleach.

Ask for feedback and reviews after every job. A simple message works: "Thank you for trusting us with your home today. Please let us know if anything needs attention, and we would be grateful if you could leave us a short review." Collect reviews on Google and WhatsApp, and screenshot them for your social media.

## 11. Keep Records, Manage Money and Grow

Many small cleaning businesses fail not because they lack clients, but because they do not manage money. Keep simple records from day one.

Use a notebook or a simple spreadsheet to record every job: date, client name, service, amount charged, amount paid, costs, and profit. At the end of each month, add up your revenue, your costs and your profit. If you made ₦600,000 in revenue and spent ₦400,000, your profit is ₦200,000. That number tells you whether to raise prices, cut costs or find more clients.

## Conclusion

Starting a cleaning service business in Nigeria is a practical path to income if you treat it like a real business from day one. Choose a niche, write a simple plan, calculate your true costs, register your business, buy good equipment, price for profit, train honest staff, and deliver excellent service on every job.

Your first job will teach you more than any guide. So do not wait for perfect conditions. Start with the equipment you can afford, tell your network you are open for business, and take that first booking. Do the job well, ask for a review, and use the money to buy better tools and reach more clients.

Clean homes and offices are not a luxury in Nigeria anymore. They are a necessity for busy families, growing offices and short-let hosts who want happy guests. If you show up on time, clean thoroughly and treat every client's home with respect, you can build a cleaning business that pays your bills, employs others, and grows year after year. Start small, stay consistent, and let your work speak for you.
