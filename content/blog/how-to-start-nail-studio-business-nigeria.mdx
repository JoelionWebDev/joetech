Nails are big business in Nigeria. Walk through any busy area in Lagos, Abuja or Port Harcourt and you will see women getting their nails done before weddings, birthdays, owambes, job interviews and church programmes. A good nail technician with a clean space and steady hands can build a loyal client base that returns every two to three weeks.

A nail studio is also one of the more affordable beauty businesses to start. You do not need a large shop or expensive machines. You need skill, hygiene, good products and a space where clients feel comfortable. This guide walks you through starting a nail studio business in Nigeria, from training to pricing to filling your appointment book.

## Why a Nail Studio Works in Nigeria

Nail care is a repeat service. Acrylics, gel polish and manicures need refilling or redoing every two to four weeks, which means a happy client can pay you many times a year. That repeat income is the foundation of a stable business.

The market is wide. Students, working women, brides, influencers and business owners all get their nails done. Social media has made nail art fashionable, and many clients now want designs, colours and styles they saw online.

The startup cost is manageable compared with a hair salon. The main investment is your training and your initial stock of polishes, tools and equipment. You can start small — even from home — and move into a proper studio as your client base grows.

## Step 1: Get Proper Training

Skill is everything in this business. Clients pay for beautiful, long-lasting nails, and they will not return if their nails lift, break or look untidy after a few days.

Take a proper nail technician course before you start charging. Many beauty academies in Lagos, Abuja and Port Harcourt offer nail training that covers manicure, pedicure, gel polish application, acrylics, nail art and hygiene. A good course lasts several weeks and includes plenty of hands-on practice.

If a formal academy is too expensive, learn from an experienced nail technician. Many established technicians take on apprentices. You will learn the practical skills, how to handle clients, and how a real studio runs day to day.

Practise on friends and family until your work is neat and consistent. Photograph every set you do. Your practice work becomes your portfolio, and a portfolio of beautiful nails is your best advertisement.

Keep learning after you start. Nail trends change quickly: new colours, new designs, new techniques. Follow nail artists on Instagram and YouTube, try new styles on willing friends, and attend workshops when you can.

## Step 2: Decide Your Business Model

You have a few options for how to start, and the right one depends on your money and your situation.

**Home-based studio** is the cheapest start. Set up a clean, comfortable corner of your home with a table, chairs and good light. This works well if you live in an area where clients can reach you easily. Keep the space separate from your living area so it feels professional.

**Mobile nail service** means you go to the client's home or office. You charge a little extra for transport, and clients love the convenience. This is a good way to start without rent, and it helps you build a client list quickly.

**Rent a chair** in an existing salon or beauty studio. You pay the salon owner a weekly or monthly fee and use their space and their foot traffic. This is a smart middle step: you get a professional location without signing a full lease.

**Open your own studio** once you have steady clients and savings. A small shop in a busy area with good foot traffic is ideal. This is the most expensive option, so do it only when your income can cover the rent comfortably.

Many successful nail technicians start at home, move to renting a chair, and then open their own studio. There is no shame in starting small; there is only risk in starting too big.

## Step 3: Know Your Startup Costs

A realistic startup budget for a small home-based nail studio is between one hundred thousand and three hundred thousand naira, depending on what you already own and the quality of products you buy.

Your biggest cost is your initial stock: gel polishes in popular colours, acrylic powder and liquid, tips, nail files, buffers, cuticle tools, base coat, top coat, nail glue, nail art brushes and decorations. Buy a good range of colours, but do not buy every shade at once. Start with the colours clients ask for most: nude, white, red, pink, black and a few trendy shades.

Equipment includes a nail table and two comfortable chairs, a UV or LED lamp for curing gel polish, a small nail drill, a steriliser or disinfectant for tools, towels, a foot bath or basin for pedicures, and good lighting. A ring light or a bright desk lamp makes a real difference to the quality of your work and your photos.

You will also need cleaning supplies, disposable items such as gloves and cotton pads, and a small stock of hand cream and cuticle oil.

Do not buy the cheapest products. Low-quality polish chips quickly and low-quality acrylic lifts, and clients will blame you. Buy reputable brands from trusted suppliers, even if they cost a little more.

## Step 4: Where to Buy Nail Products in Nigeria

Buy from suppliers who stock genuine products. Fake polishes and acrylics are common in open markets, and they damage nails and your reputation.

In Lagos, beauty supply shops in Ikeja, Yaba and the Trade Fair Complex stock a wide range of nail products. Balogun Market also has beauty suppliers, but check products carefully. Online, trusted Nigerian beauty stores and Instagram vendors supply genuine products with delivery.

Ask other nail technicians where they buy. A recommendation from someone in the trade is worth more than any advert. Once you find a reliable supplier, stick with them and build the relationship: they will tell you when new products arrive and may give you better prices as a regular customer.

Buy your most-used colours in larger quantities and keep a record of what you use. Running out of nude polish on a Saturday, your busiest day, is a mistake you only want to make once.

## Step 5: Set Up a Clean, Comfortable Space

Hygiene is not optional in a nail business. Clients notice dirty tools, dusty tables and unwashed towels, and they will not come back.

Clean and disinfect your tools after every client. Use a proper disinfectant, and keep a separate set of tools for each client if you can. Wash your hands before and after every appointment, and wear gloves when appropriate.

Your workspace should be bright, tidy and comfortable. A clean table, a comfortable chair for the client, a small mirror, and a place for the client to rest their hands all matter. Play soft music, offer water, and keep the space smelling fresh.

Display your work. A small board or album with photos of your best nail sets helps clients choose a style and shows new clients what you can do. Keep your Instagram page updated with your latest work.

Take good photos of every set you do, with the client's permission. Natural light and a plain background show the nails clearly. These photos are your marketing.

## Step 6: What Services to Offer and How to Price Them

Offer a clear menu of services so clients know what to ask for and what to pay.

A basic manicure — shaping, cuticle care and regular polish — might be priced between five thousand and ten thousand naira depending on your location. A pedicure is usually similar or slightly higher. Gel polish on natural nails costs more than regular polish because it lasts longer and needs a lamp to cure.

Acrylic full sets are your premium service and the one most clients want for events. Prices vary widely by city and by the technician's reputation, but a full set with gel polish can range from fifteen thousand to forty thousand naira. Nail art, stones, French tips and special designs attract an extra charge.

Refills, or infills, are done every two to three weeks and are priced lower than a full set. This is your repeat income: a client who gets a full set will return for refills again and again.

Soak-offs, nail repairs and single-nail fixes are small services that bring clients through the door and often lead to a bigger booking.

Research what other nail studios in your area charge. You do not need to be the cheapest; you need to be worth your price. Clean work, good products and a comfortable experience justify a fair price.

Be clear about your prices. Publish a price list on your WhatsApp Business catalogue and your Instagram page. Clients who know the price before they book are easier to serve and less likely to argue.

Collect a deposit for bookings, especially on weekends and for bridal parties. A small deposit, paid by transfer, confirms the appointment and reduces no-shows.

## Step 7: Finding Your First Clients

Start with your personal network. Announce your service on WhatsApp status and Instagram, post photos of your work, and state your prices and location clearly.

Offer an introductory discount for your first ten clients, and ask each one to post their nails and tag you. A photo of beautiful nails on a client's own page is powerful advertising.

Partner with businesses that serve the same clients: hair salons, makeup artists, fashion designers, photographers and event planners. Offer them a discount or a referral fee. A makeup artist who recommends you to every bride is worth more than any advert.

Bridal work is especially valuable. A bride needs her nails done before her wedding, and her bridesmaids often book too. Connect with wedding planners and makeup artists, and make sure your portfolio includes elegant bridal nail designs.

Use Instagram well. Post your work consistently, use your location, and reply to comments and messages quickly. Many clients find their nail technician through Instagram search, so keep your page active and professional.

Consider a loyalty offer: for example, every fifth manicure free, or a discount on a client's birthday. Small gestures keep clients coming back.

## Step 8: Running the Studio Day to Day

Use an appointment system. A simple booking book, a Google Calendar, or a WhatsApp Business setup works. Take bookings by message and confirm the day before. This prevents double bookings and empty chairs.

Keep to time. If a client's appointment is 10 a.m., start at 10 a.m. Running late frustrates clients and makes your whole day slip. Allow enough time for each service: rushing leads to mistakes.

Keep records. Note each client's name, phone number, the service they had, the colours they used, and when they should return for a refill. When you message a client two weeks later to remind them about a refill, you will be surprised how many book immediately.

Manage your money. Separate your business money from your personal money. Track your income and your spending on products so you know whether you are actually making a profit. Set aside money each week to restock products before you run out.

Handle complaints gracefully. If a nail lifts or chips within a few days, offer a free fix. A client whose problem you solve quickly becomes more loyal than a client who never had a problem.

## Step 9: Bridal Parties and Group Bookings

Bridal work is one of the most profitable parts of a nail studio. A bride usually books a full set before her wedding, and her bridesmaids, mother and friends often book too. One wedding can fill your appointment book for a whole day.

Prepare a bridal package: a full set for the bride, matching or complementary sets for the bridesmaids, and a pedicure option. Offer the package at a clear price and take a deposit when the bride books, so the date is secured.

Ask the bride for her dress colour, her theme and any photos of nails she likes. Design a sample set in advance if she wants something special, such as hand-painted flowers or stones. A trial set before the wedding day avoids surprises.

Be ready for group bookings at weekends and during wedding season. Schedule extra time between appointments, arrange your chairs so the group can sit together, and play music to make it feel like a celebration. A bride who enjoys the experience will recommend you to every friend getting married.

Work with makeup artists and photographers who serve brides. When they recommend you, their clients trust you before they even meet you. Offer the makeup artist a small thank-you, such as a free manicure, for referrals that turn into bookings.

## Step 10: Handling Walk-Ins and Busy Saturdays

Saturdays are the busiest day in a nail studio. Weddings, parties and owambes all happen at the weekend, and everyone wants their nails done on Friday or Saturday.

Use an appointment book and stick to it. When Saturday is full, say so honestly and offer the client Friday or Sunday instead. Overbooking leads to long waits, rushed work and unhappy clients.

Keep a short waiting list. If a client cancels, call the next person on the list. This fills empty slots and makes clients feel valued.

For walk-ins, be honest about the wait. If you can fit them in within thirty minutes, take them. If the wait is two hours, tell them and let them decide. A client who chooses to wait is far happier than one who was promised ten minutes and waited two hours.

On very busy days, keep your energy up. Eat before your first appointment, keep water nearby, and take short breaks between clients. Your hands are your business, so protect them: stretch your fingers, rest your wrists, and stop when your hands hurt.

## Step 11: Mistakes to Avoid

Do not skip hygiene. Reusing unsterilised tools or working with dirty hands can cause infections, and one bad story can ruin your reputation.

Do not use fake or expired products. They damage your clients' nails and your name. Buy genuine products and check expiry dates.

Do not undercharge. If your prices do not cover your products, your time and your space, you will burn out. Charge fairly and deliver quality that justifies it.

Do not take on more clients than you can serve well. A fully booked day with happy clients is better than an overbooked day with rushed work and complaints.

Do not ignore your online presence. Clients search Instagram before they book. A page with no recent photos looks closed, even if you are open.

## Growing Your Nail Studio

When your appointment book is regularly full, it is time to grow.

You can raise your prices gradually as your skill and reputation grow. Loyal clients who love your work will accept a fair increase, especially if you explain it and give them notice.

You can train an assistant or take on an apprentice. This lets you serve more clients and frees you to focus on premium services and nail art.

You can add related services: press-on nails sold online, nail care products, or a small retail shelf with hand creams and cuticle oils. Each adds income without adding much work.

You can also sell press-on nails through Instagram and WhatsApp to customers outside your area. A set of press-on nails, made to the customer's size and posted to them, turns your skill into a product that sells while you sleep.

A nail studio rewards patience, cleanliness and consistency. Your clients return because they trust your hands. Build that trust one set at a time, and your appointment book will fill itself.
