Many Nigerian small businesses sell on WhatsApp and Instagram, and that works — until the messages pile up. A customer asks for the price, you reply, they ask for your account number, you send it, they pay, they send a receipt, you confirm it, and then you arrange delivery. One order can take 20 messages. When ten customers message you at once, orders get mixed up, prices get quoted differently, and some customers give up and buy elsewhere.

A Paystack Storefront solves that problem. It is a simple online shop that Paystack provides for Nigerian businesses. You add your products with photos and prices, customers browse the shop, add items to a cart, pay with a card, bank transfer or USSD, and you get an alert when an order comes in. You do not need to build a website, hire a developer, or write any code.

This guide walks you through setting up a Paystack Storefront step by step, from creating your Paystack account to adding products, setting delivery, promoting your shop, and handling orders well.

## What a Paystack Storefront Is, and Who It Is For

A Paystack Storefront is a free online shop linked to your Paystack account. It gives you a web link you can share on WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook and TikTok. When a customer taps the link, they see your products, your prices and your business name, and they can buy without messaging you first.

It is ideal for a fashion store in Lekki, a skincare brand in Abuja, a food vendor in Ikeja, a phone accessories seller in Computer Village, a bakery in Surulere, or any small business that sells physical products and wants a simple, professional way to take orders and payments online.

Paystack is a Nigerian payment company, so it understands how Nigerians pay. Customers can pay with a Nigerian debit card, by bank transfer, with USSD, or through Opay and other supported methods. That matters, because many customers do not have an international card, and some prefer transfer to card. Offering several payment options means fewer abandoned orders.

## Before You Start: What You Will Need

Gather these things before you begin, so the setup takes less than an hour.

- **A business name.** It can be your registered business name, or the trading name customers know you by, such as "Ada's Kitchen" or "Luxe Hair Lagos".
- **An email address you check daily.** Paystack will send order alerts, payment confirmations and important notices to this email. Use an address such as info@yourbusiness.com.ng, or a Gmail address you check every day.
- **A phone number.** Paystack and your customers will use it to reach you.
- **Your bank account details.** This is the Nigerian bank account where Paystack will send your money. It should be in your business name if you have a business account, or in your personal name if you are a sole trader. Make sure the account number and bank name are correct.
- **A form of identification.** Paystack will ask you to verify your business. For a sole trader, that usually means your National ID, international passport or driver's licence. For a registered company, you may need your CAC documents. Have a clear photo or scan ready.
- **Product photos.** Take clear photos of each product you want to sell, in good light, on a clean background. One good photo per product is enough to start. You can add more photos later.
- **Your product list and prices.** Write down each product name, its price in naira, and how many you have in stock. Decide your delivery fees now, so you can set them up in the store.

## Step 1: Create Your Paystack Account

Go to paystack.com and click "Create a free account" or "Sign Up". Enter your business name, your email address and a strong password. Use a password you do not use anywhere else, and turn on two-factor authentication when Paystack offers it.

Paystack will send a verification email to your address. Open it and click the link to confirm your email. Then log in to your Paystack dashboard. This dashboard is where you will manage your store, your payments and your orders.

Complete your business profile. Paystack will ask for your business name, what you sell, your address, your phone number and your bank account details. Fill in everything honestly and completely. An incomplete profile can delay your verification and your payouts.

Submit your identification for verification. Follow the instructions on the dashboard and upload a clear photo of your ID. Make sure the photo is not blurry, and that your name and photo are easy to read. Verification can take a few hours or a few days, depending on Paystack's checks. While you wait, you can still set up your store and add products, so do not wait for verification to finish before you start building.

Check your settlement settings. Settlement is how Paystack sends your money to your bank. In Nigeria, Paystack usually settles payments to your bank account on the next working day, after deducting its transaction fee. Check the fee in your dashboard so you know what to expect. Paystack charges a percentage on each successful payment, plus a small fixed fee in some cases. That fee comes out of the payment before it reaches your bank, so factor it into your prices.

## Step 2: Create Your Storefront

Once you are logged in, look for "Storefront" or "Store" in your Paystack dashboard. The exact location can change as Paystack updates its dashboard, but it is usually under Commerce, Products or Storefront in the left menu.

Click to create a new storefront. Paystack will ask you to choose a name for your store and, in some cases, a store link. Your store link will look something like paystack.shop/your-business-name. Choose a name that matches your business name and is easy to remember and share. If your first choice is taken, try adding "ng" or "lagos" to the end, or use a short version of your name.

Add your business logo if you have one. A clear logo makes your store look professional and helps customers trust it. If you do not have a logo yet, use a clear photo of your best product, or a simple text logo with your business name, until you can get a proper logo designed.

Write a short store description. In one or two sentences, say what you sell and who it is for. For example: "Luxe Hair Lagos sells original human hair, wigs and hair care products, delivered across Nigeria." Keep it simple and honest.

Set your currency to Nigerian naira. Your prices, your delivery fees and your payments will all be in naira, which is what your customers expect.

Save your storefront and preview it. Tap the store link on your phone and see what a customer will see. Check that your business name is correct, your logo looks clear, and the page loads quickly. If something looks wrong, fix it now, before you share the link with customers.

## Step 3: Add Your Products

This is the most important part of the setup. A store with clear products and honest prices will sell. A store with blurry photos and missing prices will not.

Start with your best 10 to 20 products. Do not try to add everything at once. Add the products that sell well, that you have in stock, and that you can photograph clearly.

For each product, add:

- **A clear name.** Use the name customers know. "Bone Straight Wig — 22 inches, Natural Black" is clearer than "Wig 1". "Ankara Midi Dress — Size 12" is clearer than "Dress".
- **A good photo.** Use natural light near a window, a plain background, and show the product clearly. If you sell clothes, show the item on a hanger or on a model. If you sell food, show the actual pack the customer will receive. Avoid using photos from the internet that do not show your real product.
- **A price in naira.** Write the price clearly. If the product has different prices for different sizes, create a separate product for each size, or use product options if Paystack offers them for that product. Round prices to clean numbers, such as ₦15,000 or ₦25,500, so customers can pay easily by transfer.
- **A short description.** In two or three sentences, say what the product is, what it is made of, what size or quantity it is, and who it is for. For example: "This 22-inch bone straight wig is made from original human hair. It comes with a free wig cap and can be styled with heat."
- **Stock quantity.** Enter how many you have. If you have only five pieces, enter five. When the stock runs out, Paystack will show the product as sold out, so you will not take payment for something you do not have.
- **A category, if offered.** Group your products so customers can browse easily — for example, Wigs, Hair Care, Dresses, Shoes, Food Packs.

Check each product after you add it. Open your store link and look at the product as a customer would. Is the photo clear? Is the price right? Is the description easy to understand? Fix any mistakes now.

## Step 4: Set Up Delivery and Payment Options

Customers will not complete an order if delivery is confusing or payment is hard. Set both up clearly before you share your store link.

In your storefront settings, look for delivery options. Paystack Storefront allows you to set delivery fees by location, or to offer free delivery above a certain amount. A simple setup that works for many Nigerian businesses is:

- Delivery within Lagos: ₦3,000
- Delivery to Abuja and Port Harcourt: ₦5,000
- Delivery to other states: from ₦5,000, depending on location
- Free delivery within Lagos for orders above ₦50,000

You can adjust these fees to match what your rider or logistics company charges you. Do not offer free delivery on every order unless you have included the delivery cost in your product prices. Free delivery that you cannot afford will eat your profit.

Decide whether you will deliver yourself, use a dispatch rider, or use a logistics company such as GIG Logistics. Whichever you choose, confirm the rates and delivery times before you publish them. A customer in Abuja who orders on Friday should know whether to expect delivery on Monday or Wednesday.

Check your payment options. Paystack will handle card payments, bank transfer and USSD automatically, but make sure the options you want are turned on in your dashboard. Test the checkout yourself. Add a product to the cart, go to checkout, and see the payment page as a customer would. You do not need to complete a payment to test it — just check that the page loads, the total is correct, and the delivery fee is added correctly.

Set your order notifications. Make sure Paystack will send you an email and, if available, an SMS or WhatsApp alert when a new order comes in. A fast reply to a new order builds trust. If you miss an order for a day, the customer may think you are not serious.

## Step 5: Share Your Store Link Everywhere

Your store will not sell if nobody knows it exists. Share the link in every place your customers already find you.

Add the link to your Instagram bio. Instagram allows one link in your bio, so use your storefront link there, or use a link tool if you need to share more than one link. Write "Shop here" or "Order here" next to the link so people know what it is.

Add the link to your WhatsApp Business profile and to your WhatsApp Status. Post your store link on your Status once or twice a week, with a photo of a popular product and its price. When a customer messages you on WhatsApp to ask for a price, send them the store link instead of typing the price again. Say: "You can see all our products and prices here, and you can pay directly on the link."

Share the link on Facebook, TikTok and in your email signature. If you have a website, add a "Shop" button that links to your storefront. If you run adverts, send people to your storefront link, not to your WhatsApp number, so they can browse and pay without waiting for a reply.

## Step 6: Handle Orders Well

When an order comes in, Paystack will notify you. Open the order in your dashboard and check the details: the customer's name, phone number, delivery address, the products they ordered, the total they paid, and whether payment was successful.

Confirm the order quickly. Send the customer a short message on WhatsApp: "Thank you for your order. We have received your payment of ₦25,000 for the Bone Straight Wig. Your order will be delivered within Lagos in 2 working days. Thank you for shopping with us." That message reassures the customer and reduces the number of "Did you receive my payment?" messages you will get.

Pack the order carefully. Use a clean box or bag, include the product, and add a thank-you note if you can. A neat package makes a good impression and encourages the customer to buy again and to recommend you.

Dispatch the order on time. If you promised delivery in two days, deliver in two days. If there is a delay, tell the customer before they ask you. A short message — "Hello, your order will arrive tomorrow instead of today because of traffic. We are sorry for the delay" — is far better than silence.

## Step 7: Grow Your Storefront Over Time

Setting up the store is the start, not the end. Grow it slowly and steadily.

Add new products regularly. When you get a new product, add it to the store the same day, with a clear photo and price. A store that never changes gives customers no reason to return. A store with new products gives them a reason to check back.

Use your sales data. Your Paystack dashboard shows which products sell best, which days bring the most orders, and how customers pay. Use that information. If a product sells out every week, order more of it. If a product never sells, lower its price, improve its photo, or remove it.

Ask customers for feedback. After delivery, send a short message: "Thank you for shopping with us. Did you like the product? Is there anything we can improve?" Their answers will help you improve your products and your service.

Keep your store updated. If a price changes, update it in the store immediately. If a product is out of stock, update the quantity so customers do not pay for something you cannot deliver. An outdated store loses trust quickly.

## Common Paystack Storefront Mistakes to Avoid

Avoid these mistakes, and your store will work much better.

Do not use blurry or stolen photos. Customers will not trust a store with unclear photos, and they will not buy a product they cannot see properly.

Do not leave products without a price. A product with no price forces the customer to message you, which defeats the purpose of a storefront.

Do not forget to update your stock. Taking payment for a product you do not have leads to refunds, complaints and bad reviews.

Do not ignore your order notifications. Check your email and your dashboard at least twice a day. A slow response to a paid order worries the customer.

## Paystack Storefront Checklist

- [ ] Create a Paystack account and verify your email and identity
- [ ] Add your bank account details for settlement
- [ ] Create your storefront, add your logo and write a short description
- [ ] Add your best 10 to 20 products with clear photos, prices and stock quantities
- [ ] Set delivery fees for Lagos, Abuja, Port Harcourt and other states
- [ ] Test the checkout yourself and check your payment options
- [ ] Share your store link on Instagram, WhatsApp, Facebook and TikTok
- [ ] Confirm every order quickly, pack neatly, and deliver on time
- [ ] Check your sales and fees every week, and add new products regularly

A Paystack Storefront will not make sales on its own — you still need good products, clear photos, fair prices and fast service. But it will make selling much easier. Instead of handling every order by chat, you will have a shop that is open day and night, takes payment safely, and lets you focus on your products and your customers. Set it up this week, share the link, and start taking orders online.
