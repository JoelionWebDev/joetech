Every December in Nigeria, the same thing happens. Salaries are paid early. Bonuses land. People travel home for Christmas with money to spend. Markets fill up, tailors work through the night, and phones buzz with orders for food, clothes, shoes, hair, hampers, electronics and gifts. Then January arrives, and the spending does not stop — it changes. People buy for the New Year: new clothes for church and office, new shoes, new phones, new home items, food for visitors, and gifts for loved ones. For a small business, the weeks from mid-December to the end of January are one of the biggest selling windows of the year.

The businesses that make the most money in that window are not always the ones with the best products. They are the ones that prepared. They ordered stock early, before suppliers raised prices and before transport got slow. They set their prices before the rush, not during it. They planned their promotions, their delivery and their staff. And when the orders came flooding in, they were ready.

This guide shows you how to prepare your business for New Year sales in Nigeria, step by step. Start now, in late November, and you will enter December calm and ready — while your competitors are still scrambling.

## Step 1: Look at Last Year Before You Plan This Year

Before you order a single item, look back. If you were selling last December and January, check your records. What sold best? What sold out first? What did you order too much of and end up discounting in February? What did customers ask for that you did not have?

If you use an accounting app or a sales book, the answers are in your numbers. A food vendor might find that small chops sold out every weekend in December, while soups in litres peaked in the week before Christmas. A fashion seller might find that party dresses sold best in December, while office wear picked up in January when people returned to work. A phone seller might find that power banks and earphones were the most gifted items.

If you do not have last year's records, ask your customers and your staff. Ask your regulars what they plan to buy this New Year. Ask your tailor, your supplier or your rider what they saw last year. And look at your WhatsApp chats from last December, if you still have them. The questions customers asked then are the questions they will ask now.

Write down three lists: your best sellers, your slow movers, and the items customers asked for that you did not stock. Your New Year plan should be built on those three lists, not on a guess.

## Step 2: Order Stock Early — Before Prices Rise and Transport Slows

This is the most important step, and the one most businesses get wrong. In Nigeria, December is the worst month to buy stock at the last minute. Suppliers raise prices as demand rises. Markets get crowded. Transport from Lagos to Abuja, Onitsha or Kano gets slower and more expensive. A carton that cost ₦50,000 in November can cost ₦65,000 in mid-December, and the bus that took two days in November can take four days in December.

Order your core stock in late November, while prices are still normal and transport is still fast. Focus on your proven best sellers first. Buy enough to cover the Christmas week and the first two weeks of January, based on last year's sales plus a modest increase. If you sold 100 dresses last December, order 120, not 300. Over-ordering ties up your cash in stock that sits unsold in February.

Talk to your suppliers now. Ask them their December prices, their closing dates, and their delivery times. Some suppliers in Balogun, Onitsha Main Market and Ariaria close for a few days around Christmas and New Year. If your supplier closes on 23 December and reopens on 3 January, you need your stock before the 23rd, or you will have nothing to sell during the busiest week of the year.

Pay for stock in a way that protects you. If you must pay in advance, use a supplier you trust and keep the receipt and the chat record. If you can, pay a deposit and the balance on delivery. And inspect stock as soon as it arrives. A damaged carton discovered on 20 December can be replaced. The same carton discovered on 27 December, when the supplier is closed, cannot.

## Step 3: Set Your Prices Before the Rush

Do not wait until customers are already ordering to decide your prices. Set them now, while you are calm, and write them down.

Start with your costs. Your stock may cost more this year than last year, because the price of fabric, rice, chicken, phones and packaging has risen. Work out the true cost of each product, including transport and packaging, and set a price that leaves a real margin. Aim for at least 30 percent gross margin on most products. If your cost has risen by 20 percent and you keep last year's price, you may be selling at a loss without knowing it.

Then decide your New Year pricing strategy. You have three options, and you can mix them. First, keep your normal prices and compete on availability and service — you are in stock when others are sold out. Second, raise prices slightly on high-demand items that will sell out anyway, such as party food trays and Christmas hampers. Third, run a genuine promotion: a discount on a bundle, a free gift with a purchase, or free delivery above a certain amount.

Whatever you do, be honest. A "50 percent off" sign on a product whose price you doubled the day before is not a promotion, it is a trick, and Nigerian customers will notice. A real promotion is a real discount from your real normal price, for a clear period, with clear terms. "Buy two dresses, get the third at half price, from 15 December to 5 January" is clear. "Massive discount, hurry" is not.

Publish your New Year price list early, in your catalogue, on your WhatsApp status, and on your website. Customers who know the price before they order will buy faster, and you will spend less time negotiating in the middle of the rush.

## Step 4: Plan Your Promotions and Your Content

New Year sales are won on visibility. Customers will buy from the business they see most, and the business that makes buying easy. Plan your promotions and your content now, so you are not scrambling for ideas on 24 December.

Choose one clear offer for the season. One offer is enough. It could be a bundle: a Christmas food tray, a New Year outfit set, a hamper, or a "buy two, get one free" deal on your best seller. It could be free delivery within Lagos for orders above ₦50,000. It could be a small gift with every order — a branded pen, a small chops pack, a discount voucher for January. Pick the offer that fits your margins and your stock, and stick to it for the whole season. Changing your offer every two days confuses customers.

Plan your content in advance. Write your WhatsApp status posts, your Instagram captions and your Facebook posts for the first two weeks of December now, and save them in a note on your phone. Use photos of your actual products, your actual stock, and your actual prices. Post every day during the season: a product photo in the morning, a customer testimonial or a behind-the-scenes video in the afternoon, and a reminder of your offer in the evening. Customers buy from businesses that are visibly open, stocked and busy.

Set a start and end date for your promotion, and say it clearly. "New Year Sale: 15 December to 5 January." A clear end date creates urgency without pressure. And when the sale ends, end it. Extending a sale by two weeks because it worked well teaches customers to wait for the next extension.


### Use WhatsApp Status and Your Customer List

Your best New Year customers are the people who already bought from you. In early December, send your New Year offer to your broadcast lists — your repeat customers first, then your recent enquirers. One clear message with a photo, the price, and the last order date is enough. Do not send five messages a day; one good message a week will sell more than daily noise, and customers will not block you.

Post on your WhatsApp Status every morning during the season. Show the stock arriving, the hampers being packed, the dresses on the rack, and the food coming out of the pot. Real photos of real stock build more confidence than any polished advert, because customers can see that you are ready. Ask happy customers to share your status or forward your catalogue to their family groups. In December, a single forward in a church group or an estate group can bring ten new orders.

## Step 5: Sort Out Delivery Before the Orders Flood In

Delivery is where New Year sales are won or lost in Nigeria. A customer who orders a dress for a New Year party on 30 December needs it by 31 December, not 3 January. A customer who orders food for 1 January needs it on 1 January, hot and on time. If your delivery fails, the sale is lost, and so is the customer.

Book your riders early. Talk to your regular dispatch riders now and agree on their December availability, their prices, and their busiest days. Many riders are booked solid between 20 December and 2 January, and their prices rise. Agree your rates in advance, and keep the numbers of two or three backup riders. If your main rider falls sick or his bike breaks down on 28 December, you need a backup, not a panic.

Plan your delivery areas and cut-off times, and publish them. For example: "Orders in Lekki placed before 10 a.m. will be delivered the same day. Orders to Abuja and Port Harcourt placed before 12 noon on Monday will arrive by Wednesday. Our last delivery day before Christmas is 23 December, and we resume on 27 December." Clear cut-off times protect you from impossible promises.

For interstate orders, use waybill early. Parks and transport companies get crowded in December, and a parcel sent on 22 December may not arrive until 28 December. Send interstate orders as early as possible, tell the customer the expected arrival date, and send them the waybill number and the driver's phone number. For food and other perishable goods, be honest about what can travel and what cannot. It is better to refuse an interstate food order than to send food that arrives spoilt.

## Step 6: Prepare Your Money, Your Staff and Your Shop

Money first. December is a cash-heavy month, and you need cash flow, not just sales. Set a rule for the season: payment before dispatch for new customers, and for all interstate orders. For regular customers who have paid reliably, you can be more flexible, but do not extend credit during the rush. A customer who owes you ₦100,000 in January is a problem you will carry into the New Year.

Keep your business money separate from your personal money during the season. It is tempting to spend December profits on Christmas clothes, travel and parties. Set a weekly amount for yourself, and leave the rest in the business. You will need it in January, when sales slow down and rent, school fees and supplier bills still arrive.

Staff and help next. If you need extra hands — somebody to pack orders, answer chats, or deliver nearby — hire them now, and train them before the rush. A helper who starts on 23 December, with no training, will slow you down, not speed you up. Write down the simple rules they must follow: how to confirm an order, how to record a payment, how to pack a parcel, and what to say when a customer asks a question they cannot answer.

Finally, prepare your shop and your phone. Clean your shop, arrange your best stock where customers can see it, and make sure your sign and your contact details are visible. Charge your phone and your power bank every night. Back up your WhatsApp chats. Make sure your POS, your Opay and your Moniepoint accounts are working. A broken POS on 24 December, when customers are queuing, is a disaster you can prevent by testing it now.

## Step 7: Plan for January, Not Just December

The businesses that survive the New Year are the ones that plan past it. December is a peak. January is a valley. Sales slow down in the second half of January, when customers have spent their money and are waiting for the next salary. If you spend everything you made in December, January will be painful.

Set aside a January fund from your December profits. Before you spend anything on yourself, move a fixed amount — even ₦100,000 — into a separate account or a locked savings wallet, and do not touch it until January. That fund will pay your rent, your data, and your stock for January.

Plan a January offer too. A "New Year, New You" discount in the second week of January, a clearance sale on leftover December stock, or a loyalty offer for your December customers can keep sales moving when the market goes quiet. Send a thank-you message to every December customer in the first week of January, with a small discount for their next order. That message turns a one-time Christmas buyer into a January customer.

## Your New Year Preparation Checklist

Use this checklist, and tick each item off as you finish it.

- Review last year's sales and write your best sellers, slow movers and missing items.
- Order core stock in late November, before prices rise and transport slows.
- Confirm your suppliers' December prices, closing dates and delivery times.
- Set your prices and your New Year offer, and publish the price list.
- Plan your content for the first two weeks of December and save it on your phone.
- Book your riders, agree rates, and keep backup rider numbers.
- Publish your delivery areas, fees and cut-off times, including your last delivery day.
- Set your payment rule: payment before dispatch for new and interstate customers.
- Hire and train any extra help before the rush starts.
- Test your POS, bank apps and WhatsApp backup, and charge your power bank nightly.
- Set aside a January fund from December profits before you spend anything else.
