# How to Use Escrow Payments for Safe Transactions in Nigeria

A customer in Abuja sends ₦450,000 for a laptop. The seller in Lagos never ships it. Or the reverse happens: a seller ships a phone to Port Harcourt, and the buyer claims it never arrived and refuses to pay. Both stories are common in Nigeria, and both are the reason escrow payments exist.

Escrow is simple. A neutral third party holds the buyer's money until the buyer confirms they received what they paid for. Only then is the money released to the seller. If something goes wrong, the money stays safe while the dispute is resolved.

This guide explains exactly how escrow works in Nigeria, which platforms and services you can use, what it costs, and how to build escrow into your own business so customers trust you enough to pay first.

## What Is Escrow, in Plain Language?

Escrow is a payment arrangement with three parties: the buyer, the seller, and a trusted middleman called the escrow holder.

Here is the flow:

1. The buyer and seller agree on a price and delivery terms.
2. The buyer pays the money into the escrow account, not directly to the seller.
3. The seller ships the goods or delivers the service.
4. The buyer inspects what arrived and confirms it matches the agreement.
5. The escrow holder releases the money to the seller.

If the buyer does not confirm, or if the goods are wrong, damaged, or never arrive, the escrow holder steps in. Depending on the evidence, the money goes back to the buyer or is released to the seller. Nobody gets to keep both the money and the goods.

## Why Escrow Matters So Much in Nigeria

Nigeria has a large and growing online economy, but trust is still the biggest barrier to sales. A 2024 survey of Nigerian online shoppers repeatedly found the same fear at the top of the list: paying for something and never receiving it.

That fear costs sellers real money. Consider two Instagram vendors selling the same Ankara fabric at ₦35,000 per yard. One asks for full payment by bank transfer before delivery. The other offers escrow or payment on delivery. The second vendor will close far more sales, because the risk sits with a neutral party instead of the buyer.

Other situations where escrow is especially useful include:

- **High-value items:** phones, laptops, cars, generators, and industrial equipment where one bad deal can wipe out months of profit.
- **First-time transactions:** buying from a vendor you have never dealt with before, especially one you found on Instagram, Facebook Marketplace, or WhatsApp.
- **Custom orders:** wedding dresses, furniture, branded merchandise, and website projects where the buyer pays before the work exists.
- **Remote deals:** a buyer in Kano ordering from a seller in Enugu, where neither party can easily visit the other.
- **Freelance and digital services:** website design, logo work, and video editing, where the client pays a deposit and the freelancer delivers files.

## How Escrow Works Step by Step

The exact steps vary by platform, but the core process is the same everywhere. Here is a detailed walkthrough using a typical example: Ada in Abuja wants to buy a used iPhone 13 from Chidi, a phone dealer in Computer Village, Ikeja, for ₦520,000.

### Step 1: Agree on the terms

Before any money moves, both sides write down the agreement. This should cover the exact item, its condition, the price, the delivery method, the delivery date, and who pays the escrow fee. For Ada and Chidi, the terms are: one iPhone 13, 128GB, battery health above 85 percent, in the condition shown in the photos, delivered by courier to Abuja within three working days, for ₦520,000.

Write the terms inside the escrow platform, not only on WhatsApp. The written record is what the escrow holder uses if there is a dispute.

### Step 2: The buyer funds the escrow

Ada pays ₦520,000 plus the escrow fee into the escrow account using her bank transfer, card, or the platform's payment options. The money is held, not sent to Chidi. Ada gets a receipt and a transaction reference.

### Step 3: The seller is notified and ships

Chidi receives confirmation that the money is secured. He now ships the phone with a trackable courier and uploads the waybill number to the escrow platform. The tracking number matters: it proves the item was sent and when.

### Step 4: Delivery and inspection

The courier delivers the phone to Ada. She has an inspection window, usually 24 to 72 hours depending on the platform, to check the phone. She confirms the model, storage, battery health, and condition against the agreement and the photos.

### Step 5: Release or dispute

If everything matches, Ada clicks confirm, and the escrow holder releases ₦520,000 to Chidi, minus any seller-side fee. If the phone is wrong, damaged, or a different model, Ada opens a dispute instead of confirming. The money stays frozen while the escrow holder reviews photos, the waybill, and the chat history.

### Step 6: Resolution

The escrow holder decides based on evidence. If the seller is at fault, Ada gets a refund and returns the phone. If the buyer is being dishonest, the seller gets paid. Most disputes are resolved within a few days, though complex ones can take longer.

## Escrow Options Available to Nigerians

Nigerians can access escrow through several routes. Each has strengths and limits, so choose based on the size of the deal and how well you know the other party.

### 1. Marketplace escrow

Large marketplaces already build escrow into their checkout. When you buy on Jumia or Konga, your payment is held by the platform until you confirm delivery. You do not need to set anything up. The limitation is that this protection only works inside that marketplace. You cannot use Jumia's escrow for a deal you arranged on WhatsApp.

If you sell on these platforms, understand their release timelines. Jumia typically releases seller funds after delivery confirmation, with a settlement cycle that can take several days. Factor that delay into your cash flow planning.

### 2. Dedicated escrow platforms

Several Nigerian and international platforms offer standalone escrow services. These work for deals arranged anywhere: Instagram, WhatsApp, Facebook Marketplace, or offline. You create a transaction on the platform, invite the other party, agree the terms, and the platform holds the funds.

When evaluating a dedicated escrow platform, check these points before you use it:

- Is the company registered and identifiable, with a real office address and customer support line?
- Are funds held in a segregated account with a licensed bank or payment processor?
- What is the fee, and who pays it? Fees usually range from about 1 percent to 5 percent of the transaction value, sometimes with a minimum charge.
- How long is the inspection window after delivery?
- How are disputes handled, and how long do they take?
- What payment methods are accepted: bank transfer, card, or USSD?

Never use an escrow service recommended by the other party in a deal without checking it yourself. A common scam is for a fake buyer to insist on a fake escrow website that they control. Always verify the platform independently.

### 3. Payment processors with buyer protection

Paystack and Flutterwave, the two payment processors most Nigerian online sellers use, offer tools that add a layer of protection, though they are not full escrow services.

Paystack lets you create payment links and invoices, and its dispute process gives buyers a way to complain about a transaction. Flutterwave offers similar chargeback and dispute handling. These tools help, but they do not hold money until delivery in the way a true escrow service does. Treat them as a complement to escrow, not a replacement, especially for large orders.

If you run an online store, you can combine approaches: take card payments through Paystack or Flutterwave for standard orders, and offer escrow for high-value or custom orders where the buyer needs extra reassurance.

### 4. Bank and lawyer escrow for very large deals

For property, vehicles, and large business equipment, banks and law firms can act as escrow agents. The buyer deposits funds with the lawyer or bank, the seller transfers ownership, and the funds are released when the documents are confirmed. This is more expensive and slower, but it is appropriate when the deal runs into millions of naira and the paperwork matters as much as the goods.

A lawyer acting as escrow agent should give you a written escrow agreement stating the release conditions, the fees, and what happens in a dispute. Keep a copy.

## What Escrow Costs

Escrow is not free, and you should be clear about the cost before you start. Typical fee structures include:

- **Percentage of transaction value:** often 1 to 5 percent, with the exact rate depending on the platform and the size of the deal. A ₦500,000 transaction at 2 percent costs ₦10,000.
- **Flat fee:** some platforms charge a fixed amount per transaction, for example ₦1,000 to ₦5,000, regardless of value.
- **Minimum fee:** a platform may charge 2 percent with a minimum of ₦2,500, so small transactions cost proportionally more.
- **Split fees:** buyer and seller can agree to share the fee, or one side can absorb it as a cost of doing business.

Who pays is negotiable. In practice, sellers often absorb the fee on high-value items because escrow helps them close the sale. Buyers often pay it when they are the ones requesting the protection. State the arrangement in the written terms before funding.

## Escrow Scams to Watch Out For

Scammers know that escrow sounds safe, so they build fake escrow schemes around it. Watch for these red flags:

**The fake escrow website.** A buyer or seller sends you a link to an escrow site you have never heard of, with a professional-looking design. The site is theirs. Any money you send there is gone. Only use escrow platforms you found and verified yourself.

**The fake confirmation email.** You receive an email that looks like it comes from a real escrow platform saying payment has been received and you should ship. Check the sender address carefully, log into the platform directly rather than clicking the link, and never ship until you see the funds in your own escrow dashboard.

**The urgent buyer.** Someone pressures you to ship immediately, claiming they have already paid into escrow, but you cannot see the payment in the platform. Real escrow confirmation appears in your own account. If you cannot see it, it has not happened.

**The off-platform switch.** A deal starts on Jumia or another marketplace with built-in protection, and the other party asks you to move to WhatsApp and pay directly to save fees. That move removes your protection. Stay on the platform.

**The inspection-window trick.** A buyer asks you to ship without escrow but promises to pay on delivery, then delays inspection or claims damage after using the item. With escrow, the inspection window is fixed and documented. Without it, you have no leverage.

The golden rule is simple: if you cannot verify the escrow arrangement in an account you control, treat it as though it does not exist.

## How Sellers Can Offer Escrow and Win More Customers

If you sell online in Nigeria, offering escrow is a competitive advantage. Here is how to add it to your business properly.

### Start with your biggest-ticket items

You do not need escrow for a ₦5,000 order. Start with items above ₦50,000, where buyers hesitate most. Phones, laptops, furniture, generators, and custom orders are the natural starting point. Once the process is smooth, extend it to lower-value items if customers ask.

### Choose one platform and learn it well

Pick one escrow platform that fits your product and price range, and use it consistently. Learn its fee structure, its dispute process, and its payout timeline. When a customer asks how escrow works, you should be able to explain it in two sentences and send them a link in seconds. Hesitation kills trust.

### Put escrow on your product pages and WhatsApp catalogue

Do not hide the option. Add a line to your product descriptions: "Escrow payment available on this item. Your money is held safely until you confirm delivery." On WhatsApp Business, add the same note to your catalogue items and your business description. Customers who see the option before they ask are more likely to buy.

### Write clear terms for every escrow sale

For each escrow transaction, state the item, condition, price, delivery method, delivery date, inspection window, return condition, and who pays the fee. Send it to the buyer in writing and get their agreement before funding. Clear terms prevent most disputes before they start.

### Ship with tracking, always

Escrow disputes are decided on evidence. A waybill number, courier receipt, and photos of the item before packing are your evidence. Use a courier that provides tracking, such as GIG Logistics, DHL, or a reliable interstate bus cargo service with a receipt. Photograph the item, the packaging, and the sealed parcel. Keep these photos until the money is released.

### Price the fee into your margin

If you absorb the escrow fee, build it into your price. A ₦500,000 item with a 2 percent escrow fee costs you ₦10,000. Either add it to the price openly or treat it as a marketing cost, like the cost of running an advert. What you should not do is surprise the buyer with a fee at checkout.

## How Buyers Can Use Escrow Safely

If you are the buyer, escrow protects you only if you use it correctly. Follow these steps on every escrow purchase.

First, verify the seller independently. Check their business name, their Google Business Profile, their reviews, and their social media history. A seller with a real address, a consistent posting history, and genuine customer reviews is a lower risk than a brand-new account with no history.

Second, insist on written terms. The escrow transaction should describe the item exactly: brand, model, colour, storage, condition, and accessories included. Vague descriptions like "good condition iPhone" invite disputes. Specific descriptions prevent them.

Third, fund the escrow yourself through the official platform. Never send money to a personal account and call it escrow. Never pay through a link the seller sent you without checking the domain. Type the platform address yourself or use its official app.

Fourth, inspect properly when the item arrives. Do not confirm delivery in a hurry. Test the phone, check the laptop battery, count the items, and compare them with the photos. Use the full inspection window. Once you confirm, the money is released, and getting it back becomes much harder.

Fifth, keep all evidence. Save the escrow transaction record, the chat history, the photos, and the courier waybill. If a dispute arises, this file is what the escrow holder reviews.

## Escrow for Services and Freelance Work

Escrow is not only for physical goods. It works well for services, where the buyer pays before the work exists and the freelancer works before getting paid.

A typical arrangement for a website project works like this. A client in Lagos hires a developer to build a business website for ₦350,000. The client funds the full amount, or a first milestone of ₦175,000, into escrow. The developer builds the site and delivers a preview link. The client reviews it, requests changes, and confirms when satisfied. The escrow holder releases the milestone payment. The second milestone follows the same pattern for the final delivery.

Break large projects into milestones. A ₦1,500,000 mobile app project might have four milestones: design approval, core features, testing, and launch. Each milestone gets its own escrow release. This protects the client from paying everything upfront and protects the freelancer from doing weeks of work with no payment secured.

For freelancers, escrow platforms built for freelance work, including international ones, already work this way. If you hire freelancers on platforms that hold funds in escrow, use the feature. If you work directly with a client off-platform, propose a simple escrow arrangement through a Nigerian escrow service and explain the milestone plan in your proposal. Clients who understand the structure are more likely to agree to your price.

## Start With Your Next Big Sale

Escrow costs a small percentage and adds a day or two to a transaction, but it removes the fear that stops so many Nigerian online deals from happening. Use it on your next high-value sale or purchase, learn the process, and decide whether to make it a standard part of how you do business. The trust you build will be worth far more than the fee.
