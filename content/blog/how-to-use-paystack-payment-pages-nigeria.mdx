You sell hair, sneakers, food, or digital products on WhatsApp and Instagram. A customer says they want to buy, you send your account number, and then you wait. Sometimes they pay. Sometimes they forget. Sometimes they send a fake alert, or they pay and you have no easy way to match the payment to the order. It is slow, messy, and it costs you sales.

Paystack Payment Pages fix this problem. A payment page is a simple online checkout page that Paystack creates for you. It has your product name, your price, and a "Pay" button. Your customer clicks the link, pays with their card, bank transfer, or USSD, and the money goes straight into your Paystack balance, which you can withdraw to your bank account. You do not need a website. You do not need to write any code. You just need a Paystack account and a few minutes.

## What Are Paystack Payment Pages?

A Paystack Payment Page is a ready-made checkout page hosted by Paystack. Each page is for one product or service, and it has its own unique link, something like paystack.com/pay/yourbusiness-productname. When a customer opens that link on their phone, they see the product name, the price, and a payment form. They enter their name, email or phone number, choose how to pay, and complete the payment. Paystack confirms the payment and sends both of you an email receipt.

Think of it as a mini online shop for one product. You can create as many pages as you like — one for each product, one for a service, one for a ticket to an event, or one for a donation. You can also set a page to accept any amount the customer chooses, which is useful for services where the price varies, or for contributions.

Payment pages work well for Nigerian businesses because they accept the payment methods Nigerians actually use: Nigerian debit cards (Verve, Mastercard, Visa), bank transfers, USSD, and even Apple Pay and Google Pay for customers with those options. Your customer does not need a Paystack account to pay you. They just need their card or their bank app. The money from a payment goes into your Paystack balance first, not directly into your bank account. You then withdraw it to your Nigerian bank account whenever you want, or set up automatic settlements. Paystack usually settles funds to your bank account within one working day.

## Why Use Payment Pages Instead of Just Sending Your Account Number?

Sending your account number works, but it has problems that payment pages solve.

First, payment pages confirm payment instantly. When a customer pays through a payment page, Paystack verifies the payment and records it against that exact product. You can see in your dashboard who paid, how much they paid, and when. With a bank transfer, you have to wait for an alert, check the amount, and try to remember which customer sent it — and if two customers pay the same amount on the same day, you can easily mix them up.

Second, payment pages stop fake payment alerts. A common scam in Nigeria is a customer sending a fake bank alert or a doctored screenshot as "proof" of payment. With a payment page, you never rely on a screenshot. You check your Paystack dashboard. If the payment is there, it is real. If it is not there, it did not happen. This alone saves many sellers from losing goods to fraud.

Third, payment pages look professional. A customer who clicks a clean payment link with your business name trusts you more than a seller who only sends an account number in a chat. That trust matters most for new customers who have never bought from you before, especially if they found you through Instagram or a Facebook ad.

Fourth, payment pages let you sell while you sleep. Your link is always available. A customer can buy at 2 a.m. without waiting for you to reply to their message. If you sell digital products like ebooks, courses, or designs, you can even set up the page to deliver a download link automatically after payment. Finally, payment pages keep records. Every payment is logged, and you can download a report of your sales at the end of the week or month. That makes it easier to know which product is selling, how much you made, and what to restock.

## What You Need Before You Start

Setting up a payment page takes about 20 minutes if you have everything ready. Here is what you need:

- A Paystack account. If you do not have one, go to paystack.com and sign up with your email address and a password. You will need to verify your email.
- Your Bank Verification Number (BVN). Paystack asks for this to verify your identity.
- A Nigerian bank account in your name or your business name. This is where Paystack will send your money.
- A valid means of identification, such as your National Identity Card, driver's licence, international passport, or voter's card.
- Your business details. If you are a registered business, have your CAC certificate or business name registration ready. If you are not registered, you can still sign up as an individual or sole trader, but Paystack may ask for more information later.
- A phone number you control, and a working email address.

Paystack will review your account before you can receive real payments. This is called going live. The review usually takes a few hours to a couple of days. During the review, you can build your payment pages and test them, but you cannot collect real money until your account is approved. Do not be tempted to use someone else's Paystack account to collect payments for your business. If there is a problem, that person controls your money.

## How to Create Your First Payment Page: Step by Step

Once your Paystack account is ready, creating a payment page is straightforward. Here is the process:

1. Log in to your Paystack dashboard at dashboard.paystack.com on your phone or computer.
2. On the left menu, click on "Payments" and then "Payment Pages", or look for the "Payment Pages" option in your dashboard. The layout changes from time to time, but it is always under payments.
3. Click "Create Payment Page" or "New Payment Page".
4. Give your page a name. Use the product or service name, such as "Ankara Gown — Size 12" or "2-Day Digital Marketing Training". This name is what your customer sees, so make it clear.
5. Write a short description. One or two sentences are enough. For example: "Handmade Ankara gown, ready to wear, available in sizes 10 to 16. Delivery within Lagos takes 2 to 3 days."
6. Set the amount. Enter the price in naira. If the price varies, you can set the page to allow the customer to enter any amount, or set a fixed amount with an option for the customer to add a tip.
7. Choose what information to collect from the customer. Paystack lets you add custom fields, such as size, colour, delivery address, or phone number. Add only what you truly need. Every extra field is a reason for a customer to abandon the page. For most physical products, name, phone number, and delivery address are enough.
8. Upload a product image if you have one. A clear photo of the product increases trust and sales. Use a good photo with a clean background, taken in good light.
9. Check the page settings. You can choose whether to show the page on your Paystack storefront, and whether to redirect the customer to a thank-you page or a WhatsApp link after payment. Setting the redirect to your WhatsApp number is a smart move, because it brings the customer straight back to you to arrange delivery.
10. Click "Create Page" and copy the link. Paystack will give you a link like paystack.com/pay/yourbusiness-ankara-gown. Save it, test it yourself by opening it on your phone, and then start sharing it.

Before you share the link publicly, test the full flow. Ask a friend to pay a small amount, such as ₦100, through the page, and confirm that the money appears in your dashboard and that you receive the email receipt.

## How to Share Your Payment Link and Get Sales

A payment page only works if customers see it. Here are the best places to share it:

On WhatsApp Business, add the link to your product catalog. WhatsApp Business lets you list products with photos and prices, and you can put the payment link in the product description or send it directly in a chat when a customer asks to buy. You can also add the link to your WhatsApp status when you post a product, so viewers can tap and pay immediately. Keep your WhatsApp Business profile updated with your business name and a clear description.

On Instagram, put your most important payment link in your bio, and share product links in your stories using the link sticker. When you post a product photo, write the price in the caption and tell followers to tap the link in your bio or send you a DM for the payment link. If you have several products, you can use a free link-in-bio tool to list all your payment pages on one page, and put that single link in your bio.

On Facebook, add the payment link to your Page's shop section or post it with product photos. In Facebook groups where selling is allowed, post the product with its price and the payment link, but always follow the group's rules.

On a flyer or a business card, print a QR code that points to your payment page. Customers can scan it with their phone camera and pay on the spot. This works well at trade fairs, church bazaars, and markets in Lagos and Abuja, where customers see your product in person and want to pay immediately.

The key is to reduce friction. Every extra step between a customer deciding to buy and actually paying is a chance to lose the sale. A single link that takes them straight to a checkout page is the shortest path you can offer without a website.

## What Paystack Charges You

Paystack charges a fee on every successful payment, and it is important to understand it before you price your products.

For local Nigerian card payments, Paystack's standard fee is 1.5% of the transaction amount plus ₦100, and the fee is capped at ₦2,000. That means a ₦10,000 payment costs you ₦250 in fees, and a ₦200,000 payment costs you ₦2,000, not more, because of the cap. For international card payments, the fee is higher, usually around 3.9% plus ₦100. Bank transfer payments through Paystack also attract a fee, which is usually a flat amount depending on the payment method.

Paystack does not charge you a monthly fee for a basic account, and creating payment pages is free. You only pay when a customer pays you. That makes payment pages a low-risk way to start selling online.

You should build the fee into your prices. If a product costs you ₦8,000 and you sell it for ₦10,000, the Paystack fee of ₦250 leaves you with ₦9,750. That is still a profit, but if your margins are thin, add a small amount to your online price to cover it. Do not add the fee as a separate "Paystack charge" on the payment page, because customers dislike seeing extra charges. Just price the product to cover it.

## How to Confirm Payments and Manage Orders

When a customer pays through your payment page, Paystack sends you an email and a dashboard notification. You should check your dashboard, not just the customer's screenshot, before you deliver anything.

Your daily routine should be simple. Each morning, open your Paystack dashboard and check the transactions from the previous day. For each payment, note the customer's name, the product they bought, the amount, and any delivery details they entered. Then message each customer on WhatsApp to confirm their order and agree a delivery time.

For deliveries within Lagos, confirm the delivery address before you dispatch. For deliveries outside Lagos, confirm the state and the delivery fee, and collect the fee before you send the goods.

If a customer says they paid but you cannot find the payment in your dashboard, ask them for the payment reference number from their email receipt or their bank alert. You can search for that reference in your dashboard. If it is not there, the payment did not reach Paystack, and the customer should check with their bank. Never deliver goods based on a screenshot alone.

Settlements are also important. Paystack settles your balance to your bank account, usually the next working day. Check your bank account regularly to confirm that settlements are arriving. If a settlement is delayed by more than two working days, contact Paystack support.

## Tips to Sell More with Payment Pages

Use clear product names and honest descriptions. A page called "Hair" will not sell as well as "Bone Straight Wig, 22 Inches, Natural Colour". Customers buy what they understand.

Use good photos. A payment page with a clear, well-lit photo of the actual product converts far better than a page with no photo or a blurry one. Take photos in daylight, against a plain background, and show the product from more than one angle if you can.

Set a fixed price. Pages with a fixed price are easier for customers than pages where they must type an amount. Only use the "pay what you want" option for donations or services with variable pricing.

Follow up with customers who clicked but did not pay. If a customer asked for your payment link on WhatsApp and went quiet, send a polite follow-up the next day: "Good morning, just checking if you still want the Ankara gown. The payment link is still active." Many sales are closed by a single follow-up message.

Keep your pages up to date. If a product is out of stock, deactivate its page or update the description to say so. Nothing frustrates a customer more than paying for a product that is not available.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

The first mistake is sending the wrong link. If you have several payment pages, it is easy to send the link for the wrong product. Double-check the link before you send it, especially when you are chatting with several customers at once.

The second mistake is never testing the page. Some sellers create a page and share it without ever opening it themselves. Then a customer reports that the page shows the wrong price or a missing field. Test every page before you share it.

The third mistake is ignoring your dashboard. Some sellers rely only on bank alerts and never check Paystack. Then they miss a payment, or they do not notice that a settlement failed. Check your dashboard every day. It takes two minutes.

The fourth mistake is sharing your Paystack login with staff or an assistant. Your Paystack account controls your money. Never share your password or your login code with anyone. If you need staff to help with orders, Paystack allows you to add team members with limited permissions. Use that instead.

The fifth mistake is not completing your account verification. If Paystack asks for more documents, provide them quickly. An unverified account can be limited or suspended, and your money can be held. Keep your BVN, ID, and business documents ready, and respond to Paystack's emails promptly.
