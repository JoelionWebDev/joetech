Every day, millions of Nigerians visit Nairaland. They ask which phone to buy, where to find a good tailor in Lekki, how much a website should cost, which estate agent to trust in Abuja, and where to buy affordable furniture in Port Harcourt. They are not just browsing. Many of them are ready to buy, and they trust answers from real people more than they trust adverts.

That is why Nairaland, despite being one of the oldest forums in Nigeria, is still a powerful place to find customers. It costs nothing to join, it ranks well on Google, and a single helpful thread can bring you enquiries for months. But it only works if you use it the right way. Spam it, and you will be banned, ignored, or mocked. Help people, and you will build a reputation that brings customers to you.

This guide shows you how to use Nairaland properly: setting up a credible profile, choosing the right sections, writing posts people actually read, answering questions, and turning readers into paying customers without breaking the rules.

## What Nairaland Is — And Why It Still Works

Nairaland is a Nigerian online forum, founded in 2005, with sections covering almost everything: business, technology, jobs, politics, romance, health, education, property, cars, phones, fashion, and food. Users create threads, reply to others, and search old threads before asking a new question. A thread from 2023 can still appear on Google in 2026 when someone searches for the same problem.

For a business, that is valuable. A well-written thread about "How much does a business website cost in Lagos?" can be found by a new person every week. Unlike a WhatsApp Status that disappears in 24 hours, or an Instagram post that sinks in a day, a Nairaland thread keeps working. It is slow marketing, but it is durable marketing.

Nairaland works best for businesses where customers research before they buy: websites, branding, phones and gadgets, real estate, cars, education, training, health products, fashion, food, and services like cleaning, catering, photography and repairs. If your customer needs to ask questions before paying, Nairaland is a good place to be.

It works less well for impulse products that people buy without thinking, and it does not work at all if you treat it like a billboard. Nairaland users are quick to spot adverts disguised as advice, and they will call it out publicly. Your approach must be helpful first, promotional second.

## Step 1: Create a Credible Profile

Before you post anything, set up your account properly. Register with a username that matches your business or your name, not a random string of numbers. "Joetech" or "IgonoJoel" is far more credible than "user938475." Use a clear profile picture — your logo or a professional photo of you — and fill in your signature with your business name, what you do, your location, and your contact details.

Your signature appears under every post you make, so it is valuable space. Keep it short and useful: "Joetech — Websites, Branding & Digital Marketing, Lekki, Lagos. WhatsApp: 0816 709 9131. joetech360.ng." That tells a reader who you are without you having to repeat it in every thread.

Do not create multiple accounts to praise your own business. Nairaland moderators and users can spot this quickly, and it destroys trust. One honest account, used consistently, is worth more than five fake accounts.

Spend your first few days reading, not posting. Search for threads about your industry. See what questions people ask, what answers get praised, and what posts get ignored or reported. Notice the tone: Nairaland rewards direct, practical answers, not corporate language. A reply that says "Here is what I charge and why" will do better than a reply full of marketing slogans.

## Step 2: Choose the Right Sections

Posting in the wrong section is one of the fastest ways to get ignored or moved by a moderator. Nairaland has dozens of sections, and each has its own audience and rules. Take time to find the ones that fit your business.

Useful sections for many businesses include Business, Technology, Webmasters, Computers, Phones, Properties, Autos, Jobs and Vacancies, Education, Health, Fashion, and Food. A website designer should spend time in Business, Technology and Webmasters. A real estate agent belongs in Properties. A fashion seller belongs in Fashion and Business. A food vendor belongs in Food and Business.

Read the pinned rules at the top of each section before you post. Some sections ban direct adverts. Some require a price in the title. Some ban links. Breaking a section rule can get your thread hidden or your account banned, so five minutes of reading saves you a lot of trouble.

Also pay attention to where your customers actually are. If you sell luxury homes in Lekki, the Properties section matters more than the general Business section. If you sell phone accessories, Phones matters most. Go where the buyers already gather, instead of shouting in a section they never visit.

## Step 3: Answer Questions Before You Advertise

The golden rule of Nairaland is simple: help first, sell second. If your first ten posts are all adverts for your business, people will ignore you. If your first ten posts are helpful answers, people will start to trust you, and then they will want to know what you sell.

Search for questions you can answer honestly. "How do I start a POS business?" "What is the best laptop for a student under ₦300,000?" "How much should I pay for a logo in Nigeria?" "Who can design a website for my church?" Answer in plain language, with real numbers, real steps, and real examples from Lagos, Abuja or Port Harcourt.

Be specific. Instead of "It depends," say "For a small business website with five pages, expect ₦350,000 to ₦550,000 in Lagos, depending on the design and whether you need a blog." Specific answers get thanked, quoted and remembered. Vague answers get ignored.

Do not answer every question. Answer the ones where you genuinely know the answer. If you do not know, say so, or say nothing. A wrong answer on Nairaland will be corrected publicly, and that correction will follow your username.

When you answer well, add a short, natural line at the end if it is relevant: "I build websites for small businesses in Lekki, so feel free to message me if you need help." That is not spam. That is a helpful person offering a service. The difference is that the help came first.

## Step 4: Write Threads People Actually Read

When you are ready to start your own threads, write them like a helpful guide, not like a flyer. A good Nairaland thread has a clear title, a useful first post, and a reason for people to reply.

Your title matters more than anything else. It should state a clear benefit or a clear question. "How to Choose a Website Designer in Lagos (And Avoid Losing Your Money)" will get more clicks than "Best Website Designer in Lagos — Cheap Prices!!!" The first promises useful information. The second looks like an advert, and Nairaland users have learned to skip adverts.

Your first post should deliver value immediately. Do not write two lines and then say "DM me for details." Write a full, useful post: the steps, the prices, the mistakes to avoid, and the questions to ask. A thread that teaches something will be shared, bookmarked and found on Google. A thread that only says "We sell X, call us" will sink.

Use short paragraphs, simple headings, and lists. Nairaland readers are often on their phones, on slow data, and in a hurry. Long walls of text without breaks are hard to read. Break your post into sections, use numbered steps, and keep sentences short.

Include a clear call to action at the end, but keep it to one or two lines. "If you need a website for your business, message me on WhatsApp on 0816 709 9131 or visit joetech360.ng." That is enough. Do not repeat your phone number five times in the same post.

## Step 5: Post in the Marketplace the Right Way

Nairaland has a marketplace culture, and many buyers go there specifically to buy. If you sell physical products, you can post in the relevant buy-and-sell sections, but you must follow the format that buyers expect.

State the price in the post. Nairaland buyers hate "DM for price." A post without a price looks suspicious, and many users will not bother to ask. State the exact price, the condition (new or used), the location, and the delivery options. "Original Samsung Galaxy A54, used, ₦185,000, Lekki, Lagos. Delivery available in Lagos for ₦2,500, nationwide by dispatch."

Add clear photos. Take them in daylight, show the real product from several angles, and include a photo of any defect if the item is used. Honest photos build trust and reduce complaints. A buyer who sees a scratch in the photo and still buys will not complain when the item arrives.

Do not post the same advert ten times a day. One good thread, updated with a reply when you have new stock, is better than ten identical threads. Repetition looks like spam and can get you banned. If your thread sinks, you can reply to it with a useful update — a new price, a new colour, a customer review — which brings it back to the top without breaking the rules.

Be careful with payment. Nairaland has scammers, and buyers know it. Offer payment on delivery where possible, or use a trusted escrow or a verifiable business account. Never ask a new buyer to pay the full amount upfront to a personal account with no proof of your business. Build trust first, and sales will follow.

## Step 6: Build Trust With Proof, Not Promises

On Nairaland, trust is everything. Users cannot see your shop, so they judge you by your words, your history, and your proof.

Share real work. If you are a web designer, share screenshots of websites you built, with the client's permission. If you are a tailor, share photos of clothes you made, worn by real customers. If you are a food vendor, share photos of your food in real packaging. Real work beats a long list of claims.

Share customer feedback. A short testimonial — "Mrs Ade from Ikeja ordered 20 lunch packs and said the food arrived hot and on time" — is more convincing than "We are the best in Lagos." Ask happy customers if you can quote them, and use their first name and area, not a full personal detail.

Be consistent. A user who has posted helpfully for six months is trusted far more than a new account that appeared yesterday with a big advert. Nairaland shows your join date and post count, and experienced users check them. Consistency is your credibility.

Handle complaints publicly and calmly. If a customer complains in your thread, do not delete the comment or insult them. Reply politely, explain what happened, and offer to fix it. A calm, fair reply to a complaint can win you more customers than a perfect advert, because it shows how you treat people when things go wrong.

Never claim what you cannot prove. Do not say you have an office in Victoria Island if you work from home in Ajah. Do not say you have served government agencies if you have not. Nairaland users will check, and a lie will follow you across the forum.

## Step 7: Turn Readers Into Customers

Traffic is not sales. A thread with 5,000 views is useless if nobody messages you. You need a clear path from the thread to the sale.

Make it easy to contact you. Put your WhatsApp number in your signature and at the end of your posts. A WhatsApp link is even better, because the reader can tap once and start chatting. If you have a website, link to the exact page for the product or service, not just the homepage.

Reply quickly. When someone replies to your thread or sends you a private message, reply the same day, ideally within an hour. Nairaland users often message three sellers at once and buy from the first one who replies helpfully. A slow reply loses the sale to a faster competitor.

Move the conversation to WhatsApp or a call once the person is interested. Nairaland is good for discovery, but WhatsApp is better for closing a sale, sharing photos, confirming payment, and arranging delivery. Do not try to do the whole sale in a public thread.

Keep a record of where your customers come from. When a new customer messages you, ask politely: "How did you find us?" If they say "Nairaland," note which thread. Over time, you will learn which threads bring buyers and which bring only views, and you can focus your effort on the ones that work.

## What Not to Do on Nairaland

- **Do not spam.** Posting the same advert in five sections, or replying to every thread with your advert, will get you reported and banned.
- **Do not use fake accounts to praise yourself.** Users and moderators will notice, and your reputation will be damaged.
- **Do not insult other users.** Arguments in a thread may get views, but they scare away buyers.
- **Do not post without a price.** "DM for price" frustrates buyers and reduces enquiries.
- **Do not share fake testimonials.** One exposed fake review can end your credibility on the forum.
- **Do not ignore the moderators.** If a moderator moves or hides your thread, read the reason, learn from it, and do not repost the same thread immediately.
- **Do not promise what you cannot deliver.** A failed delivery to a Nairaland customer can become a public complaint thread that ranks on Google.

## A Simple Weekly Nairaland Routine

You do not need to spend all day on Nairaland. A focused routine of about 30 minutes a day, five days a week, is enough to build a presence.

1. **Monday:** Search for three questions in your industry and answer them helpfully.
2. **Tuesday:** Reply to comments on your own threads, and thank people who replied.
3. **Wednesday:** Start one useful thread, such as a guide, a price breakdown, or a common mistake to avoid.
4. **Thursday:** Answer two new questions and update a marketplace post if you have new stock.
5. **Friday:** Review your messages. Which threads brought enquiries? Which brought nothing? Plan next week's thread based on what worked.

After a month, you will have 20 helpful answers, four useful threads, and a profile that looks trustworthy. After three months, you will have a steady stream of enquiries from people who found you through a search or a thread they read weeks ago. That is how Nairaland works: slow at first, then steady.

Nairaland rewards patience and honesty. It punishes spam and shortcuts. If you show up, answer questions, share real work, state your prices clearly, and treat people well, the forum will send you customers — not just once, but for months after each good thread you write.
