# How to Handle Chargebacks and Payment Disputes as a Nigerian Seller

You sold a smartwatch for ₦85,000 on your online store. The customer paid by card, the courier delivered it, and the money settled into your account. Three weeks later, ₦85,000 disappears from your balance, plus a ₦15,000 chargeback fee. The customer told their bank they never received the item, or never authorised the payment. You have the delivery proof, but the money is already gone.

That is a chargeback, and it is one of the most painful experiences a Nigerian online seller can face. You lose the product, the sale, and a fee, all at once.

This guide explains what chargebacks are, why they happen in Nigeria, how to respond when one hits your Paystack or Flutterwave account, and, most importantly, how to prevent most of them before they happen.

## What Is a Chargeback?

A chargeback is when a cardholder asks their bank to reverse a card payment. The bank takes the money from the seller's account and returns it to the cardholder while the claim is investigated.

Chargebacks were designed to protect consumers from fraud: a stolen card, a payment they never made, or goods that never arrived. But the process is heavily weighted toward the buyer. The bank starts from the assumption that the customer may be right, and the seller must prove otherwise with evidence.

This is different from a refund. A refund is a voluntary return of money by the seller, usually after the customer complains directly. A chargeback bypasses the seller entirely. The customer goes to the bank, the bank acts, and the seller finds out after the money is gone.

## How a Chargeback Works in Nigeria

The process usually follows these steps:

1. **The customer complains to their bank.** They call their bank or visit a branch and say a payment was unauthorised, or that goods or services were not delivered or not as described.
2. **The bank raises the chargeback.** The customer's bank, called the issuing bank, sends the claim through the card network to your payment processor, Paystack or Flutterwave.
3. **The processor notifies you.** You get an email and a dashboard alert telling you a chargeback has been filed, the amount, the reason code, and the deadline to respond. Deadlines are short, often 7 to 14 days.
4. **The money is debited.** The transaction amount, plus a chargeback fee, is taken from your account balance or your next settlement. On Paystack, the fee is typically around ₦15,000 per chargeback. Flutterwave's fee varies by case.
5. **You submit evidence.** You upload proof: the invoice, the delivery waybill, chat history, photos, and your terms. This is called representment.
6. **The bank decides.** The issuing bank reviews your evidence and decides whether to uphold or reverse the chargeback. This can take 30 to 90 days. If you win, the money is returned. If you lose, the money stays with the customer.

## The Main Reasons Customers File Chargebacks

Chargebacks fall into a few clear categories. Knowing which one you face tells you how to respond.

### True fraud

Someone used a stolen card to buy from your store. The real cardholder spots the transaction and reports it. You did nothing wrong, but you still lose the money and the goods, because the payment was never legitimate. This is the most common cause of chargebacks for sellers of phones, laptops, gadgets, and other high-value items that fraudsters can resell quickly.

### Goods not received

The customer says the item never arrived. Sometimes the courier failed. Sometimes the item was delivered to a neighbour or a gateman and the customer did not know. Sometimes the customer is lying. Without a signed proof of delivery, this claim is hard to fight.

### Goods not as described

The item arrived, but the customer says it was the wrong colour, the wrong size, damaged, or a fake. This often comes from poor product photos or vague descriptions. A dress that looked red in the photo but arrived maroon can trigger a chargeback if the customer feels misled.

### Friendly fraud

This is the most frustrating category. The customer received the goods, used them, and then filed a chargeback anyway, claiming they never got them or never authorised the payment. They may have forgotten the purchase, or a family member used their card, or they simply want the item for free. Friendly fraud is rising in Nigeria as more people shop online, and it is the hardest to prove without strong delivery evidence.

### Processing errors

Duplicate charges, wrong amounts, or a payment taken after a refund was already given. These are usually your fault, or your system's fault, and the fastest response is to refund quickly and apologise before the customer goes to the bank.

## What a Chargeback Really Costs You

The visible cost is the transaction amount plus the fee. But the real cost is bigger:

- **The fee.** Around ₦15,000 per chargeback on Paystack, and a comparable fee on Flutterwave. On a ₦20,000 sale, the fee alone is 75 percent of the sale value.
- **The lost product.** If you shipped the item and the chargeback succeeds, you lose the goods and the money.
- **Your chargeback ratio.** Card networks track the percentage of your transactions that become chargebacks. If your ratio goes above roughly 1 percent, your payment processor may flag your account, raise your fees, hold your settlements, or close your account.
- **Your time.** Gathering evidence, writing a response, and following up takes hours per case.
- **Your reputation with your processor.** Repeated chargebacks mark you as a high-risk merchant, which can lead to longer settlement delays and stricter reviews.

A seller doing ₦2,000,000 a month in card sales with a 1 percent chargeback rate loses ₦20,000 in sales plus about ₦15,000 in fees per case. Two cases a month means roughly ₦70,000 gone, before counting the lost products. Prevention is far cheaper than fighting chargebacks after the fact.

## What to Do the Moment a Chargeback Arrives

Speed and evidence decide whether you win. Treat every chargeback alert as urgent.

### Step 1: Read the reason code

Open the dispute in your Paystack or Flutterwave dashboard and read the exact reason: fraud, goods not received, goods not as described, processing error, or credit not processed. Your response must match the reason. Evidence that proves delivery does not help if the claim is about a duplicate charge.

### Step 2: Check your own records

Find the order. Confirm the date, the amount, the card used, the delivery address, and what was shipped. Check your chat history with the customer. Did they complain to you first? Did they confirm delivery? Did they ask for a refund? Save screenshots of everything.

### Step 3: Contact the customer

If the reason is goods not received or not as described, message the customer politely. Ask what happened. Sometimes the item was delivered to a neighbour and the customer did not know. Sometimes a refund would have satisfied them, and they filed a chargeback because they could not reach you. A calm message can lead the customer to withdraw the chargeback, which is the fastest possible win.

Do not threaten the customer or accuse them of fraud in writing. Keep the tone professional. Your messages may be read by the bank.

### Step 4: Gather your evidence pack

Build a file for the transaction. Include:

- The order invoice or receipt showing the item, price, and date.
- The payment confirmation.
- The delivery waybill with tracking number, and the courier's delivery confirmation page.
- A photo or scan of the signed proof of delivery, if you have one.
- Photos of the item as listed and as packed.
- Screenshots of your chat with the customer, especially any message where they confirmed receipt or were happy with the item.
- Your refund and return policy as published on your website.
- Your terms of service and the customer's acceptance of them at checkout.

For digital products or services, include login records, download logs, email delivery confirmations, and screenshots of the delivered work.

### Step 5: Submit before the deadline

Upload your evidence through the Paystack or Flutterwave dashboard before the deadline shown. Late submissions are usually rejected automatically. Write a short, factual summary: what was ordered, when it was paid for, when it was delivered, and what proof you are attaching. Number your evidence so the reviewer can follow it.

### Step 6: Follow up and record the outcome

After submitting, track the case. If you win, the money is returned to your account. If you lose, note the reason and check whether anything in your process could have prevented it. Keep a simple chargeback log: date, order number, amount, reason, evidence submitted, and outcome. Patterns in that log will show you where your business is vulnerable.

## How to Respond on Paystack

Paystack sends chargeback notifications by email and shows them in the Disputes section of your dashboard. Open the dispute to see the amount, the reason, and the response deadline.

Paystack provides a guided evidence form. Upload your documents in the formats requested, usually PDF or image files, and keep each file clearly named: "Invoice-ORDER-1234.pdf" rather than "document1.pdf".

Paystack's team reviews your submission before sending it to the bank. If your evidence is weak, they may tell you. Listen to that feedback. It is better to accept a loss on a weak case and focus your energy on the strong ones than to submit thin evidence everywhere.

Note that Paystack deducts the disputed amount and fee from your balance as soon as the chargeback is raised. If your balance is too low, it will be taken from your next settlement. Keep a buffer in your account so a chargeback does not bounce your other obligations.

## How to Respond on Flutterwave

Flutterwave's process is similar. Disputes appear in your dashboard under Transactions or Disputes, with the reason and the deadline. You respond by uploading evidence through the dashboard.

Flutterwave also allows you to message the customer through its system in some cases. Use that channel if it is available, because the conversation is recorded and can support your case.

One difference to note: Flutterwave processes payments for many international merchants, so its dispute timelines can follow the card network's international rules. Deadlines may be shorter than you expect. Check the dashboard daily when a dispute is open, and do not wait until the last day to submit.

## Preventing Chargebacks: The Seller's Checklist

Winning a chargeback is good. Not getting one at all is better. Most chargebacks are preventable with a few disciplined habits.

### Use clear, honest product listings

Take your own photos. Show the item from several angles. State the colour, size, material, brand, and condition exactly. If a phone has a small scratch, say so and show it. If a dress runs small, say so. Customers who know exactly what they are buying rarely claim the item was not as described.

Write your prices and delivery fees clearly. Surprise charges at checkout are a common trigger for disputes.

### Confirm every order with the customer

Before you ship a card-paid order above ₦50,000, confirm it. Send a WhatsApp message: "Thank you for your order of one Samsung Galaxy A54, ₦285,000, to be delivered to 14 Allen Avenue, Ikeja. Please confirm this is correct." A confirmed order is strong evidence, and it catches mistakes like wrong addresses before they become disputes.

For very high-value orders, call the customer. A two-minute call that confirms the order and the delivery address stops a large share of fraud, because fraudsters using stolen cards rarely want to talk on the phone.

### Ship with tracking and proof of delivery

Never ship a card-paid order without a trackable courier. Use GIG Logistics, DHL, or another courier that gives you a tracking number and a delivery confirmation page. For orders above ₦100,000, ask the courier for a signed proof of delivery and keep the scan.

Photograph the parcel before sealing it, with the waybill visible. If a customer claims the box arrived empty, that photo is your defence.

### Make refunds easy

Many chargebacks happen because the customer could not get a refund from you. If your WhatsApp replies are slow, your return address is unclear, or your policy says "no refunds," the customer goes to the bank instead.

Publish a clear refund and return policy on your website and in your WhatsApp catalogue. State how many days the customer has to return an item, who pays return shipping, and how long a refund takes. Then honour it. A ₦40,000 refund is cheaper than a ₦40,000 chargeback plus a ₦15,000 fee.

### Send delivery confirmation messages

When the courier marks an order delivered, message the customer: "Your order has been delivered. Please confirm you received it in good condition." If they reply yes, screenshot it. That single message is one of the strongest pieces of evidence you can submit.

### Use 3D Secure and AVS checks

Make sure your Paystack or Flutterwave checkout uses 3D Secure, which asks the customer to confirm the payment with a code from their bank. 3D Secure shifts liability for fraud chargebacks toward the bank in many cases, because the bank verified the cardholder. Check your processor settings to confirm it is enabled.

Paystack and Flutterwave also offer fraud tools such as velocity checks, which flag multiple rapid orders from the same card. Turn these on. They will block some legitimate orders, but they block far more fraudulent ones.

### Watch for fraud signals

Be cautious with orders that show several of these signs at once:

- A first-time customer ordering several high-value items in one go.
- A delivery address that is very different from the billing address, especially in another city.
- Multiple failed card attempts followed by a successful one.
- An order placed late at night for expensive gadgets with rush delivery.
- A customer who refuses to confirm the order by phone or WhatsApp.

Any one of these alone is normal. Several together should make you pause, confirm the order, or ask for payment by bank transfer instead of card.

### Keep records for at least 180 days

Card networks allow chargebacks for up to 120 days after the transaction, and in some cases longer. Keep your invoices, waybills, chat screenshots, and delivery confirmations for at least six months. A simple folder per month on Google Drive, with one subfolder per order number, is enough. When a chargeback arrives on day 110, you will be glad the evidence is still there.

## Chargebacks for Services and Digital Products

Service businesses face a different version of the problem. A client pays ₦250,000 for a logo, receives the files, and then files a chargeback claiming the service was not delivered. There is no courier waybill to prove delivery.

Protect yourself with a paper trail. Use a written contract or proposal that the client accepts before payment. Break the project into milestones with written sign-off at each stage. Deliver files by email so there is a timestamped record. Keep the client's approval messages. When a client writes "I love it, thank you," screenshot it immediately.

For online courses and digital downloads, keep access logs showing when the customer logged in, which lessons they opened, and what they downloaded. A customer who completed six lessons before filing a chargeback has a weak case, and your logs prove it.

For subscription services, send a reminder email three days before each renewal, and make cancellation a one-click process in the customer's account. Customers who can cancel easily rarely file chargebacks.

## Building a Chargeback-Resistant Business

The sellers who suffer least from chargebacks share a few habits. They keep every order as a file: invoice, waybill, chat, and delivery confirmation, all saved in one place. They reply to customers fast, because a customer who gets an answer within an hour rarely escalates to the bank. They are honest in their listings, so the photo and the description match the product. They switch on the fraud tools their processor provides instead of leaving them off. And they watch their numbers: monthly chargeback count, chargeback ratio, and the reason behind each case. When the number rises, they change something, whether that is tighter confirmation for high-value orders, a different courier, clearer photos, or a simpler refund process.

Chargebacks will never disappear entirely. As long as cards exist, some customers will misuse the protection they offer. But a seller who documents everything, delivers with proof, and answers customers quickly can keep chargebacks rare, win most of the ones that do arrive, and protect the business they worked hard to build.
